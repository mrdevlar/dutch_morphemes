From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`bloeien`** (to bloom/flower) can be analyzed through two lenses: **synchronic morphology** (how the word is structured in modern Dutch) and **diachronic morphology** (its historical etymological development).

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes.

---

### 1. Synchronic Analysis (Modern Dutch)
In contemporary Dutch, the word is segmented into two primary morphemes: the **lexical stem** and the **inflectional suffix**.

| Morpheme | Form | Type | Function/Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`bloei-`** | Stem | Lexical (Root) | Carries the core semantic meaning: "flowering" or "bloom". |
| **`-en`** | Suffix | Inflectional | Marks the verb as the **infinitive** form. |

**Explanation:**
*   **`bloei-`**: This is the verbal stem. It is identical to the noun *de bloei* (the bloom/flowering). In modern Dutch, this is treated as a single, indivisible unit of meaning within this specific word.
*   **`-en`**: This is the standard infinitive marker for Dutch verbs (e.g., *lopen*, *werken*, *zien*). It indicates that the verb is in its base form, unconjugated.

**Phonological Note:**
*   The spelling `oei` represents the diphthong **/ui/** (as in *huis*).
*   The ending `-en` is typically pronounced as a schwa **/ə/** in standard speech (e.g., /ˈbluː.jə/), though the /n/ may be pronounced in careful speech or at the end of a sentence.

### 2. Diachronic Analysis (Etymology & History)
To understand *why* the stem is spelled `bloei-` rather than `bloem-` (flower), we must look at the historical derivation. This reveals a hidden morphological layer that is no longer productive in modern Dutch.

**Historical Breakdown:**
1.  **Root:** **`bloem-`** (Proto-Germanic *\*blōmō*, meaning "flower").
2.  **Derivational Suffix:** **`-i-`** (Proto-Germanic *-jan-*).
3.  **Inflectional Suffix:** **`-en`** (Proto-Germanic *-aną*).

**The Process:**
*   **Base:** The noun *bloem* (flower).
*   **Derivation:** In Old Dutch, a verbal suffix *-jan-* was added to the root to create a verb meaning "to cause to flower" or "to flower." This suffix triggered a phonological change (palatalization) that turned the *m* into a vowel glide and added an *i* sound.
*   **Result:** *bloem* + *-jan-* $\rightarrow$ *bloeien*.

**Linguistic Significance:**
While modern speakers perceive `bloei` as a single stem, historically it is a **denominal verb** (a verb derived from a noun). The `i` in `bloei` is a fossilized remnant of the old verbalizer suffix *-jan-*.

### 3. Grammatical Context
To see how these morphemes function in the language, we can look at the conjugation. The morpheme boundaries remain consistent, but the inflectional ending changes.

*   **Infinitive:** `bloei` + `en` (bloeien)
*   **Present (Ik):** `bloei` + `` (bloeien $\rightarrow$ ik bloei)
*   **Present (Hij):** `bloei` + `t` (bloeit)
*   **Past (Weak):** `bloei` + `de` (bloeide)
*   **Past Participle:** `ge` + `bloei` + `d` (gebloeid)

**Observation:**
The stem **`bloei-`** remains invariant across all tenses. This confirms that `bloei-` is the stable lexical morpheme, while the endings (`-en`, `-t`, `-de`, `-d`) are inflectional morphemes that change based on tense, person, and number.

### Summary
*   **Word:** `bloeien`
*   **Synchronic Morphemes:** `bloei` (Stem) + `en` (Infinitive)
*   **Diachronic Morphemes:** `bloem` (Root) + `i` (Verbalizer) + `en` (Infinitive)
*   **Meaning:** To produce flowers; to flourish.
