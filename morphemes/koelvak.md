From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`koelvak`** is a **compound noun** (*samengesteld zelfstandig naamwoord*). It consists of two free morphemes joined together to create a new lexical item with a specific meaning.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into two distinct morphemes:

> **koel** + **vak**

There are no inflectional morphemes (such as plural markers or verb endings) attached to this specific form; it is the singular, indefinite noun.

---

### 2. Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The First Morpheme: `koel`
*   **Part of Speech:** Adjective (functioning as a modifier).
*   **Meaning:** "Cool" or "cold."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *kōl*, related to Old High German *kūl* and English *cool*. It stems from the Proto-Germanic root **\*kōluz*.
*   **Function in Compound:** This is the **modifier** (or dependent). It restricts the meaning of the head noun. It specifies the *quality* or *condition* of the compartment. In Dutch compounding, the adjective appears in its stem form (without the inflectional *-e* that might appear in a sentence like *een koel vak*).

#### B. The Second Morpheme: `vak`
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun.
*   **Meaning:** "Compartment," "section," "slot," or "field."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *vac*, likely borrowed from Low German *fack* or related to Old French *fais* (bundle/section). It is cognate with the German *Fach*.
*   **Function in Compound:** This is the **head** (or head noun). In Dutch compounds, the rightmost element determines the grammatical category of the whole word. Since *vak* is a noun, *koelvak* is a noun. Semantically, the compound refers to a specific *type* of *vak*.

---

### 3. Compound Structure and Semantics

*   **Type of Compound:** **Endocentric**.
    *   The meaning of the whole word is a hyponym (a specific subtype) of the head. A *koelvak* is a type of *vak*.
*   **Relationship:** **Modifier-Head**.
    *   The relationship is attributive: "A *vak* that is *koel*."
*   **Orthography:** It is a **closed compound**. In modern Dutch, these two words are written together without a space or hyphen.
*   **Phonology (Stress):** In Dutch compounds, the primary lexical stress typically falls on the **first element**.
    *   Pronunciation: **KOEL**-vak (IPA: /ˈkul.vɑk/).
    *   This distinguishes it from a phrase where stress might be distributed differently.

### 4. Contextual Usage
While the literal translation is "cool compartment," in modern usage, *koelvak* almost exclusively refers to a specific section within a refrigerator (*koelkast*).
*   It often distinguishes a shelf or drawer designed for cooling (but not freezing) from the main cooling area or the freezer (*vriesvak*).
*   Sometimes it refers specifically to the vegetable drawer, as that area is kept cool but often slightly less cold than the rest of the fridge to prevent freezing produce.
