From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`redelijk`** is a derivational adjective formed from a nominal root. Below is a detailed morphological breakdown, etymological context, and semantic analysis.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation

The word can be segmented into two primary morphemes:

> **red-** + **-elijk**

*   **red-**: The lexical root (stem).
*   **-elijk**: The derivational suffix.

---

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Root: `red-`
*   **Source:** This stem is derived from the Dutch noun **`rede`** (meaning "reason," "logic," or "speech").
*   **Morphophonology:** In Dutch morphology, when deriving adjectives from nouns ending in a schwa (like *rede*), the final vowel is typically dropped to create the stem.
    *   *Noun:* `rede` /ˈreː.də/
    *   *Stem:* `red-` /reːd/
*   **Etymology:** The root traces back to Old Dutch *rēda* and Proto-Germanic *\*rēdą*. It is cognate with the German *Rat* (counsel/advice) and the archaic English sense of "read" (as in "to counsel" or "to advise," related to the modern word *reason* via the concept of logic, though *reason* itself is Latinate).
*   **Meaning:** Logic, intellect, rationality, or argument.

#### B. The Suffix: `-elijk`
*   **Type:** Derivational suffix (forms adjectives from nouns or verbs).
*   **Base Form:** The standard adjectival suffix in Dutch is **`-lijk`** (cognate with English *-ly* and German *-lich*).
*   **Allomorphy:** The form **`-elijk`** is an allomorph of `-lijk`. The epenthetic vowel **`-e-`** (schwa) is inserted between the stem and the suffix for phonological ease.
    *   *Phonological Rule:* In Dutch, it is often difficult to pronounce a sequence of a dental stop (`d`) followed immediately by a lateral (`l`) without a vowel buffer. Therefore, *red-lijk* becomes *red-elijk*.
    *   *Comparison:*
        *   *Mens* (human) + *lijk* → *Menselijk* (human-like)
        *   *Vriend* (friend) + *lijk* → *Vriendelijk* (friendly)
        *   *Red* (reason) + *lijk* → *Redelijk* (reasonable)
*   **Function:** It transforms the abstract noun concept ("reason") into a quality or characteristic ("having reason").

---

### 3. Semantic Development

The meaning of the word has shifted slightly from its literal morphological composition to its modern usage:

1.  **Literal Composition:** "Reason-like" or "Possessing reason."
2.  **Primary Meaning (Adjective):** **Reasonable** or **Rational**.
    *   *Example:* *Een redelijk mens* (A reasonable person).
3.  **Secondary Meaning (Adjective):** **Fair** or **Just**.
    *   *Example:* *Een redelijke prijs* (A fair price).
4.  **Tertiary Meaning (Adverb):** **Quite** or **Moderately**.
    *   In modern colloquial Dutch, *redelijk* is frequently used as an adverb of degree, weakening the intensity of an adjective.
    *   *Example:* *Het is redelijk warm* (It is reasonably/quite warm).

### 4. Syntactic Category

*   **Input:** Noun (*rede*)
*   **Process:** Derivation (Nominalization to Adjectivalization)
*   **Output:** Adjective (*redelijk*)
*   **Inflection:** As a Dutch adjective, it can be inflected for gender/number when used attributively (e.g., *een redelijke man* vs. *de redelijke man*), though the spelling remains the same in this specific case.

### Conclusion
Linguistically, **`redelijk`** is a classic example of Dutch **derivational morphology**. It demonstrates how a nominal concept (*rede*) is converted into a qualitative descriptor (*redelijk*) through the addition of the suffix *-lijk*, modified by an epenthetic vowel *-e-* to satisfy Dutch phonotactic constraints.
