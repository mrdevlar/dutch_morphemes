From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`verplichten`** (to oblige, to compel, to require) can be analyzed through its morphological structure, etymology, and semantic composition.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **ver-** + **plicht** + **-en**

---

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Prefix: `ver-`
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix.
*   **Origin:** Proto-Germanic **\*far-**, Old High German *far-*, Old English *for-*.
*   **Function:** In Dutch, the prefix *ver-* is highly productive and versatile. It often indicates:
    1.  **Change of state:** Transforming something into a different condition.
    2.  **Completion or Result:** Bringing an action to a conclusion.
    3.  **Transitivity:** Turning an intransitive concept or a noun into a transitive verb.
*   **In this context:** Here, *ver-* takes the concept of "duty" (*plicht*) and creates a state of being bound by that duty. It adds the sense of "making into" or "binding." It transforms the noun *plicht* into the adjectival/participial concept *verplicht* (obligated/mandatory).

#### B. The Root: `plicht`
*   **Type:** Lexical Root (Lexeme).
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun (in isolation).
*   **Meaning:** Duty, obligation, responsibility.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *plichte*, Old Dutch *plehta*, and Proto-Germanic **\*plehtō**.
*   **Cognates:**
    *   **English:** *Plight* (though the meaning shifted from "pledge/duty" to "predicament" in modern English).
    *   **German:** *Pflicht* (duty).
    *   **English:** *Pledge* (shares a related Proto-Germanic root **\*pleg-**).
*   **Phonology:** The root ends in a voiceless alveolar stop /t/. This affects the conjugation of the verb (e.g., the past tense is *verplichtte*, doubling the 't' to maintain the short vowel sound).

#### C. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix.
*   **Function:** Infinitive Marker.
*   **Grammar:** In Dutch, the standard ending for the infinitive form of a weak verb is *-en*.
*   **Stem:** The stem of the verb is *verplicht*. When conjugated in the present tense for *ik* (I) or *hij/zij/het* (he/she/it), the *-en* is dropped (*ik verplicht*). When conjugated for *wij/jullie/zij* (we/you/they), the *-en* remains (*wij verplichten*).

---

### 3. Derivational Chain
To understand the word fully, it helps to see the derivational hierarchy:

1.  **Noun:** *Plicht* (Duty)
2.  **Adjective/Participle:** *Ver-* + *Plicht* = **Verplicht** (Obligatory / Mandatory / Obligated)
    *   *Example:* "Een verplichte les" (A mandatory lesson).
3.  **Verb:** *Verplicht* + *-en* = **Verplichten** (To oblige / To compel)
    *   *Example:* "De wet verplicht ons" (The law obliges us).

### 4. Semantic Composition
When you combine the meanings of the morphemes, the logic of the word becomes clear:

*   **`plicht`** = The concept of duty.
*   **`ver-`** = To bind or bring into a state.
*   **`-en`** = To perform the action.

**Literal construction:** "To bind into a state of duty."
**Actual meaning:** To oblige, to compel, or to require.

### 5. Syntactic Usage
*   **Transitivity:** *Verplichten* is a transitive verb. It requires a direct object (the person being obliged) and often a prepositional phrase indicating the action required (*verplichten tot iets* - to oblige someone to something).
*   **Reflexive:** It can be used reflexively (*zich verplichten*), meaning "to commit oneself."

This structure is nearly identical to the German word **`verpflichten`**, highlighting the close relationship between the two West Germanic languages.
