Here is the breakdown of the morphemes, analyzed from both a **synchronic** (current usage) and **diachronic** (etymological/historical) perspective.

### 2. Morpheme-by-Morpheme Analysis

#### **A. Root: `materi-`**
*   **Type:** Bound Root (Latinate)
*   **Origin:** Latin *materia* (meaning "substance," "timber," or "matter").
*   **Function:** This is the semantic core of the word. It refers to physical substance or matter.
*   **Linguistic Note:** In modern Dutch, `materi-` is not a free morpheme (you cannot use it alone as a word like "materi"). It is a **bound root** that must attach to suffixes to form valid words (e.g., *materiaal*, *materie*).

#### **B. Suffix 1: `-aal`**
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix
*   **Origin:** Latin *-alis* (via French *-al*).
*   **Function:** Adjectival/Nominalizer. It turns the root into a noun or adjective relating to the root.
*   **Result:** `materi` + `aal` = **`materiaal`** (Material).
*   **Meaning:** "Relating to matter/substance."

#### **C. Suffix 2: `-ist`**
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix (Agent Noun)
*   **Origin:** Latin/Greek *-ista*.
*   **Function:** Agentive. It indicates a person who practices, believes in, or is associated with the preceding concept.
*   **Result:** `materiaal` + `ist` = **`materialist`** (Materialist).
*   **Meaning:** "A person who believes in materialism" (either philosophically or regarding consumer goods).

#### **D. Suffix 3: `-isch`**
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix (Adjectival)
*   **Origin:** Latin/Greek *-iscus* (via German *-isch*).
*   **Function:** Adjectivalizer. It turns a noun (often a person or a movement) into an adjective describing a quality or relation.
*   **Result:** `materialist` + `isch` = **`materialistisch`** (Materialistic).
*   **Meaning:** "Characterized by materialism" or "relating to a materialist."

### 3. Summary of Derivation Chain

The word is built in layers, shifting the part of speech at each step:

1.  **`materi-`** (Noun Root: Matter)
    ↓ *Add `-aal`*
2.  **`materiaal`** (Noun/Adj: Material)
    ↓ *Add `-ist`*
3.  **`materialist`** (Noun: Materialist [Person])

4.  **`materialistisch`** (Adjective: Materialistic)

### 5. Semantic Nuance
In Dutch, *materialistisch* carries two distinct meanings, inherited from the root *materi* (matter):
1.  **Philosophical:** Relating to the belief that only physical matter exists (Philosophical Materialism).
2.  **Consumerist:** Relating to an excessive concern for material possessions and wealth (Consumer Materialism).

The morphology does not distinguish between these two; context determines the meaning.
