From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`waaien`** (meaning "to blow," specifically regarding wind) can be analyzed as a **monomorphemic root** combined with an **inflectional suffix**.

Here is the detailed morphological breakdown:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word consists of two morphemes:
> **`waai`** + **`-en`**

### 2. Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Root: `waai-`
*   **Type:** Lexical Root (Free Morpheme / Stem)
*   **Function:** Carries the core semantic meaning of the word.
*   **Meaning:** "To blow" (in the context of wind).
*   **Phonology:** In standard Dutch, this is pronounced /ʋaːj/.
    *   The **`aa`** represents a long open back vowel /aː/.
    *   The **`i`** functions orthographically to create a glide sound /j/ (similar to the 'y' in English "boy"), resulting in the diphthong-like sound /aːj/.
*   **Morphological Status:** While it appears bound in the infinitive form (`waaien`), this root can function as a free morpheme in the imperative mood (e.g., *"Waai!"* — "Blow!"). In the context of the infinitive, it serves as the **verb stem**.

#### B. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix (Bound Morpheme)
*   **Function:** Marks the **infinitive** form of the verb.
*   **Grammar:** In Dutch, the suffix `-en` is the standard marker for the dictionary form (infinitive) of regular (weak) verbs.
*   **Phonology:** Pronounced as a schwa followed by a nasal /ə(n)/, often reduced to just /n/ in rapid speech.
*   **Morphological Status:** This is a **bound morpheme**; it cannot stand alone and must attach to a verb stem.

### 3. Morphological Classification
*   **Word Formation Process:** **Inflection**.
    *   The addition of `-en` does not change the part of speech (it remains a verb) nor does it change the core meaning (it remains "to blow"). It only changes the grammatical form to the infinitive.
*   **Morphological Type:** Dutch is generally a fusional language, but in this specific instance, the morphology is **agglutinative** in nature because the boundary between the stem (`waai`) and the suffix (`-en`) is clear and the suffix has a single, specific grammatical function.

### 4. Validation via Conjugation
To confirm that `waai-` is the true stem, we can look at how the word behaves in other grammatical contexts. The stem remains consistent while the suffix changes:

*   **Infinitive:** `waai` + `en` → **waaien** (to blow)
*   **Present (1st/3rd person):** `waai` + `t` → **waait** (he/she blows)
*   **Present (1st person):** `waai` + `∅` → **waai** (I blow)
*   **Past Tense:** `waai` + `de` → **waaid** (blowed)
