From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch verb **`eindigen`** (to end, to finish) can be analyzed through its morphological structure. It is a complex word formed through derivation from a noun root.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **eind** + **ig** + **en**

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### **A. Root: `eind`**
*   **Type:** Free morpheme / Lexical Root.
*   **Origin:** Derived from the noun **`einde`** (the end).
*   **Morphophonology:** In Dutch, when the noun *einde* is used as a stem for derivation, the final schwa vowel (*-e*) is often dropped (apocope). Thus, *einde* becomes the stem **`eind`**.
*   **Meaning:** "End," "limit," "boundary," or "conclusion."
*   **Related Words:** *eindeloos* (endless), *eindstation* (terminal station), *bepalen* (to determine/fix the end).

#### **B. Derivational Suffix: `-ig`**
*   **Type:** Derivational suffix (Adjectivizer).
*   **Function:** This suffix typically turns a noun or verb stem into an adjective. It conveys the meaning "having the quality of" or "characterized by."
*   **Intermediate Form:** *eind* + *ig* = **`eindig`**.
*   **Meaning of Intermediate:** "Finite," "having an end," or "ending." (Note: While *eindig* exists as an adjective in Dutch, it is less common in everyday speech than the verb form, often replaced by *beperkt* for "limited").
*   **Productivity:** This is a productive suffix in Dutch (e.g., *kracht* [strength] → *krachtig* [powerful]; *aard* [nature] → *aardig* [nice]).

#### **C. Inflectional Suffix: `-en`**
*   **Type:** Inflectional suffix (Infinitive marker).
*   **Function:** This is the standard marker for the **infinitive form** of Dutch verbs. It turns the adjectival stem into a verb.
*   **Meaning:** "To [verb]."
*   **Grammar:** It marks the word as a verb in its base form (e.g., *lopen*, *maken*, *eindigen*).

### 3. Word Formation Process (Derivation)
The creation of this word follows a specific derivational path common in Germanic languages:

1.  **Noun:** *Einde* (The end)
2.  **Stem Adjustment:** *Eind* (Removal of the nominal schwa)
3.  **Adjective Formation:** *Eind* + *-ig* → *Eindig* (Finite / Having an end)
4.  **Verb Formation:** *Eindig* + *-en* → *Eindigen* (To end / To finish)

**Semantic Shift:**
The meaning shifts from a static state ("having an end") to an action ("to bring to an end" or "to come to an end").

### 4. Related Morphological Variants
To fully understand the morphology, it helps to look at the transitive counterpart:

*   **`Beëindigen`** (To terminate / To finish something)
    *   **`Be-`**: A verbal prefix that often makes a verb transitive (acting upon an object) or perfective.
    *   **`ëind`**: Note the diaeresis (ë) here. Because the prefix *be-* is added directly to the stem *eind*, the vowel *ei* is kept distinct to prevent mispronunciation, though in *eindigen* the *ei* is already clear.
    *   **`-ig`**: Same adjectival suffix.
    *   **`-en`**: Same infinitive suffix.

**Usage Distinction:**
*   *Het verhaal **eindigt**.* (The story ends.) — Intransitive (Subject ends itself).
*   *Ik **beëindig** het gesprek.* (I terminate the conversation.) — Transitive (Subject acts on object).
