From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`afronden`** is a complex verb formed through derivational and inflectional morphology. It is a classic example of a **separable verb** (*scheidbaar werkwoord*).

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation of its components:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **`af`** + **`rond`** + **`en`**

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### **A. `af-` (Prefix / Particle)**
*   **Type:** Separable prefix (*scheidbaar voorvoegsel*).
*   **Origin:** Germanic (cognate with English *off* and German *ab*).
*   **Function:** It acts as a directional or completive modifier.
*   **Meaning:** Literally "off" or "away." In the context of verbs, it often implies **completion**, **termination**, or **separation**.
*   **Syntactic Behavior:** In a main clause, this prefix separates from the verb stem and moves to the end of the sentence (e.g., *Ik rond het project **af***). In the infinitive form (as analyzed here), it remains attached.

#### **B. `rond` (Root / Stem)**
*   **Type:** Adjectival Root (Deadjectival Verb).
*   **Origin:** Middle Dutch *rond*, from Old French *rond*, ultimately from Latin *rotundus* (round).
*   **Function:** This is the lexical core of the word. Originally an adjective meaning "round" or "circular."
*   **Derivation:** In Dutch, adjectives can be converted into verbs by adding the infinitive suffix *-en* (a process called **denominalization** or **deadjectival derivation**).
*   **Semantic Shift:** While `rond` means "round," in this verbal context, it refers to the mathematical or conceptual act of "rounding" (making a number rounder) or metaphorically "smoothing out" a process to a conclusion.

#### **C. `-en` (Suffix)**
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix.
*   **Function:** Marks the verb as the **infinitive** form.
*   **Grammar:** This is the standard ending for the majority of Dutch verbs in the dictionary form (e.g., *lopen*, *werken*, *zien* -> *zien* is irregular, but *maken* follows the rule). It carries no semantic meaning of its own but is required for grammatical correctness in the infinitive.

---

### 3. Semantic Composition (Meaning Construction)
The meaning of `afronden` is not simply the sum of "off" + "round." It involves a semantic shift:

1.  **Literal/Mathematical:** To round a number (e.g., rounding 3.14159 to 3.14). Here, `af` implies cutting off the decimals, and `rond` implies the resulting approximation.
2.  **Figurative/Idiomatic:** To **finish**, **complete**, or **finalize** something.
    *   *Example:* "Het project afronden" (To finish the project).
    *   *Logic:* You are "rounding off" the edges of a task to make it complete and whole.

### 4. Syntactic and Morphological Features

*   **Separability:** Because `af-` is a separable prefix, the word behaves differently depending on sentence structure.
    *   *Infinitive:* `...om het werk **afronden**.`
    *   *Main Clause:* `Ik **rond** het werk **af**.` (Prefix moves to end).
    *   *Past Participle:* `Ik heb het werk **afgerond**.` (Note the insertion of **`ge-`** between the prefix and the stem, which is a rule for separable verbs in Dutch).

*   **Word Class:** It functions as a transitive verb (it takes a direct object).
    *   *Subject:* De student (The student)
    *   *Verb:* rondt ... af (rounds off)
    *   *Object:* de cijfers (the numbers)
