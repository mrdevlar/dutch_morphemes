From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch verb **`hinderen`** (meaning *to hinder, to obstruct, or to bother*) can be analyzed through its morphological structure, etymology, and grammatical function.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word consists of two distinct morphemes:

> **`hinder`** + **`-en`**

---

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Root/Stem: `hinder-`
*   **Type:** Lexical Morpheme (Free/Bound depending on context).
*   **Function:** This is the semantic core of the word. It carries the primary meaning of "obstruction," "nuisance," or "being in the way."
*   **Part of Speech:** In this specific word, it functions as a **verbal stem**. However, in modern Dutch, `hinder` also exists independently as a noun (*de hinder*), meaning "hindrance" or "nuisance."
*   **Etymology:**
    *   **Middle Dutch:** *hinderen*
    *   **Old Dutch:** *hindera* / *hindron*
    *   **Proto-Germanic:** *\*hindr-* (related to the concept of "behind" or "obstacle").
    *   **Cognates:** It is directly related to the English verb **`to hinder`** and the noun **`hindrance`**. It is also cognate with the German verb **`hindern`**.
*   **Semantic Field:** The root implies an action that creates difficulty or stops progress.

#### B. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Type:** Inflectional Morpheme.
*   **Function:** This is the **infinitive marker**. In Dutch, adding `-en` (or sometimes just `-n`) to a verb stem creates the dictionary form of the verb.
*   **Grammatical Role:**
    1.  **Infinitive:** *Ik wil **hinderen**.* (I want to hinder.)
    2.  **Plural Present Tense:** *Wij **hinderen**.* (We hinder.)
*   **Phonology:** In spoken Dutch, the final `-n` is often elided (dropped), resulting in a schwa sound /ə/ at the end (pronounced roughly as *hin-də*).

---

### 3. Derivational and Inflectional Context

To fully understand the morphology, it helps to see how these morphemes interact with others to form related words:

*   **Inflection (Conjugation):**
    *   **Past Tense:** `hinder` + `-de` (weak verb suffix) → *hinderde* (hindered).
    *   **Past Participle:** `ge-` (prefix) + `hinder` + `-d` (suffix) → *gehinderd* (hindered).
*   **Derivation (Word Formation):**
    *   **Prefixation:** Adding the inseparable prefix `ver-` creates **`verhinderen`** (to prevent/impede). Here, `ver-` intensifies or completes the action of the root `hinder`.
    *   **Nominalization:** Adding the suffix `-ing` creates **`hindering`** (hindrance/obstruction).

### 5. Linguistic Note on "Weak" Verbs
`Hinderen` is classified as a **regular weak verb** (sterk/zwak distinction in Dutch). This means it does not change its vowel stem in the past tense (unlike strong verbs like *lopen* → *liep*). It simply adds dental suffixes (`-de` or `-t`) to the stem `hinder-`. This regularity is a common feature in Germanic languages for verbs derived from nouns or adjectives.
