From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`uitgebreid`** is a complex morphological structure derived from a separable verb. It functions primarily as a **past participle** (voltooid deelwoord) which is frequently used as an **adjective** (bijvoeglijk naamwoord).

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes, followed by a detailed explanation of their function and etymology.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation

The word can be segmented into four distinct morphemes:

> **`uit`** + **`ge`** + **`breid`** + **`d`**

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. `uit` (Separable Prefix)
*   **Type:** Prefix (Voorvoegsel).
*   **Function:** This is a **separable prefix** (scheidbaar voorvoegsel). In the infinitive form of the verb, it is separated from the stem (e.g., *uit*breiden). However, in the past participle form, it remains attached to the front of the word.
*   **Meaning:** Literally "out" or "outward."
*   **Semantic Contribution:** It adds a directional or completive aspect to the root. It implies expanding something *outward* or making something cover a larger area.
*   **Example:** *uit* (out) + *breiden* (to extend) = *uitbreiden* (to expand).

#### B. `ge` (Participial Prefix)
*   **Type:** Infix/Prefix (Deelwoord voorvoegsel).
*   **Function:** This is the standard marker for the **past participle** in Dutch for most verbs.
*   **Placement Rule:** In Dutch morphology, when a verb has a separable prefix (like *uit*), the *ge-* is inserted **between** the prefix and the root stem.
    *   *Infinitive:* `uit` + `breiden`
    *   *Participle:* `uit` + `ge` + `breid` + `d`
*   **Phonology:** It is pronounced as a schwa /ə/ (unstressed).

#### C. `breid` (Root/Stem)
*   **Type:** Lexical Root (Woordstam).
*   **Function:** This carries the core semantic meaning of the word.
*   **Etymology:** This stem is historically derived from the adjective **`breed`** (wide/broad).
    *   *Proto-Germanic:* *\*brēdaz* (wide).
    *   *Cognates:* English *broad*, German *breit*.
    *   *Derivation:* The verb *breiden* (in this context) is a denominal verb, meaning it was formed from the adjective *breed* to mean "to make wide" or "to extend."
*   **Note on Homonyms:** There is another verb *breiden* (to knit), which is etymologically distinct but shares the same spelling. In *uitgebreid*, we are dealing with the "expand" meaning.

#### D. `d` (Inflectional Suffix)
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix (Vervoegingsuitgang).
*   **Function:** This marks the **past participle** ending for weak verbs.
*   **Spelling Rule:** In Dutch, weak verbs form their past participle by adding *-d* or *-t* to the stem (the "t-kofschip" rule).
    *   The stem *breid* ends in a voiced consonant (/d/).
    *   Therefore, the participle suffix is **-d**.
    *   *Orthography:* While the stem ends in *d* and the suffix is *d*, Dutch orthography does not double the final consonant in this specific participle form (unlike the past tense *breidde*). Thus, it is spelled with a single *d* at the end.

### 3. Grammatical Context & Syntax

To fully understand the morphology, one must look at how the word behaves in a sentence.

**1. As a Past Participle (Verb):**
It is the past participle of the separable verb **`uitbreiden`** (to expand).
*   *Sentence:* "We hebben het huis **uitgebreid**." (We have expanded the house.)
*   *Structure:* `uit` (prefix) + `ge` (participle marker) + `breid` (stem) + `d` (ending).

**2. As an Adjective:**
It is frequently used attributively to describe a noun, meaning "extensive," "detailed," or "comprehensive."
*   *Sentence:* "Dit is een **uitgebreide** uitleg." (This is an extensive explanation.)
*   *Morphological Change:* When used as an adjective, an inflectional **-e** is added to the participle form (because the noun is common gender/singular or plural).
    *   *Base:* `uitgebreid`
    *   *Inflected:* `uitgebreid` + `e` = `uitgebreide`.

### 5. Linguistic Insight: The "Ge-" Sandwich
The most distinct feature of this word from a Dutch morphological perspective is the placement of **`ge`**.
In English, the past participle of "expand" is simply "expanded" (prefix *ex-* + root *pand* + suffix *-ed*).
In Dutch, because the verb has a separable prefix (*uit-*), the participle marker (*ge-*) cannot go to the very front (which would be *geuitbreid*, incorrect). It must be "sandwiched" between the prefix and the stem. This is a hallmark of Dutch separable verb morphology.

*   **Incorrect:** *geuitbreid*
*   **Correct:** *uitgebreid*
