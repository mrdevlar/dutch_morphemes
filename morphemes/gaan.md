From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`gaan`** (meaning "to go") is analyzed as follows.

### 1. Morphological Breakdown
While `gaan` appears to be a single unit, in Dutch morphology, it is technically composed of **two morphemes**:

| Morpheme | Form | Type | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Root** | `ga-` | Free / Lexical | Carries the core semantic meaning ("go"). |
| **Suffix** | `-n` | Bound / Grammatical | Marks the **infinitive** form (allomorph of `-en`). |

**Total Morphemes:** 2 (`ga` + `n`)

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis

#### A. The Root: `ga-`
*   **Type:** Lexical Morpheme (Free).
*   **Function:** This is the **verb stem**. It contains the primary meaning of the word.
*   **Usage:** This root appears in various conjugated forms where the infinitive marker is removed or changed.
    *   *Imperative:* `Ga!` (Go!)
    *   *Present Tense (1st/2nd Person):* `Ik ga` (I go), `Jij gaat` (You go).
    *   *Past Participle:* `ge-gaan` (gone).

#### B. The Suffix: `-n`
*   **Type:** Inflectional Morpheme (Bound).
*   **Function:** This marks the **infinitive** (the dictionary form of the verb).
*   **Linguistic Note:** In standard Dutch, the infinitive suffix is typically **`-en`** (e.g., `loop` + `en` = `lopen`). However, for verbs whose stem ends in a vowel (like `ga-`, `zie-`, `doe-`), the `e` is dropped in spelling and pronunciation to avoid a hiatus (two vowels next to each other).
    *   *Theoretical:* `ga` + `en`
    *   *Actual:* `ga` + `n` → `gaan`
*   **Homophony:** Interestingly, this `-n` is identical in form to the plural present tense marker (e.g., `wij gaan` / "we go"), though its grammatical function differs slightly depending on the context.

---

### 3. Classification
*   **Word Class:** Verb (Strong Verb / Class 3).
*   **Morphological Structure:** **Bi-morphemic** (Root + Suffix).
    *   *Note:* In some simplified linguistic models, `gaan` is treated as a **monomorphemic lexical item** because the suffix is not productive in the same way as regular verbs. However, the stem/suffix analysis is more accurate for understanding conjugation.
*   **Inflection Type:** **Ablaut (Strong Verb)**.
    *   Unlike regular verbs that add `-te` or `-de` for the past tense (e.g., `loop` → `liep`), `gaan` changes its internal vowel.
    *   *Infinitive:* `ga`**`a`**`n`
    *   *Past:* `g`**`i`**`ng`
    *   *Participle:* `ge`**`aa`**`n`

### 4. Etymological Context (Diachronic Morphology)
To understand why the morphology is irregular, it helps to look at the history:
*   **Proto-Germanic:** *\*ganganą*
*   **Old Dutch:** *gangan*
*   **Middle Dutch:** *gaen*
*   **Modern Dutch:** *gaan*

The word has retained its Germanic root structure. The vowel change (`a` → `i` → `a`) is a remnant of the Proto-Indo-European **ablaut system**, which is why `gaan` is classified as a **strong verb** (*sterk werkwoord*).

In the word **`gaan`**, you have the **lexical root `ga-`** combined with the **infinitive suffix `-n`**. While it functions as a single dictionary entry, morphologically it is a combination of a stem and a grammatical marker indicating the non-finite verb form.
