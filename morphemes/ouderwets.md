From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`ouderwets`** is a compound adjective. To understand its structure, we must analyze it at two levels: the compound level (how the two main parts join) and the internal morphological level (the roots and affixes within those parts).

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes.

### 1. Visual Segmentation
The word can be segmented into four distinct morphemes:

> **`oud`** + **`-er`** + **`wet`** + **`-s`**

Alternatively, at the compound level, it is often viewed as two constituents:
> **`ouder`** + **`wets`**

### 2. Morpheme-by-Morpheme Analysis

#### A. `oud` (Root)
*   **Category:** Adjective Root
*   **Meaning:** "Old"
*   **Function:** This is the semantic core of the first part of the word. It denotes age or antiquity.
*   **Cognates:** English *old*, German *alt*, Swedish *gammal* (different root), Danish *gammel*.

#### B. `-er` (Suffix)
*   **Category:** Comparative Suffix
*   **Meaning:** "-er" (comparative degree)
*   **Function:** Attached to *oud*, it creates the comparative form *ouder* ("older").
*   **Note:** In this specific compound, the comparative form is lexicalized. It does not necessarily mean "older than something else" in a direct comparison, but rather implies a state of being "more old" or "of a previous time."

#### C. `wet` (Root)
*   **Category:** Noun Root
*   **Meaning:** "Law," "Custom," or "Usage"
*   **Function:** This is the semantic core of the second part.
*   **Crucial Linguistic Note:** In modern Dutch, *wet* primarily means "statute" or "legislation" (e.g., *de wet* = the law). However, in this compound, it retains an older, broader meaning closer to **"custom," "usage,"** or **"established way of doing things."**
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *wet*, Old Dutch *weta*, Proto-Germanic *\*wēdō*. It is related to the concept of "that which is known" or "established."
*   **Cognates:** German *Weise* (way/manner) is often cited as a cognate in meaning, though etymologically distinct. It is related to the concept of "law" in English (via Germanic roots), but specifically the "customary law" aspect.

#### D. `-s` (Suffix / Linking Element)
*   **Category:** Genitive Marker / Fugenelement (Linking Element)
*   **Meaning:** Possessive or Relational ("of")
*   **Function:** Historically, this is the **genitive singular** ending of the noun *wet*. In modern Dutch morphology, it is classified as a **linking element** (*tussenvoegsel*) that connects the two parts of the compound.
*   **Translation:** It translates to "of."
*   **Pattern:** This is common in Dutch compounds (e.g., *liefdesverklaring* = declaration *of* love, *vaderszoon* = son *of* a father).

### 3. Semantic Composition (How the meaning is built)

The meaning is constructed through a **Genitive Compound** structure:

1.  **`ouder`** (Older)
2.  **`wets`** (Of custom/usage)

**Literal Translation:** "Of older custom" or "According to older usage."
**Modern Meaning:** "Old-fashioned," "outdated," or "antiquated."

The word implies that the subject adheres to the *wet* (customs/ways) of the *ouder* (older) times, rather than the current ones.
