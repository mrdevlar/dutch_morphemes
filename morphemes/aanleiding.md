From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`aanleiding`** is a classic example of **derivational morphology**. It is a noun formed from a verb through the addition of a suffix, which itself is built upon a separable verb compound.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **`aan`** + **`leid`** + **`-ing`**

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### **A. `aan` (Prefix / Particle)**
*   **Type:** Separable prefix (in the verb context) / Preposition.
*   **Literal Meaning:** "On," "at," "to," or "towards."
*   **Function:** In the verb *aanleiden* (to lead to/initiate), *aan* acts as a directional particle. It adds a nuance of **initiation** or **connection**. It implies setting something in motion or directing a process toward a specific point.
*   **Etymology:** Cognate with the English preposition **"on"** and the German **"an"**. It comes from the Proto-Germanic *\*ana*.

#### **B. `leid` (Root / Verb Stem)**
*   **Type:** Verb stem.
*   **Source Verb:** *leiden* (to lead).
*   **Literal Meaning:** To guide, to conduct, to steer, or to lead.
*   **Function:** This is the semantic core of the word. It provides the action concept.
*   **Etymology:** Cognate with the English verb **"lead"** and the German **"leiten"**. It comes from the Proto-Germanic *\*laidijaną*.
*   **Note:** In the infinitive *leiden*, the stem is *leid-*. The *d* is retained in the noun form.

#### **C. `-ing` (Suffix)**
*   **Type:** Derivational suffix (Nominalizer).
*   **Function:** This suffix turns the verb (*aanleiden*) into a noun (*aanleiding*). It indicates the **action**, **process**, or **result** of the verb.
*   **Meaning:** It creates an abstract noun referring to the event or state of "leading-on."
*   **Etymology:** Cognate with the English suffix **"-ing"** (as in *building* or *meeting*) and the German **"-ung"**. It comes from the Proto-Germanic *\*-ingaz*.

---

### 3. Syntactic and Semantic Construction

To understand the meaning fully, we must look at the verb from which the noun is derived:

1.  **The Verb:** ***Aanleiden***
    *   This is a **separable verb** (*scheidbaar werkwoord*).
    *   *Example:* "De storm **leidde** de brand **aan**." (The storm led to the fire / triggered the fire).
    *   Meaning: To initiate, to trigger, to lead up to.

2.  **The Noun:** ***Aanleiding***
    *   By adding *-ing*, the action becomes a concept.
    *   **Literal Translation:** "The leading-on" or "The act of leading to."
    *   **Actual Meaning:** **Occasion, cause, trigger, or reason.**
    *   **Usage:** It is often used to describe the immediate trigger for an event, rather than the deep underlying cause.
        *   *Example:* "Dit incident gaf de **aanleiding** voor de oorlog." (This incident gave the **occasion/trigger** for the war.)

### 4. Linguistic Comparison (Cognates)

Because Dutch, English, and German share a Germanic root, we can see the morphological parallels:

| Language | Word | Morphemes | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Dutch** | **Aanleiding** | *aan* + *leid* + *ing* | Occasion / Trigger |
| **German** | *Anlass* | *an* + *lass* (from *lassen*) | Occasion / Reason |
| **English** | *Leading* | *lead* + *ing* | (Gerund) |
| **English** | *On-lead* | (Not a standard word) | N/A |

*Note: While English has "lead" and "on," it does not have a direct compound cognate for "aanleiding." The closest semantic equivalent in English is "occasion" or "trigger."*

### 5. Summary
*   **Word Class:** Noun (De-word).
*   **Morphological Process:** Derivation (Verb $\rightarrow$ Noun).
*   **Structure:** [Prefix] + [Root] + [Suffix].
*   **Core Concept:** The process of something being "led to" a specific point, resulting in an **occasion** or **cause**.
