From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`blijken`** can be analyzed through its morphological structure, etymology, and semantic function.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word consists of two morphemes:
> **`blijk`** + **`-en`**

| Morpheme | Type | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`blijk`** | **Root / Stem** | Carries the core lexical meaning (evidence, appearance, proof). |
| **`-en`** | **Inflectional Suffix** | Marks the verb as the **infinitive** form. |

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Root: `blijk`
*   **Status:** This is a **free morpheme** in Dutch, meaning it can stand alone as an independent word, though its usage is specific.
*   **Nominal Form:** As a noun, *`de blijk`* means "a sign," "a token," or "a proof."
    *   *Example:* "Een **blijk** van dank" (A token of appreciation).
*   **Verbal Stem:** In the verb *`blijken`*, this root functions as the stem. It undergoes vowel gradation (ablaut) in the past tense because it is a **strong verb**:
    *   Infinitive: *blijk*en
    *   Past Singular: *bleek*
    *   Past Participle: *ge*bleek*en*
*   **Semantic Meaning:** The core meaning relates to **manifestation** or **becoming evident**. It implies that something hidden has come to light or a truth has been revealed.
*   **Productivity:** This root is productive in derivational morphology. It forms the basis for the adjective **`blijkbaar`** (apparent/evident), where the suffix *`-baar`* (equivalent to English *-able*) is added to the root.

#### B. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Status:** **Bound morpheme**. It cannot stand alone.
*   **Function:** This is the standard **infinitive marker** for Dutch verbs.
    *   *Contrast:* If you remove `-en`, you get the stem `blijk`. To conjugate it, you add personal endings (e.g., *ik blijk*, *jij blijkt*).
    *   *Note:* In some dialects or informal speech, the `-en` may be reduced to `-n` (e.g., *blijk'n*), but in standard morphology, it is analyzed as `-en`.

---

### 3. Etymological Perspective
To fully understand the morphology, one must look at the historical development of the root `blijk`.

*   **Proto-Germanic:** The root traces back to **`*blīkaną`**, meaning "to shine" or "to appear."
*   **Cognates:** It is related to the English word **`bleak`** (originally meaning "pale" or "shining," before shifting to "cold/dismal") and the German **`bleichen`** (to bleach/shine).
*   **Semantic Shift:** Over time, the meaning shifted from the physical act of "shining/appearing" to the abstract concept of "becoming evident" or "turning out to be."
    *   *Old Meaning:* To shine/appear.
    *   *Modern Meaning:* To prove to be / to turn out.
*   **Relation to `blij` (Happy):** There is a common confusion between *`blijken`* and the adjective *`blij`* (happy). Etymologically, they are related (both come from the concept of brightness/shining), but in modern Dutch, they are distinct lexical items. *`Blij`* implies an emotional state, while *`blijken`* implies a logical revelation.

### 4. Syntactic Usage Note
From a syntactic-semantic perspective, *`blijken`* is often used as an **impersonal verb**.
*   *Structure:* `Het` + `blijkt` + `dat`...
*   *Example:* "**Het blijkt** dat hij gelijk had." (It turns out that he was right.)
*   Here, the morpheme `blijk` carries the weight of the revelation, while the dummy subject `het` (it) fills the grammatical slot.

### Summary
*   **Word:** `blijken`
*   **Breakdown:** `blijk` (Root: proof/appearance) + `-en` (Infinitive suffix).
*   **Type:** Strong Verb.
*   **Core Concept:** To become evident or to prove to be.
