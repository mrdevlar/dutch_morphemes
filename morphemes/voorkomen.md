From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`voorkomen`** is a fascinating example of a **compound verb** (samenstelling) that exhibits **polysemy** (multiple meanings) based on stress patterns and semantic composition.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

1.  **`voor-`** (Prefix)
2.  **`kom-`** (Root/Stem)
3.  **`-en`** (Infinitive Suffix)

**Structure:** `[voor-] + [kom-] + [-en]`

---

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Prefix: `voor-`
*   **Type:** Separable Prefix (*scheidbaar voorvoegsel*).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *for*, cognate with German *vor* and English *fore*.
*   **Core Semantics:** "Before," "in front of," "forward," or "ahead."
*   **Function:** In this compound, it acts as a directional or temporal modifier. It dictates the spatial or temporal relationship of the action performed by the root verb.

#### B. The Root: `kom-`
*   **Type:** Verbal Stem (*stam*).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *kōman*, cognate with German *kommen* and English *come*.
*   **Core Semantics:** "To move towards the speaker," "to arrive," or "to appear."
*   **Function:** This carries the primary lexical meaning of motion or existence.

#### C. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix (*infinitiefuitgang*).
*   **Function:** Marks the verb as an **infinitive** (the base form of the verb).
*   **Note:** In Dutch morphology, the infinitive ending is almost always *-en* (or *-n* after a vowel). It does not carry lexical meaning itself but is required for the grammatical form.

---

### 3. Semantic Composition & Polysemy
The most linguistically interesting aspect of `voorkomen` is that the combination of `voor` + `komen` yields two distinct meanings depending on **phonological stress** and **context**.

#### Meaning 1: "To Prevent" / "To Avoid"
*   **Stress Pattern:** **VOOR**-ko-men (Stress on the prefix).
*   **Literal Logic:** "To come **before**."
*   **Semantic Shift:** If you "come before" an event, you anticipate it and stop it from happening.
*   **Syntactic Behavior:** It is a **separable verb**. In a main clause, the prefix separates from the root.
    *   *Example:* "Ik wil dat **voorkom**en." (I want to prevent that.)
    *   *Conjugated:* "Ik **kom** dat **voor**." (I prevent that.)

#### Meaning 2: "To Occur" / "To Appear" / "To Exist"
*   **Stress Pattern:** voor-**KO**-men (Stress on the root).
*   **Literal Logic:** "To come **forward**" or "to come into view."
*   **Semantic Shift:** Something that "comes forward" into the realm of reality is said to occur or exist.
*   **Syntactic Behavior:** It is also a **separable verb**, but the meaning dictates the separation logic.
    *   *Example:* "Dit **komt** vaak **voor**." (This occurs often.)

### 5. Comparative Linguistics (Germanic Cognates)
To understand the morphology better, it helps to look at the Germanic family:

*   **English:** *Fore* + *come* (Archaic/Non-standard, but the logic holds). English uses "prevent" (Latin root) or "anticipate" for the first meaning, and "occur" for the second.
*   **German:** *Vor* + *kommen*.
    *   *Vorkommen* (to occur/happen).
    *   *Verhindern* (to prevent) is the usual German equivalent for the "prevent" meaning, though *vorbeugen* (to prevent/anticipate) shares the *vor-* logic.

### Conclusion
Linguistically, `voorkomen` is a **separable compound verb** where the semantic weight of the prefix `voor-` interacts with the root `kom-` to create a polysemous word. The distinction between "preventing" and "occurring" is encoded not just in the dictionary definition, but in the **prosody** (stress) of the spoken word.
