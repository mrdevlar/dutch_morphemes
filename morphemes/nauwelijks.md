From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`nauwelijks`** (meaning "hardly," "scarcely," or "barely") is a complex word formed through derivational morphology. It consists of three distinct morphemes that have evolved over time to create a lexicalized adverb.

Here is the breakdown and explanation of the morphemes:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation

| Morpheme | Form | Type | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Root** | `nauw` | Free Morpheme (Adjective) | Carries the core lexical meaning ("narrow"). |
| **2. Suffix** | `-elijk` | Derivational Suffix | Turns the root into an adjective meaning "characterized by." |
| **3. Suffix** | `-s` | Inflectional/Derivational Suffix | Adverbializer (turns the adjective into an adverb). |

**Full Structure:** `nauw` + `-elijk` + `-s`

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Root: `nauw`
*   **Part of Speech:** Adjective.
*   **Meaning:** "Narrow," "tight," or "close."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *nau*, Old Dutch *nau*. It is cognate with the English word **"narrow"** (both come from the Proto-Germanic *\*nawwaz*).
*   **Semantic Role:** This provides the physical imagery for the word. Originally, it referred to physical space (e.g., a narrow path).

#### B. The Derivational Suffix: `-elijk`
*   **Function:** This suffix creates adjectives from nouns or other adjectives. It is cognate with the English suffix **"-ly"** (as in *friend* $\rightarrow$ *friendly*).
*   **Internal Structure:** Historically, this suffix is a combination of two older elements:
    1.  **`-el-`**: An interfix or derivational element often used to form abstract nouns or adjectives.
    2.  **`-ijk`**: An adjective-forming suffix (cognate with English *-ic* or *-y*).
    *   *Note:* In modern synchronic analysis (how the language works today), linguists usually treat **`-elijk`** as a single unit because speakers no longer perceive the internal split between *-el-* and *-ijk*.
*   **Semantic Shift:** When attached to *nauw*, it creates the concept of "having the quality of narrowness." However, *nauwelijk* is not used as a standalone adjective in modern Dutch; it exists almost exclusively within the adverb *nauwelijks*.

#### C. The Adverbial Suffix: `-s`
*   **Function:** This is an **adverbializer**. It turns the adjective form into an adverb.
*   **Historical Origin:** This *-s* originates from the Old Dutch genitive ending **`-es`**. In older Germanic languages, adverbs were often formed by using the genitive case of an adjective (e.g., "in a narrow way" $\rightarrow$ "of a narrow kind").
*   **Cognates:** This is similar to English adverbs that end in *-s* derived from genitive forms, such as **"always"** (all + ways), **"sometimes"**, or **"elsewhere"**.
*   **Necessity:** In this specific lexicalized word, the *-s* is mandatory. You cannot say *nauwelijk* as an adverb; it must be *nauwelijks*.

---

### 3. Semantic Evolution (From "Narrow" to "Hardly")

The meaning of *nauwelijks* is a classic example of **semantic narrowing** and **metaphorical extension**:

1.  **Literal:** *Nauw* means "narrow" (physical space).
2.  **Metaphorical:** If a margin is "narrow," there is very little room left.
3.  **Abstract:** If there is very little room for time, opportunity, or quantity, the action is performed with difficulty or scarcity.
4.  **Result:** "Narrowly" $\rightarrow$ "Barely" / "Hardly."

*   **Example:** *Ik heb **nauwelijks** tijd.* (I have **hardly** any time.)
    *   *Literal interpretation:* I have a **narrow** amount of time.

### 4. Comparison with English

| Dutch | English | Morphological Parallel |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `nauw` | `narrow` | Germanic Root |
| `-elijk` | `-ly` | Adjective/Adverb Suffix |
| `-s` | (Genitive -s) | Adverbial Marker (e.g., *always*) |

While English uses *hardly* (from *hard* + *-ly*) or *barely* (from *bare* + *-ly*), Dutch uses the spatial concept of *narrowness* to express the same logical limitation.

### 5. Summary
From a linguistic standpoint, **`nauwelijks`** is a **lexicalized adverb**. It is formed by taking the adjective root **`nauw`**, applying the adjectival suffix **`-elijk`**, and finally applying the fossilized genitive adverbial suffix **`-s`**. The word demonstrates how spatial concepts (narrowness) are frequently mapped onto abstract concepts (scarcity) in Germanic languages.
