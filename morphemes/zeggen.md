From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`zeggen`** (meaning "to say" or "to tell") can be analyzed as a verb in its **infinitive** form.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation of its components.

### 1. Morphemic Segmentation
The word consists of two morphemes:

> **`zeg`** + **`-en`**

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Root: `zeg-`
*   **Type:** Free Morpheme / Lexical Root (Stem).
*   **Function:** This is the core of the word. It carries the primary semantic meaning ("to say," "to tell," "to speak").
*   **Phonology:** In Standard Dutch, this is pronounced /zɛx/ (where the 'g' is a voiced velar fricative).
*   **Morphological Status:** This is the invariant stem used for conjugation.
    *   *Ik zeg* (I say)
    *   *Jij zegt* (You say)
    *   *Wij zeggen* (We say)
    *   *Gezegd* (Said - Past Participle)
*   **Note on Spelling (The Double 'g'):** You may notice the spelling contains a double 'g' (`zeggen`), but the morphological root is technically just `zeg-`. The doubling is a historical artifact known as **West Germanic Gemination**.
    *   In Proto-Germanic, the word was *\*sagjaną*.
    *   The suffix *-jan-* caused the preceding consonant (*g*) to double (geminate).
    *   Over time, the *-j-* sound disappeared, but the consonant doubling remained in the spelling and pronunciation to preserve the vowel length distinction. Therefore, in Modern Dutch morphology, the double 'g' is considered part of the orthographic representation of the stem, not a separate morpheme.

#### B. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Type:** Bound Morpheme / Inflectional Suffix.
*   **Function:** This is the **infinitive marker**. In Dutch, almost all verbs take this suffix to indicate the base, unconjugated form of the verb (equivalent to "to" + verb in English).
*   **Phonology:** Pronounced as a schwa /ə/ followed by a nasal /n/ (often syllabic /n̩/).
*   **Grammar:** It distinguishes the infinitive from the conjugated forms (e.g., *zeg* vs. *zeggen*).

### 3. Historical & Etymological Context
To fully understand the morphology, it helps to look at the word's evolution:

1.  **Proto-Indo-European:** *\*sek-* (to cut, divide, speak).
2.  **Proto-Germanic:** *\*sagjaną* (to say).
    *   Here, the *-j-* was a derivational suffix that turned the root into a verb.
3.  **Old Dutch:** *seggian*.
    *   The gemination (doubling of the 'g') became fixed here due to the *-j-*.
4.  **Modern Dutch:** *zeggen*.
    *   The *-j-* is lost, leaving the double consonant as a fossilized marker of the historical suffix.

### 5. Linguistic Classification
*   **Word Class:** Verb (Transitive/Intransitive).
*   **Verb Type:** Irregular / Mixed.
    *   While the infinitive looks regular (*-en*), the past tense is **`zei`** (strong verb pattern), and the past participle is **`gezegd`** (weak verb pattern). This makes *zeggen* a "mixed" verb in Dutch linguistics, though the infinitive form itself follows the standard weak verb morphology.
