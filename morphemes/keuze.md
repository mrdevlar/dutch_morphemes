From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`keuze`** (meaning "choice" or "selection") is an interesting case because its morphological structure involves both synchronic (modern) and diachronic (historical) layers.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes, the phonology, and the etymology.

### 1. Morphological Breakdown (Synchronic Analysis)

In modern Dutch morphology, `keuze` is typically analyzed as a **deverbal noun** (a noun derived from a verb). While it functions as a single lexical unit today, it can be broken down into two morphemes:

| Morpheme | Form | Type | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Root / Stem** | `keuz-` | Bound Root | Carries the core lexical meaning ("to choose/selection"). |
| **Suffix** | `-e` | Derivational / Nominalizer | Marks the word as a singular noun (schwa ending). |

**Total Morphemes:** 2 (Root + Suffix)
*Note: In strict synchronic analysis, some linguists might argue `keuze` is **monomorphemic** (one morpheme) because the internal structure is no longer productive (you cannot apply this rule to new verbs).*

### 2. The Relationship to the Verb (`Kiezen`)

To fully understand the morphemes, one must look at the verb from which it is derived: **`kiezen`** (to choose).

*   **Verb Stem:** `kiez-`
*   **Noun Stem:** `keuz-`

There is a morphophonemic alternation (a sound change) between the verb and the noun. This is a remnant of **Germanic Ablaut** (vowel gradation).
*   The vowel shifts from **`ie`** (/i/) in the verb to **`eu`** (/øː/) in the noun.
*   This is similar to the English relationship between the verb **`choose`** and the noun **`choice`**.

Therefore, the morphological derivation is:
> `kiez` (Verb Root) + [Ablaut Shift: `ie` → `eu`] + `-e` (Noun Suffix) = **`keuze`**

### 3. Phonological Analysis (IPA)

*   **Word:** `keuze`
*   **IPA:** `/køːzə/`
*   **Breakdown:**
    *   `/k/`: Voiceless velar plosive.
    *   `/øː/`: Long front rounded vowel (the `eu` sound).
    *   `/z/`: Voiced alveolar fricative.
    *   `/ə/`: Schwa (the unstressed `e` at the end).

### 4. Etymological Analysis (Diachronic)

Looking at the history of the word explains *why* the morphemes are structured this way.

*   **Proto-Germanic:** `*keusaną` (verb: to choose).
*   **Old Dutch:** `kius` / `kioza`.
*   **Middle Dutch:** `keuse` / `keuze`.
*   **Modern Dutch:** `keuze`.

The word is a **cognate** with the English word **`choice`**.
*   English `choic` + `e` (from Old French `chois`, from Old French `choisir`, from Frankish `*kausjan`).
*   Dutch `keuz` + `e`.

### 5. Inflection (Pluralization)

To see the morphemes in action, we can look at the plural form: **`keuzes`**.

*   **`keuz-`**: Root (unchanged).
*   **`-e`**: Singular nominal ending.
*   **`-s`**: Plural suffix.

This confirms that `-e` is treated as part of the singular stem form in the lexicon, while `-s` is the inflectional suffix added for plurality.

### Summary
*   **Word:** `keuze`
*   **Meaning:** Choice / Selection
*   **Morphemes:** `keuz-` (Root) + `-e` (Nominal Suffix).
*   **Key Feature:** Irregular vowel change (`ie` → `eu`) from the related verb `kiezen`.
*   **Linguistic Category:** Deverbal Noun (derived from a verb).
