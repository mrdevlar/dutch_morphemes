From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`overigens`** is a complex word formed through derivational morphology. It is a fossilized form that has evolved from an adjective phrase into a fixed sentence adverb.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into two distinct morphemes:

> **overig** + **-ens**

---

### 2. Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Root: `overig`
*   **Part of Speech:** Adjective (frozen in this context).
*   **Meaning:** "Remaining," "other," "rest," or "the rest of."
*   **Etymology:**
    *   Derived from the Middle Dutch *overich* and Old Dutch *overig*.
    *   It is itself derived from the preposition **`over`** (over, above, across) + the adjectival suffix **`-ig`** (equivalent to English *-y* or *-ish*).
    *   **Semantic Shift:** Originally, *over* meant "above" or "across." In the context of *overig*, the meaning shifted from "that which is left over" (spatially above or remaining) to "the remainder."
*   **Current Usage:** In modern Dutch, *overig* is still used as an adjective (e.g., *de overige mensen* = the remaining people), but in *overigens*, it is no longer inflected for gender or number.

#### B. The Suffix: `-ens`
*   **Part of Speech:** Adverbial suffix (fossilized genitive ending).
*   **Function:** This suffix turns the adjective into an **adverb**. Specifically, it creates a sentence adverb (zinsbijwoord) that modifies the entire clause rather than a specific verb.
*   **Etymology:**
    *   This is a relic of the **genitive case** from Old and Middle Dutch.
    *   Historically, the genitive plural ending for adjectives was *-es* or *-ens*.
    *   Over time, this case ending lost its grammatical function of indicating possession or relation and became a fixed marker for adverbs of manner or time.
*   **Cognates/Comparisons:**
    *   **Dutch:** *anders* (otherwise), *tevens* (also), *overigens* (by the way).
    *   **English:** This is similar to the fossilized genitive *-s* found in English adverbs like *always* (from *all* + *ways*), *sometimes*, *once*, and *twice*.

---

### 3. Historical Evolution (Diachronic View)

To understand the word fully, one must look at its historical development:

1.  **Stage 1 (Preposition):** *Over* (Over/Above).
2.  **Stage 2 (Adjective):** *Overig* (Remaining/Leftover).
    *   *Literal sense:* "That which is over/above."
    *   *Figurative sense:* "That which remains."
3.  **Stage 3 (Genitive Phrase):** *Van het overige* (Of the remaining).
    *   In Middle Dutch, adverbs were often formed using the genitive case.
4.  **Stage 4 (Fossilization):** *Overigens*.
    *   The genitive ending *-ens* attached to the stem *overig*.
    *   The phrase lost its literal meaning of "of the remainder" and grammaticalized into a discourse marker meaning "incidentally" or "by the way."

### 4. Syntactic and Pragmatic Function

While the morphology explains *how* the word is built, linguistics also looks at *how* it is used:

*   **Category:** Sentence Adverb (Zinsbijwoord).
*   **Position:** It is flexible but most commonly appears at the beginning of a sentence or clause (e.g., *"Overigens, ik heb je gebeld"*).
*   **Pragmatics:** It functions as a **discourse marker**. It signals a transition, introduces a side note, or adds information that is relevant but not central to the main topic. It is functionally equivalent to English "by the way" or "incidentally."
