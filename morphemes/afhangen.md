From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`afhangen`** is a classic example of a **separable compound verb** (*scheidbaar werkwoord*).

Below is a detailed morphological, syntactic, and semantic breakdown of the word.

### 1. Morphological Breakdown

The word consists of two distinct morphemes:

| Morpheme | Form | Type | Meaning / Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`af`** | *af* | **Particle / Separable Prefix** | Indicates direction (down/off) or result. |
| **`hangen`** | *hangen* | **Verbal Root (Stem)** | The core action: "to hang". |

#### A. The Particle: `af`
*   **Category:** In this context, `af` functions as a **verb particle** (often historically analyzed as a preposition or adverb that has been lexicalized with the verb).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *af*, cognate with German *ab* and English *off*.
*   **Function:** It modifies the meaning of the root verb. While *hangen* simply means "to hang," adding *af* adds a directional or relational nuance (hanging *down* from something, or hanging *off* of something).
*   **Status:** It is a **free morpheme** (it can stand alone as an adverb, e.g., *De lamp is af* - The lamp is off), but in this compound, it acts as a bound element of the lexical unit.

#### B. The Root: `hangen`
*   **Category:** Strong Verb (Class 3).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *hangan*, cognate with German *hängen* and English *hang*.
*   **Conjugation:** It follows the strong verb pattern:
    *   Infinitive: *hangen*
    *   Past Singular: *hing*
    *   Past Participle: *gehangen*
*   **Transitivity:** It can be intransitive (something hangs) or transitive (someone hangs something). In *afhangen*, it is primarily used intransitively.

---

### 2. Syntactic Behavior: Separability

The most distinct linguistic feature of `afhangen` is its **separability**. In Dutch grammar, this is known as a *scheidbaar werkwoord*.

*   **Infinitive Form:** Written as one word (*afhangen*).
    *   *Ik wil dit niet **afhangen**.* (I do not want to depend on this.)
*   **Main Clause (V2 Position):** The particle `af` separates from the verb and moves to the end of the clause.
    *   *Het **hangt** van het weer **af**.* (It depends on the weather.)
    *   *Structure:* Subject + Finite Verb + ... + Particle.
*   **Subordinate Clause:** The verb remains together at the end.
    *   *... omdat het van het weer **afhangt**.* (... because it depends on the weather.)
*   **Past Participle:** The prefix *ge-* is inserted **between** the particle and the stem.
    *   *Het is **afgehangen**.* (It has depended / It has hung down.)
    *   *Morphology:* `af` + `ge` + `hangen` + `t` (past participle ending).

---

### 3. Semantic Analysis (Meaning)

The combination of the morphemes creates a polysemous word (a word with multiple related meanings).

#### A. Literal Meaning (Spatial)
*   **Meaning:** To hang down; to be suspended from a point.
*   **Example:** *De gordijnen hangen af.* (The curtains hang down.)
*   **Logic:** The particle `af` adds a downward vector to the state of `hangen`.

#### B. Figurative Meaning (Abstract/Relational)
*   **Meaning:** To depend on; to be contingent upon.
*   **Example:** *De beslissing hangt van jou af.* (The decision depends on you.)
*   **Logic:** This is a metaphorical extension. Just as an object physically hangs *from* a support, an abstract outcome is supported by (dependent on) a condition. This is the most common usage in modern Dutch.

---

### 4. Derivational Morphology

The morphemes in `afhangen` serve as the base for other related words in the Dutch lexicon, demonstrating how the root and particle interact in word formation:

1.  **`Afhankelijk`** (Adjective):
    *   *af* + *hang* + *-elijk* (suffix turning verb to adjective).
    *   Meaning: Dependent.
2.  **`Afhankelijkheid`** (Noun):
    *   *afhankelijk* + *-heid* (suffix turning adjective to noun).
    *   Meaning: Dependency.
3.  **`Afhankelijk maken`** (Verb phrase):
    *   To make dependent.

### Summary
Linguistically, **`afhangen`** is a **separable compound verb** formed by the directional particle **`af`** and the strong verb root **`hangen`**. Its syntax requires the particle to detach in main clauses, and its semantics range from the physical act of hanging downwards to the abstract concept of dependency.
