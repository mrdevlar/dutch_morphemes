From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`voorbeeld`** (meaning "example") is a classic example of a **compound noun** (*samenstelling*).

Here is the morphological breakdown and linguistic analysis of the word.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word consists of two free morphemes joined together to form a single lexical unit:

> **`voor`** + **`beeld`** = **`voorbeeld`**

### 2. Analysis of Individual Morphemes

#### A. `voor` (Prefix/Preposition)
*   **Part of Speech:** Preposition (functioning here as a bound modifier in a compound).
*   **Literal Meaning:** "Before," "in front of," or "for."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *for*, related to the German *vor* and the English prefix *fore-* (as in *foreword* or *forefront*).
*   **Function in Compound:** In this context, it acts as a **temporal and spatial modifier**. It indicates that the "image" comes *before* the action or the main event. It suggests precedence or a model to be looked at first.

#### B. `beeld` (Head Noun)
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun.
*   **Literal Meaning:** "Image," "picture," "form," or "shape."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *bild*, related to the German *Bild* and the English *behold* (cognates via Proto-Germanic *\*bildaz*).
*   **Function in Compound:** This is the **head** of the compound. In Dutch compounding, the second element usually determines the grammatical category (it is a noun) and the core semantic meaning.
*   **Semantic Shift:** While *beeld* literally means a visual picture, in this compound, it has undergone semantic broadening to mean a "representation" or a "model."

### 3. Semantic Composition (How the meaning is built)
The meaning of the compound is **endocentric**, meaning the whole word refers to a specific type of the head noun (*beeld*).

*   **Literal Construction:** "A picture placed before."
*   **Figurative Meaning:** An image or model presented *beforehand* to illustrate how something should be done.
*   **Translation:** Example.

The logic follows the idea that an example is a "model" (beeld) that is placed "in front of" (voor) the learner or observer to guide them.

### 4. Comparative Linguistics (Cognates)
Looking at related Germanic languages highlights the transparency of this morphology:

*   **German:** *Vorbild* (Literally: *Vor* + *Bild*). In German, *Vorbild* often means "role model" or "ideal," whereas *Beispiel* is used for "example." However, the structure is identical.
*   **English:** English does not have a direct cognate for this specific compound. We use the Latin-derived *example*. However, the components exist separately:
    *   *For* (related to *voor*).
    *   *Be* + *Hold* (related to *beeld* via the concept of seeing/forming).
*   **Frisian:** *Foarbyld* (Very similar structure).

### 5. Phonological and Orthographic Features
*   **Stress Pattern:** In Dutch compound nouns, the primary stress almost always falls on the **first element**.
    *   Pronunciation: **`VOOR`**beeld (not voor`BEELD`).
    *   This distinguishes it from a phrase where the stress might fall differently.
*   **Pluralization:** When pluralizing a compound noun in Dutch, the plural suffix attaches to the **head** (the second element).
    *   Singular: *het voorbeeld*
    *   Plural: *de voorbeelden* (The suffix *-en* is added to *beeld* $\rightarrow$ *beelden*).
*   **Spelling:** The word is written as one solid unit (closed compound), which is standard for Dutch nouns.

### 6. Derivatives
Because the morphemes are productive, they can be used to create other words:
*   **`voorbeeldig`**: (Adjective) Exemplary / Example-like. (Suffix *-ig* added to the whole compound).
*   **`voorbeeldloos`**: (Adjective) Without example / Unprecedented. (Suffix *-loos* meaning "without").
*   **`afbeelding`**: (Noun) Image/Depiction. (Uses the same root *beeld* with the prefix *af-*).
