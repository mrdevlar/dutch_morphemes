From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch verb **`vergaan`** can be analyzed through **morphology** (the structure of words) and **etymology** (word history).

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word consists of two distinct morphemes:

> **ver-** + **gaan**

*   **`ver-`**: A derivational prefix.
*   **`gaan`**: The lexical root (base verb).

---

### 2. Analysis of the Root: `gaan`
*   **Form:** Infinitive of the verb *to go*.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Proto-Germanic **\*ganganą**, which traces back to the Proto-Indo-European root **\*ghengʰ-** (meaning "to step" or "to walk").
*   **Core Semantics:** Movement, progression, or transition from one state/place to another.
*   **Grammatical Function:** It provides the primary action of the word.

### 3. Analysis of the Prefix: `ver-`
*   **Form:** An inseparable verbal prefix.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Proto-Germanic **\*fur-**, related to the Latin prefix *per-* (meaning "through," "thoroughly," or "to destruction"). It is cognate with the English prefix *for-* (as in *forget*, *forbid*, *forsake*).
*   **Grammatical Function:** It is a **derivational morpheme**. It changes the meaning of the root verb and creates a new lexical item.
*   **Semantic Value:** In the context of *vergaan*, the prefix *ver-* carries a specific semantic load common in Dutch:
    1.  **Completion/Thoroughness:** Doing the action completely.
    2.  **Negative Outcome/Destruction:** Often implies a transition into a ruined, lost, or non-existent state.
    3.  **Disappearance:** Moving away until nothing remains.

### 4. Semantic Synthesis (Compositional Meaning)
When you combine the morphemes, the literal composition is roughly **"to go thoroughly"** or **"to go away completely."**

However, in Dutch, this combination has undergone **semantic specialization**:
*   **Literal:** To go away.
*   **Idiomatic/Actual Meaning:** To perish, to decay, to sink, to expire, or to pass away.

The prefix *ver-* transforms the neutral motion of *gaan* (going) into a destructive or terminal process.
*   *Het schip vergaat* = The ship goes (away) $\rightarrow$ The ship sinks/perishes.
*   *Het hout vergaat* = The wood goes (away) $\rightarrow$ The wood rots/decays.
*   *De tijd vergaat* = Time goes (away) $\rightarrow$ Time passes/expires.

### 5. Grammatical Properties
From a syntactic and morphological standpoint, *vergaan* exhibits specific traits associated with the prefix *ver-*:

1.  **Inseparability:** The prefix *ver-* is **inseparable**. It does not detach from the root in main clauses.
    *   *Correct:* Hij **vergaat**. (He perishes.)
    *   *Incorrect:* Hij gaat **ver**. (This changes the meaning entirely to "He goes far.")
2.  **Stress Pattern:** Because the prefix is inseparable, the stress falls on the root syllable.
    *   Pronunciation: *ver-GAAN* (not *VER-gaan*).
3.  **Past Participle:** Inseparable verbs in Dutch do not take the *ge-* prefix in the past participle.
    *   *Infinitive:* vergaan
    *   *Participle:* **vergaan** (not *gevergaan*).
4.  **Verb Class:** It is a **strong verb** (irregular).
    *   Present: *ik verga*
    *   Past: *ik verging*
    *   Participle: *vergaan*

### 6. Related Morphological Patterns
The prefix *ver-* functions similarly in other Dutch verbs to indicate a negative or transformative change:
*   *Verbranden* (*ver-* + *branden*): To burn down (destroy by fire).
*   *Verliezen* (*ver-* + *liezen* [archaic root]): To lose.
*   *Verdwijnen* (*ver-* + *dwijnen*): To disappear.
*   *Verrotten* (*ver-* + *rotten*): To rot.

| **gaan** | Root | To go, to move, to proceed. |
| **vergaan** | Verb | To go away completely $\rightarrow$ To perish/decay/sink. |
