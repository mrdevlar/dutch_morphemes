From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`ontbreken`** is a verb that can be segmented into three distinct morphemes. It is a compound of a derivational prefix, a lexical root, and an inflectional suffix.

Here is the breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
**`ont-`** + **`brek-`** + **`-en`**

| Morpheme | Type | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`ont-`** | Derivational Prefix | Changes meaning (negation/removal) |
| **`brek-`** | Root (Stem) | Core lexical meaning ("break") |
| **`-en`** | Inflectional Suffix | Marks the infinitive form |

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Prefix: `ont-`
*   **Classification:** Derivational Prefix (Inseparable).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *unt-*, cognate with German *ent-* and English *un-* or *out-*. It stems from the Proto-Germanic *\*undi-*.
*   **Function:** In Dutch morphology, `ont-` typically indicates **reversal**, **removal**, **separation**, or **negation**.
    *   *Examples:* *ontsluiten* (to unlock/open up), *ontwaken* (to wake up/come out of sleep), *ontvangen* (to receive/get).
*   **Stress:** In words with the `ont-` prefix, the stress always falls on the root syllable, not the prefix (e.g., *ont-BRE-ken*). This marks it as an inseparable prefix.

#### B. The Root: `brek-`
*   **Classification:** Lexical Root (Stem).
*   **Base Verb:** *breken* (to break).
*   **Etymology:** From Proto-Germanic *\*brekaną*.
*   **Cognates:** English **break**, German *brechen*, Swedish *bryta*.
*   **Semantic Shift:** This is the most interesting linguistic aspect of the word.
    *   **Literal:** To physically fracture or snap.
    *   **Figurative:** If something "breaks away" from a whole, it is no longer present. Therefore, the semantic shift moves from "fracturing" to "being absent" or "failing to be present."
    *   *Historical Context:* In Middle Dutch, the concept was often tied to a link in a chain breaking or a part separating from the whole, resulting in a lack.

#### C. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Classification:** Inflectional Suffix.
*   **Function:** This is the standard marker for the **infinitive** form of Dutch verbs.
*   **Grammar:** It does not change the core meaning of the word but indicates the grammatical mood (non-finite).
    *   *Conjugation:* When conjugated, this suffix changes (e.g., *ik ontbreek*, *jij ontbreekt*).

### 3. Semantic Composition (How the meaning is built)

To understand the word linguistically, we must look at how the morphemes combine semantically:

1.  **`breken`** = To break.
2.  **`ont-`** = Away / Off / Negation.
3.  **Literal Combination:** "To break away" or "to be broken off."
4.  **Current Meaning:** "To be missing," "to be absent," or "to lack."

**The Logic:**
If a component of a system has "broken away" (`ont-` + `breken`), it is no longer there. Therefore, the system **lacks** that component.

*   *Example:* "De sleutel **ontbreekt**." (The key **is missing**.)
    *   *Linguistic implication:* The key has "broken away" from the set of items that should be present.

### 4. Grammatical Note
Although *ontbreken* is derived from the strong verb *breken* (which conjugates as *breken - brak - gebroken*), *ontbreken* retains this **strong verb conjugation** pattern in the past tense:
*   Present: *ontbreekt*
*   Past: *ontbrak*
*   Past Participle: *ontbroken*

This confirms that the prefix `ont-` is purely derivational and does not alter the internal vowel gradation (ablaut) of the root verb.
