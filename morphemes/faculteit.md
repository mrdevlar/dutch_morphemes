From a linguistic perspective, the analysis of the Dutch word **`faculteit`** (faculty) requires distinguishing between its **synchronic** structure (how it functions in modern Dutch) and its **diachronic** structure (its historical etymology).

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes.

### 1. Synchronic Analysis (Modern Dutch)
In contemporary Dutch, the word is treated as a **loanword** (leenwoord). To a native speaker, it functions largely as a single unit, but linguists can identify a productive suffix within it.

*   **Word:** `faculteit`
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun (Zelfstandig naamwoord)
*   **Morphological Structure:** `facult` + `-teit`

| Morpheme | Type | Meaning/Function | Status |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`facult-`** | **Bound Root** | Carries the core lexical meaning ("power/ability"). | Cannot stand alone in Dutch. |
| **`-teit`** | **Derivational Suffix** | Forms an abstract noun (often indicating a state, quality, or institution). | Productive in Dutch (e.g., *universiteit*, *identiteit*). |

**Why is `-teit` a morpheme?**
Although it is of foreign origin, `-teit` is a **productive suffix** in Dutch. It is used to create nouns from adjectives or stems of Latin/French origin (e.g., *diversiteit*, *kwaliteit*, *realiteit*). Because it consistently adds the meaning of "abstract quality" or "institution," it is analyzed as a distinct morpheme in Dutch morphology.

**Pluralization:**
The plural form is **`faculteiten`**. This confirms that `faculteit` is the stem. The plural suffix `-en` is added to the end of the word, not inside it.

### 2. Diachronic Analysis (Etymology)
To understand the internal structure of the root `facult-`, we must look at the word's history. The word entered Dutch via French (*faculté*) from Latin (*facultas*).

**Latin Origin:** *facultas*
**French Intermediary:** *faculté*

We can break the etymological root down further:

| Morpheme | Language | Original Form | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`fac-`** | Latin | *facere* | "to do" or "to make" |
| **`-ult-`** | Latin | *-ult-* | A suffix forming a noun stem (related to ability/capacity). |
| **`-as`** | Latin | *-tas* | Abstract noun suffix (state or quality). |

**Semantic Evolution:**
1.  **Latin:** *facultas* originally meant "power," "ability," or "opportunity" (derived from the ability to *do* something).
2.  **French:** *faculté* retained the meaning of "ability" but began to be used for "department of learning."
3.  **Dutch:** *faculteit* specialized to mean specifically a **division of a university** (e.g., *de faculteit der geneeskunde*).

### 3. Phonological Note
From a phonological perspective, the stress pattern is significant for morphological identification.
*   **Pronunciation:** /faˈkʏl.tɛi̯t/
*   **Stress:** The stress falls on the final syllable (**-teit**).
*   **Vowel Change:** The Dutch diphthong `ei` /ɛi/ corresponds to the French `é` /e/ and the Latin `a` (in the suffix *-tas*). This vowel shift is typical of how Latin/French loanwords were adapted into Dutch orthography and phonology.

*   **Total Morphemes:** 2 (in modern Dutch analysis).
*   **Breakdown:** `facult` (bound root) + `-teit` (abstract noun suffix).
*   **Type:** Derivational loanword.
*   **Key Insight:** While it looks like one solid block to a learner, the suffix `-teit` is a recognizable grammatical marker in Dutch that signals an abstract noun or institution of Latin/French origin.
