From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`bedrijfskunde`** is a complex noun formed through **derivation** and **compounding**. It is a classic example of Dutch nominal compounding, where multiple morphemes are strung together to create a specific academic or professional field of study.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation.

### 1. Primary Segmentation
At the surface level (synchronic morphology), the word is a compound of two nouns connected by a linking element:

> **`bedrijf`** + **`-s-`** + **`kunde`**

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. `bedrijf` (Noun)
*   **Meaning:** Business, company, enterprise, operation.
*   **Internal Structure (Diachronic/Deep Morphology):**
    While modern speakers treat *bedrijf* as a single root, historically and etymologically, it is derived from a verb.
    *   **Prefix: `be-`**
        *   **Type:** Derivational prefix.
        *   **Function:** In Dutch, *be-* often turns an intransitive verb into a transitive one or creates a noun indicating the result or scope of an action.
    *   **Root: `drijf`**
        *   **Source:** The verb *drijven* (to drive, to float, to urge, to push).
        *   **Function:** This is a bound root in this context. It does not stand alone as a noun in modern Dutch.
    *   **Suffix/Ending: `-f`**
        *   **Function:** Part of the nominalization process from the verb *drijven*.
    *   **Literal Etymology:** "That which is driven" or "The activity of driving/operating." Over time, the meaning shifted from general "activity" to specific "commercial enterprise."

#### B. `-s-` (Interfix / Linking Element)
*   **Type:** Interfix (*tussenvoegsel*).
*   **Function:** This is a phonological and morphological linker used to connect two nouns in a compound.
*   **Linguistic Note:** Historically, this was a genitive marker (possessive 's'), implying "the *kunde* of the *bedrijf*." In modern Dutch, it is a fossilized linking sound required by the phonotactics of the compound. It is common when the first noun ends in a consonant cluster or specific sounds, though its usage is often lexicalized (you just have to memorize which compounds take an *s*).

#### C. `-kunde` (Noun / Suffix)
*   **Meaning:** Science, field of study, knowledge, skill.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from the verb *kunnen* (to be able to / to know).
*   **Function:** In this context, it acts as a **derivational suffix**. It transforms the concept of "business" into the "academic study of business."
*   **Cognates:** It is cognate with the German suffix *-kunde* (e.g., *Völkerkunde* - ethnology) and relates to the English suffix *-craft* (in the sense of skill) or *-logy* (though *-kunde* implies more practical knowledge than pure theory).

### 3. Semantic Composition
The meaning of the compound is compositional, meaning the whole is derived from the sum of its parts:

1.  **`bedrijf`** = Business / Enterprise
2.  **`kunde`** = Science / Study / Knowledge
3.  **`bedrijfskunde`** = The science/study of business.

In English, the closest equivalent is **Business Administration** or **Business Studies**.

### 4. Morphological Classification
*   **Word Class:** Noun (Substantive).
*   **Word Formation Process:**
    1.  **Derivation:** *drijven* (verb) $\rightarrow$ *bedrijf* (noun).
    2.  **Compounding:** *bedrijf* (noun) + *kunde* (noun) $\rightarrow$ *bedrijfskunde*.
*   **Head of the Compound:** The right-hand element (**`kunde`**). In Dutch compounds, the head determines the grammatical category and the core meaning. Therefore, *bedrijfskunde* is a type of *kunde* (field of study), not a type of *bedrijf* (company).

### 5. Summary Table

| Morpheme | Type | Origin/Source | Meaning/Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **be-** | Prefix | Germanic *bi-* | Creates noun/verb from root; indicates scope. |
| **drijf** | Root | Verb *drijven* | To drive/operate; implies activity. |
| **-s-** | Interfix | Genitive marker | Links the two nouns phonologically. |
| **-kunde** | Suffix/Noun | Verb *kunnen* | Field of study, science, knowledge. |

### 6. Comparative Linguistics
*   **German:** The equivalent is often *Betriebswirtschaftslehre* (more formal/academic) or *Betriebskunde*. Note the cognate *Betrieb* (from *treiben*, to drive) matches *bedrijf*.
*   **English:** *Business Administration*. Note that English uses a noun adjunct (*Business*) + Head (*Administration*), whereas Dutch uses a noun + suffix (*-kunde*).
*   **French:** *Gestion* or *Administration des entreprises*.

This breakdown illustrates how Dutch morphology allows for the creation of precise, long compound nouns by stacking meaningful units (morphemes) together.
