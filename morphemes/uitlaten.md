From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`uitlaten`** is a classic example of a **separable verb** (*scheidbaar werkwoord*).

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation of its components.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word consists of two distinct morphemes:

1.  **`uit`** (Particle / Prefix)
2.  **`laten`** (Verb Stem)

**Structure:** `[Particle: uit] + [Verb: laten]`

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Particle: `uit`
*   **Category:** Preposition / Adverb / Verbal Particle.
*   **Literal Meaning:** "Out," "outside," or "away."
*   **Morphological Status:** In the infinitive form (`uitlaten`), it is written as one word, but it functions as a **separable prefix**. In Dutch morphology, this is distinct from inseparable prefixes (like *be-*, *ge-*, *ver-*).
*   **Phonology:** In a separable verb, the primary stress falls on the particle.
    *   Pronunciation: **ÚIT**-la-ten (Stress on *uit*).
    *   Contrast this with inseparable verbs like *beLÁVEN* (to save), where the stress is on the stem.

#### B. The Verb: `laten`
*   **Category:** Strong/Weak Verb (Infinitive).
*   **Literal Meaning:** "To let," "to allow," "to leave," or "to cause."
*   **Morphological Status:** This is the lexical root (the free morpheme) that carries the core verbal action.
*   **Polysemy:** *Laten* is highly polysemous (has multiple meanings). It can mean permission ("I let him go"), causation ("I had the house painted"), or omission ("I left the bread out").

---

### 3. Syntactic Behavior (The "Separable" Rule)
From a syntactic and morphological perspective, the most important feature of `uitlaten` is its **separability**.

While the infinitive is written as one word (`uitlaten`), in a main clause, the particle `uit` detaches and moves to the end of the sentence.

*   **Infinitive:** Ik wil de hond **uitlaten**. (I want to walk the dog.)
*   **Main Clause:** Ik **laat** de hond **uit**. (I walk the dog.)
*   **Past Participle:** Ik heb de hond **uitgelaten**. (Note the insertion of the *ge-* morpheme between the particle and the stem.)

This behavior confirms that `uit` is not a bound derivational prefix (like *un-* in English *undo*), but a **verbal particle** that forms a compound verb with the stem.

---

### 4. Semantic Composition (Meaning)
The meaning of `uitlaten` is not simply the sum of "out" + "let." It is an idiomatic compound where the particle modifies the verb to create specific meanings.

1.  **Literal/Physical:** To let something out of a container or space.
    *   *Example:* "De lucht uit de band laten." (Let the air out of the tire.)
2.  **Idiomatic (Most Common):** To take a pet (usually a dog) outside for exercise.
    *   *Example:* "Ik moet de hond uitlaten." (I have to walk the dog.)
    *   *Linguistic Note:* Here, `uit` implies "outside" and `laten` implies "to allow to move."
3.  **Abstract:** To express or vent thoughts/emotions.
    *   *Example:* "Zijn frustraties uitlaten." (To vent one's frustrations.)
4.  **Omission:** To leave something out (exclude).
    *   *Example:* "Een regel uit de tekst laten." (To leave a rule out of the text.)

### 5. Cross-Linguistic Comparison
To understand this linguistically, it helps to compare it to English:
*   **Dutch:** `uitlaten` (Separable Verb)
*   **English:** `to let out` (Phrasal Verb)

Both languages use a **Verb + Particle** construction. However, Dutch orthography requires the infinitive to be written as a single word (`uitlaten`), whereas English keeps them separate (`let out`).
