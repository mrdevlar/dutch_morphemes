From a linguistic perspective, the analysis of the Dutch word **`echter`** requires distinguishing between its **synchronic** (current, modern) structure and its **diachronic** (historical/etymological) structure.

Here is the breakdown:

### 1. Synchronic Analysis (Modern Dutch)
In contemporary Dutch morphology, **`echter`** is generally considered **monomorphemic** (consisting of a single morpheme).

*   **Status:** It is a **lexicalized** or **fossilized** function word.
*   **Reasoning:** While the word historically contains parts, a modern Dutch speaker cannot productively analyze it as "root + suffix." You cannot take the adjective *echt* (true) and add *-er* to create *echter* in a new context. The meaning of *echter* ("however") is no longer transparently derived from *echt* ("true").
*   **Part of Speech:** Conjunction / Adverb.
*   **Morpheme Count:** 1 (`echter`).

### 2. Diachronic Analysis (Etymology & History)
If we look at the history of the word (diachronic morphology), it is **bimorphemic**. It is composed of a root and an adverbial suffix.

| Morpheme | Form | Type | Original Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Root** | `echt` | Adjective | True, real, legitimate (from Old Dutch *echt*) |
| **Suffix** | `-er` | Adverbial | Indicates manner or degree (Old Dutch *-er*) |

**Historical Construction:**
*   **`echt`** + **`-er`** $\rightarrow$ **`echter`**
*   **Literal Historical Meaning:** "In truth" or "Truly."

### 3. Semantic Shift (Grammaticalization)
The most interesting linguistic aspect of `echter` is how its meaning changed over time, a process known as **grammaticalization**.

1.  **Stage 1 (Literal):** Originally, it meant "in truth" or "indeed."
    *   *Example:* "Hij is echter gekomen" (He has indeed come).
2.  **Stage 2 (Contrastive):** Through usage, it began to introduce a statement that contradicted or qualified the previous one. If you say "X is true, **however** Y is also true," the word shifts from emphasizing truth to emphasizing contrast.
3.  **Stage 3 (Modern):** It is now exclusively an adversative conjunction meaning "however," "nevertheless," or "but." The connection to the word *echt* (true) is opaque to native speakers.
