From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`universitair`** (meaning "university-related" or "academic") can be analyzed through **morphology** (the study of word structure) and **etymology** (word origins).

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
In modern Dutch morphology, the word is segmented into two primary morphemes:

> **universit-** + **-air**

| Morpheme | Type | Origin | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **universit-** | **Bound Root / Stem** | Latin *universitas* | Carries the core lexical meaning ("university" or "whole"). |
| **-air** | **Derivational Suffix** | Latin *-arius* (via French *-aire*) | Converts the noun/stem into an adjective. |

---

### 2. Detailed Explanation of Morphemes

#### A. The Root: `universit-`
*   **Status:** This is a **bound root**. In modern Dutch, you cannot use `universit-` as a standalone word (you cannot say *"Ik ga naar de universit"*). It must be attached to a suffix.
*   **Relation to Noun:** It shares the same stem as the noun **`universiteit`** (university).
    *   *Universiteit* = `universit-` + `-eit` (Dutch nominal suffix from Latin *-itas*).
    *   *Universitair* = `universit-` + `-air` (Adjectival suffix).
*   **Etymology:** The root comes from the Latin noun *universitas*, meaning "community," "whole," or "corporation."
    *   *Internal Latin Breakdown:*
        *   **`uni-`**: One / Whole (from *unus*).
        *   **`vers-`**: Turn (from *vertere*).
        *   **`-it-`**: Abstract noun suffix (forming *universitas*, literally "turning into one whole").

#### B. The Suffix: `-air`
*   **Status:** **Derivational Suffix**. It changes the part of speech from a noun/stem to an adjective.
*   **Function:** It indicates **pertinence** or **relation**. It answers the question "What kind?" or "Relating to what?"
*   **Etymology:** This suffix entered Dutch primarily through **French** (*-aire*), which borrowed it from **Latin** (*-arius*).
    *   Latin *universitarius* → French *universitaire* → Dutch *universitair*.
*   **Pronunciation:** In Dutch, the stress typically falls on the syllable immediately preceding the suffix: *u-ni-ver-**SI**-tair*.

---

### 3. Semantic Composition
When you combine the morphemes, the meaning is constructed logically:

1.  **`universit-`**: The concept of the university (the institution of higher learning).
2.  **`-air`**: Pertaining to / Characterized by.
3.  **Result**: "Pertaining to the university."

**Example Usage:**
*   *Een **universitaire** opleiding* (A university education).
*   *De **universitaire** wereld* (The academic/university world).

---

### 4. Comparative Morphology (Pattern Recognition)
The suffix **`-air`** is a productive pattern in Dutch for forming adjectives from nouns of Latin/French origin. Recognizing this pattern helps in understanding other words:

| **Literair** | *liter-* (literature) | *-air* | Literary |

| **Personnelair** | *personnel-* (personnel) | *-air* | Personnel-related |

### 5. Summary
From a linguistic standpoint, **`universitair`** is a **derived adjective**. It is formed by taking the Latin-derived bound root **`universit-`** and attaching the adjectival suffix **`-air`**. This process is a classic example of **borrowing** (from Latin/French) and **derivational morphology** within the Dutch language system.
