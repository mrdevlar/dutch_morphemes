From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`duidelijk`** (meaning *clear*, *obvious*, or *evident*) can be analyzed morphologically by breaking it down into its constituent parts (morphemes).

Here is the breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word is composed of two primary morphemes:

> **duid** + **elijk**

*   **duid-**: The root (bound stem).
*   **-elijk**: The derivational suffix.

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Root: `duid-`
*   **Type:** Bound Root (Stem).
*   **Source:** This stem comes from the Dutch verb **`duiden`**.
*   **Meaning:** *To indicate, to interpret, to point out, or to designate.*
*   **Linguistic Note:** In modern Dutch, `duid` cannot stand alone as an independent word (you cannot say "Ik duid"). It is a **bound morpheme** that only appears when attached to inflectional endings (e.g., *duid-en*, *duid-de*) or derivational suffixes (e.g., *duidelijk*).
*   **Etymology:** The verb *duiden* is historically related to the concept of making something known or pointing it out. This is cognate with the German verb *deuten* (to interpret/point out).

#### B. The Suffix: `-elijk`
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix.
*   **Function:** It converts a verb (or noun) stem into an **adjective**.
*   **Meaning:** It generally conveys the meaning of "having the quality of," "capable of being," or "characterized by." It is the Dutch equivalent of the English suffix **-ly** (as in *friend-ly*) or **-able** (as in *read-able*).
*   **Phonology/Spelling:** While the core suffix is often cited as *-lijk*, in Dutch orthography and morphology, an epenthetic schwa vowel (**e**) is frequently inserted before *-lijk* when the stem ends in a consonant to facilitate pronunciation. Thus, *duid* + *lijk* becomes *duid-elijk*.
    *   *Comparison:* *Vriend* (friend) + *lijk* → *Vriendelijk* (friendly).
    *   *Comparison:* *Zeker* (sure) + *lijk* → *Zekerlijk* (surely - note the lack of 'e' here due to phonotactic rules).

---

### 3. Semantic Composition (How the meaning is built)

When you combine the meanings of the morphemes, you get the literal and evolved meaning of the word:

1.  **Literal Construction:** "Having the quality of being pointed out/indicated."
2.  **Semantic Shift:** If something is "capable of being clearly pointed out" or "easy to interpret," it is **clear** or **obvious**.
3.  **Result:** *Duidelijk* = **Clear**.

This semantic evolution is very similar to the German cognate **`deutlich`** (clear), which is derived from *deuten* (to interpret) + *-lich*.

---

### 4. Derivational Family
Understanding the morphemes helps explain related words in the Dutch lexicon:

*   **`duiden`** (Verb): To indicate/interpret. (Base verb).
*   **`duidelijk`** (Adjective): Clear. (Derived from *duid* + *elijk*).
*   **`duidelijker`** (Comparative Adjective): Clearer. (*duidelijk* + *-er*).
*   **`duidelijkst`** (Superlative Adjective): Clearest. (*duidelijk* + *-st*).
*   **`duidelijkheid`** (Noun): Clarity. (*duidelijk* + *-heid*).
    *   *Note:* In *duidelijkheid*, the word *duidelijk* acts as the base, but historically it is still *duid* + *elijk* + *heid*.
