From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`lezing`** is a **derived noun** formed through a process called **derivation**. It consists of two distinct morphemes: a verbal root and a nominalizing suffix.

Here is the breakdown and explanation of the morphemes.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented as follows:

> **`lez-`** + **`-ing`**

*   **`lez-`**: The verbal root (stem).
*   **`-ing`**: The derivational suffix.

---

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Root: `lez-` (from the verb *lezen*)
*   **Source:** This morpheme comes from the Dutch infinitive verb **`lezen`** (to read).
*   **Meaning:** The core semantic meaning is "to read" or "to gather/pick" (historically).
*   **Phonological Note (The s/z alternation):**
    *   In Dutch orthography, the verb is spelled **`lezen`**.
    *   However, the underlying root is phonologically **`/lez/`**.
    *   **Final Devoicing:** Dutch has a rule called *final devoicing* (eindklankverduistering). When a voiced consonant (like /z/) appears at the end of a syllable or word, it becomes voiceless (spelled as `s`).
        *   *Infinitive:* `lezen` [ˈleː.zə(n)] (The `z` is voiced because it is followed by a vowel).
        *   *Stem (conjugation):* `ik lees` [ləs] (The `s` is voiceless because it is at the end of the word).
        *   *Derived form:* `lezing` [ˈleː.zɪŋ] (The `z` is voiced again because the suffix `-ing` starts with a vowel).
    *   Therefore, while the spelling changes between *lezen* and *lezing*, the underlying morpheme remains the same.

#### B. The Suffix: `-ing`
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix (Nominalizer).
*   **Function:** It converts a verb (an action) into a noun (a thing, concept, or result of the action).
*   **Etymology:** This suffix comes from the Proto-Germanic **`*-ingaz`**. It is cognate with the English suffix **`-ing`** (as in *reading*, *building*, *meeting*).
*   **Semantic Role:** In this context, it creates a **verbal noun** (or gerund). It refers to the *act* of reading or the *instance* of reading.

---

### 3. Semantic Development (Polysemy)
The combination of these morphemes creates a word with two primary meanings, which is common in Dutch nominalizations:

1.  **The Act:** The physical action of reading a text (e.g., *"De lezing van dit boek duurt lang"* — The reading of this book takes a long time).
2.  **The Event:** A public talk or lecture (e.g., *"Hij gaf een lezing over geschiedenis"* — He gave a lecture on history).
    *   *Why the shift?* Historically, lectures were often delivered by reading a prepared text aloud to an audience. Over time, the word for the "act of reading aloud" expanded to mean the "talk itself," even if the speaker is not strictly reading from a script.

### 4. Summary Table

| Morpheme | Form | Category | Meaning/Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Root** | `lez-` | Verb Stem | To read (underlying form of *lezen*) |
| **Suffix** | `-ing` | Derivational | Turns the verb into a noun (action/result) |
| **Total** | **`lezing`** | **Noun** | **Reading / Lecture** |

### 5. Cross-Linguistic Comparison
To understand the structure better, compare it to English:

*   **Dutch:** `lezen` (verb) + `-ing` → `lezing` (noun)
*   **English:** `read` (verb) + `-ing` → `reading` (noun)

Both languages use the exact same Germanic morphological strategy to turn an action into a noun, though the spelling of the root differs due to different orthographic conventions.
