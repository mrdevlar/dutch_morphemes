From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`onszelf`** is a **compound word** formed through the combination of two free morphemes. It functions as a **reflexive pronoun**.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into two distinct morphemes:
> **ons** + **zelf**

### 2. Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. Morpheme 1: `ons`
*   **Lexical Category:** Personal Pronoun (1st Person Plural).
*   **Grammatical Form:** Oblique Case (Accusative/Dative).
    *   In Dutch, personal pronouns distinguish between a **strong** (nominative) form and a **weak** (oblique) form.
    *   Strong form: *wij* (we).
    *   Weak form: *ons* (us).
    *   In the compound `onszelf`, the weak form is required because the pronoun functions as the object of the verb (reflexively).
*   **Meaning:** "us".
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *uns*, from Proto-Germanic *\*uns*, and ultimately Proto-Indo-European *\*ns*. It is cognate with the English word **us** and the German word **uns**.

#### B. Morpheme 2: `zelf`
*   **Lexical Category:** Adverb / Adjective / Pronoun (depending on context).
*   **Function in Compound:** Intensifier / Reflexive Marker.
*   **Meaning:** "self".
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *selb*, from Proto-Germanic *\*selbaz*, and Proto-Indo-European *\*selbʰ-*. It is cognate with the English word **self**, the German word **selbst**, and the Swedish word **själv**.
*   **Linguistic Note:** While *zelf* can stand alone as an independent word (e.g., *Ik heb het zelf gedaan* — "I did it myself"), in this context, it acts as a derivational element that converts a personal pronoun into a reflexive pronoun.

### 3. Morphological Process: Endocentric Compounding
*   **Type:** This is a **compound** (specifically a pronominal compound).
*   **Structure:** The structure is `[Pronoun] + [Reflexive Marker]`.
*   **Head:** The compound is **endocentric**, meaning the meaning of the whole is a specific type of the second element. However, in reflexive pronouns, the semantic weight is shared. Syntactically, the whole unit functions as a noun phrase (pronoun).
*   **Productivity:** This is a **productive pattern** in Dutch. You can apply this rule to almost all personal pronouns to create reflexives:
    *   *ik* (I) $\rightarrow$ *me* + *zelf* = **mezelf**
    *   *jij* (you sg.) $\rightarrow$ *je* + *zelf* = **jezelf**
    *   *hij/zij/het* (he/she/it) $\rightarrow$ *zich* + *zelf* = **zichzelf**
    *   *wij* (we) $\rightarrow$ *ons* + *zelf* = **onszelf**
    *   *jullie* (you pl.) $\rightarrow$ *jullie* + *zelf* = **julliezelf**
    *   *zij* (they) $\rightarrow$ *zich* + *zelf* = **zichzelf**

### 4. Phonology and Stress
*   **Orthography:** In modern Standard Dutch, it is written as a single closed compound word (*onszelf*). Historically or in emphatic contexts, it might occasionally appear spaced (*ons zelf*), but the closed form is the standard lexical entry.
*   **Stress Pattern:** Unlike many Dutch compounds where the primary stress falls on the first element (e.g., *BOEKtafel*), reflexive pronouns typically carry the primary stress on the second element, **`zelf`**.
    *   Pronunciation: /ɔnsˈzɛlf/
    *   This stress shift highlights the reflexive nature of the word.

### 5. Syntax and Semantics
*   **Reflexive Function:** The primary function is to indicate that the subject and the object of the verb are the same entity.
    *   *Example:* "Wij wassen **onszelf**." (We wash **ourselves**.)
*   **Emphatic Function:** It can also be used for emphasis (intensive), distinguishing the subject from others.
    *   *Example:* "Wij hebben het **onszelf** gedaan." (We did it **ourselves** [without help].)
*   **Reciprocity:** In some contexts, it can imply a reciprocal action (doing something to each other), though the dedicated reciprocal pronoun *elkaar* is more common for this specific meaning.
