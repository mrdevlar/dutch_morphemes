From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`ontworpen`** is the **past participle** of the verb *ontwerpen* (to design). It is a complex word formed through derivation (adding a prefix) and inflection (marking tense/participle status).

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation of the morphemes.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
We can segment the word into the following components:

> **ont-** + **werp** + **-en**
> *(with a vowel change: e $\rightarrow$ o)*

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Prefix: `ont-`
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix (Inseparable).
*   **Function:** This prefix modifies the meaning of the root verb. In Dutch, *ont-* often indicates a process of separation, removal, or the beginning/completion of an action.
*   **Etymology:** Cognate with the English prefix *un-* (as in *undo*) or the German *ent-*.
*   **Phonology:** In *ontwerpen*, the stress falls on the root (**werp**), not the prefix. This classifies it as an **inseparable verb**. Unlike separable verbs (e.g., *opbellen*), the prefix does not detach in conjugated forms (e.g., *hij ontwerpt*, not *hij werpt op*).
*   **Semantic Contribution:** When added to *werpen* (to throw), *ont-* shifts the meaning from a physical action to an abstract one. It implies "throwing out" ideas or plans.

#### B. The Root: `werp`
*   **Type:** Lexical Root (Stem).
*   **Meaning:** "Throw."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Proto-Germanic **\*werpaną*. It is cognate with the English verb **throw** and the German verb **werfen**.
*   **Morphophonology:** In the infinitive (*ontwerp**en***), the stem vowel is **e**. However, in the past participle form, this vowel undergoes **ablaut** (vowel gradation), changing to **o**. This is a hallmark of **strong verbs** in Germanic languages.

#### C. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix.
*   **Function:** Marks the **Past Participle** (Voltooid Deelwoord).
*   **Distinction:** While the infinitive form also ends in *-en* (*ontwerp**en***), the function here is different. In the participle, *-en* signals that the verb is in a non-finite form used for perfect tenses (e.g., *heeft ontworpen*) or passive voice (e.g., *is ontworpen*).
*   **Note:** In weak verbs, the participle suffix is often *-d* or *-t* (e.g., *gemaakt*). The *-en* ending here confirms that *ontwerpen* is a **strong verb**.

#### D. The Vowel Change (Ablaut): `e` $\rightarrow$ `o`
*   **Type:** Suprasegmental Morphophonemic Process.
*   **Explanation:** This is not a separate morpheme, but a morphological process triggered by the tense/aspect.
*   **Pattern:** *Ontwerpen* belongs to **Strong Verb Class 3**. The vowel pattern for this class generally follows *e - o - o* (Infinitive - Past Participle).
    *   Infinitive: *ontwerp**e**n*
    *   Past Participle: *ontworp**o**n* (spelled *ontworpen*)
*   **Significance:** This internal vowel change is the primary grammatical marker of the past tense/participle for this specific verb, rather than just adding a suffix like *-d*.

### 3. Semantic Evolution (Etymology)
The shift in meaning from "throw" to "design" is a classic example of semantic broadening and metaphor:
1.  **Literal:** *Werpen* = To throw a physical object.
2.  **Metaphorical:** *Ontwerpen* = To "throw out" a sketch, a plan, or an idea onto paper.
3.  **Modern:** To design, draft, or plan.
