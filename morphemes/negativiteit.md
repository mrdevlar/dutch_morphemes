From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`negativiteit`** (meaning "negativity") can be analyzed through both its **synchronic** (current structure) and **diachronic** (historical/etymological) morphology.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
Visually, the word can be segmented into three distinct morphological units:

> **negat** + **iv** + **iteit**

### 2. Morpheme-by-Morpheme Explanation

#### A. `negat-` (Bound Root)
*   **Type:** Bound Root (Lexical Morpheme)
*   **Origin:** Latin *negare* ("to deny" or "to say no").
*   **Function:** This is the core semantic unit carrying the primary meaning of "denial" or "negative."
*   **Note:** In modern Dutch, this root does not exist as a free word (you cannot say "negat"). It appears in related words like *negatie* (negation) and *negatief* (negative).

#### B. `-iv-` (Adjectival Suffix)
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix
*   **Origin:** Latin *-ivus* (via French *-if*).
*   **Function:** This suffix turns the root into an adjective, indicating "tending to" or "relating to."
*   **Context:** In the standalone adjective **`negatief`**, this suffix is realized as *-ief*. However, when forming the noun, the stem reverts to the Latin form *-iv-*.
*   **Morphophonology:** This is where the **f/v alternation** occurs. In the adjective *negatief*, the sound is voiceless /f/. In the noun stem *negativ-*, the sound becomes voiced /v/. This is a common pattern in Dutch Latinate words (e.g., *actief* → *activiteit*, *positief* → *positiviteit*).

#### C. `-iteit` (Nominalizing Suffix)
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix
*   **Origin:** Latin *-itas* (via French *-ité*).
*   **Function:** This suffix converts the adjective into an abstract noun. It denotes a state, quality, or condition.
*   **Meaning:** It adds the meaning of "the quality of being..." to the stem.
*   **Pronunciation:** In Dutch, this is pronounced as a sequence of vowels: /ɛi.tɛit/ (often sounding like *ei-teit*).

### 3. Synchronic vs. Diachronic Analysis

To fully understand the word, it helps to look at it from two angles:

**1. Synchronic (Modern Dutch Structure):**
In the mind of a modern Dutch speaker, the word is often processed as:
*   **Base:** *negatief* (Adjective)
*   **Process:** Stem change (*negatief* → *negativ-*) + Suffix (*-iteit*)
*   **Result:** *negativiteit*

**2. Diachronic (Historical/Etymological Structure):**
Historically, the word was borrowed directly from Latin (*negativitas*) or French (*négativité*). The breakdown follows the Latin construction:
*   **Root:** *neg-* (deny)
*   **Suffix 1:** *-at-* (participle marker)
*   **Suffix 2:** *-iv-* (adjective marker)
*   **Suffix 3:** *-itas* (noun marker)
