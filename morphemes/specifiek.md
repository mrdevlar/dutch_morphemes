From a linguistic perspective, the analysis of the Dutch word **`specifiek`** requires distinguishing between **synchronic morphology** (how the word is structured in modern Dutch) and **diachronic morphology** (its historical etymological structure).

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes.

### 1. Synchronic Analysis (Modern Dutch)
In contemporary Dutch, *specifiek* is generally treated as a **loanword** that functions as a single lexical unit. However, for morphological analysis, it can be segmented into a root and a suffix, as it participates in Dutch derivational patterns.

*   **Word:** `specifiek`
*   **Part of Speech:** Adjective
*   **Morphemes:** 2 (Root + Suffix)
    1.  **`specif-`** (Root/Stem)
    2.  **`-iek`** (Adjectival Suffix)

**Explanation:**
*   **`specif-`**: This is the bound root. It carries the core semantic meaning ("specific"). It cannot stand alone as a word in Dutch (you cannot say *"Dit is specif"*).
*   **`-iek`**: This is a borrowed adjectival suffix (from French *-ique* / Latin *-icus*). It turns the root into an adjective. This suffix is productive in Dutch for loanwords (e.g., *energetiek*, *karakteristiek*, *publiek*).

### 2. Diachronic Analysis (Etymology)
To understand the *meaning* of the morphemes, we must look at the word's origin. *Specifiek* entered Dutch via French (*spécifique*) from Latin (*specificus*). In Latin, the word was transparently compound.

*   **Origin:** Latin *specificus*
*   **Morphemes:** 2 (Noun Stem + Agentive Suffix)
    1.  **`species`** (Noun Stem)
    2.  **`-ficus`** (Suffix)

**Detailed Breakdown:**

| Morpheme | Origin | Literal Meaning | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`speci-`** | Latin *species* | "Kind," "Form," "Appearance," "Look" | Defines the category or type. |
| **`-fic-`** | Latin *facere* | "To make," "To do" | Indicates the action of creating or defining. |
| **`-us`** | Latin | Adjectival ending | Marks the word as an adjective. |

**Semantic Construction:**
Literally, *specificus* means **"making a kind"** or **"pertaining to a kind."**
*   Over time, the meaning shifted from "making a kind" to "distinguishing a kind," and finally to the modern meaning of "clearly defined" or "particular."

### 3. Phonological and Orthographic Notes
*   **Stress:** In Dutch, the stress falls on the second syllable: *spe-**CI**-fiek* (/speˈsifɛik/).
*   **Spelling:** The Latin *ph* (from *species*) became *f* in Dutch. The Latin *-icus* became *-iek* (via French *-ique*).
*   **Vowel:** The *ie* represents a long /i/ sound (similar to English "see").

### 4. Derivational Family
To confirm the morphological structure, we can look at how the word changes in Dutch. This proves that **`specif-`** acts as the stable root.

*   **`specifiek`** (Adjective): Specific.
    *   *Structure:* `specif-` + `-iek`
*   **`specificiteit`** (Noun): Specificity.
    *   *Structure:* `specif-` + `-iciteit` (Suffix *-iteit* added to the stem).
*   **`specifiseren`** (Verb): To specify.
    *   *Structure:* `specif-` + `-iseren` (Verb suffix).

### Summary
*   **Linguistically:** It is a **loanword** that has been nativized.
*   **Morphologically:** It consists of the root **`specif-`** and the suffix **`-iek`**.
*   **Etymologically:** It is composed of **`species`** (kind) + **`-ficus`** (making).

While a modern Dutch speaker perceives it as one chunk of meaning, its internal structure reveals a history of "making a kind" which evolved into "being particular."
