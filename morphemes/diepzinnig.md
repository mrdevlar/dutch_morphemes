From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`diepzinnig`** (meaning *profound*, *deep-thinking*, or *philosophical*) is a complex adjective formed through compounding and derivation.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation of its components.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **diep** + **zin** + **-ig**

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. `diep` (Free Morpheme)
*   **Category:** Adjective (Root)
*   **Meaning:** "Deep."
*   **Function:** In this compound, it acts as a modifier. It does not refer to physical depth (like a hole), but rather metaphorical or intellectual depth.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *diep*, which comes from Proto-Germanic **\*deupaz*.
*   **Cognates:** English *deep*, German *tief*, Danish *dyb*.

#### B. `zin` (Free Morpheme)
*   **Category:** Noun (Root)
*   **Meaning:** "Sense," "mind," "intention," or "meaning."
*   **Function:** This is the semantic core of the word. In the context of *diepzinnig*, it refers to the intellect or the capacity for thought.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *sin*, from Proto-Germanic **\*sinnaz* (mind, sense, direction).
*   **Cognates:** English *sense* (via Latin *sentire*, but related conceptually), German *Sinn*, English *sin* (in the archaic sense of "journey" or "aim," e.g., "on the sin of the road").
*   **Note:** In modern Dutch, *zin* is polysemous. It can mean "sentence" (grammatical), "desire" (e.g., *zin hebben* = to feel like), or "sense/mind" (e.g., *gezond verstand* = healthy sense). Here, it specifically denotes the **mind/intellect**.

#### C. `-ig` (Bound Morpheme)
*   **Category:** Derivational Suffix
*   **Meaning:** "Having the quality of," "full of," or "characterized by."
*   **Function:** This suffix turns the noun *zin* into the adjective *zinnig* (sensible/rational). When combined with *diep*, it creates an adjective describing someone or something that possesses the quality of "deep sense."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *-ag*, from Proto-Germanic **\*-agaz*.
*   **Cognates:** English *-y* (as in *sunny*, *happy*), German *-ig* (as in *königlich*), Swedish *-ig*.

### 3. Structural Hierarchy
Linguistically, the construction happens in two layers:

1.  **Derivation:** The noun **`zin`** combines with the suffix **`-ig`** to form the adjective **`zinnig`** (meaning *sensible* or *rational*).
2.  **Compounding:** The adjective **`diep`** is compounded with **`zinnig`** to form **`diepzinnig`**.

**Formula:** `[ diep ] + [ [ zin ] + [ -ig ] ]`

### 4. Semantic Shift (Literal vs. Figurative)
*   **Literal Construction:** "Deep" + "Sense/Mind" + "Having the quality of."
*   **Literal Translation:** "Having a deep mind" or "Possessing deep sense."
*   **Actual Usage:** The meaning has lexicalized (become a fixed unit). It no longer just means "having a lot of thoughts," but specifically refers to **profound wisdom**, **insight**, or **philosophical depth**. It is often used to describe literature, speeches, or people who think deeply about complex issues.
