From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`doen`** (meaning "to do") is primarily analyzed as a **verb** in its **infinitive** form.

While it appears to be a single unit, morphologically it can be broken down into **two morphemes**: a lexical root (stem) and a grammatical suffix. However, because *doen* is an irregular verb, the relationship between these morphemes is more complex than in regular verbs.

Here is the breakdown:

### 1. The Morphological Breakdown

| Morpheme | Form | Type | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Root/Stem** | **`doe-`** | Free / Lexical | Carries the core semantic meaning ("to do"). |
| **2. Suffix** | **`-n`** | Bound / Grammatical | Marks the **infinitive** form of the verb. |

**Total Morphemes:** 2

---

### 2. Detailed Explanation of Morphemes

#### A. The Root: `doe-`
*   **Lexical Meaning:** This morpheme carries the semantic content of the action. It corresponds to the English root "do".
*   **Stem Identification:** In Dutch morphology, the stem is typically identified by removing the infinitive ending.
    *   *Infinitive:* `doen`
    *   *Minus Infinitive Marker:* `doe`
*   **Usage:** This stem appears in the present tense conjugation for the first and third person singular:
    *   *Ik **doe*** (I do)
    *   *Hij **doe*** (He does — *Note: actually 'doet' due to consonant doubling rules, but the vowel remains*)
*   **Irregularity:** Unlike regular verbs (e.g., *werken* → stem *werk-*), the vowel in this root changes in other tenses (a phenomenon called **ablaut** or vowel gradation).
    *   Present: *doe* (/du/)
    *   Past: *deed* (/de:t/)
    *   Past Participle: *gedaan* (/ɣəda:n/)
    *   *Linguistic Note:* Because the vowel changes so drastically, some linguists argue that `doe-` is not a stable underlying root, but rather a surface realization of a deeper root that undergoes suppletion or strong vowel mutation.

#### B. The Suffix: `-n`
*   **Grammatical Function:** This is the **infinitive marker**. In Dutch, the standard infinitive suffix is **`-en`**.
*   **Phonological Reduction:** You might wonder why it is spelled `-n` instead of `-en`.
    *   Standard Rule: Stem + `-en` (e.g., *werk* + *en* = *werken*).
    *   Exception Rule: If the stem ends in a vowel (like `doe-`), the `-e-` of the suffix is elided (dropped) to avoid a sequence of two vowels (*doeen* would be phonologically awkward in standard Dutch).
    *   Therefore, the suffix is realized as the allomorph **`-n`**.
*   **Comparison:** This happens with other vowel-final stems as well:
    *   *gaan* (to go) → Stem *ga-* + *-n*
    *   *zien* (to see) → Stem *zie-* + *-n*

---

### 3. Diachronic Perspective (Etymology)

Looking at the history of the word adds another layer to the morphological analysis:

*   **Old Dutch:** *dōn*
*   **Proto-Germanic:** *\*dōną*
*   **Proto-Indo-European (PIE):** *\*dʰeh₁-* (meaning "to put, set, do")

Historically, the word was a **strong verb** (Class VII). The modern Dutch form `doen` preserves the nasal consonant from the Proto-Germanic past participle suffix, which eventually became part of the infinitive form in the Germanic languages.

### 4. Summary

In a synchronic (current state) analysis of Modern Dutch:
1.  **`doe-`** is the **stem** carrying the meaning.
2.  **`-n`** is the **infinitive suffix** (a reduced form of *-en*).

However, due to the verb's irregularity (specifically the vowel change in the past tense *deed* and participle *gedaan*), the root is not as transparent as in regular verbs. In a strict morphological sense, it is **bimorphemic** (two parts), but in terms of lexical storage, it is often treated as a single irregular unit.
