### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### **A. The Root: `tut-`**
*   **Type:** Lexical Morpheme (Free/Bound depending on analysis).
*   **Function:** Carries the core semantic meaning of the word.
*   **Word Class:** Originally a Noun, functioning here as a Verbal Root.
*   **Explanation:**
    *   **Denominative Derivation:** This is a **denominative verb**, meaning it is derived from a noun. The noun *`tut`* exists in Dutch (historically meaning "snout" or "nose," and in modern colloquial usage, "dummy" or "pacifier").
    *   **Semantic Shift:** The meaning has evolved from "suckling" (related to a pacifier/snout) to the modern colloquial meaning of "fiddling," "tinkering," or "messing around with something."
    *   **Expressive Morphology:** The root exhibits **sound symbolism** (phonesthetics). The short, plosive /t/ sound and the repetition of the consonant suggest a repetitive, small, or fidgety action. This is common in Dutch verbs describing manual dexterity or minor adjustments (e.g., *kloppen*, *tikken*).

#### **B. The Suffix: `-en`**
*   **Type:** Inflectional Morpheme.
*   **Function:** Verbalizer / Infinitive Marker.
*   **Explanation:**
    *   **Infinitive:** In Dutch, the suffix `-en` is the standard marker for the infinitive form of a verb (the dictionary form).
    *   **Conjugation:** This suffix is removed to form the present tense stem (e.g., *ik tut*, *jij tut*) and is replaced by other endings for past tense or participles (e.g., *tutte*, *getut*).

### 3. Orthographic & Phonological Note (The Double `t`)
You may notice the spelling is **`tutten`** (with two `t`s), but the morphological root is **`tut-`**.

*   **Phonology:** The vowel `u` in this word is **short** (/ʏ/).
*   **Orthographic Rule:** In Dutch spelling, a short vowel in a closed syllable must be followed by a double consonant to prevent the vowel from being read as long.
*   **Morphological Implication:** The second `t` is **not** a separate morpheme. It is an orthographic device to preserve the pronunciation of the root `tut-` when the suffix `-en` is added.
    *   *Incorrect analysis:* `tut` + `t` + `en`
    *   *Correct analysis:* `tut` + `en` (with orthographic gemination).

### 5. Usage Context
While the morphological breakdown is straightforward, the usage is specific. `Tutten` is rarely used transitively on its own. It is almost always used with the preposition **`aan`** (to tinker *with* something).
*   *Example:* "Hij is **aan het tutten**." (He is tinkering/fiddling.)
*   *Example:* "Ik **tut** aan de radio." (I am fiddling with the radio.)

This construction (`aan het` + verb + `en`) is the **progressive aspect** in Dutch, formed using the verb *zijn* (to be) + the preposition *aan* + the infinitive.
