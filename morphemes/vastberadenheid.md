From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`vastberadenheid`** is a complex noun formed through compounding and derivation. It can be segmented into three distinct morphemes.

Here is the breakdown:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
**`vast`** + **`beraden`** + **`heid`**

*   **`vast`**: Free morpheme (Adjective/Adverb)
*   **`beraden`**: Bound morpheme (Past Participle of a verb, functioning as part of a compound adjective)
*   **`heid`**: Derivational Suffix (Nominalizer)

---

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. `vast`
*   **Category:** Free morpheme (can stand alone as a word).
*   **Part of Speech:** Adjective / Adverb.
*   **Literal Meaning:** Fixed, firm, solid, steady, fast.
*   **Function in Compound:** It acts as an adverbial modifier to the participle `beraden`. It specifies *how* the deliberation was done (firmly).
*   **Etymology:** From Old Dutch *vast*, Proto-Germanic **\*fastuz*.
*   **Cognates:** English **"fast"** (in the sense of "hold fast" or "firm," not speed), German *fest*.

#### B. `beraden`
*   **Category:** Complex morpheme (Verb root + Prefix + Inflection).
*   **Structure:** `be-` + `raden` + `-en` (infinitive/participle marker).
*   **Part of Speech:** Past Participle (used adjectivally).
*   **Literal Meaning:** Deliberated, resolved, advised.
*   **Sub-analysis:**
    *   **`be-`**: A verbal prefix that often makes a verb transitive or adds a sense of "affecting" or "covering."
    *   **`raden`**: The root verb meaning "to advise," "to counsel," or "to guess."
    *   **`-en`**: The standard ending for Dutch infinitives and past participles of weak verbs.
*   **Function in Compound:** It provides the core action/state. `Vastberaden` literally means "firmly deliberated."
*   **Etymology:** `Rad` comes from Proto-Germanic **\*rēdaną* (to advise, interpret).
*   **Cognates:** English **"read"** (originally meant to advise or interpret runes), **"rede"** (counsel/advice, archaic), German *raten*.

#### C. `heid`
*   **Category:** Derivational Suffix (Bound morpheme).
*   **Part of Speech:** Nominalizer (turns an adjective into a noun).
*   **Literal Meaning:** -ness, -hood, -ity.
*   **Function:** It abstracts the quality of the adjective `vastberaden` into a concrete concept or state of being.
*   **Etymology:** From Old Dutch *heit*, Proto-Germanic **\*-haiti*.
*   **Cognates:** English **"-hood"** (e.g., childhood, brotherhood), German **"-heit"** (e.g., *Freiheit*).

---

### 3. Semantic Composition (How the meaning builds)

The meaning of the word is constructed hierarchically:

1.  **`raden`**: To advise/deliberate.
2.  **`beraden`**: To deliberate upon something (to form a plan).
3.  **`vastberaden`**: Firmly deliberated $\rightarrow$ **Determined / Resolved** (Adjective).
4.  **`vastberadenheid`**: The state or quality of being determined $\rightarrow$ **Determination / Resolve** (Noun).

### 4. Linguistic Summary
*   **Word Class:** Noun (Abstract).
*   **Stress Pattern:** In Dutch compounds, primary stress typically falls on the first element. Pronunciation: **`VAST`**-be-ra-den-heid.
*   **Morphological Type:** This is a **derived compound**. First, two roots are compounded (`vast` + `beraden`) to form an adjective. Then, a suffix (`-heid`) is added to derive a noun.
