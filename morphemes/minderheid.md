From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`minderheid`** (meaning "minority") is a **derived noun** formed through the process of suffixation. It belongs to the Germanic language family and shares significant morphological history with English.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes, moving from the surface structure to the deeper etymological roots.

### 1. Surface Morphological Segmentation
At the synchronic level (how the word is structured in modern Dutch), the word consists of two morphemes:

> **`minder`** + **`-heid`**

#### **Morpheme 1: `minder`**
*   **Category:** Adjective (Base/Root)
*   **Meaning:** "Less," "fewer," or "inferior in quantity."
*   **Function:** It provides the semantic core of the word.
*   **Linguistic Note:** In modern Dutch, *minder* functions as the comparative form of the adjective *weinig* (little/few). However, historically, it is the comparative of the root *min*.

#### **Morpheme 2: `-heid`**
*   **Category:** Derivational Suffix
*   **Meaning:** "State," "condition," "quality," or "nature of."
*   **Function:** It is a **nominalizer**. It converts the adjective *minder* into an abstract noun.
*   **Cognates:** This suffix is directly cognate with the English suffixes **`-hood`** (e.g., *childhood*, *brotherhood*) and **`-ness`** (e.g., *darkness*, *kindness*).
*   **Phonology:** In pronunciation, the *h* is often silent or very soft, and the *ei* is pronounced as a diphthong /ɛi/ (similar to the 'ay' in "day"), resulting in /ˈmɪn.dər.hɛi̯t/.

### 2. Diachronic (Historical) Breakdown
If we dig deeper into the etymology (diachronic morphology), the first morpheme (`minder`) can be further segmented. This reveals the Proto-Germanic roots.

> **`min`** + **`-der`** + **`-heid`**

#### **Sub-Morpheme: `min`**
*   **Origin:** Proto-Germanic *\*miniz*.
*   **Meaning:** "Little" or "Less."
*   **Cognates:** English *mean* (in the sense of "lowly"), German *minder*, and related to the Latin *minus*.

#### **Sub-Morpheme: `-der`**
*   **Origin:** Proto-Germanic comparative suffix *\*-iz-*.
*   **Function:** Marks the **comparative degree**.
*   **Cognates:** This is the direct ancestor of the English comparative suffix **`-er`** (e.g., *fast* → *fast**er***).
*   **Result:** *min* + *-der* = *minder* ("less" or "smaller").

---

### 3. Semantic Composition
The meaning of the whole word is constructed logically from its parts:

1.  **`min`** (Little)
2.  **`-der`** (Comparative: Less)
3.  **`-heid`** (State/Condition)

**Literal Translation:** "The state or condition of being less."
**Actual Meaning:** "Minority" (a group smaller in number than the rest).

To understand the morphology fully, it helps to compare it with English equivalents:

| Dutch | English | Morphological Structure |

However, English does have the native equivalent construction in words like *childhood* (child + hood) or *manhood* (man + hood), which function exactly like *minderheid*.
