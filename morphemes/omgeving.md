From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`omgeving`** (meaning "environment," "surroundings," or "vicinity") is a complex word formed through compounding and derivation.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **`om`** + **`geven`** + **`-ing`**

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. `om` (Prefix / Preposition)
*   **Category:** Preposition functioning as a verbal prefix.
*   **Meaning:** "Around," "about," or "surrounding."
*   **Function:** In this context, it modifies the meaning of the verb root. It indicates spatial enclosure.
*   **Linguistic Note:** In Dutch, `om` creates an **inseparable verb** when attached to `geven`. This is crucial because inseparable verbs do not take the standard past participle prefix `ge-` (unlike separable verbs).

#### B. `geven` (Root Verb)
*   **Category:** Lexical Verb (Strong/Irregular).
*   **Meaning:** Literally "to give."
*   **Semantic Shift:** In the compound `omgeven`, the meaning has shifted from "to give" to "to place around" or "to enclose." This is a common semantic bleaching and specialization in Germanic languages.
*   **Crucial Distinction:** The `ge-` at the start of `geven` is **part of the root**, not the past participle marker.
    *   *Regular Verb Example:* `werken` (to work) → Participle `gewerkt` (with `ge-` prefix) → Noun `werking` (the `ge-` is dropped).
    *   *This Word:* `geven` (root contains `ge-`) → Participle `omgeven` (no extra `ge-` added because `om-` is inseparable) → Noun `omgeving` (the root `ge-` remains).

#### C. `-ing` (Suffix)
*   **Category:** Derivational Suffix (Nominalizer).
*   **Function:** Converts the verb (or participle) into a noun.
*   **Meaning:** It typically denotes the **result**, **process**, or **state** of the action.
*   **Formation:** It attaches to the stem of the past participle. Since the past participle of `omgeven` is `omgeven`, the noun becomes `omgeving`.

### 3. Morphological Process
The formation of this word follows a specific derivational path:

1.  **Compounding:** The preposition `om` combines with the verb `geven` to create the inseparable verb **`omgeven`** (to surround).
2.  **Participle Formation:** The past participle of `omgeven` is **`omgeven`**. (Note: Because `om-` is an inseparable prefix, the standard participle prefix `ge-` is *not* added).
3.  **Nominalization:** The suffix **`-ing`** is added to the participle stem to create the abstract noun **`omgeving`**.

### 4. Etymological Context
*   **Origin:** Middle Dutch *omgeven*.
*   **Root:** Old High German *umbigeban*.
*   **Composition:** *umbi* (around) + *geban* (to give/place).
*   **Evolution:** Over time, the literal sense of "giving around" evolved into the spatial sense of "surrounding," and finally into the modern abstract sense of "environment."

### 5. Summary Table

| Morpheme | Type | Meaning | Role in Word |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **om** | Prefix | Around | Indicates spatial enclosure. |
| **geven** | Root | To give/place | The core action (semantically shifted to "surround"). |
| **-ing** | Suffix | State/Result | Turns the action into a noun (the environment). |

### 6. Linguistic Insight: The "Ge" Trap
A common point of confusion in Dutch morphology is the `ge-` in `omgeving`.
*   In words like **`werking`** (from *werken*), the `ge-` is absent because it was the participle marker which is dropped during nominalization.
*   In **`omgeving`**, the `ge-` **remains** because it is an intrinsic part of the root verb `geven`.
*   Therefore, `omgeving` is not `om` + `ge` (participle) + `ven` + `ing`. It is `om` + `geven` (root) + `ing`.

### Final Translation of Structure
Literally, the morphemes construct the meaning:
**"The state/result of [placing] [around]."** → **Environment.**
