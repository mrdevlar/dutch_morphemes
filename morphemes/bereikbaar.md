From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`bereikbaar`** (meaning "reachable" or "accessible") can be broken down into four distinct morphological components.

Here is the morphological segmentation and explanation:

### 1. Segmentation
**`be-`** + **`reik`** + **`-a-`** + **`-baar`**

---

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### **A. Prefix: `be-`**
*   **Function:** Derivational Prefix.
*   **Type:** Inseparable prefix (it cannot be separated from the verb in conjugation).
*   **Meaning/Effect:** In Dutch, the prefix *be-* often transforms a verb into a transitive one (adding an object) or adds a sense of "covering," "affecting," or "completing" an action.
*   **In this context:** It modifies the root *reiken* (to reach/stretch) to create *bereiken* (to reach/attain a goal). It shifts the meaning from the physical act of stretching out to the successful attainment of a target.
*   **Etymology:** Cognate with the English prefix *be-* (as in *befriend*, *beset*) and German *be-*.

#### **B. Root: `reik`**
*   **Function:** Lexical Root (Stem).
*   **Source:** Derived from the verb *reiken* (to reach, to stretch, to extend).
*   **Meaning:** The core semantic concept of extending one's hand or attaining something.
*   **Etymology:** From Proto-Germanic **\*rīkjaną*. It is a direct cognate with the English word **reach** and the German word **reichen**.
*   **Note:** In the infinitive *bereiken*, the stem is *bereik* (formed by *be-* + *reik*).

#### **C. Linking Vowel (Interfix): `-a-`**
*   **Function:** Phonological Linker (Voegklank).
*   **Analysis:** This is a specific orthographic and phonological feature of Dutch morphology. While the suffix *-baar* attaches directly to many stems (e.g., *leesbaar* from *lezen*), certain stems require a linking vowel to facilitate pronunciation or due to historical lexicalization.
*   **Spelling:** This explains why the word is spelled **`bereikbaar`** rather than the theoretically expected **`bereikbaar`**. The *-a-* acts as a buffer between the velar stop /k/ and the bilabial stop /b/.
*   **Linguistic Note:** In some analyses, this *-a-* is considered part of the suffix *-baar* in this specific lexical item, but morphologically it functions as an interfix.

#### **D. Suffix: `-baar`**
*   **Function:** Derivational Suffix.
*   **Part of Speech Change:** Converts a Verb (*bereiken*) into an Adjective (*bereikbaar*).
*   **Meaning:** Indicates **potentiality** or **passive capability**. It means "capable of being [verb]ed."
*   **Equivalent:** It corresponds to the English suffix **-able** or **-ible** (e.g., *readable*, *visible*).
*   **Etymology:** From Old Dutch *-bar*, related to the German *-bar* and English *-able* (via Latin *-bilis* influence on Germanic).
*   **Pronunciation:** The *aa* represents a long open /aː/ sound.

---

### 3. Semantic Construction
The meaning of the word is constructed hierarchically:

1.  **`reik`**: The concept of reaching.
2.  **`be-` + `reik`**: The act of successfully attaining or reaching a target (*bereiken*).
3.  **`-baar`**: The quality of being able to be reached.
4.  **Result**: "Capable of being attained/reached."

### 4. Usage and Nuance
*   **Physical vs. Abstract:** While *reikbaar* (without *be-*) exists, it is less common and often implies physical reachability (e.g., "Can you reach that shelf?"). **`Bereikbaar`** is the standard term for **accessibility** in broader contexts, such as:
    *   *Is het adres bereikbaar?* (Is the address accessible/reachable?)
    *   *Is hij telefonisch bereikbaar?* (Is he reachable by phone?)
    *   *Een bereikbaar doel* (An attainable goal).
