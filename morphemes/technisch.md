From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`technisch`** (meaning "technical") can be analyzed through its morphological structure and etymology.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word consists of two distinct morphemes:

> **techn-** + **-isch**

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Root: `techn-`
*   **Type:** Lexical Root (Bound Stem)
*   **Origin:** Ancient Greek *τέχνη* (*tekhnē*).
*   **Meaning:** "Art," "craft," "skill," or "method."
*   **Function:** This morpheme carries the core semantic content of the word. It defines the subject matter (technology/craftsmanship).
*   **Linguistic Note:** In Dutch, `techn-` is a **bound root**. You cannot use it as a standalone word (you cannot say *"Ik ben techn"*). It must be attached to a suffix or appear within a derived noun like *techniek* (technique/technology) or *technicus* (technician).
*   **Phonology:** In Dutch, the `ch` in this root represents the voiceless velar fricative **/x/** (similar to the 'ch' in Scottish *loch* or German *Bach*). This differs from English, where the cognate root *techn-* is pronounced with a /k/ sound (due to the Second Germanic Sound Shift).

#### B. The Suffix: `-isch`
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix
*   **Origin:** Greek *-ικός* (*-ikos*) $\rightarrow$ Latin *-icus* $\rightarrow$ French *-ique* $\rightarrow$ Dutch *-isch*.
*   **Meaning:** "Pertaining to," "characterized by," or "of the nature of."
*   **Function:** This is an **adjectivalizer**. It converts the noun stem (*techn-*) into an adjective.
*   **Productivity:** This is a highly productive suffix in Dutch. It is used to create adjectives from nouns of foreign (mostly Latin/Greek) origin.
    *   *Historie* (History) $\rightarrow$ *Historisch* (Historical)
    *   *Politiek* (Politics) $\rightarrow$ *Politiek* (Political) (Note: spelling varies slightly)
    *   *Economie* (Economy) $\rightarrow$ *Economisch* (Economic)
*   **Comparison:** It is the direct cognate of the English suffixes **-ic** or **-ical** (e.g., *technic* $\rightarrow$ *technical*).

### 3. Semantic Composition
When combined, the morphemes create the following meaning:

*   **techn-** (craft/skill) + **-isch** (pertaining to) = **"Pertaining to craft/skill."**

Over time, the semantic scope narrowed from general "craft" to specifically "scientific or engineering skill," resulting in the modern meaning of **"technical."**

### 4. Related Morphological Family
To understand the root `techn-` better, it helps to look at its related forms in the Dutch lexicon:

*   **Noun:** *De techniek* (The technique/technology) — *Suffix: -iek*
*   **Agent Noun:** *De technicus* (The technician) — *Suffix: -icus*
*   **Adjective:** *Technisch* (Technical) — *Suffix: -isch*
*   **Adverb:** *Technisch* (Technically) — *Same form as adjective, context-dependent*
