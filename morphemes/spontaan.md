From a linguistic perspective, the analysis of the Dutch word **`spontaan`** requires distinguishing between **synchronic morphology** (how the word functions in modern Dutch) and **diachronic morphology** (its historical etymological structure).

Here is the breakdown:

### 1. Synchronic Analysis (Modern Dutch)
In the context of contemporary Dutch grammar, **`spontaan`** is considered **monomorphemic**.

*   **Morpheme:** `spontaan`
*   **Type:** Root / Lexeme (Adjective)
*   **Explanation:** To a modern Dutch speaker, this word cannot be divided into smaller meaningful units.
    *   There is no independent Dutch morpheme `spont` (you cannot say "Ik ben spont").
    *   There is no productive Dutch suffix `-aan` attached to this specific root (unlike in *Amerikaan*, where `-aan` denotes origin).
    *   Therefore, it functions as a single, unanalyzable unit of meaning.

### 2. Diachronic Analysis (Etymology)
If we look at the history of the word (diachronic perspective), it is **polymorphemic**, borrowed from Latin via French and German.

*   **Structure:** `spont-` + `-aan`
*   **Origin:** Latin *spontaneus*

#### A. The Root: `spont-`
*   **Source:** Latin *sponte* (ablative case of *spons*).
*   **Original Meaning:** "Of one's own accord," "voluntarily," or "of free will."
*   **Etymological Note:** The Latin noun *spons* is related to *spondere* (to pledge). The core concept is an action taken by one's own will rather than by force.
*   **Dutch Adaptation:** The final *-e* of the Latin ablative *sponte* was dropped during the borrowing process (common in loanwords entering Germanic languages).

#### B. The Suffix: `-aan`
*   **Source:** Latin *-aneus* (adjective-forming suffix).
*   **Intermediate Forms:**
    *   French: *spontané*
    *   German: *spontan*
*   **Function:** It turns the root into an adjective meaning "pertaining to" or "characterized by."
*   **Dutch Adaptation:** In Dutch orthography, a single `a` usually represents a short vowel (as in *kat*). To represent the long vowel sound /aː/ found in the French/German source, Dutch spelling rules require doubling the vowel to **`aa`**. Thus, *-aneus* / *-an* became `-aan`.

### 3. Morphological Behavior in Dutch
Even though `spontaan` is a loanword, it has been fully integrated into Dutch morphology. It behaves like a regular Dutch adjective:

1.  **Inflection:**
    *   **Predicative:** *De actie was **spontaan**.* (The action was spontaneous.)
    *   **Attributive (with 'de'):** *Een **spontane** actie.* (A spontaneous action.)
    *   **Attributive (with 'het'):** *Een **spontaan** besluit.* (A spontaneous decision.)
    *   *Note:* The addition of the inflectional suffix `-e` in *spontane* is a standard Dutch grammatical process applied to the loanword root.

2.  **Derivation:**
    *   **Noun:** *Spontaanheid* (Spontaneity).
        *   Breakdown: `spontaan` + `-heid` (abstract noun suffix).
    *   **Adverb:** *Spontaan* (Spontaneously).
        *   In Dutch, the adjective form often serves as the adverb without change.

While **`spontaan`** is historically composed of a root and a suffix, linguistically it is treated as a **single morpheme** in modern Dutch. The internal structure is fossilized; speakers do not actively analyze the parts when using the word, though the etymology explains the meaning ("of one's own will").
