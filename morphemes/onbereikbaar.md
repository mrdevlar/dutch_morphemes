From a morphological perspective, the Dutch word **`onbereikbaar`** is a complex adjective formed through derivational morphology. It consists of three distinct morphemes.

Here is the breakdown and linguistic explanation of each component:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented as follows:

> **`on-`** + **`bereik`** + **`-baar`**

---

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### A. `on-` (Prefix)
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix.
*   **Function:** Negation. It reverses the meaning of the base word to which it is attached.
*   **Meaning:** "Not" or "Un-".
*   **Linguistic Context:** This is a productive negative prefix in Dutch, cognate with the English prefix **`un-`** (e.g., *unhappy*) and the German prefix **`un-`** (e.g., *unmöglich*).
*   **Example:** *onmogelijk* (impossible), *onwaar* (untrue).

#### B. `bereik` (Root / Stem)
*   **Type:** Lexical Root (Verbal Stem).
*   **Source:** Derived from the verb **`bereiken`** (to reach).
*   **Meaning:** "Reach," "access," or "scope."
*   **Linguistic Context:** In this specific construction, `bereik` functions as the verbal stem. While `bereik` can also stand alone as a noun (meaning "reach" or "range"), here it provides the semantic core of the action.
*   **Etymology:** From Middle Dutch *bereiken*, related to the concept of extending or stretching out.

#### C. `-baar` (Suffix)
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix.
*   **Function:** Adjectivalizer / Possibility Marker. It turns a verb stem into an adjective indicating capability or possibility.
*   **Meaning:** "-able" or "-ible".
*   **Linguistic Context:** This is one of the most common suffixes in Dutch for creating adjectives from verbs. It is cognate with the English suffix **`-able`** (e.g., *readable*) and the German suffix **`-bar`** (e.g., *lesbar*).
*   **Example:** *leesbaar* (readable), *draagbaar* (portable).

---

### 3. Derivational Hierarchy (Step-by-Step Construction)

The word is built in two layers of derivation:

1.  **Layer 1 (Verb $\rightarrow$ Adjective):**
    *   **`bereik`** (stem of *to reach*) + **`-baar`** (able)
    *   **Result:** **`bereikbaar`** (reachable / accessible).
    *   *Part of Speech:* Adjective.

2.  **Layer 2 (Adjective $\rightarrow$ Negated Adjective):**
    *   **`on-`** (not) + **`bereikbaar`** (reachable)
    *   **Result:** **`onbereikbaar`** (unreachable / inaccessible).
    *   *Part of Speech:* Adjective.

### 5. Summary
*   **Word Class:** Adjective.
*   **Phonological Stress:** The primary stress in Dutch compound adjectives usually falls on the root syllable. In *onbe**rei**kbaar*, the stress is on **`rei`**.
*   **Semantic Value:** The word describes something that cannot be reached, accessed, or attained.
