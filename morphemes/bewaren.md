From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch verb **`bewaren`** (meaning "to keep," "to preserve," or "to save") can be analyzed through its morphological structure, etymology, and semantic composition.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word consists of three distinct morphemes:

> **be-** + **waar** + **-en**

*   **be-**: Derivational Prefix
*   **waar**: Lexical Root (Stem)
*   **-en**: Inflectional Suffix

---

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Prefix: `be-`
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix.
*   **Function:** In Dutch morphology, the prefix *be-* is highly productive. It often serves to **transitivize** a verb (turning an intransitive concept into a transitive action) or to indicate that an action is applied **thoroughly** or **to an object**.
*   **Semantic Contribution:** It adds the sense of "applying the action to something."
    *   *Example:* *kleden* (to dress oneself) $\rightarrow$ *bekleden* (to clad/cover something).
    *   *In `bewaren`:* It shifts the meaning from the abstract concept of "keeping/guarding" to the specific act of "keeping **something**."
*   **Pronunciation:** It is unstressed and pronounced as a schwa /bə/.

#### B. The Root: `waar`
*   **Type:** Lexical Root (Bound in modern usage).
*   **Meaning:** Historically, this root meant "to guard," "to protect," or "to watch over."
*   **Modern Status:** In contemporary Dutch, this root is **not productive** as a standalone verb. You cannot say *"Ik waar het"* (I keep it). The meaning is fossilized within the prefixed form *bewaren*.
*   **Homograph Warning:** It is crucial to distinguish this root from the modern Dutch words *waar* (adjective: "true") and *waar* (adverb: "where"). While they look identical, they are etymologically distinct:
    *   *Bewaren* root: Proto-Germanic **\*warjaną* (to guard/protect).
    *   *Waar* (true): Proto-Germanic **\*wairaz* (perceived/real).
    *   *Waar* (where): Proto-Germanic **\*hwar* (location).
*   **Stem Form:** When conjugated, the infinitive suffix *-en* is dropped, leaving the stem **`bewaar-`** (e.g., *ik bewaar*, *hij bewaart*).

#### C. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix.
*   **Function:** This is the standard marker for the **infinitive** form of Dutch verbs.
*   **Grammar:** It indicates the verb is in its base form, used after modal verbs (e.g., *kunnen bewaren*) or as a noun (e.g., *het bewaren van...*).
*   **Pronunciation:** In standard Dutch, the final *-n* is often elided in casual speech, sounding like /bəˈʋaːrə/, but it is written and pronounced fully in formal contexts.

---

### 3. Etymological Context
To understand the morphology fully, we must look at the word's history:

*   **Old Dutch:** *biwarian*
*   **Middle Dutch:** *bewaren*
*   **Proto-Germanic:** *\*bi-warjaną*
    *   *\*bi-* (prefix, meaning "around" or "about")
    *   *\*warjaną* (verb, meaning "to guard/protect")
*   **Cognates:**
    *   **German:** *bewahren* (identical meaning and structure).
    *   **English:** *ware* (as in "wary," "warranty," "aware"). The English verb *to ware* is archaic but shares the same Germanic root meaning "to guard."

### 4. Summary of Composition
| Morpheme | Type | Meaning/Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **be-** | Prefix | Transitivity / Application to object |
| **waar** | Root | Guard / Protect / Keep safe |
| **-en** | Suffix | Infinitive Marker |
| **bewaren** | **Word** | **To keep/preserve (something)** |

### 5. Linguistic Note on "Lexicalization"
From a morphological perspective, *bewaren* is considered a **lexicalized** compound. While it is structurally transparent (Prefix + Root + Suffix), the root *waar* has lost its independent verbal meaning in modern Dutch. Therefore, a learner cannot simply learn the root *waar* and apply the prefix *be-* to create new words; they must learn *bewaren* as a specific vocabulary item.
