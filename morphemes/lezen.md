From a linguistic (morphological) perspective, the Dutch word **`lezen`** (meaning "to read") is a **bimorphemic** word. It consists of a lexical root and an inflectional suffix.

Here is the breakdown and explanation of the morphemes:

### 1. The Morphemic Breakdown

| Morpheme | Form | Type | Function/Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Root** | `lez-` | Lexical (Free) | Carries the core semantic meaning ("read"). |
| **Suffix** | `-en` | Inflectional (Bound) | Marks the verb as an **infinitive** (and plural present). |

**Structure:** `lez-` + `-en` → **`lezen`**

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis

#### A. The Root: `lez-`
*   **Semantic Content:** This morpheme carries the primary lexical meaning of the word: the action of reading.
*   **Phonological Variation (Ablaut):** `lezen` is a **strong verb** (Class 7 historically). This means the root vowel changes depending on the tense (a process called *ablaut* or vowel gradation).
    *   Infinitive: *le**z**en* (/leːz/)
    *   Past Singular: *l**a**s* (/lɑs/)
    *   Past Participle: *ge**le**zen* (/ɣəleːz/)
    *   *Note:* Even though the spelling changes (e.g., *lees* in the present singular), the underlying morphological root is identified as `lez-`.
*   **Voicing:** The root ends in the voiced fricative /z/. In Dutch phonology, this consonant undergoes **final devoicing** when it appears at the end of a syllable or word without a vowel following it.
    *   In *lezen*, the /z/ is followed by a vowel, so it remains voiced.
    *   In the present tense singular (*ik lees*), the /z/ is at the end of the stem, so it devoices to /s/ (spelled `s`).

#### B. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Grammatical Function:** This is an **inflectional morpheme**. It does not change the part of speech or the core meaning; rather, it indicates the grammatical form of the verb.
*   **Specific Role:** In Dutch, `-en` is the standard marker for the **infinitive** form of the verb (the "to" form in English).
    *   *Example:* *lopen* (to walk), *werken* (to work), *lezen* (to read).
*   **Secondary Role:** This same suffix is also used for the **plural present tense** (e.g., *wij lezen* = "we read").

---

### 3. Diachronic Perspective (Etymology)
While the synchronic (current) analysis treats `lez-` as a single root, looking at the history of the word adds depth to the morphological understanding:

*   **Proto-Germanic:** The root derives from **\*lesaną**, meaning "to gather" or "to pick."
*   **Semantic Shift:** In Old Dutch (*lesan*) and Old High German (*lesan*), the meaning was literally "to gather" (e.g., gathering wood or crops). Over time, the meaning shifted metaphorically to "to gather letters/words," resulting in the modern meaning "to read."
*   **Morphological Stability:** The `-en` ending is a direct continuation of the Proto-Germanic infinitive ending *-an*.

### 4. Summary
In modern Dutch morphology, **`lezen`** is not analyzed as `le` + `zen` (which would be incorrect), nor is it a single unbreakable unit. It is composed of the verb stem **`lez-`** combined with the infinitive marker **`-en`**. The complexity of the word arises from the fact that it is a strong verb, meaning the root vowel and consonant voicing change depending on the grammatical context, even though the morphemic boundaries remain consistent.
