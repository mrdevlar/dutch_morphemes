From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`mentaal`** is a **derived adjective** formed through the combination of a bound root and a derivational suffix. It is a loanword (internationalism) adopted from Latin via French.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into two distinct morphemes:

> **`ment`** + **`-aal`**

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Root: `ment`
*   **Type:** Bound Root (Combining Form)
*   **Origin:** Latin *mens, mentis* (genitive).
*   **Meaning:** "Mind," "intellect," or "thought."
*   **Status in Dutch:** This is not a free morpheme in modern Dutch; you cannot use "ment" as a standalone word. It only appears in derived words (e.g., *mentale*, *mentaliteit*, *mentalist*).
*   **Semantic Function:** It provides the core lexical meaning of the word, relating to the mind or psychological processes.

#### B. The Suffix: `-aal`
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix
*   **Origin:** Latin *-alis* (via French *-al*).
*   **Meaning:** "Pertaining to," "characterized by," or "of the nature of."
*   **Function:** It converts the noun/root (*ment*) into an adjective.
*   **Dutch Orthography:** In Dutch, the Latin suffix *-alis* is adapted as **-aal**. The double vowel **`aa`** is required by Dutch spelling rules to indicate a long vowel sound (/aː/) in an open syllable.
*   **Productivity:** This is a highly productive suffix in Dutch, used extensively with loanwords (e.g., *nationaal*, *sociaal*, *fysicaal*, *lokaal*).

### 3. Etymology and Loanword Status
*   **Path:** Latin *mentalis* → French *mental* → Dutch *mentaal*.
*   **Classification:** It is an **internationalism**. Because it was borrowed as a complete unit from a Romance language, the internal morphology is transparent to speakers of Dutch, even though the root is not native to the Germanic language family.
*   **Cognates:**
    *   English: *mental*
    *   German: *mental*
    *   French: *mental*

### 4. Phonology and Orthography
*   **Pronunciation:** /mɛnˈtaːl/
*   **Stress:** The stress falls on the final syllable (**-taal**). This is typical for Dutch words ending in *-aal* (e.g., *natu**raal***, *materi**aal***).
*   **Spelling:** The **`aa`** represents the long open /aː/ sound. If it were a short vowel, it would be spelled *mental* (which is the spelling in English and German, but not Dutch).

### 5. Syntactic Inflection
While the base form is **`mentaal`**, Dutch adjectives inflect based on gender, number, and definiteness.
*   **Predicative (after 'to be'):** *Hij is **mentaal** sterk.* (He is mentally strong.)
*   **Attributive (before noun, indefinite):** *Een **mentaal** probleem.* (A mental problem.)
*   **Attributive (before noun, definite/plural):** *De **mentale** kracht.* (The mental strength.)
    *   *Note:* In the inflected form, the suffix effectively becomes **-ale**, adding an inflectional morpheme *-e* to the derivational structure.
