From a linguistic (morphological) perspective, the Dutch word **`beoordeling`** (meaning "evaluation," "assessment," or "judgment") can be segmented into three distinct morphemes.

Here is the breakdown:

### 1. Segmentation
**`be-`** + **`oordel`** + **`-ing`**

---

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### **A. `be-` (Prefix)**
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix.
*   **Function:** In Dutch, the prefix *be-* is highly productive. It typically attaches to a verb stem to create a new transitive verb. It often implies:
    *   **Transitivization:** Turning an intransitive concept into an action done *to* an object.
    *   **Scope/Completeness:** Indicating that the action affects the whole object or covers a specific area.
    *   **Instrumentality:** Doing something *with* or *by means of* the root concept.
*   **In this context:** It transforms the base concept of "judging" into "applying judgment to something specific" (to evaluate/assess).
*   **Example:** *denken* (to think) → *bedenken* (to devise/consider); *spelen* (to play) → *bespelen* (to play an instrument/operate).

#### **B. `oordel` (Root / Verbal Stem)**
*   **Type:** Bound Root / Verbal Stem.
*   **Function:** This carries the core semantic meaning of the word ("judgment" or "verdict").
*   **Status:** This is not a free morpheme in modern Dutch (you cannot say "oordel" as a standalone word). It is the stem derived from the noun **`oordeel`** (judgment) and the verb **`oordelen`** (to judge).
*   **Phonology:** It retains the long vowel /oː/ (spelled *oo*) from the base noun.

#### **C. `-ing` (Suffix)**
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix.
*   **Function:** **Nominalization**. It converts a verb stem into a noun. Specifically, it creates an *action noun* (nomen actionis) or a *result noun* (nomen rei actae).
*   **Grammar:** It assigns the noun the common gender (*de*-woord).
*   **Pluralization:** The plural is formed by adding *-en* to this suffix (*beoordeling**en***).
*   **English Equivalent:** Similar to the English suffix *-ing* (as in "building" or "judging") or *-tion* (as in "evaluation").

---

### 3. Derivational History (The "Life Cycle" of the Word)
To understand the morphology fully, it helps to trace the derivation steps:

1.  **Base Noun:** *Oordeel* (Judgment).
2.  **Verb Formation:** *Oordelen* (To judge).
    *   *Stem:* *oordel-*
3.  **Prefixation:** *Beoordelen* (To evaluate/assess).
    *   *Stem:* *beoordel-*
4.  **Nominalization:** *Beoordeling* (Evaluation/Assessment).
    *   *Structure:* [ **be-** + **oordel** ] + **-ing**

*Note: In step 4, the infinitive ending *-en* from the verb `beoordelen` is dropped and replaced by the nominalizing suffix `-ing`.*

---

### 4. Deep Etymology (The Root `oordel`)
While `oordel` acts as a single morpheme in modern synchronic analysis, it is historically a compound itself. Understanding this adds depth to the linguistic picture:

*   **`Oor`**: In Old Dutch (*ōr*), this did not mean "ear" (which is *oor* in modern Dutch, but etymologically distinct). It meant "cause," "origin," or "judgment."
*   **`Deel`**: Means "part" or "share" (cognate with English *deal* or *dole*).
*   **Literal Meaning:** "A share of the cause" or "a part of the judgment."
*   **Evolution:** Over time, the two words fused into the single lexical unit *oordeel*, which then served as the base for the verb *oordelen*.
