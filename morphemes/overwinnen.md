From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`overwinnen`** (meaning "to overcome," "to conquer," or "to win over") can be segmented into three distinct morphemes.

Here is the breakdown:

### 1. Segmentation
**`over`** + **`winn`** + **`en`**

---

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### A. `over-` (Prefix)
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix.
*   **Origin:** Proto-Germanic *\*uber*, cognate with English *over* and German *über*.
*   **Function:** In this context, it modifies the meaning of the base verb. While the preposition *over* usually means "over" or "across," as a verbal prefix here, it adds the nuance of **transcending**, **surpassing**, or **completing** an action.
*   **Morphological Class:** It is an **inseparable prefix** (*niet-scheidbaar voorvoegsel*).
    *   *Evidence:* The primary stress falls on the root (*wín*), not the prefix (*over*).
    *   *Example:* *Ik heb de ziekte **overwonnen**.* (I have overcome the illness). Note that the past participle does *not* take the *ge-* prefix (unlike separable verbs or regular verbs), which is a hallmark of inseparable prefixes in Dutch.

#### B. `winn-` (Stem / Root)
*   **Type:** Verbal Stem.
*   **Base Verb:** *winnen* (to win).
*   **Function:** This carries the core lexical meaning of the word: gaining, acquiring, or achieving victory.
*   **Phonology/Orthography:** The double *n* is retained from the infinitive *winnen*. In Dutch morphology, the stem is derived by removing the infinitive ending *-en*.
    *   *Infinitive:* *winnen*
    *   *Stem:* *winn* (e.g., *wij winn**en***, *hij winn**t***).
*   **Verb Class:** This is a **strong verb** (sterk werkwoord). It undergoes vowel gradation (ablaut) in the past tense.
    *   Present: *winnen*
    *   Past: *won* (singular) / *wonnen* (plural)
    *   Past Participle: *overwonnen*

#### C. `-en` (Suffix)
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix.
*   **Function:** This marks the **infinitive** form of the verb. It also serves as the ending for the first and second person plural in the present tense (*wij/jullie winnen*).
*   **Status:** This is a grammatical marker, not part of the lexical root.

---

### 3. Morphological Process Summary

The formation of `overwinnen` involves two linguistic processes:

1.  **Derivation (Prefixation):** The prefix `over-` is attached to the stem `winn-` to create a new lexical item with a modified meaning ("to win" $\rightarrow$ "to overcome").
2.  **Inflection:** The suffix `-en` is attached to mark the non-finite (infinitive) form.

### 4. Linguistic Nuance: Inseparability
A crucial linguistic feature of `overwinnen` is its **stress pattern**.
*   **Inseparable:** *over**wín**nen* (Stress on the root).
*   **Separable (Hypothetical):** If it were a separable verb (like *opbellen*), the stress would be on the prefix: *óverwinnen*.

Because the stress is on the root, `over-` is classified as an inseparable prefix. This dictates its conjugation:
*   **Correct:** *Ik heb het **overwonnen**.* (No *ge-*).
*   **Incorrect:** *Ik heb het **geoverwonnen**.*
