From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`volwassene`** (meaning "adult") is a complex word formed through compounding and derivation. It is a **substantivized adjective** (an adjective functioning as a noun).

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation of its components.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **`vol`** + **`wassen`** + **`-e`**

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### **Morpheme 1: `vol`**
*   **Category:** Free morpheme (Adjective).
*   **Meaning:** "Full."
*   **Function:** In this compound, it acts as a prefix-like modifier indicating completeness or degree.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Proto-Germanic **`*fullaz`**. It is cognate with the English word **`full`**, German **`voll`**, and Swedish **`full`**.
*   **Semantic Contribution:** It implies that the process of growing is complete.

#### **Morpheme 2: `wassen`**
*   **Category:** Bound morpheme (Past Participle of a verb).
*   **Meaning:** "Grown."
*   **Function:** This is the past participle of the verb **`wassen`**.
    *   *Crucial Distinction:* In modern Dutch, there are two verbs spelled *wassen*. One means "to wash" (cognate with English *wash*), and the other means "to grow" (cognate with English *wax*, as in a "waxing moon"). In *volwassen*, it refers to **growth**.
*   **Morphological Note:** Historically, the past participle of *wassen* (to grow) would be *gewassen*. However, in compound words, the prefix *ge-* is often dropped (a process known as **prefix truncation**). Thus, *volwassen* is a compound of *vol* + *wassen* (participle).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Proto-Germanic **`*waxaną`**. It is cognate with the English verb **`to wax`** (meaning to increase or grow, e.g., "the moon waxes").
*   **Semantic Contribution:** It provides the core action/state: having grown.

#### **Morpheme 3: `-e`**
*   **Category:** Suffix (Derivational).
*   **Function:** **Nominalizer**. It converts the adjective *volwassen* ("adult/mature") into a noun referring to a person ("an adult").
*   **Grammar:** In Dutch, when an adjective is used as a noun to refer to a person (substantivization), it typically takes the weak adjective ending *-e*.
    *   *Example:* *een rijke* (a rich person), *een oude* (an old person).
*   **Semantic Contribution:** It personifies the state of being "fully grown."

### 3. Semantic Composition (Literal Meaning)
When you combine the meanings of the morphemes, the literal translation is:
> **"Full" + "Grown" + "One/Person"**
> $\rightarrow$ **"A fully grown person"**

### 4. Grammatical Context: Singular vs. Plural
It is important to note that the suffix `-e` in *volwassene* is **singular**.
*   **Singular:** *de volwassene* (the adult)
*   **Plural:** *de volwassenen* (the adults)

In the plural form, an additional suffix **`-n`** is added to the singular form. This is a common pattern in Dutch for weak nouns and substantivized adjectives (e.g., *de jongen* $\rightarrow$ *de jongens*, though *volwassenen* is irregular in that it keeps the *-e* and adds *-n*).

### Conclusion
Linguistically, **`volwassene`** is a **compound participle** (*vol* + *wassen*) that has been **nominalized** via the suffix *-e*. It is a transparent compound where the meaning of the whole is directly derived from the sum of its parts, making it a classic example of Germanic word formation.
