From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`invullen`** is a complex verb formed through derivation and inflection. It is a classic example of a **separable verb** (*scheidbaar werkwoord*).

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation of the morphemes.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **in** + **vul** + **en**

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. `in-` (Prefix / Particle)
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix (specifically, a separable particle).
*   **Origin:** Preposition/Adverb.
*   **Function:**
    *   **Semantic:** It adds a directional or completive aspect to the root verb. While *vullen* means "to fill," *invullen* implies filling something **into** a specific space (like a form) or filling it **up** completely.
    *   **Syntactic:** In Dutch grammar, this is classified as a **separable prefix**. In the infinitive form, it is attached to the verb. However, in a main clause sentence, it separates from the verb and moves to the end of the clause (e.g., *Ik vul het formulier **in***).
*   **Linguistic Note:** This is a productive process in Germanic languages (compare English *fill in* vs. *fill*).

#### B. `vul-` (Root / Stem)
*   **Type:** Lexical Root.
*   **Origin:** The verb *vullen* (to fill).
*   **Function:** This carries the core **lexical meaning** of the word. It denotes the physical or metaphorical action of making something full.
*   **Phonology/Orthography:** The root is phonemically /vʏl/. In the infinitive *vullen*, the 'l' is doubled orthographically to ensure the preceding vowel ('u') remains short (/ʏ/). In the stem form used here (*vul-*), the vowel remains short.

#### C. `-en` (Suffix)
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix.
*   **Function:** **Infinitive Marker.**
*   **Grammar:** This is the standard ending for the infinitive form of regular (weak) verbs in Dutch. It does not change the meaning of the word but indicates the grammatical form (non-finite verb).
*   **Comparison:** If the verb were conjugated for the first person singular present tense, this suffix would disappear (e.g., *ik vul*), leaving only the root.

### 3. Morphological Process Summary

| Morpheme | Form | Type | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Prefix** | `in-` | Derivational | Adds directional/completive meaning; makes verb separable. |
| **Root** | `vul-` | Lexical | Core meaning ("to fill"). |
| **Suffix** | `-en` | Inflectional | Marks the infinitive form. |

### 4. Syntactic Interaction (The "Separable" Feature)
From a morphosyntactic perspective, `invullen` is interesting because the prefix `in-` is not permanently fused to the root.

*   **Infinitive:** `invullen` (The morphemes are contiguous).
*   **Main Clause:** `Ik vul het formulier in.` (The prefix `in` is displaced to the end of the sentence).
*   **Subordinate Clause:** `...dat ik het formulier invul.` (The prefix returns to the front of the verb).

This behavior distinguishes it from inseparable verbs (like *begrijpen* - to understand), where the prefix *be-* never separates.

### 5. Etymological Context
*   **`in-`**: From Proto-Germanic *\*in*, cognate with English *in*.
*   **`vul-`**: From Proto-Germanic *\*fullaz*, cognate with English *full*.
*   **`-en`**: From Proto-Germanic infinitive ending *\*-janą*.

### Conclusion
Linguistically, **`invullen`** is a **derived infinitive verb**. It consists of a **derivational prefix** (`in-`) that modifies the meaning of the **lexical root** (`vul-`), followed by an **inflectional suffix** (`-en`) that marks the grammatical mood (infinitive). Its status as a separable verb is a defining characteristic of its morphological structure in Dutch syntax.
