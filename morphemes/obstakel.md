*   **Reasoning:** A native Dutch speaker cannot decompose the word into meaningful parts that exist independently in the current language.

*   **Conclusion:** For a speaker of modern Dutch, the word is a **bound root** or a **lexicalized loanword**. It functions as a single unit of meaning.

### 3. Diachronic Analysis (Etymological Morphemes)
To understand the internal logic of the word, we must look at its history. The word entered Dutch via French (*obstacle*), which borrowed it from Latin (*obstaculum*).

Etymologically, the word consists of **three morphemes**:

#### A. `ob-` (Prefix)
*   **Origin:** Latin preposition *ob*.
*   **Meaning:** "Against," "in front of," or "in the way of."
*   **Function:** Directional/Prepositional prefix.
*   **Cognates:** English *ob-* (as in *object*, *oppose*), German *ob-* (rare, usually *gegen-*).

#### B. `stac-` (Root)
*   **Origin:** Latin verb *stāre*.
*   **Meaning:** "To stand."
*   **Form:** This is the stem form used in the noun derivation.
*   **Function:** The core semantic action.
*   **Cognates:** English *stand*, *station*, *status*; German *stehen*.

#### C. `-culum` (Suffix)
*   **Origin:** Latin instrumental suffix *-culum* (diminutive/instrumental).
*   **Meaning:** "Instrument," "means," or "that which performs the action."
*   **Function:** Nominalizer (turns the verb "to stand" into a noun "that which stands").
*   **Evolution:**
    *   Latin: *-culum*
    *   French: *-cle* (e.g., *obstacle*)
    *   Dutch: *-kel* (phonological adaptation to Dutch nominal patterns like *model*).

### 4. Semantic Composition
When you combine these etymological morphemes, the literal meaning becomes clear:

> **`ob-`** (against) + **`stac-`** (stand) + **`-culum`** (instrument)

This perfectly matches the modern definition of an obstacle: something that stands in your way.
