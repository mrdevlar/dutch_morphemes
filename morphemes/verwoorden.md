From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch verb **`verwoorden`** (meaning *to express*, *to put into words*, or *to verbalize*) is a classic example of **derivational morphology**, specifically **denominal verb formation** (creating a verb from a noun).

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation of the word.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **ver-** + **woord** + **-en**

---

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Root: `woord`
*   **Category:** Noun (Substantive)
*   **Meaning:** "Word"
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Proto-Germanic **\*wurdą**, which stems from the Proto-Indo-European root **\*wr̥dʰos** (meaning "saying" or "speech").
*   **Cognates:** English *word*, German *Wort*, Old Norse *orð*.
*   **Function:** This is the **lexical root**. It carries the core semantic content of the word. In this derivation, the noun serves as the base material for the action.

#### B. The Prefix: `ver-`
*   **Category:** Inseparable Prefix
*   **Function:** **Derivational**. It changes the part of speech (from Noun to Verb) and modifies the meaning.
*   **Semantic Role:** In this specific context, `ver-` indicates a **process of transformation** or **manifestation**. It implies taking an abstract concept (a thought or feeling) and transforming it *into* the form of the root noun (words).
    *   *Note on Productivity:* The prefix `ver-` is highly productive in Dutch. It can indicate completion (*verdrinken* - to drown), change of state (*veranderen* - to change), or, as here, verbalization.
*   **Phonological Marker (Stress):** In Dutch, inseparable prefixes are **unstressed**. The stress falls on the root syllable: *ver-**WOOR**-den*. This distinguishes it from separable verbs (like *opzeggen*, where stress is on the prefix).
*   **Grammatical Marker:** Because `ver-` is an inseparable prefix, the past participle does **not** take the standard `ge-` prefix.
    *   *Infinitive:* verwoorden
    *   *Past Participle:* verwoord (not *geverwoord*)

#### C. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Category:** Inflectional Suffix
*   **Function:** **Infinitive Marker**.
*   **Grammar:** In Dutch, the suffix `-en` (or sometimes just `-n` after a vowel) marks the citation form of the verb (the dictionary form). It indicates that the word is a verb in its base state, not conjugated for person or tense.

---

### 3. Morphological Process: Denominalization
The formation of `verwoorden` is a process called **denominalization**.

1.  **Base:** `woord` (Noun: Word)
2.  **Derivation:** `ver-` + `woord` $\rightarrow$ `verwoord` (Verb Stem: To word/express)
3.  **Inflection:** `verwoord` + `-en` $\rightarrow$ `verwoorden` (Infinitive Verb)

**Semantic Shift:**
The meaning shifts from the concrete object ("a word") to the **action of creating or using that object** to convey meaning. It is semantically similar to the English construction "to word something," though English rarely uses "word" as a verb in this specific way (preferring "to verbalize" or "to articulate").

### 4. Summary Table

| Morpheme | Type | Meaning/Function | Stress |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **ver-** | Prefix (Inseparable) | Transformation / Process / Verbalization | Unstressed |
| **woord** | Root (Noun) | Word | **Stressed** |
| **-en** | Suffix (Inflectional) | Infinitive Marker | Unstressed |

### 5. Linguistic Context
*   **Transitivity:** `Verwoorden` is a transitive verb. It requires a direct object (e.g., *Ik verwoord mijn gevoelens* / "I express my feelings").
*   **Register:** It is a standard, slightly formal verb. In casual speech, Dutch speakers might use *zeggen* (to say) or *zeggen hoe je het voelt* (to say how you feel), but `verwoorden` is the precise term for the cognitive act of translating thought into language.
*   **Comparison:** In German, a similar concept exists with *verworten* (archaic/rare) or *ausdrücken* (to express). English uses the Latinate *verbalize* (from *verbum* + *-alize*), whereas Dutch uses the native Germanic root *woord* + the native prefix *ver-*.
