From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch verb **`worden`** is a morphologically simple but syntactically complex word. It is the **infinitive** form of the verb meaning "to become" or used as an auxiliary for the passive voice.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into two distinct morphemes:

> **word** + **-en**

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Root: `word-`
*   **Type:** Lexical Morpheme (Root/Stem).
*   **Function:** This carries the core semantic meaning of the word.
*   **Meaning:** It signifies a change of state, transition, or "becoming."
*   **Etymology:** It derives from the Proto-Germanic **\*werþaną**. It is cognate with the Old English *weorðan* (which evolved into the English word *worth* in the sense of "to become," as in "it came to pass") and the German *werden*.
*   **Morphological Note:** This is a **strong verb** (irregular verb). While `word-` is the stem used in the infinitive and present tense (e.g., *ik word*, *jij wordt*), the vowel changes in the past tense due to **ablaut** (vowel gradation).
    *   *Infinitive:* word-en
    *   *Past Tense:* **werd** (vowel change from *o* to *e*)
    *   *Past Participle:* **geworden** (prefix *ge-* + stem *word* + suffix *-en*)

#### B. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Type:** Inflectional Morpheme (Suffix).
*   **Function:** This marks the **infinitive** form of the verb.
*   **Grammar:** In Dutch, the standard ending for the infinitive of most verbs is *-en*. (Note: If the stem ends in a vowel, the *-e-* is often dropped, leaving just *-n*, e.g., *gaan* from *ga-* + *-n*, but for *word-*, the full *-en* is retained).
*   **Bound Morpheme:** This suffix cannot stand alone; it must attach to a verb stem to function grammatically.

### 3. Morphological Classification
*   **Word Class:** Verb.
*   **Derivational Status:** This is a **non-derived** word in its current form. It consists of a root plus a standard inflectional ending. It is not a compound (like *huiswerk*) nor does it have a derivational prefix (like *be-* in *beworden*, which doesn't exist, or *ver-* in *verworden*).
*   **Language Type:** Dutch is generally considered a **fusional** language (where one affix can convey multiple grammatical meanings, though here *-en* primarily signals the infinitive). However, the segmentation here is relatively transparent (agglutinative-like) compared to more complex inflectional systems.

### 4. Syntactic and Functional Context
While morphology deals with the structure of the word, understanding `worden` requires noting its dual function in syntax, which does not change its morphology but changes its semantic weight:

1.  **Copula (Linking Verb):** It indicates a change of state.
    *   *Example:* "Hij **wordt** leraar." (He **becomes** a teacher.)
2.  **Passive Auxiliary:** It is used to form the passive voice (similar to "to be" in English passive, but specifically for dynamic events).
    *   *Example:* "Het huis **wordt** gebouwd." (The house **is being** built.)
