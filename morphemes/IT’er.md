From a morphological perspective, the Dutch word **`IT'er`** can be segmented into two distinct morphemes, with an additional orthographic element.

Here is the breakdown and linguistic explanation:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word is structured as follows:
**`IT`** + **`'`** + **`er`**

*   **`IT`**: Root/Base Morpheme
*   **`er`**: Derivational Suffix
*   **`'`**: Orthographic Marker (Not a morpheme)

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Root: `IT`
*   **Type:** **Acronym / Loanword Root**.
*   **Origin:** English (*Information Technology*).
*   **Function:** In this context, `IT` functions as a **noun stem**. It denotes a specific field of industry or activity.
*   **Linguistic Status:** It is a **borrowed morpheme**. While it is an acronym in English, in Dutch it has been lexicalized as a noun (e.g., *de IT*).
*   **Semantic Role:** It provides the semantic content regarding the *domain* of work (technology/computing).

#### B. The Suffix: `-er`
*   **Type:** **Derivational Suffix** (specifically, an **Agentive Nominalizer**).
*   **Function:** This suffix converts a base (usually a verb, but also a noun denoting a field) into a noun denoting a **person** associated with that action or field.
*   **Productivity:** This is a highly productive suffix in Dutch.
    *   *Verb:* `werken` (to work) $\rightarrow$ `werker` (worker).
    *   *Noun (Field):* `IT` (field) $\rightarrow$ `IT'er` (person in the field).
*   **Semantic Role:** It adds the meaning "one who works in" or "one who specializes in."

#### C. The Apostrophe: `'`
*   **Type:** **Orthographic Convention** (Grapheme).
*   **Linguistic Status:** **Not a morpheme.** It carries no meaning or grammatical function itself.
*   **Function:** It serves as a **boundary marker**.
*   **Dutch Spelling Rule:** According to the *Groene Boekje* (the official Dutch spelling guide), an apostrophe is required when adding a suffix to an abbreviation or acronym that ends in a vowel sound, or to prevent misreading.
    *   Without the apostrophe, `ITer` might be visually confusing or mispronounced as a single syllable (like the Latin word *iter*).
    *   The apostrophe signals to the reader that `er` is a separate suffix added to the acronym `IT`.
    *   *Note:* This rule applies to other similar Dutch words, such as `HR'er` (Human Resources) or `PR'er` (Public Relations).

---

### 3. Morphological Process: Derivation
The formation of `IT'er` is an example of **derivational morphology**.

1.  **Input:** A noun/acronym (`IT`) representing a field.
2.  **Process:** Suffixation (`-er`).
3.  **Output:** A new noun (`IT'er`) representing a person.
4.  **Word Class Change:** While `IT` is a noun (field), `IT'er` is also a noun (person). However, the semantic category shifts from **Abstract/Domain** to **Concrete/Agent**.

### 5. Related Linguistic Phenomena
*   **Pluralization:** The plural is formed by adding `-s` to the end of the word: `IT'ers`. The suffix `-er` remains intact.
*   **Gender:** In Dutch, `IT'er` is a *de*-word (common gender), regardless of the gender of the person (e.g., *de IT'er*, *de IT'ers*).
*   **Analogy:** This follows the pattern of other professional designations in Dutch that use the `-er` suffix on loanwords or acronyms (e.g., *manager*, *trainer*, *IT'er*).
