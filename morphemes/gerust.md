From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`gerust`** is a **participial adjective**. It is derived from a verb and functions grammatically as an adjective, though its structure retains the morphology of a past participle.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation of the morphemes:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **`ge-`** + **`rust`** + **`-t`**

### 2. Detailed Explanation of Morphemes

#### A. The Prefix: `ge-`
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix (specifically, a participial prefix).
*   **Function:** In Dutch, the prefix **`ge-`** is the standard marker used to form the **past participle** of most verbs. It indicates that an action has been completed or a state has been reached.
*   **Context:** While `ge-` is primarily verbal, when attached to a participle that has become lexicalized (fixed in the lexicon), it often marks the word as an adjective describing a state resulting from that action.
*   **Example:** *lopen* (to walk) $\rightarrow$ *gelopen* (walked); *rusten* (to rest) $\rightarrow$ *gerust* (rested/at ease).

#### B. The Root: `rust`
*   **Type:** Lexical Root (Stem).
*   **Origin:** This comes from the verb **`rusten`** (to rest) and the noun **`rust`** (rest, peace, quiet).
*   **Etymology:** It stems from the Proto-Germanic **`*rūstō`**, meaning "rest" or "peace." It is cognate with the English word **`rest`** and the German **`Ruhe`** (though German uses *Ruhe* for the noun and *geruht* for the participle, which is archaic).
*   **Meaning:** The core semantic meaning is "rest," "quiet," or "peace."

#### C. The Suffix: `-t`
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix (Participial Ending).
*   **Function:** This is the standard ending for the past participle of **weak verbs** (regular verbs) in Dutch that end in a voiceless consonant (like `t`, `k`, `p`, `s`, `f`, `ch`).
*   **Phonology/Orthography:** The verb stem is `rust-`. When forming the participle, the suffix `-t` is added.
    *   *Theoretical form:* `rust` + `t` = `rustt`.
    *   *Actual form:* **`gerust`**.
    *   *Note:* In Dutch orthography, when the stem already ends in `t`, the participle ending is often not doubled in writing (unlike the simple past tense, where *rusten* becomes *rustte*). The single `t` at the end of `gerust` serves as the participle marker.

### 3. Grammatical Category & Semantic Shift

**Part of Speech:**
While morphologically a past participle, **`gerust`** functions primarily as an **adjective**.
*   **Predicative:** *Wees gerust.* (Be reassured/calm.)
*   **Attributive:** *Een gerust gevoel.* (A reassured feeling.)
*   **Inflection:** As an adjective, it can take the inflectional suffix `-e` when used attributively before a noun (e.g., *gerust**e***), confirming its adjectival status.

**Semantic Evolution:**
1.  **Literal:** "Having rested" (Physical state).
2.  **Figurative:** "At ease," "reassured," "confident," or "safe" (Mental/Emotional state).
    *   *Example:* *Ik ben er gerust van* (I am sure of it / I am at ease about it).
