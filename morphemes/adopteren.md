From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch verb **`adopteren`** (to adopt) can be analyzed by breaking it down into its constituent morphemes—the smallest units of meaning or grammatical function.

Here is the breakdown:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
Visually, the word can be segmented as follows:

> **`adopt`** + **`eer`** + **`en`**

Alternatively, in synchronic Dutch morphology (how the language works today), the suffix is often treated as a single unit:

> **`adopt`** + **`eren`**

However, for a detailed linguistic explanation, separating the derivational suffix from the inflectional ending provides more insight.

---

### 2. Morpheme-by-Morpheme Explanation

#### A. The Root: `adopt-`
*   **Type:** Bound Lexical Root (Free in English, Bound in Dutch).
*   **Meaning:** This carries the core semantic meaning of "choosing" or "taking on."
*   **Status in Dutch:** Unlike in English, where *adopt* is a free-standing verb, in Dutch, this root cannot stand alone. You cannot say *"Ik adopt"* (I adopt). It must be attached to a verbal suffix.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from the Latin verb *adoptare*.

#### B. The Verbalizing Suffix: `-eer-`
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix.
*   **Function:** This morpheme converts the root into a verb stem. It is characteristic of **internationalisms** (loanwords) in Dutch that originate from Latin or French.
*   **Phonology:** It carries the primary stress of the word (*adop-TE-ren*).
*   **Etymology:** This corresponds to the Latin infinitive ending *-āre*. In French, this became *-er* (e.g., *adopter*). Dutch adapted this as *-eren*.
*   **Note:** Do not confuse this with the Dutch verb *eren* (to honor). In *adopteren*, the *-eer-* is purely a suffix marker, not the verb *eren*.

#### C. The Infinitive Marker: `-en`
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix.
*   **Function:** This marks the verb as an **infinitive** (the base form of the verb, equivalent to "to adopt" in English).
*   **Grammar:** In Dutch, the standard infinitive ending for weak verbs is *-en*.
*   **Stem:** If you remove this, you get the verb stem: *adopteer-* (e.g., *ik adopteer*, *hij adopteert*).

---

### 3. Etymological History
To understand why the morphemes look this way, we must look at the word's history:

1.  **Latin:** *adoptare*
    *   *ad-* (to/towards) + *optare* (to choose).
    *   The *-are* ending marks the first conjugation verb.
2.  **French:** *adopter*
    *   The Latin *-are* evolved into the French infinitive *-er*.
3.  **Dutch:** *adopteren*
    *   Dutch borrowed the word from French but adapted the spelling to fit Dutch phonology and morphology.
    *   Dutch adds the *-n* to the French *-er* to create the standard Dutch infinitive *-eren*.

### 4. Grammatical Classification
*   **Verb Class:** Weak Verb (Class 1).
*   **Conjugation Pattern:** Regular.
    *   *Ik adopteer* (I adopt)
    *   *Jij adopteert* (You adopt)
    *   *Wij adopteren* (We adopt)
    *   *Gij adopteert* (You [archaic] adopt)
    *   *Zij adopteren* (They adopt)
*   **Past Tense:** *adopteerde* (stem + *-de*).
*   **Past Participle:** *ge-adopt-eerd* (prefix *ge-* + stem + *-d*).
