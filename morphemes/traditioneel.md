Here is the breakdown of the morphemes, moving from the surface structure of modern Dutch to the deeper historical roots.

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Stem: `tradition-`
*   **Status:** Bound Root / Stem
*   **Meaning:** "Tradition" or "the act of handing down."
*   **Linguistic Note:** This is a crucial point in Dutch morphology. The Dutch noun for "tradition" is **`traditie`**. However, when forming the adjective, the language does not simply add *-eel* to *traditie* (which would result in *traditieel*). Instead, it utilizes the stem **`tradition-`**.
*   **Why?** This is a result of **loanword adaptation**. The word was borrowed from French (*traditionnel*) or Latin (*traditionalis*). In these source languages, the stem includes the *-n-*. Dutch retained this stem for the adjective to maintain the phonological and orthographic integrity of the international loanword, even though the native Dutch noun form is slightly different (*traditie*).

#### B. The Suffix: `-eel`
*   **Status:** Derivational Suffix
*   **Function:** Adjectivalizer. It turns a noun or noun-stem into an adjective.
*   **Meaning:** "Pertaining to," "characterized by," or "of the nature of."
*   **Origin:** Derived from the Latin suffix *-alis* (via French *-el*).
*   **Orthography:** The double **`ee`** represents a **long vowel** (/eː/) in Dutch orthography. It is not two separate morphemes; it is a single grapheme representing one sound.
*   **Cognate:** This is directly equivalent to the English suffix **-al** (e.g., *tradition* $\rightarrow$ *traditional*).

### 3. Etymological Deep Dive (Diachronic)
If we look deeper into the history of the stem `tradition-`, we can break it down further into its Latin components. This is useful for understanding the semantic evolution of the word.

1.  **`tradit-`** (Root)
    *   **Origin:** Latin verb *tradere*.
    *   **Composition:** *trans-* (across) + *dare* (to give).
    *   **Literal Meaning:** "To hand over" or "to deliver."
    *   **Semantic Shift:** Over time, "handing over" evolved to mean "handing down" knowledge, customs, or beliefs from one generation to the next.

2.  **`-ion`** (Nominal Suffix)
    *   **Origin:** Latin *-io* / *-ionis*.
    *   **Function:** Turns a verb into a noun indicating an action or process.
    *   **Result:** *Traditio* (The act of handing over).

3.  **`-eel`** (Adjectival Suffix)
    *   **Origin:** Latin *-alis*.
    *   **Function:** Pertaining to the noun.
    *   **Result:** *Traditionalis* $\rightarrow$ *Traditioneel*.

### 4. Summary of Linguistic Features

*   **Word Class:** Adjective (*bijvoeglijk naamwoord*).
*   **Stress Pattern:** The stress falls on the syllable immediately preceding the suffix: *tra-di-ti-**o**-neel*.
*   **Inflection:** As an adjective, it can be inflected.
    *   *Predicative:* "De gewoonte is **traditioneel**." (The custom is traditional.)
    *   *Attributive (uninflected):* "Een **traditioneel** feest." (A traditional party.)
    *   *Attributive (inflected):* "De **traditionele** gewoonte." (The traditional custom.)
*   **Morphological Process:** This is an example of **derivation** (creating a new word from an existing one) involving a **loan stem**.
