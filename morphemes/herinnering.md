From a morphological perspective, the Dutch word **`herinnering`** (meaning "memory" or "remembrance") can be segmented into three distinct morphemes.

Here is the breakdown:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
**`her`** + **`inner`** + **`ing`**

---

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. Prefix: `her-`
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix.
*   **Function:** Indicates repetition, restoration, or a return to a previous state.
*   **Meaning:** "Again," "back," or "re-".
*   **Linguistic Context:** In Dutch, *her-* is cognate with the German *her-* (though German *her-* often indicates direction, while Dutch *her-* in this context aligns more with the concept of "back" or "again," similar to English *re-*).
*   **Semantic Role:** It implies bringing something *back* to the present moment or consciousness.

#### B. Root/Stem: `inner`
*   **Type:** Bound Root (Lexical Root).
*   **Function:** Carries the core semantic meaning of the word.
*   **Meaning:** Relates to the "mind," "heart," or "internal self."
*   **Linguistic Context:** This root is not a free morpheme in Modern Dutch (you cannot use *inner* as a standalone word). It is historically related to the Middle Dutch noun *inner* (mind/heart) and the adjective *innerlijk* (inner/internal).
*   **Derivation:** Historically, there was a verb *inneren* (to bring to mind), which is now obsolete. The root survives primarily within the prefixed verb *herinneren* (to remind/recollect).

#### C. Suffix: `-ing`
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix.
*   **Function:** Nominalizer (turns a verb into a noun).
*   **Meaning:** Indicates the action, process, or result of the verb.
*   **Linguistic Context:** This is a highly productive suffix in Dutch (and German *-ung*, English *-ing*).
*   **Phonology:** It attaches directly to the verb stem without a linking vowel in this specific case.

---

### 3. The Derivational Process
To understand the morphology fully, it helps to trace the word's formation from the verb:

1.  **Base Concept:** The root **`inner`** (mind/internal).
2.  **Verb Formation:** The root is verbalized (historically) and prefixed to create the verb **`herinneren`** (*her-* + *inner* + *-en*).
    *   *Meaning:* "To bring back to the mind."
3.  **Nominalization:** The verb stem **`herinner-`** takes the nominal suffix **`-ing`**.
    *   *Result:* **`herinnering`**.
    *   *Meaning:* "The act of bringing back to the mind" $\rightarrow$ "Memory."

### 4. Comparative Linguistics
*   **English:** There is no direct cognate. The closest semantic equivalent is **"remembrance"** (*re-* + *member* + *-ance*), which shares the "back/again" prefix logic (*re-* = *her-*).
*   **German:** The cognate is **`Erinnerung`**.
    *   German uses the prefix *er-* (indicating a result or completion) rather than *her-*.
    *   German: *er-* + *inner-* + *-ung*.
    *   Dutch: *her-* + *inner-* + *-ing*.
    *   This highlights a slight divergence in how the two languages conceptualized the "bringing to mind" process (German: achieving the state of mind; Dutch: bringing it back).
