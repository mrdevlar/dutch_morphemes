From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`opleveren`** is a complex verb formed through derivation and compounding. It is a **separable verb** (*scheidbaar werkwoord*).

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation of its components:

### 1. Morpheme Breakdown

| Morpheme | Type | Function/Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`op-`** | **Prefix** | Directional/Aspectual prefix (meaning "up" or "completion"). |
| **`lever-`** | **Root/Stem** | The verbal stem meaning "to supply" or "to deliver." |
| **`-en`** | **Suffix** | Infinitive marker (standard Dutch verb ending). |

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis

#### A. The Prefix: `op-`
*   **Type:** Separable Prefix (*scheidbaar voorvoegsel*).
*   **Semantics:** In Dutch, the prefix *op-* generally carries spatial meaning ("up") or aspectual meaning ("completion" or "result").
*   **In this context:** When attached to *leveren*, *op-* shifts the meaning from simply "supplying" to "handing over," "submitting," or "producing a final result." It implies a transfer of ownership or the completion of a task directed toward a recipient.
*   **Syntax:** Because it is a separable prefix, in a main clause, it detaches from the verb stem and moves to the end of the sentence.
    *   *Infinitive:* Ik wil het **opleveren**.
    *   *Main Clause:* Ik **lever** het **op**. (I deliver it up).

#### B. The Root: `lever-`
*   **Base Verb:** *Leveren* (to deliver, to supply, to provide).
*   **Etymology:** This root is a loanword. It comes from Middle Dutch *leveren*, which was borrowed from Old French *livrer* (to deliver), which in turn derives from the Latin *librare* (to weigh, to balance).
*   **Morphological Status:** In modern Dutch, *lever-* is a **bound root** within the verb system. You cannot use *lever* as a standalone verb in the present tense (you must say *levert*, *leveren*, etc.).
    *   *Note:* Do not confuse this with the noun *de lever* (the liver), which is a homograph but etymologically distinct (from Latin *hepar* via Germanic).

#### C. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Type:** Infinitive Suffix / Verbalizer.
*   **Function:** This is the standard ending for Dutch verbs in the infinitive form (similar to *-en* in German or *-er* in French). It marks the word as a verb.
*   **Phonology:** In this specific word, the *e* is often schwa-ized (pronounced as a neutral sound /ə/) in rapid speech, though it remains distinct in careful pronunciation.

---

### 3. Semantic Composition (How the meaning is built)

To understand the full meaning, we look at how the prefix modifies the root:

1.  **`Leveren`**: To supply goods or services.
2.  **`Op-`**: Adds the nuance of **finality** or **transfer**.
3.  **`Opleveren`**: To deliver something *to* someone (often implying the end of a process).

**Common Usages:**
*   **Construction:** *De aannemer levert het huis op.* (The contractor hands over/delivers the house.)
*   **Academic:** *Je moet je werk op tijd opleveren.* (You must submit your work on time.)
*   **Result-oriented:** *Dit project levert veel geld op.* (This project yields/produces a lot of money.)

### 4. Syntactic Evidence of Morphology

A key linguistic feature of `opleveren` is visible in the **Past Participle**. In Dutch, when a verb has a separable prefix, the participle marker `ge-` is inserted **between** the prefix and the stem.

*   **Infinitive:** `op` + `lever` + `en`
*   **Past Participle:** `op` + `ge` + `lever` + `d` $\rightarrow$ **`opgeleverd`**

This insertion proves that `op-` is a distinct morphological unit that is syntactically separable from the verb stem `lever-`.

### Summary
**`Opleveren`** is a **separable compound verb** consisting of the directional prefix **`op-`**, the French-derived verbal stem **`lever-`**, and the infinitive suffix **`-en`**. It semantically denotes the act of delivering, submitting, or yielding a result.
