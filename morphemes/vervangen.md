From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`vervangen`** (meaning "to replace" or "to substitute") can be analyzed through **morphology** (structure), **etymology** (history), and **semantics** (meaning).

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes:

### 1. Morphemic Breakdown
The word consists of three distinct morphemes:

> **`ver-`** + **`vang`** + **`-en`**

| Morpheme | Type | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`ver-`** | **Prefix** (Derivational) | Modifies the meaning of the root; creates a new lexical item. |
| **`vang`** | **Root** (Lexeme) | The core semantic meaning ("to catch/grasp"). |
| **`-en`** | **Suffix** (Inflectional) | Marks the verb as an infinitive (dictionary form). |

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Prefix: `ver-`
*   **Type:** Inseparable Prefix (*onafscheidelijk voorvoegsel*).
*   **Phonology:** In Dutch, when `ver-` is attached to a verb, the primary stress shifts to the root syllable. Therefore, the pronunciation is `ver-VANG-en`, not `VER-vang-en`.
*   **Function:** In this context, `ver-` indicates a **change of state** or a **transformation**. While `ver-` can sometimes imply a negative outcome (e.g., *verbranden* - to burn down), here it implies a **substitution** or **taking over**.
*   **Grammar:** Because it is an inseparable prefix, the past participle does *not* take the `ge-` prefix.
    *   *Correct:* `vervangen` (I have replaced)
    *   *Incorrect:* `gevervangen`

#### B. The Root: `vang`
*   **Origin:** This root comes from the Proto-Germanic **`*fang-`**, meaning "to catch" or "to grasp."
*   **Related Words:** It is cognate with the Dutch verb **`vangen`** (to catch), the English word **`fang`** (to catch/grasp, though now mostly used for teeth), and the German **`fangen`**.
*   **Verb Class:** Historically, this is a **strong verb** (*sterk werkwoord*). This is evident in its conjugation pattern, which involves **ablaut** (vowel gradation):
    *   Infinitive: `ver**vang**en` (a)
    *   Past Tense: `ver**ving**` (e)
    *   Past Participle: `ver**vangen**` (a)
    *   *Note:* The root `vang` retains the strong conjugation pattern of the base verb `vangen` (vang - ving - gevangen).

#### C. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix.
*   **Function:** This is the standard marker for the **infinitive** (*onbepaalde wijs*) in Dutch. It indicates the verb is in its base form, not conjugated for person or number.
*   **Variation:** In the plural present tense (e.g., "we replace"), this suffix remains (`wij vervang**en**`). In the singular present tense, it drops (`ik vervang`).

---

### 3. Semantic Composition (How the meaning is built)

The combination of these morphemes creates a meaning that is not immediately obvious from the literal sum of the parts.

1.  **Literal Construction:** `ver-` (change/away) + `vang` (catch) + `-en` (verb).
2.  **Literal Interpretation:** "To catch in a changed state" or "To catch away."
3.  **Actual Meaning:** "To replace" or "To substitute."

**The Semantic Shift:**
Linguistically, this is an example of **semantic bleaching** and **metaphorical extension**.
*   The original concept of "catching" (`vangen`) evolved to mean "taking hold of a position."
*   When combined with `ver-`, it implies taking hold of a position *previously held by someone else*.
*   Therefore, `vervangen` implies **seizing the place of another**.

### 4. Related Derivations
Understanding the morphemes allows you to understand related words in the Dutch lexicon:

*   **`vervanging`** (noun): `ver-` + `vang` + `-ing` (nominalization suffix). Meaning: "Replacement."
*   **`vervangbaar`** (adjective): `ver-` + `vang` + `-baar` (suffix meaning "-able"). Meaning: "Replaceable."
*   **`vervanger`** (noun): `ver-` + `vang` + `-er` (agent suffix). Meaning: "Substitute" or "Replacement person."

### Summary
From a linguistic standpoint, **`vervangen`** is a **derived strong verb**. It is formed by adding the inseparable derivational prefix **`ver-`** to the Germanic root **`vang`** ("catch"), inflected with the infinitive suffix **`-en`**. The meaning has shifted from the physical act of catching to the abstract act of substitution, while retaining the strong verb vowel alternation (`a` - `e` - `a`) of its root.
