From a linguistic perspective, the analysis of the Dutch word **`melodie`** requires distinguishing between its **synchronic** structure (how it is analyzed by speakers today) and its **diachronic** structure (its historical etymology).

Here is the breakdown:

### 1. Synchronic Analysis (Modern Dutch Morphology)
In modern Dutch morphology, **`melodie`** is considered a **monomorphemic** word.

*   **Morpheme Count:** 1
*   **Type:** Free Root (Lexeme)
*   **Explanation:** To a contemporary Dutch speaker, the word cannot be broken down into smaller meaningful units (morphemes). The components that make up the word historically are no longer "productive" or recognizable in the Dutch language. You cannot isolate a prefix or suffix within *melodie* that carries meaning on its own in modern usage.
*   **Status:** It is a **loanword** (specifically from French *mélodie*, ultimately from Latin and Greek). Because it is a loanword, its internal structure is opaque to the native speaker.

### 2. Diachronic Analysis (Etymological Breakdown)
While it functions as one morpheme today, linguists can trace its history back to Ancient Greek, where it was originally composed of two distinct morphemes. This is the "hidden" structure:

*   **Origin:** Ancient Greek *melōidia* (μέλῳδία)
*   **Component 1:** ***mel-*** (from *melos* / μέλος)
    *   **Meaning:** Song, tune, limb, or member.
    *   **Function:** The semantic core referring to the musical aspect.
*   **Component 2:** ***-ōidia*** (from *aeidein* / ἀείδειν)
    *   **Meaning:** To sing.
    *   **Function:** A verbal noun suffix indicating the action of singing.
*   **Evolution:**
    1.  Greek: *melos* + *aeidein* $\rightarrow$ *melōidia*
    2.  Latin: *melodia*
    3.  French: *mélodie*
    4.  Dutch: *melodie*

**Linguistic Note:** In the transition to Dutch, the boundary between *mel-* and *-odie* was lost. The final *-ie* is not the Dutch nominalizing suffix *-ie* (as seen in words like *politie* or *industrie*); rather, it is a phonological adaptation of the Greek/Latin ending *-ia*.

### 3. Morphological Behavior (Derivation and Inflection)
Although *melodie* itself is a single root, it serves as a base for further Dutch morphological processes. This confirms its status as a root morpheme:

*   **Pluralization:**
    *   *melodie* + *-ën* $\rightarrow$ **`melodieën`**
    *   Here, *-ën* is the plural morpheme. Note the spelling change (the *ie* becomes *ieë* to maintain pronunciation).
*   **Adjectival Derivation:**
    *   *melodie* + *-sch* $\rightarrow$ **`melodisch`**
    *   Here, *-sch* is a productive Dutch suffix meaning "-ic" or "-al" (turning a noun into an adjective).
*   **Diminutization:**
    *   *melodie* + *-tje* $\rightarrow$ **`melodietje`**
    *   Here, *-tje* is the diminutive morpheme.
