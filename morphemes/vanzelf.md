From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`vanzelf`** is a fascinating example of **lexicalization**, where a phrase evolves into a single lexical unit.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word consists of two distinct morphemes:

> **`van`** + **`zelf`**

### 2. Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. `van`
*   **Category:** Preposition.
*   **Literal Meaning:** "of," "from," or "by."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *fan*, originating from Proto-Germanic *\*fan*. It is cognate with the English preposition "of" (though English "of" and Dutch "van" have diverged in usage, they share a Germanic root).
*   **Function in this compound:** It indicates origin or source. In this context, it suggests the source of the action is internal rather than external.

#### B. `zelf`
*   **Category:** Reflexive/Emphatic Pronoun (functioning adverbially here).
*   **Literal Meaning:** "self."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *selb*, originating from Proto-Germanic *\*selbaz*. It is cognate with English "self" and German "selbst."
*   **Function in this compound:** It refers to the subject acting upon itself or existing in its own right.

### 3. Syntactic and Semantic Process

#### Lexicalization of a Prepositional Phrase
Historically, this was a free prepositional phrase (*van zelf* = "from self"). Over time, through frequent usage, it underwent **lexicalization**.
*   **Orthography:** In modern Standard Dutch (according to the *Groene Boekje* spelling rules), it is written as one closed compound word (**`vanzelf`**) when used as an adverb.
*   **Grammaticalization:** The phrase shifted from a literal spatial/abstract description to a fixed **adverb**.

#### Semantic Shift (Idiomatization)
The meaning of the compound is not merely the sum of its parts, though the logic remains transparent:
*   **Literal Composition:** "From self" / "Of itself."
*   **Idiomatic Meaning:** "Automatically," "naturally," "without effort," or "without external intervention."
*   **Nuance:** It implies that a process happens due to its own nature or momentum, requiring no outside force to make it happen.

### 4. Grammatical Function
*   **Part of Speech:** Adverb (*bijwoord*).
*   **Usage:** It modifies verbs to indicate the manner of the action.
    *   *Example:* "Het gaat **vanzelf**." (It goes **automatically** / It takes care of **itself**.)
    *   *Example:* "Dat komt **vanzelf**." (That will come **naturally** / in due time.)

### 5. Related Morphological Forms
To understand the root `zelf` better, it helps to compare it with other Dutch words containing the same morpheme:
*   **`zelf`** (pronoun): "I did it **myself**." (*Ik heb het **zelf** gedaan.*)
*   **`zelfstandig`** (adjective): "Independent" (literally: "standing by self").
*   **`vanzelfsprekend`** (adjective/adverb): "Self-evident" or "of course" (literally: "from self speaking").

### Conclusion
Linguistically, **`vanzelf`** is a **closed compound** formed from a preposition and a pronoun. It represents a fossilized prepositional phrase that has grammaticalized into a single adverbial unit, retaining the transparent semantic logic of "originating from the self" to mean "happening automatically."
