From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch verb **`schelen`** is a fascinating example of semantic shift and morphological regularity. Below is a detailed breakdown of its morphemes, etymology, and semantic evolution.

### 1. Morphological Breakdown

The word is a standard Dutch infinitive verb. It consists of two distinct morphemes:

| Morpheme | Form | Type | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`schel-`** | Root / Stem | Lexical Morpheme | Carries the core semantic meaning (to differ/separate). |
| **`-en`** | Suffix | Grammatical Morpheme | Marks the infinitive form of the verb. |

**Structure:** `schel` + `en` = **`schelen`**

*   **Conjugation Note:** In the past tense, the stem vowel lengthens (`schel` → `scheelde`), which is characteristic of **Class 1 weak verbs** in Dutch. The past participle is `gescheeld`.

---

### 2. Etymological Analysis

To understand the root `schel-`, we must look at its historical lineage.

*   **Proto-Indo-European (PIE):** The ultimate root is reconstructed as **`*kel-`** or **`*skel-`**, meaning "to cut," "to split," or "to separate."
*   **Proto-Germanic:** Evolved into **`*skel-`**.
*   **Old Dutch / Old High German:** Appeared as **`scelan`**.
*   **Modern Dutch:** **`schelen`**.

**Cognates (Related Words in other languages):**
Because the root implies "splitting" or "hard outer layers," you can find cognates in English and German that share this physical imagery:
*   **English:** *Shell, scale, skull, shale, shield.* (All relate to a hard outer layer or something split off).
*   **German:** *Schalen* (to peel/shell), *Schale* (bowl/peel).
*   **Dutch:** *Schil* (peel/skin), *Schelp* (shell).

---

### 3. Semantic Evolution (Meaning Shift)

The most interesting linguistic aspect of `schelen` is how the meaning shifted from a physical action to an abstract concept.

1.  **Original Meaning (Physical):** "To split" or "to separate."
    *   *Connection:* Related to `schil` (peel). If you peel something, you separate the skin from the fruit.
2.  **Intermediate Meaning (Spatial):** "To be apart."
    *   If two objects are separated, there is a distance between them.
3.  **Modern Meaning (Abstract):** "To differ" or "To matter."
    *   **To Differ:** If two things are separated/distinct, they are not the same.
        *   *Example:* `De prijzen schelen.` (The prices differ.)
    *   **To Matter:** If there is a difference, it creates a distinction in value or importance.
        *   *Example:* `Het schelt me niets.` (It matters nothing to me / It makes no difference to me.)
    *   **To Be Wrong:** If something is separated from the "correct" norm, it is amiss.
        *   *Example:* `Er schelt iets.` (Something is wrong / Something is off.)

---

### 4. Syntactic and Pragmatic Usage

From a syntactic perspective, `schelen` behaves uniquely in Dutch:

*   **Impersonal Construction:** It is frequently used with the dummy subject `het` (it).
    *   *`Het schelt mij niets.`* (Literally: "It differs me nothing.")
*   **Dative Object:** When expressing *who* it matters to, the object takes the dative case (historically), which in modern Dutch is marked by the pronoun form (`mij`, `jou`, `hem`), not the accusative (`me`, `je`, `hem` though colloquially `me` is often used).
    *   *Formal:* `Het schelt **mij** niets.`
    *   *Colloquial:* `Het schelt **me** niets.`

### 5. Related Morphological Families

The root `schel-` generates several other words in Dutch, showing the productivity of this morpheme:

1.  **`Schil` (Noun):** The peel or skin (directly from the "splitting" root).
2.  **`Schillen` (Verb):** To peel (e.g., potatoes). This is the transitive action of the root.
3.  **`Verschil` (Noun):** Difference.
    *   *Breakdown:* `ver-` (prefix indicating change/completion) + `schil` (root of `schelen`).
    *   *Literal:* "A splitting apart."
4.  **`Schel` (Adjective):** Shrill or cheeky.
    *   *Note:* This is likely a different etymological branch (related to sound resonance), though often conflated in modern spelling.

### Summary
The word **`schelen`** is composed of the root **`schel-`** (meaning to split/separate) and the infinitive suffix **`-en`**. Linguistically, it represents a classic case of **semantic bleaching and abstraction**, where a physical concept of "separation" evolved into the abstract concepts of "difference," "importance," and "error."
