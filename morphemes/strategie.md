To understand the internal logic of the word, we must look at its Greek origins. The Dutch word was borrowed via French (*stratégie*) and Latin (*strategia*), which derived from Ancient Greek.

**Breakdown:** `strat` + `eg` + `ie` (historical)

| Morpheme | Origin | Original Meaning | Evolution |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`strat-`** | Greek *stratos* | "Army" or "host" | The military context. |
| **`-eg-`** | Greek *agein* | "To lead" or "to drive" | The action of command. |
| **`-ie`** | Greek *-ia* | "State of" or "office of" | Abstract noun marker. |

**Historical Construction:**
1.  **`stratēgos`**: A compound of *stratos* (army) + *agos* (leader). This meant "General" (the leader of the army).
2.  **`stratēgia`**: The abstract noun form of *stratēgos*, meaning "the office or art of a general."
3.  **`strategie`**: The Dutch adaptation of the Latin/French form.
