From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`uitkomen`** is a classic example of a **separable compound verb** (*scheidbaar werkwoord*).

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation of its components.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
Visually, the word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **`uit`** + **`kom`** + **`-en`**

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. `uit` (Verbal Particle / Preposition)
*   **Type:** Free morpheme (can stand alone as a preposition or adverb).
*   **Category:** Directional particle.
*   **Meaning:** "Out" or "out of."
*   **Function:** In this compound, it acts as a **verbal particle**. It modifies the direction or result of the action denoted by the verb.
    *   *Note:* In Dutch morphology, this is often referred to as a "separable prefix." Unlike inseparable prefixes (like *be-* or *ver-*), this element detaches from the verb in main clauses.

#### B. `kom` (Verb Root / Stem)
*   **Type:** Bound morpheme (cannot stand alone without inflection in standard Dutch).
*   **Category:** Lexical root.
*   **Meaning:** "Come."
*   **Function:** This is the core semantic unit of the word. It provides the fundamental action.
    *   *Etymology:* Cognate with the English verb "come" and the German "kommen."

#### C. `-en` (Infinitive Suffix)
*   **Type:** Bound morpheme (inflectional suffix).
*   **Category:** Infinitive marker.
*   **Function:** This suffix marks the verb as the **infinitive** (the dictionary form).
    *   *Variation:* In other grammatical contexts, this suffix changes or disappears (e.g., *ik kom* [I come], *hij komt* [he comes], *gekomen* [come - past participle]).

---

### 3. Syntactic Behavior: Separability
The most defining linguistic feature of `uitkomen` is its **separability**.

In Dutch syntax, when `uitkomen` is used in a main clause (V2 word order), the particle `uit` separates from the verb stem and moves to the end of the clause.

*   **Infinitive:** `uitkomen`
*   **Main Clause:** "Ik **kom** morgen **uit**." (I come out tomorrow / I turn out tomorrow.)
*   **Subordinate Clause:** "...dat ik morgen **uitkom**." (...that I come out tomorrow.)

**Proof of Separability (Past Participle):**
When forming the past participle, the prefix `ge-` is inserted **between** the particle and the stem, rather than before the particle.
*   *Correct:* `uit` + `ge` + `kom` + `en` = **`uitgekomen`**
*   *Incorrect:* `ge` + `uit` + `kom` + `en` (This would imply an inseparable verb).

### 4. Semantic Analysis (Compositionality)
While the word is morphologically transparent, its meaning is not always purely compositional (i.e., "come" + "out" does not always equal the literal sum of the parts).

1.  **Literal Meaning:** To physically exit a space.
    *   *Example:* "De auto komt uit de garage." (The car comes out of the garage.)
2.  **Idiomatic Meaning 1 (Result):** To turn out / to result.
    *   *Example:* "Hoe komt het uit?" (How does it turn out?)
3.  **Idiomatic Meaning 2 (Fit):** To fit (clothing).
    *   *Example:* "De broek komt goed uit." (The pants fit well / come out well on me.)
4.  **Idiomatic Meaning 3 (Relationship):** To get along with.
    *   *Example:* "Ik kom goed met hem uit." (I get along well with him.)

### 5. Contrast with Inseparable Verbs
To understand `uitkomen` fully, it helps to contrast it with an inseparable verb like **`ontkomen`** (to escape).

| Feature | **`uitkomen`** (Separable) | **`ontkomen`** (Inseparable) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Stress** | Stress on particle (**UIT**-ko-men) | Stress on stem (ont-KO-men) |
| **Prefix `ge-`** | `uit`**`ge`**`komen` | `ont`**`kom`**`en` (No `ge-`) |
| **Main Clause** | Ik **kom** ... **uit**. | Ik **ontkom** ... (No separation) |
| **Origin** | Preposition + Verb | Prefix + Verb |

### Summary
Linguistically, **`uitkomen`** is a **separable compound verb** consisting of a directional particle (**`uit`**), a verbal root (**`kom`**), and an infinitive suffix (**`-en`**). Its syntax requires the particle to detach in main clauses, and its semantics range from literal spatial movement to various idiomatic expressions regarding results, fit, and relationships.
