From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch verb **`trekken`** can be analyzed through morphology (structure), etymology (history), and semantics (meaning).

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes:

### 1. Morphological Breakdown

The word consists of **two morphemes**:

| Morpheme | Form | Type | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`trek-`** | Root / Stem | **Free / Lexical** | Carries the core semantic meaning ("pull," "draw"). |
| **`-en`** | Suffix | **Bound / Inflectional** | Marks the verb as an **infinitive** (non-finite form). |

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Root: `trek-`
*   **Lexical Category:** This is the lexical root of the verb.
*   **Phonology:** It contains the consonant cluster /tr/ and the short vowel /ɛ/ (as in *bed*).
*   **Morphological Status:**
    *   In the context of the verb *trekken*, it is a **bound morpheme** because it cannot stand alone as a verb in modern Dutch (you cannot say *"Ik trek"* without conjugation, though the stem is used in the present tense *ik trek*).
    *   However, `trek` exists independently as a **free morpheme** in Dutch as a noun (e.g., *een trek* meaning "a pull," "a migration," or "a feature/characteristic").
*   **Etymology:**
    *   **Old Dutch:** *trecken*
    *   **Middle Dutch:** *trecken*
    *   **Proto-Germanic:** *\*trekkaną*
    *   **Proto-Indo-European (PIE):** Likely derived from the root **\*terh₁-**, meaning "to rub, drag, or pull."
    *   **Cognates:**
        *   **English:** *track* (originally a path made by dragging/pulling), *tract* (via Latin *trahere*, which is a distant cognate).
        *   **German:** *trecken* (archaic/dialectal for pulling), *Zug* (train/draft/pull - related concept).
        *   **Frisian:** *trekke*.

#### B. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Grammatical Category:** Inflectional Suffix.
*   **Function:** In Dutch grammar, `-en` is the standard marker for the **infinitive** form of verbs (the dictionary form).
*   **Comparison:** It is analogous to the English "to" (e.g., *to pull*), but in Dutch, it is a suffix attached to the stem.
    *   *werken* (to work)
    *   *lopen* (to walk)
    *   *trekken* (to pull)
*   **Phonology:** In many dialects, the final *-n* is dropped in speech (apocope), making it sound like *trekke* /ˈtrɛkə/.

### 3. Semantic Analysis (Polysemy)

From a semantic perspective, the root `trek-` is highly **polysemous** (has multiple related meanings). The core concept is "movement caused by force," which branches into several specific meanings in Dutch:

1.  **Physical Pulling:** *De kar trekken* (To pull the cart).
2.  **Migration:** *De vogels trekken* (The birds migrate/move south).
3.  **Attracting:** *Dat trekt veel publiek* (That attracts a lot of public).
4.  **Developing (Photography):** *Een foto trekken* (To develop a photo).
5.  **Chess:** *Een zet trekken* (To make a move).
6.  **Withdrawal:** *Zich terugtrekken* (To withdraw oneself).

### 4. Derivational Morphology (Word Formation)

The root `trek-` is productive and forms many other words through derivational morphology:

*   **`trekking`** (Noun): The act of pulling or a specific migration route.
    *   *Morphemes:* `trek` + `-ing` (nominalizer) + `-en` (plural/inflection).
*   **`trekker`** (Noun): One who pulls (e.g., a tractor or a person).
    *   *Morphemes:* `trek` + `-er` (agentive suffix).
*   **`aftrekken`** (Separable Verb): To deduct or to pull away.
    *   *Morphemes:* `af` (prefix) + `trek` + `-en`.
*   **`trekpleister`** (Noun): A tourist attraction (literally "pull-stone").
    *   *Morphemes:* `trek` + `pleister` (plaster/stick).

### Summary
Linguistically, **`trekken`** is a **monomorphemic stem** (`trek-`) combined with a **standard infinitive inflection** (`-en`). Its history traces back to the Proto-Germanic concept of dragging or pulling, and its modern usage demonstrates significant semantic broadening from physical force to abstract concepts like attraction and migration.
