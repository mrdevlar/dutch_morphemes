From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`samenleving`** (meaning "society" or "community") is a complex word formed through both **derivation** and **compounding**.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation of its constituent morphemes.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **samen** + **lev** + **-ing**

Alternatively, it can be viewed structurally as a compound of two parts, where the second part is itself a derived word:

> **[ samen ]** + **[ [ lev ] + [ -ing ] ]**

---

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. `samen` (Adverb / Prefix)
*   **Type:** Free morpheme (functioning as a prefix in this compound).
*   **Original Part of Speech:** Adverb.
*   **Meaning:** "Together," "with each other," or "jointly."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *samana*, related to the German *zusammen* and the English *same* (historically related to the concept of "together").
*   **Function:** In this context, it modifies the action of the root verb to indicate that the action is performed collectively by multiple subjects.

#### B. `lev` (Verb Root)
*   **Type:** Bound root (in this specific nominalized form).
*   **Source Verb:** *leven* (to live).
*   **Meaning:** "To live," "to be alive."
*   **Phonological Note:** When the verb *leven* is nominalized, the infinitive ending *-en* is dropped, leaving the stem *lev-*.
*   **Etymology:** From Proto-Germanic *\*libaną*.

#### C. `-ing` (Derivational Suffix)
*   **Type:** Bound morpheme (Suffix).
*   **Function:** **Nominalizer**. It converts a verb (action) into a noun (concept, process, or state).
*   **Meaning:** Indicates the "act of," "process of," or "result of."
*   **Grammar:** In Dutch, this suffix typically creates a feminine noun (historically), though in modern Dutch, it is a common gender noun (*de* word).
*   **Etymology:** From Proto-Germanic *\*-ingaz*.

---

### 3. Syntactic and Semantic Construction

To understand the word fully, we must look at the order of operations in its formation:

1.  **Derivation:** The verb *leven* (to live) takes the suffix *-ing* to become *leving* (living/life).
2.  **Compounding:** The adverb *samen* (together) is compounded with the noun *leving*.
    *   *Literal Translation:* "Together-living."
    *   *Semantic Shift:* While "living together" can refer to a couple cohabiting, in the context of *samenleving*, the meaning broadens to refer to the **social structure** or the **collective existence** of a group of people. Hence, the standard English translation is **"society."**

### 4. Grammatical Properties
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun (*Zelfstandig naamwoord*).
*   **Gender:** Common (*De* samenleving).
*   **Plural:** *samenlevingen* (The plural suffix *-en* is added to the end of the compound).
*   **Stress Pattern:** Primary stress falls on the first syllable: **SÁ**-men-le-ving. This is typical for Dutch compounds, where the first element carries the main stress.
