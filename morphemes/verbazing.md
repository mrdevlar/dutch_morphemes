From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`verbazing`** (meaning "amazement," "astonishment," or "wonder") can be analyzed through its morphological structure and etymological history.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word consists of three distinct morphemes:
> **`ver-`** + **`baz-`** + **`-ing`**

---

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Prefix: `ver-`
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix.
*   **Function:** In Dutch, `ver-` is a highly productive prefix used to create verbs. It often indicates a **change of state**, a **transition**, or a **completion** of an action.
*   **In this context:** It transforms the root into a verb (*verbazen*) indicating a change in the subject's mental state. It implies "to cause to become..." or "to undergo a process of..."
*   **Examples:** *veranderen* (to change), *verkleinen* (to make smaller), *vergeten* (to forget).

#### B. The Root: `baz-`
*   **Type:** Lexical Root (Fossilized).
*   **Origin:** This root is derived from the noun **`baas`** (master, boss, or head).
*   **Etymological Nuance:** This is the most complex part of the word. In Middle Dutch, the verb *verbazen* originally meant "to make someone lose their *baas*."
    *   Historically, *baas* could refer to one's "head," "mind," or "composure."
    *   Therefore, *verbazen* literally meant "to make someone lose their head/composure" or "to confuse."
    *   Over time, the meaning shifted from "confusion" to the specific type of confusion caused by something unexpected: **astonishment**.
*   **Modern Status:** The root `baz-` is no longer a productive verb in modern Dutch. You cannot say *"ik baas"* (I master) in this sense. It is a **fossilized root** that only survives in this specific word family (*verbazen*, *verbazing*, *verbazend*).
    *   *Note:* There is a modern verb *bazen* (to boss people around), but that is etymologically distinct (derived from *baas* + agentive *-en* meaning "to act as a boss").

#### C. The Suffix: `-ing`
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix (Nominalizer).
*   **Function:** This suffix turns a verb into a noun (de-verbal noun).
*   **Meaning:** It indicates the **action**, **process**, or **result** of the verb.
*   **In this context:** It turns the action of "being amazed" (*verbazen*) into the abstract noun "amazement" (*verbazing*).
*   **Grammar:** This suffix typically creates a *de*-woord (common gender noun) in Dutch.
*   **Examples:** *lezen* (to read) → *lezing* (reading/lecture); *werken* (to work) → *werking* (functioning/effect).

---

### 3. Semantic Evolution (The "Why")
To understand the word fully, one must understand the semantic shift:
1.  **Original:** *Baas* = Master/Head/Control.
2.  **Verb:** *Ver-bazen* = To cause someone to lose their control/mastery over their thoughts.
3.  **Modern:** *Verbazen* = To astonish (because you are momentarily "at a loss" for words or reaction).
4.  **Noun:** *Verbaz-ing* = The state of being astonished.

### Summary
**`Verbazing`** is a noun formed by adding the nominalizing suffix **`-ing`** to the verb stem **`verbaz-`**. The verb itself is constructed from the prefix **`ver-`** (indicating change) and the archaic root **`baz-`** (related to *baas*/master), historically implying a loss of composure that evolved into the modern meaning of astonishment.
