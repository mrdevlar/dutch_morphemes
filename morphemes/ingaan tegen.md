From a linguistic perspective, it is important to clarify a key distinction first: **"`ingaan tegen`" is not a single word.** It is a **multi-word verbal phrase** (specifically, a separable verb combined with a preposition).

In Dutch linguistics, this is analyzed as a combination of a **separable verb** (*scheidbaar werkwoord*) and a **preposition** (*voorzetsel*).

Here is the morphological and syntactic breakdown of the components.

---

### 1. Morphological Breakdown

We must analyze the two distinct lexical units separately.

#### A. The Verb: `ingaan`
This is a **separable compound verb**. It consists of two morphemes:

1.  **`in-` (Prefix / Particle)**
    *   **Type:** Bound morpheme (in this context) / Prepositional particle.
    *   **Origin:** Derived from the preposition *in* (in/into).
    *   **Function:** It modifies the direction or state of the root verb. In *ingaan*, it indicates entering a state, starting something, or moving inward.
    *   **Morphological Status:** In the infinitive (*ingaan*), it is attached to the verb. In a main clause, it separates and moves to the end of the sentence.

2.  **`gaan` (Root)**
    *   **Type:** Free morpheme (Lexical root).
    *   **Meaning:** To go, to walk, to move.
    *   **Function:** The core semantic action of the verb.

*   **Combined Meaning of `ingaan`:** Literally "to go in." Figuratively, it often means "to enter into (an agreement)," "to start," or "to comply with" (usually when paired with the preposition *op*, e.g., *ingaan op een aanbod*).

#### B. The Preposition: `tegen`
1.  **`tegen` (Preposition)**
    *   **Type:** Free morpheme.
    *   **Meaning:** Against, opposite, towards.
    *   **Function:** It governs the object of the sentence and establishes the relationship of opposition.

---

### 2. Syntactic Structure (How they fit together)

When combined as **`ingaan tegen`**, they form a specific idiomatic construction.

*   **Structure:** [Verb *ingaan*] + [Preposition *tegen*]
*   **Meaning:** To run counter to, to contradict, to violate, or to go against.
*   **Syntax (Word Order):** Because *ingaan* is a **separable verb**, the prefix *in-* splits from the root *gaan* in main clauses.

**Example:**
> *Dit **gaat** in **tegen** de regels.*
> (This goes against the rules.)

*   **`gaat`**: Conjugated root verb (*gaan*).
*   **`in`**: Separated prefix (placed after the verb).
*   **`tegen`**: Preposition (placed after the prefix).
*   **`de regels`**: Object of the preposition.

**Infinitive Form:**
> *...om dit **in te gaan tegen** de regels.* (Incorrect)
> *...om dit **te gaan** in **tegen** de regels.* (Incorrect)
> *Correct:* *...om hier **te gaan** in **tegen**...* (Rare)
> *Most Common:* *...om hier **in te gaan tegen**...* (Wait, standard usage is actually: *...om hier **te gaan** in **tegen**...* is awkward).

*Correction on Syntax:*
Actually, in the infinitive with *te*, the separable prefix usually stays attached to the verb stem in standard Dutch, but the preposition *tegen* remains separate.
*   **Infinitive:** *ingaan tegen* (e.g., "Het is verboden om in te gaan tegen de wet").
*   **Main Clause:** *gaat ... in tegen* (e.g., "Het gaat in tegen de wet").

---

### 3. Semantic Analysis (Meaning Shift)

Linguistically, this is an example of **semantic bleaching** and **idiomatization**.

1.  **Literal:** *Gaan* (move) + *in* (into) + *tegen* (against).
2.  **Idiomatic:** The physical movement of "going" is lost. The phrase now refers to an abstract logical or legal contradiction.
3.  **Collocation:** While *ingaan* usually pairs with *op* (to accept/consider), pairing it with *tegen* creates a specific meaning of **conflict**.

### 4. Common Confusion: `ingaan tegen` vs. `tegengaan`

A crucial linguistic distinction for learners is the difference between this phrase and the single verb **`tegengaan`**.

| Feature | **`ingaan tegen`** | **`tegengaan`** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Structure** | Verb + Preposition | Compound Verb |
| **Morphemes** | `in` + `gaan` + `tegen` | `tegen` + `gaan` |
| **Meaning** | To go against (often laws, rules, nature) | To counteract, to oppose actively |
| **Usage** | *Dit gaat in tegen de wet.* (This violates the law.) | *We moeten de verspreiding tegengaan.* (We must counteract the spread.) |
| **Separability** | **Separable** (*gaat ... in tegen*) | **Separable** (*gaat ... tegen*) |

### Summary
From a morphological perspective, **`ingaan tegen`** is not a single word but a syntactic unit composed of:
1.  **`in`**: A separable prefix indicating direction/state.
2.  **`gaan`**: The verbal root indicating motion/action.
3.  **`tegen`**: A preposition indicating opposition.

Together, they function as a **prepositional verb phrase** meaning "to contradict" or "to violate."
