From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`voertaal`** is a **compound noun** (*samengesteld zelfstandig naamwoord*). It is formed through the process of **compounding**, where two or more morphemes are joined to create a new lexical item with a specific meaning.

Here is the breakdown and explanation of the morphemes:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into two distinct morphemes:

> **voer** + **taal**

*   **voer-**: Verbal stem (Modifier)
*   **-taal**: Noun (Head)

---

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. Morpheme 1: `voer-`
*   **Category:** Verbal Stem (*werkwoordstam*).
*   **Source Verb:** *voeren* (to lead, to conduct, to steer, to carry).
*   **Form:** This is the bare stem of the verb. In Dutch compounding, verbs are typically reduced to their stem form when acting as the first element of a compound (e.g., *leer* from *leren*, *werk* from *werken*).
*   **Meaning:** In this specific context, *voeren* does not mean "to feed" (which is a homophone *voer* meaning fodder). Instead, it retains the meaning of **"to conduct"** or **"to lead."**
*   **Function:** It acts as a **modifier**. It specifies the *type* or *function* of the language. It implies "the language that is being conducted/used to lead the instruction."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *voeren*, Old Dutch *fōrian*, and Proto-Germanic *\*fōrjaną*. It is cognate with the English word *fare* (as in "farewell" or "journey") and *forage*.

#### B. Morpheme 2: `-taal`
*   **Category:** Noun (*zelfstandig naamwoord*).
*   **Source:** The independent noun *taal*.
*   **Form:** Free morpheme (can stand alone as a word).
*   **Meaning:** Language.
*   **Function:** It acts as the **head** (*hoofd*) of the compound. In Dutch (a Germanic language), compounds are **head-final**, meaning the last element determines the grammatical category and the core meaning of the whole word. Therefore, a *voertaal* is a type of *taal*.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *tale*, Old Dutch *tala*, and Proto-Germanic *\*talō*. It is cognate with the English word *tale* (story) and *tell*.

---

### 3. Syntactic and Semantic Structure

*   **Compound Type:** **Endocentric Compound**.
    *   The meaning of the whole word is a hyponym (a specific subtype) of the head. A *voertaal* is a specific kind of *taal*.
*   **Semantic Relationship:** **Verb-Object / Purpose**.
    *   The relationship between the modifier and the head is often interpreted as "the language **for** conducting (instruction)."
    *   It functions similarly to English compounds like *driving* (verb stem) + *license* (noun) = *driving license* (the license for driving).
*   **Linking Elements:**
    *   There is **no linking morpheme** (such as *-s-*, *-en-*, or *-t-*) between the two parts.
    *   *Note:* In some Dutch compounds derived from verbs, a *-t-* is inserted (e.g., *voertuig* is sometimes analyzed as *voer* + *t* + *tuig*, though this is debated). However, in *voertaal*, the stem *voer* attaches directly to *taal* without an intervening consonant.

| Morpheme | Type | Origin | Meaning | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **voer-** | Bound Stem | Verb *voeren* | To lead/conduct | Modifier (Specifies purpose) |
| **-taal** | Free Root | Noun *taal* | Language | Head (Determines category) |
| **voertaal** | Compound Noun | Dutch | Language of instruction | Whole Word |

### 5. Usage Context
In modern Dutch, **`voertaal`** is almost exclusively used in educational or administrative contexts. It refers to the **medium of instruction** (the language in which a course or school operates).
*   *Example:* "De voertaal op deze school is Nederlands." (The language of instruction at this school is Dutch.)

### Conclusion
Linguistically, **`voertaal`** is a transparent Germanic compound consisting of a verbal stem (*voer-*) modifying a noun head (*-taal*). It semantically encodes the concept of "the language used to conduct [education/communication]."
