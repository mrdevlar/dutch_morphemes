From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch verb **bereiken** (meaning "to reach," "to achieve," or "to attain") can be segmented into three distinct morphemes.

Here is the breakdown:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
**be- + reik + -en**

| Morpheme | Type | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **be-** | Derivational Prefix | Changes valency/meaning |
| **reik** | Root (Stem) | Core lexical meaning |
| **-en** | Inflectional Suffix | Marks infinitive form |

---

### 2. Detailed Explanation of Morphemes

#### **A. The Prefix: `be-`**
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix.
*   **Function:** In Dutch morphology, the prefix *be-* is highly productive. It often serves to **transitivize** an intransitive verb or to indicate that an action is performed **upon** an object or **to completion**.
*   **Semantic Effect:**
    *   Without the prefix, the root *reiken* implies a directional action (stretching out, reaching *towards* something).
    *   With the prefix *be-*, the focus shifts to the **result** or the **target**. It implies successfully making contact with the object or achieving the goal.
    *   *Example:* *Reiken* (to reach out/stretch) vs. *Bereiken* (to reach/attain a destination or goal).

#### **B. The Root: `reik`**
*   **Type:** Lexical Root (Stem).
*   **Core Meaning:** To stretch, to extend, to reach.
*   **Etymology:** This root comes from the Proto-Germanic **\*rīkaną**.
    *   It is cognate with the English word **reach**.
    *   It is cognate with the German verb **reichen**.
    *   It is cognate with the Old English *rīcan*.
*   **Spelling Note:** The vowel combination **ei** in Dutch represents a long /eː/ sound. It is important not to confuse this root with the Dutch noun *rijk* (meaning "rich" or "kingdom"), which comes from a different Proto-Germanic root (**\*rīkijaz* - ruler/king).

#### **C. The Suffix: `-en`**
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix.
*   **Function:** This is the standard marker for the **infinitive** form of Dutch verbs.
*   **Grammar:** It indicates that the verb is in its base form (e.g., "to reach"). In conjugated forms, this suffix is often dropped or changed (e.g., *hij bereikt* - he reaches).

---

### 3. Semantic Evolution and Usage

The addition of the prefix *be-* creates a specific semantic nuance known as **causative-transitive** derivation.

1.  **`Reiken` (Root only):** Often implies the physical act of stretching an arm or extending something.
    *   *Example:* "Ik reik naar de appel." (I am reaching for the apple.)
2.  **`Bereiken` (Prefix + Root):** Implies the successful completion of the action or the attainment of an abstract goal.
    *   *Example:* "Ik heb de top bereikt." (I have reached the summit.)
    *   *Example:* "We moeten een akkoord bereiken." (We must reach an agreement.)

### 4. Summary Table

| Component | Morpheme | Meaning/Role |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Prefix** | **be-** | Makes the action transitive/completive ("to make reach"). |
| **Root** | **reik** | The concept of stretching or extending (cognate with *reach*). |
| **Suffix** | **-en** | Infinitive marker ("to..."). |
| **Total** | **bereiken** | "To reach/achieve." |

### 5. Linguistic Note on Cognates
If you speak English, you can see the relationship clearly:
*   Dutch **reik** $\approx$ English **reach**
*   Dutch **be-** $\approx$ English **be-** (as in *bespeak*, *bedim* - though less productive in English now) or the causative sense of *make*.
*   Dutch **bereiken** $\approx$ English **to reach** (in the sense of attaining).

This word is a classic example of how Germanic languages use prefixes to alter the grammatical valency (the number of arguments a verb takes) of a root word.
