From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`kom`** presents an interesting case because it is a **homograph**: it is spelled the same way but functions as two distinct lexical items (a verb and a noun) with different etymological origins.

In both cases, the word is **monomorphemic** (composed of a single morpheme), but the analysis differs slightly depending on its grammatical function.

Here is the breakdown for both meanings:

---

### 1. `kom` as a Verb (Imperative)
**Meaning:** "Come!" (2nd person singular imperative).
**Lemma (Dictionary Form):** *komen* (to come).

*   **Morphological Breakdown:**
    *   **`kom`** (Root/Stem): This is the lexical root carrying the core meaning of "movement toward the speaker."
    *   **`Ø`** (Zero Morpheme): In Dutch, the imperative singular is formed by taking the verb stem and adding no suffix. The absence of the infinitive ending *-en* signals the imperative mood.

*   **Analysis:**
    *   **Type:** Inflected form.
    *   **Structure:** Stem + Ø (Imperative).
    *   **Contrast:**
        *   Infinitive: *kom* + *en*
        *   Present Indicative (1st/3rd person): *kom* + *t* (e.g., *hij komt*)
        *   Imperative: *kom* + *Ø*

### 2. `kom` as a Noun
**Meaning:** "Bowl" (a container for food).
**Lemma:** *kom*.

*   **Morphological Breakdown:**
    *   **`kom`** (Root): This is a free morpheme representing the object.
    *   **`Ø`** (Zero Morpheme): This indicates the singular number.

*   **Analysis:**
    *   **Type:** Lexical noun (monomorphemic).
    *   **Structure:** Root + Ø (Singular).
    *   **Contrast:**
        *   Singular: *kom* + *Ø*
        *   Plural: *kom* + *men* (e.g., *kommen*)

### Key Linguistic Takeaway
While `kom` looks like a single unit, a linguist analyzes it based on **paradigms** (how the word changes in different contexts).

1.  **If it is the verb:** It is the **stem** of the verb *komen*. The morpheme boundary is technically between the stem and the null inflection that marks the imperative mood.
2.  **If it is the noun:** It is a **root** noun. It is a "free morpheme" because it can stand alone as a word, but it is not "derived" (it has no prefixes or suffixes attached to create it).

In both instances, **`kom` is a single, indivisible morpheme** in its current form. It cannot be broken down further into smaller meaningful units (unlike a word like *on-wel-kom-stig*, which has multiple morphemes).
