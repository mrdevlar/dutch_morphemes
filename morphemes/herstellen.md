From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch verb **`herstellen`** (meaning *to repair, restore, or fix*) can be analyzed through its morphological structure, etymology, and phonological properties.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **`her-`** + **`stell`** + **`-en`**

---

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Prefix: `her-`
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix (Inseparable).
*   **Meaning:** "Again," "back," or "anew."
*   **Function:** It modifies the meaning of the base verb to indicate repetition or restoration to a previous state.
*   **Linguistic Note (Stress):** In Dutch, there are two prefixes that look like `her-`.
    1.  **Directional `her-`** (meaning "hither/here"): This is separable and stressed (e.g., *`her`komen* - to come back).
    2.  **Repetitive `her-`** (meaning "again"): This is **inseparable** and **unstressed**. In `herstellen`, the stress falls on the root (`her-`**`stel`**`-len`), not the prefix. This classifies it as an inseparable prefix verb (*onafscheidelijk werkwoord*).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *her-*, Old Dutch *hir-*, related to the Proto-Germanic *\*hir-* (again/back). It is cognate with the English prefix *heir-* (in the sense of succession/restoration) and the German *her-* (in the sense of *herstellen*).

#### B. The Root: `stell`
*   **Type:** Lexical Root (Verbal Stem).
*   **Meaning:** "To place," "to put," "to set," or "to stand."
*   **Function:** This carries the core semantic content of the action.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Proto-Germanic *\*stalljaną*.
*   **Cognates:** This root is closely related to the English words **`set`**, **`stand`**, **`stall`**, and **`still`**, as well as the German verb **`stellen`**.
*   **Morphology:** In modern Dutch, `stell` is a bound root in this context (you cannot use "stell" as a standalone word; it requires the infinitive ending `-en` or conjugation endings like `-t` or `-de`).

#### C. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix.
*   **Meaning:** Marks the **Infinitive** form of the verb.
*   **Function:** It indicates that the verb is in its base, non-conjugated form (the form found in a dictionary).
*   **Grammar:** This is the standard infinitive marker for Dutch verbs (e.g., *maken* (to make), *lopen* (to walk), *zijn* (to be)).

---

### 3. Semantic Synthesis (How the meaning is built)
When you combine these morphemes, the literal semantic composition is:

*   `stell` (to place/put) + `her-` (again/back) = **"To place again"** or **"To set back."**

Over time, this literal meaning evolved into the modern figurative meanings:
1.  **To repair:** Putting a broken object back into its functional state.
2.  **To restore:** Bringing a system or situation back to order.
3.  **To recover:** (Reflexive: *zich herstellen*) Bringing one's health back to a previous state.

### 4. Grammatical Context
*   **Verb Class:** Weak verb (regular conjugation).
    *   *Past Tense:* `herstelde` (root `stell` + past tense marker `-de`).
    *   *Past Participle:* `hergesteld` (prefix `her-` + root `stell` + participle marker `-t` + infinitive marker `-en` dropped).
*   **Syntax:** Because `her-` is an inseparable prefix, the verb does not split in a main clause.
    *   *Correct:* "Ik **herstel** de computer." (I repair the computer.)
    *   *Incorrect:* "Ik stel de computer her."
