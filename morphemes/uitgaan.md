From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`uitgaan`** is a classic example of a **separable compound verb** (*scheidbaar samengesteld werkwoord*).

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation of its components.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word consists of two distinct morphemes:

> **`uit`** + **`gaan`**

#### Morpheme A: `uit`
*   **Category:** Prepositional Particle / Adverb.
*   **Status:** Free Morpheme (it can stand alone as a word, e.g., *Het licht is uit* / "The light is off").
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *ūt*, cognate with German *aus* and English *out*.
*   **Function:** In this context, it acts as a **directional prefix**. It modifies the root verb to indicate movement from an interior to an exterior space, or a state of completion/separation.
*   **Phonology:** In the compound verb, the primary stress falls on this syllable (**ÚIT**-gaan). This distinguishes it from inseparable verbs (like *beGAAN*), where the stress falls on the root.

#### Morpheme B: `gaan`
*   **Category:** Verb (Infinitive).
*   **Status:** Free Morpheme (it is the lexical root).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *gān*, cognate with German *gehen* and English *go*.
*   **Function:** This is the **lexical core** of the word. It provides the fundamental action (movement/locomotion).
*   **Conjugation:** As the verb root, it carries the grammatical tense and agreement (e.g., *ik ga*, *jij gaat*, *wij gaan*).

---

### 2. Morphological Process: Separable Compounding
Linguistically, `uitgaan` is formed through **compounding**, but with a specific syntactic behavior known as **separability**.

*   **Infinitive Form:** In the dictionary form (and after modal verbs), the morphemes are written as a single unit: *Ik wil **uitgaan*** ("I want to go out").
*   **Finite Form (Main Clause):** In a standard main clause, the particle `uit` separates from the verb `gaan` and moves to the end of the sentence.
    *   *Example:* *Ik **ga** vanavond **uit**.* (I go tonight out).
    *   *Linguistic Term:* This is often analyzed as a **Verb-Particle Construction** (similar to English phrasal verbs like "go out").
*   **Subordinate Clause:** In a dependent clause, the morphemes remain attached.
    *   *Example:* *...dat ik vanavond **uitga**.* (...that I tonight go-out).

### 3. Semantic Analysis (Meaning)
The meaning of the compound is not always strictly the sum of its parts (non-compositional semantics), though it is largely transparent here.

1.  **Literal Meaning:** To physically leave a building or enclosed space.
    *   *Example:* *We gaan het huis uit.* (We are going out of the house.)
2.  **Social/Idiomatic Meaning:** To go out for entertainment (nightlife, dating, dining).
    *   *Example:* *Zij gaan vaak uit.* (They often go out [to clubs/bars].)
3.  **Logical/Mathematical Meaning:** To assume or suppose (often used in the phrase *uitgaan van*).
    *   *Example:* *We gaan ervan uit dat het waar is.* (We assume [go out from] that it is true.)
4.  **State Change:** To extinguish (regarding lights or fires).
    *   *Example:* *Het licht gaat uit.* (The light goes out.)

### Conclusion
From a morphological standpoint, **`uitgaan`** is a **separable verb** composed of the directional particle **`uit`** and the motion verb **`gaan`**. Its linguistic significance lies in its stress pattern (stress on the prefix) and its syntactic behavior (splitting in finite main clauses), which are hallmark features of Dutch separable compound verbs.
