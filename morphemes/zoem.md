### 2. Phonological and Semantic Analysis (Phonosemantics)

While `zoem` is one morpheme, its internal phonological structure is not arbitrary. It is a classic example of **onomatopoeia** (sound symbolism). The sounds chosen for this morpheme iconically represent the meaning.

*   **/z/ (Voiced Alveolar Fricative):**
    *   **Phonetics:** Produced by forcing air through a narrow channel made by the tongue and the alveolar ridge, with vocal cord vibration.
    *   **Semantics:** This sound mimics the continuous, vibrating noise of a bee or an engine. It is a "noisy" consonant.
*   **/uː/ (Long Close Back Rounded Vowel):**
    *   **Phonetics:** Represented by the digraph *oe* in Dutch.
    *   **Semantics:** The length and resonance of the vowel suggest a sustained, humming tone rather than a short, sharp click.
*   **/m/ (Voiced Bilabial Nasal):**
    *   **Phonetics:** Produced by closing the lips and allowing air to escape through the nose.
    *   **Semantics:** Nasals often convey resonance or a "humming" quality. The closure of the lips at the end mimics the containment of the sound.

**Linguistic Concept:** This is **phonetic iconicity**. The form of the morpheme resembles the sound it describes.

*   **Cognates:**
