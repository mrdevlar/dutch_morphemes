From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`schilderen`** (meaning "to paint") can be analyzed through both **synchronic** (current structure) and **diachronic** (historical/etymological) morphology.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **`schild`** + **`er`** + **`en`**

---

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. Root: `schild`
*   **Type:** Free Morpheme / Lexical Root
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun (historically)
*   **Meaning:** "Shield"
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Proto-Germanic **`*skelduz`**, related to the English word *shield* and German *Schild*.
*   **Semantic Shift:** This is the most interesting linguistic feature of the word. Originally, a *schild* was a physical shield. In the Middle Ages, shields were often decorated with coats of arms or paintings. Through **metonymy** (where the object is associated with the action performed on it), the word shifted from the object ("shield") to the action of decorating it ("to paint").
    *   *Note:* In modern Dutch, the root *schild* still exists independently as the noun for "shield," but in this specific word, it carries the semantic weight of "painting."

#### B. Derivational Suffix: `-er`
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix (Agentive)
*   **Function:** This suffix typically turns a verb or noun into an **agent noun** (a person who performs an action).
*   **Formation:** `schild` (shield/paint) + `-er` = **`schilder`** (painter).
*   **Linguistic Role:** In the context of the verb *schilderen*, this suffix is fossilized within the stem. It creates the noun *schilder* (the painter), which then serves as the base for the verb.
*   **Phonology:** The addition of `-er` does not trigger a vowel change in the root *schild* (unlike some other Germanic derivations), keeping the vowel /ɪ/ (short i).

#### C. Inflectional Suffix: `-en`
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix
*   **Function:** **Infinitive Marker**.
*   **Grammar:** In Dutch, the standard ending for the infinitive form of a verb is `-en` (e.g., *lopen*, *werken*, *zien* → *zien* is irregular, but *schilderen* is regular).
*   **Verbalizer:** While `-en` marks the infinitive, it also functions as a **verbalizer** here, turning the noun stem *schilder-* into a verb.
*   **Conjugation:** This suffix is removed and replaced during conjugation (e.g., *ik schilder* [I paint], *hij schildert* [he paints]).

---

### 3. Summary of Derivation Process

The word formation follows a **denominal derivation** path (deriving a verb from a noun):

1.  **Base:** `schild` (Noun: Shield)
2.  **Step 1 (Nominalization):** `schild` + `-er` → **`schilder`** (Noun: Painter / One who paints shields)
3.  **Step 2 (Verbalization):** `schilder` + `-en` → **`schilderen`** (Verb: To paint)

### 4. Linguistic Classification
*   **Word Class:** Verb (Infinitive)
*   **Verb Type:** Weak (Regular) Verb.
    *   *Past Tense:* *schilderde* (stem + `-de`)
    *   *Past Participle:* *geschilderd* (prefix `ge-` + stem + `-t`)
*   **Morphological Transparency:** The word is **transparent**. A Dutch speaker can intuitively recognize the relationship between the noun *schilder* (painter) and the verb *schilderen* (to paint), even if the original connection to *schild* (shield) is less obvious to the average modern speaker.

### 5. Comparison with English
*   **Dutch:** *schilderen* (to paint)
*   **English:** *to shield* (to protect) vs. *to paint* (to color).
*   **Observation:** English kept the original meaning of the Germanic root (*shield*) separate from the artistic action (*paint*, from Latin *pictura*). Dutch merged the concepts, likely due to the historical practice of painting heraldry on shields.
