Here is the breakdown of the morphemes:

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Root: `schreeuw-`
*   **Classification:** This is a **free morpheme** (specifically, a lexical root). It cannot be broken down further into smaller meaningful units in Modern Dutch.
*   **Semantics:** It denotes the action of emitting a loud, harsh sound with the voice.
*   **Phonology:** In Standard Dutch, this is pronounced approximately as **/sxreːʋ/**.
    *   **`sch`**: Represents the voiceless velar fricative **/x/** (like the *ch* in Scottish *loch* or German *Bach*).
    *   **`ee`**: Represents a long close-mid front unrounded vowel **/eː/**.
    *   **`uw`**: Represents a labio-velar approximant **/ʋ/** (similar to the English *v*, but softer).
*   **Etymology:**
    *   Derived from Middle Dutch **`schreuen`**.
    *   Traces back to Old Dutch **`skriowan`**.
    *   It is a cognate with the German verb **`schreien`** and the English word **`shriek`** (though English *shriek* underwent different sound shifts).
    *   *Note on Onomatopoeia:* Linguists often classify this root as having **sound-symbolic** properties. The harsh /x/ sound and the long, open vowel /eː/ mimic the acoustic quality of a loud, strained voice.

#### B. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Classification:** This is a **bound morpheme** (specifically, an inflectional suffix).
*   **Function:**
    1.  **Infinitive Marker:** In Dutch, the dictionary form of a verb almost always ends in *-en*.
    2.  **Plural Present:** It also marks the first-person plural (*wij schreeuwen*) and second/third-person plural (*jullie/zij schreeuwen*).
*   **Phonology:** Pronounced as a schwa **/ə/**, often with a nasalized off-glide **/n/** depending on the speech rate and dialect. In rapid speech, the /n/ may be dropped (e.g., *schreeuwe*).

### 3. Morphological Process & Derivation
While `schreeuwen` itself is a simple verb, the root `schreeuw-` is highly productive in Dutch morphology. It serves as the base for several derived words:

1.  **Nominalization (Noun formation):**
    *   `schreeuw` + `-` (zero derivation) $\rightarrow$ **`de schreeuw`** (the shout/scream).
    *   `ge-` + `schreeuw` + `-` $\rightarrow$ **`het geschreeuw`** (the act of shouting / the noise).
    *   *Morphological Note:* The prefix `ge-` is a common nominalizer for verbs in Dutch, often implying a collective or continuous action.

2.  **Agent Noun (Doer of the action):**
    *   `schreeuw` + `-er` $\rightarrow$ **`schreeuwer`** (shouter).
    *   *Morphological Note:* The suffix `-er` indicates the person performing the action.

3.  **Adjectival/Participial:**
    *   `ge-` + `schreeuw` + `-d` $\rightarrow$ **`geschreeuwd`** (shouted).
    *   *Morphological Note:* This is the past participle form. Unlike some strong verbs (which change the vowel, e.g., *zingen* $\rightarrow$ *gezongen*), `schreeuwen` is a **weak verb**, meaning it forms its past tense and participle by adding dental suffixes (`-de` / `-d`).
