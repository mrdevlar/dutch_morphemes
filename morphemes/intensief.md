From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`intensief`** is a **derived adjective**. It is not a compound word (two independent words joined together), but rather a single lexical unit formed by combining a root with a derivational suffix.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into two distinct morphemes:

> **`intens`** + **`-ief`**

---

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### **Morpheme 1: `intens-` (The Root/Stem)**
*   **Type:** Lexical Root (in this context).
*   **Status:** In modern Dutch, *intens* can function as a free morpheme (an independent adjective meaning "intense"), but within the word *intensief*, it functions as the bound stem carrying the core semantic load.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from the Latin past participle **`intensus`**, which comes from the verb **`intendere`** (to stretch, strain, or direct).
    *   *In-* (towards) + *tendere* (to stretch).
*   **Meaning:** It conveys the concept of "stretched to the limit," "strained," or "high degree."
*   **Phonology:** In Dutch, this is pronounced /ɪnˈtɛns/.

#### **Morpheme 2: `-ief` (The Suffix)**
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix.
*   **Function:** Adjectivizer. It transforms the root into an adjective. It indicates "tending to," "having the nature of," or "relating to."
*   **Etymology:** This is a loan suffix from Latin **`-ivus`**, which entered Dutch via French **`-if`**.
*   **Dutch Orthography:** In Dutch, this suffix is consistently spelled with an **`f`** at the end (unlike English, which uses `v` in *-ive*). This is a standard orthographic rule for Latinate loanwords in Dutch (e.g., *actief*, *passief*, *positief*).
*   **Meaning:** It adds the nuance of "characterized by" or "pertaining to."

---

### 3. Etymological Path
The word is a **loanword** (specifically a learned borrowing) rather than a native Germanic construction.

1.  **Latin:** *intendere* (to stretch) $\rightarrow$ *intensus* (stretched) $\rightarrow$ *intensivus* (tending to stretch/strain).
2.  **French:** *intensif*.
3.  **Dutch:** *intensief*.

Because it is a loanword, it does not follow the native Germanic patterns of Dutch derivation (like adding *-lijk* or *-baar*). Instead, it retains its Romance morphology.

---

### 4. Semantic Nuance: *Intens* vs. *Intensief*
While the root is the same, the addition of the suffix changes the specific usage in Dutch:

*   **`Intens` (Adjective):** Often refers to the **quality** or **feeling** of something.
    *   *Example:* "Een **intens** gevoel" (An intense feeling).
*   **`Intensief` (Adjective):** Often refers to the **method**, **concentration**, or **effort** applied to something.
    *   *Example:* "Een **intensieve** behandeling" (An intensive treatment).

### 5. Related Morphological Family
To fully understand the morphology, it helps to look at the word family derived from the same root:

*   **`Intensiteit`** (Noun): Root `intens` + Suffix `-iteit` (Latin *-itas*). Meaning: Intensity.
*   **`Intensiveren`** (Verb): Root `intens` + Suffix `-er` + Suffix `-en`. Meaning: To intensify.
*   **`Intensief`** (Adjective): Root `intens` + Suffix `-ief`. Meaning: Intensive.
