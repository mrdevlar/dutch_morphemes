From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`zolang`** is a **compound** formed from two distinct free morphemes. It serves as a prime example of **grammaticalization**, where a lexical phrase evolves into a functional grammatical marker.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented as follows:
> **`zo`** + **`lang`**

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### Morpheme A: `zo`
*   **Form:** *zo*
*   **Part of Speech:** Degree Adverb (Gradatiebijwoord)
*   **Meaning:** "so," "thus," "in this manner."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *sō*. It is cognate with the English *so* and German *so*.
*   **Function:** In this compound, *zo* acts as a **degree modifier**. It indicates the extent or intensity of the following element. It points to a specific degree of duration.

#### Morpheme B: `lang`
*   **Form:** *lang*
*   **Part of Speech:** Adjective / Adverb (Adjectief / Bijwoord)
*   **Meaning:** "long" (referring to time duration or physical length).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *lang*. It is cognate with the English *long* and German *lang*.
*   **Function:** This is the **semantic head** of the compound. It provides the core meaning regarding time or duration.

### 3. Syntactic and Semantic Synthesis

When combined, these morphemes create a unit that functions in two primary ways, depending on context and orthography (spelling):

#### A. As a Subordinating Conjunction (Lexicalized: `zolang`)
*   **Spelling:** Written as one word.
*   **Meaning:** "As long as" (temporal condition).
*   **Linguistic Process:** This is an example of **grammaticalization**. Originally a phrase meaning "so long" (duration), it evolved into a conjunction introducing a temporal clause.
*   **Example:** *`Zolang` je leeft, moet je leren.* ("**As long as** you live, you must learn.")
*   **Note:** In older Dutch or for emphasis, this is sometimes expanded to *`zolang als`* (literally "so long as"), where *als* is a comparative particle. However, modern standard Dutch accepts *zolang* alone.

#### B. As an Adverbial Phrase (Phrasal: `zo lang`)
*   **Spelling:** Written as two words.
*   **Meaning:** "For so long" (emphasizing the degree of duration).
*   **Linguistic Process:** Here, the morphemes retain their individual lexical meanings. *Zo* modifies *lang* to indicate a specific quantity of time, often implying surprise or emphasis.
*   **Example:** *Ik heb `zo lang` gewacht.* ("I waited **for so long**.")

### 5. Linguistic Insight: Compounding vs. Phrasing
In Dutch linguistics, the distinction between **`zolang`** (one word) and **`zo lang`** (two words) is significant.
*   **`zolang`** is treated as a single lexical item (a conjunction). It cannot be interrupted by other words.
*   **`zo lang`** is a syntactic phrase. It allows for modification (e.g., *`zo` **heel** `lang`* — "so very long").

Therefore, while the morphemes are identical, the **morphological boundary** determines the grammatical function of the word in a sentence.
