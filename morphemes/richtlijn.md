From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`richtlijn`** is a **compound noun** (*samenstelling*). It consists of two distinct morphemes that combine to create a specific semantic meaning.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Segmentation
The word is segmented as follows:
> **richt** + **lijn**

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### Morpheme A: `richt-`
*   **Type:** Bound Stem (derived from a verb).
*   **Base Form:** The verb *richten* (to direct, to aim, to align, to straighten).
*   **Etymology:**
    *   Derived from Old Dutch *rihten*.
    *   Traces back to the Proto-Germanic *\*rehtjan* (to make right/straight).
    *   Cognate with the English word *right* (in the sense of "straight" or "correct") and the German *richten*.
*   **Meaning in Compound:** In this context, it functions as a **modifier**. It implies direction, orientation, or guidance. It transforms the physical concept of the second morpheme into a functional or abstract concept.
*   **Grammatical Note:** This is the stem of the verb without the infinitive ending *-en*. In Dutch compounding, verb stems are frequently used to modify nouns (e.g., *slaap* + *kamer* = *slaapkamer*).

#### Morpheme B: `-lijn`
*   **Type:** Free Root (Noun).
*   **Base Form:** The noun *lijn* (line).
*   **Etymology:**
    *   Derived from Middle Dutch *line*.
    *   Borrowed from Latin *linea* (thread, linen, line).
    *   Cognate with English *line* and German *Linie*.
*   **Meaning in Compound:** This is the **head** of the compound. In Dutch morphology, the head (usually the second element) determines the grammatical gender and the plural form of the new word.
    *   *Lijn* is a *de*-word (common gender).
    *   *Richtlijn* is also a *de*-word.
    *   Plural: *Lijnen* $\rightarrow$ *Richtlijnen*.
*   **Semantic Role:** It provides the core object (a line), which is then specified by the first morpheme.

### 3. Syntactic and Semantic Structure

*   **Compound Type:** Endocentric Compound.
    *   The meaning of the whole word is a specific type of the head noun (*lijn*). A *richtlijn* is a specific kind of *lijn*.
*   **Semantic Relationship:**
    *   **Literal:** A line used for directing or aiming (e.g., a surveyor's line).
    *   **Figurative/Abstract:** A "line" of thought or policy. In modern usage, it almost exclusively refers to a **guideline**, **principle**, or **directive**.
*   **Semantic Shift:** There is a metaphorical shift from the physical domain (a drawn line) to the normative domain (a rule to follow).

### 4. Special Contextual Usage (Legal/Political)
From a sociolinguistic perspective, the word has a specific technical meaning within the European Union context.
*   **EU Directive:** In Dutch law, an EU *Richtlijn* is a legislative act that sets a goal for all EU countries to achieve, but leaves the national authorities the choice of form and methods to achieve it.
*   **Contrast:** This distinguishes it from a *Verordening* (Regulation), which is directly applicable without national implementation.

### Conclusion
Linguistically, **`richtlijn`** is a transparent compound where the verbal stem *richt-* modifies the noun *lijn*. While etymologically it suggests a "straightening line," semantically it has evolved to represent an abstract path of conduct or a set of rules to be followed.
