From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch verb **`melden`** (meaning "to report," "to notify," or "to announce") can be analyzed through **morphology** (the study of word structure) and **etymology** (word history).

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes in `melden`.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word consists of two distinct morphemes:

| Morpheme | Form | Type | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`meld-`** | Root / Stem | **Lexical Morpheme** | Carries the core semantic meaning. |
| **`-en`** | Suffix | **Inflectional Morpheme** | Marks the infinitive form of the verb. |

**Formula:** `meld` + `en` = `melden`

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Root: `meld-`
*   **Type:** Bound Root (Lexical Stem).
    *   In modern Dutch, this root cannot stand alone as a complete word in isolation (you cannot say "Ik meld" without a subject, and the bare stem is not a dictionary entry). It requires inflectional endings to function grammatically.
*   **Semantics:**
    *   The core meaning is "to make known," "to communicate information," or "to register."
    *   It implies a transfer of information from a source to a receiver, often in an official or formal context (e.g., reporting a crime, registering a birth).
*   **Etymology:**
    *   **Middle Dutch:** *melden*
    *   **Old Dutch:** *meldian*
    *   **Proto-Germanic:** *\*meldōną* (to announce, report).
    *   **Cognates:** It is directly cognate with the German verb **`melden`** and the English verb **`meld`**.
        *   *Note on English:* While English "meld" originally meant "to report" (similar to Dutch), the meaning shifted in modern English to primarily mean "to mix" or "to combine" (especially in card games). The Dutch meaning has remained closer to the original Germanic sense of "announcing."
*   **Morphophonology:**
    *   The root ends in a voiceless alveolar stop `/d/`. In Dutch weak verb conjugation, this often triggers gemination (doubling) in the past tense (e.g., *meld**d**e*), though in the infinitive, it remains single.

#### B. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix.
*   **Function:**
    *   **Infinitive Marker:** In Dutch, the suffix `-en` is the standard marker for the **infinitive** form of a verb (the "to" form in English).
    *   **Verb Class:** It indicates that `melden` is a **weak verb** (regular verb). Strong verbs in Dutch often use `-en` as well, but the conjugation patterns differ (vowel changes vs. dental suffixes).
*   **Phonology:**
    *   Pronounced as a schwa followed by a nasal: `/ə.n/`.
    *   In rapid speech, the final `-n` is often dropped (apocope), resulting in a pronunciation closer to `/ˈmɛldə/`.

### 3. Derivational Productivity (Word Family)
To understand the morpheme `meld-` fully, it is helpful to look at how it combines with other morphemes to create related words. This demonstrates the **productivity** of the root.

1.  **`melding`** (Noun)
    *   `meld-` (root) + `-ing` (nominalizing suffix).
    *   Meaning: "A report" or "a notification."
2.  **`aangemeld`** (Participle/Adjective)
    *   `aan-` (separable prefix indicating direction/completion) + `meld-` (root) + `-d` (past participle marker).
    *   Meaning: "Registered" or "signed up."
3.  **`melder`** (Agent Noun)
    *   `meld-` (root) + `-er` (agentive suffix).
    *   Meaning: "The reporter" or "the notifier."
4.  **`gemeld`** (Past Participle)
    *   `ge-` (participle prefix) + `meld-` (root) + `-d` (past participle marker).
    *   Meaning: "Reported."

### 4. Syntactic Context
From a syntactic perspective, the morphological structure dictates how the word behaves in a sentence:
*   **Transitivity:** `Melden` is typically a **transitive verb**, requiring a direct object (e.g., *Ik meld **het incident*** / I report **the incident**).
*   **Reflexive Usage:** It can also be used reflexively with the morpheme `zich` (e.g., *zich melden* / to show up or register oneself).

### Summary
Linguistically, **`melden`** is a **monomorphemic stem** (`meld-`) combined with a **verbal inflectional suffix** (`-en`). It is a regular weak verb of Germanic origin that retains the original semantic field of "public announcement" found in its Proto-Germanic ancestor, distinguishing it from its English cognate which has undergone semantic shift.
