From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`zonnebril`** (sunglasses) is a **compound noun**. It is formed by combining two independent nouns with a linking element.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **`zon`** + **`-en-`** + **`bril`**

---

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### **Morpheme 1: `zon`**
*   **Type:** Free morpheme (Root).
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun.
*   **Meaning:** "Sun."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *sunna*, which traces back to Proto-Germanic *\*sunnōn* and ultimately Proto-Indo-European *\*sóh₂wl̥* (the same root as English *sun* and Latin *sol*).
*   **Function:** In this compound, it acts as the **modifier**. It specifies the purpose or type of the object.

#### **Morpheme 2: `-en-`**
*   **Type:** Bound morpheme (Linking element / *Fuguelement*).
*   **Function:** This is a connecting sound inserted between the two nouns.
*   **Linguistic Origin:** Historically, this element often originates from the **plural form** of the first noun (*zonnen*) or the genitive singular (*zonnes*). In modern Dutch morphology, it is treated as a fossilized linking element required by the phonological or grammatical class of the first noun.
    *   *Note:* Not all Dutch compounds have this. For example, *fietsbel* (bicycle bell) does not use a linking element because *fiets* does not take *-en* in the plural (*fietsen* is the plural, but the compound rules vary). However, for *zon*, the plural is *zonnen*, so the linking *-en-* is used in the compound.
*   **Pronunciation:** It is pronounced as a schwa sound /ə/ (like the 'a' in "about").

#### **Morpheme 3: `bril`**
*   **Type:** Free morpheme (Root).
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun.
*   **Meaning:** "Glasses" or "Spectacles."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *bril*, likely borrowed from Latin *beryllus* (beryl), referring to the clear stone originally used for lenses.
*   **Function:** In this compound, it acts as the **head**.
    *   **Head:** The element that determines the grammatical category and core meaning of the whole word. A *zonnebril* is a type of *bril*.
    *   **Number:** In Dutch, *bril* is singular (one pair of glasses), whereas English uses the plural "glasses."

---

### 3. Structural Classification

*   **Compound Type:** **Endocentric Compound**.
    *   The meaning of the whole word is a hyponym (a specific type) of the head. A *zonnebril* is a specific type of *bril*.
*   **Semantic Relationship:** **Purpose/Function**.
    *   The relationship between the modifier and the head is "glasses *for* the sun" or "glasses *against* the sun."

### 4. Orthography and Phonology
*   **Spelling:** In Dutch, when a noun ending in a vowel or specific consonants is compounded, the linking element is often retained in the spelling. Hence, it is written as *zonnebril* rather than *zonbril*.
*   **Stress:** The primary stress falls on the first syllable of the first constituent: **`ZON`**-ne-bril (/ˈzɔ.nə.brɪl/). This is typical for Dutch compounds, where the stress pattern distinguishes them from phrases.
