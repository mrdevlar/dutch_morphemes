From a linguistic perspective, the analysis of the Dutch word **`instituut`** requires distinguishing between its **synchronic** structure (how it functions in modern Dutch) and its **diachronic** structure (its historical etymology).

### 1. Synchronic Analysis (Modern Dutch)
In contemporary Dutch, **`instituut`** is considered a **monomorphemic** word. This means that to a modern speaker, it is perceived as a single, indivisible unit of meaning (a root).

*   **Morpheme:** `instituut`
*   **Type:** Free Root (Lexeme)
*   **Word Class:** Noun (Het-word)
*   **Meaning:** An organization established for a specific purpose (e.g., research, education, art).

**Why is it monomorphemic in Dutch?**
While the word has internal history, the parts that make it up are no longer productive in the Dutch language. A Dutch speaker cannot take the prefix *in-* and apply it to other words to create new meanings, nor can they identify *-tuut* as a suffix. Therefore, linguistically, it is treated as one atomic block.

### 2. Diachronic Analysis (Etymology)
If we look at the history of the word, it is a **loanword** borrowed from French (*institut*), which in turn came from Latin (*institutum*). In its original Latin form, it was polymorphemic.

**Latin Origin:** *institutum*
**Breakdown:**
1.  **Prefix:** `in-`
    *   **Meaning:** "Into," "on," or "upon."
    *   **Function:** Directional or intensifying prefix.
2.  **Root:** `statu-` (from the verb *statuere*)
    *   **Meaning:** "To set up," "to establish," "to place."
    *   **Ultimate Root:** *stare* ("to stand").
3.  **Suffix:** `-tum`
    *   **Meaning:** Past participle suffix (neuter noun).
    *   **Function:** Turns the verb into a noun indicating the result of the action (that which has been set up).

**Literal Etymological Meaning:** "That which has been set up/established."
