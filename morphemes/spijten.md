From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`spijten`** can be broken down into two distinct morphemes. It is a classic example of a **denominal verb** (a verb derived from a noun).

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### **A. The Root: `spijt`**
*   **Type:** Free Morpheme (Lexical Root)
*   **Original Part of Speech:** Noun
*   **Meaning:** "Regret," "sorrow," or "pity."
*   **Function:** This is the semantic core of the word. In modern Dutch, *spijt* exists independently as a noun (e.g., *Ik heb spijt* = "I have regret").
*   **Phonology:** The digraph **`ij`** represents the diphthong /ɛi/ (similar to the 'ei' in English 'eye' or 'ay' in 'say'). The final **`t`** is a voiceless alveolar stop.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *spijt*. Historically, this root is related to the concept of "damage" or "harm" (cognate with the English word *spite* in its older sense of malice or injury, though the meanings diverged). In older usage, *spijt* could refer to physical damage or loss, which evolved into the emotional feeling of regret.

#### **B. The Suffix: `-en`**
*   **Type:** Bound Morpheme
*   **Function:**
    1.  **Derivational (Verbalizer):** In the context of the lexicon, this suffix converts the noun *spijt* into a verb. This process is called **denominalization**.
    2.  **Inflectional (Infinitive Marker):** In the context of the verb's conjugation, *-en* marks the **infinitive** form.
*   **Phonology:** Pronounced as a schwa /ə/ followed by a nasal /n/ (often elided in rapid speech, sounding like /spɛi̯tə/).
*   **Pattern:** This is the standard infinitive ending for regular (weak) verbs in Dutch (e.g., *lopen*, *werken*, *spijten*).

---

### 3. Morphological Process: Denominalization

The formation of *spijten* follows a specific derivational pattern common in Germanic languages:

1.  **Base:** Noun (*spijt*)
2.  **Process:** Add verbal suffix (*-en*)
3.  **Result:** Verb (*spijten*)

Unlike English, which often uses "zero-derivation" (conversion) to turn nouns into verbs (e.g., *to google*, *to email*), Dutch frequently uses the suffix *-en* to perform this category change explicitly.

### 4. Syntactic and Semantic Context

While the morphology is straightforward, the syntax of *spijten* is unique and worth noting for a complete linguistic picture:

*   **Impersonal Construction:** *Spijten* is almost exclusively used as an **impersonal verb**.
    *   *Correct:* **Het** spijt **me**. (Literally: "It regrets me.")
    *   *Incorrect:* **Ik** spijt **het**. (Literally: "I regret it" — this is not standard Dutch).
*   **Psych-Verb:** Linguistically, this classifies *spijten* as a **psych-verb** (psychological verb) where the Experiencer (the person feeling the emotion) is the grammatical object (*me*), and the Stimulus (the cause of the emotion) is the grammatical subject (*het*).

### 5. Related Morphological Family

To understand the root `spijt` better, it helps to look at its morphological family:

*   **`spijt`** (Noun): Regret.
*   **`spijten`** (Verb): To regret.
*   **`spijtig`** (Adjective): Sorry / Regretful (Root `spijt` + Adjectival Suffix `-ig`).
*   **`spijtbetuiging`** (Noun): Apology (Root `spijt` + Verb `betuigen` + Nominal Suffix `-ing`).

### Summary
The word **`spijten`** consists of the lexical root **`spijt`** (regret) and the verbalizing/infinitive suffix **`-en`**. It represents a denominal verb formation where a noun denoting an emotion has been grammaticalized into a verb used primarily in an impersonal construction to express apology or remorse.
