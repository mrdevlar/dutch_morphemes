From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`inclusief`** is a loanword (leenwoord) derived from Latin. It is a complex word composed of three distinct morphemes.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented as follows:
> **in-** + **clus** + **-ief**

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### **A. Prefix: `in-`**
*   **Type:** Bound derivational prefix.
*   **Origin:** Latin *in-* (meaning "in," "into," or "within").
*   **Function:** It modifies the root to indicate direction or location. In this context, it suggests containment or being "inside" a boundary.
*   **Note:** While *in* exists as a standalone preposition in Dutch, here it functions strictly as a bound prefix attached to the Latin root.

#### **B. Root: `clus`**
*   **Type:** Bound root (Lexical morpheme).
*   **Origin:** Latin *claudere* (to close, shut) or the past participle stem *clusus*.
*   **Function:** This carries the core semantic meaning of the word. It relates to the act of closing or enclosing.
*   **Linguistic Note:** This is not a free morpheme in Dutch; you cannot use "clus" on its own. It is a "fossilized" root found in many loanwords (e.g., *exclusief*, *clausule*, *reclusie*).
*   **Semantic Shift:** Originally meaning "shut," in this context, it implies "shut within" or "contained."

#### **C. Suffix: `-ief`**
*   **Type:** Derivational suffix.
*   **Origin:** Latin *-ivus* (via French *-if*).
*   **Function:** It converts the verbal/root concept into an adjective. It indicates "tending to," "having the nature of," or "relating to."
*   **Dutch Adaptation:** In Dutch, this suffix is used to create adjectives from Latin/French stems (e.g., *actief*, *passief*, *negatief*).

### 3. Etymological Path
1.  **Latin:** *inclusivus* (from *includere* = to shut in).
2.  **French:** *inclusif*.
3.  **Dutch:** *inclusief*.

### 4. Semantic Synthesis
When you combine the morphemes, the literal construction is:
*   **in-** (inside) + **clus** (closing/shutting) + **-ief** (adjective marker).
*   **Literal Meaning:** "Having the nature of closing inside."
*   **Actual Meaning:** "Including," "containing," or "inclusive of."

There is a semantic shift from the physical act of "shutting" to the abstract concept of "including." This is the opposite of **`exclusief`** (*ex-* meaning "out" + *clus* + *-ief*), which means "shutting out" or "excluding."

### 5. Grammatical Usage in Dutch
While the morphology is Latin, the syntax in Dutch is specific:
*   **Adjective:** *Een **inclusieve** prijs* (An inclusive price). Note the inflection *-e* added to the stem.
*   **Preposition:** *De prijs is **inclusief** BTW* (The price is inclusive of VAT). In this usage, the word functions as a preposition, a common grammaticalization path for adjectives in Dutch.
