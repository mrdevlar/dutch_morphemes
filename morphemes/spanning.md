From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`spanning`** is a **deverbal noun** (a noun derived from a verb). It is composed of two primary morphological components: a verbal stem and a nominalizing suffix.

Here is the breakdown and explanation of the morphemes:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented as follows:

> **`spann`** + **`ing`**

*(Note: While the semantic root is `span`, the morphological stem used for derivation is `spann-`.)*

---

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Stem: `spann-`
*   **Type:** Verbal Stem (Bound Morpheme in this context).
*   **Source:** This stem comes from the Dutch infinitive verb **`spannen`** (to tense, to stretch, to span, to rig).
*   **Semantic Core:** The core meaning lies in the root **`span`**, which relates to stretching, tension, or tightening.
*   **The Double 'n':** In Dutch morphology, the verb *spannen* has a stem ending in a double consonant (`spann-`). When deriving a noun, this stem is retained.
    *   *Infinitive:* `spann` + `en`
    *   *Noun:* `spann` + `ing`
*   **Phonological Function:** The doubling of the 'n' is orthographic (spelling) and phonological. In Dutch, a short vowel (like the /a/ in *span*) in a closed syllable must be followed by a double consonant to indicate that the vowel remains short. If it were spelled *`spaning`*, the 'a' might be pronounced as a long vowel (like in *`na`*).

#### B. The Suffix: `-ing`
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix.
*   **Function:** **Nominalization**. It converts a verb (an action or process) into a noun (a state, result, or concept).
*   **Meaning:** It indicates the **process**, **state**, or **result** of the action described by the verb.
    *   *Spannen* = The act of stretching/tensing.
    *   *Spanning* = The state of being stretched/tensed (tension), or the result (voltage).
*   **Productivity:** This is a highly productive suffix in Dutch. It is used to create thousands of nouns from verbs (e.g., *werken* → *werking*, *lezen* → *lezing*, *koken* → *koking*).

---

### 3. Semantic Shift
The addition of the suffix `-ing` shifts the meaning from a dynamic action to a static concept:

| Morpheme | Part of Speech | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`spann`** (from *spannen*) | Verb | To stretch, to tense, to apply voltage. |
| **`-ing`** | Suffix | Turns the verb into a noun. |
| **`spanning`** | Noun | **Tension** (physical or emotional), **Voltage** (electrical), **Suspense**. |

### 4. Important Linguistic Distinction (Dutch vs. English)
It is crucial to distinguish this Dutch word from the English word "spanning."

*   **English:** "Spanning" is primarily the **present participle** or **gerund** of the verb "to span" (e.g., "The bridge is spanning the river").
*   **Dutch:** "Spanning" is **strictly a noun**.
    *   If a Dutch speaker wants to say "spanning" as a participle (e.g., "spanning a wire"), they use the suffix **`-end`**: *`spannend`*.
    *   Therefore, the Dutch morpheme **`-ing`** does not function as a participle marker; it functions exclusively as a noun-forming suffix.

### Summary
*   **Word:** `spanning`
*   **Structure:** `spann` (Verbal Stem) + `ing` (Nominalizing Suffix)
*   **Literal Construction:** "The state/process of tensing."
*   **Common Translation:** Tension, Voltage, Suspense.
