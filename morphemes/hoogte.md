From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`hoogte`** (meaning "height," "altitude," or "elevation") is a clear example of **derivational morphology**. It is formed by combining a lexical root with a derivational suffix to change the word's part of speech and meaning.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into two distinct morphemes:

> **hoog** + **-te**

| Morpheme | Type | Category | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **hoog** | Free | Adjective | Lexical Root (Semantic core) |
| **-te** | Bound | Suffix | Derivational (Nominalizer) |

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Root: `hoog`
*   **Type:** Free Morpheme (it can stand alone as an independent word).
*   **Part of Speech:** Adjective.
*   **Meaning:** "High" or "tall."
*   **Phonology:** In Standard Dutch, this is pronounced /ɦoːɣ/. It contains a long vowel /oː/ (spelled *oo*) and a voiced velar fricative /ɣ/ (spelled *g*).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *hōg*, which comes from Proto-Germanic **\*haukaz*. It is a cognate with the English word **high** and the German word **hoch**.
*   **Role:** This morpheme provides the core semantic content. It defines the quality being measured.

#### B. The Suffix: `-te`
*   **Type:** Bound Morpheme (it cannot stand alone).
*   **Part of Speech:** Derivational Suffix.
*   **Function:** **Nominalization**. It converts the adjective *hoog* into an abstract noun. Specifically, it denotes a measure, state, or quality associated with the root.
*   **Phonology:** Pronounced /tə/ (schwa).
*   **Etymology:** This suffix derives from the Proto-Germanic feminine noun suffix **\*-tō**.
*   **Cognate Connection:** This is the direct Dutch equivalent of the English suffix **-th** found in words like *height*, *width*, *depth*, and *length*.
    *   Dutch: *hoog* + *-te* = **hoogte**
    *   English: *high* + *-th* = **height**
*   **Productivity:** This suffix is highly productive in Dutch. It is commonly used to form abstract nouns from adjectives, particularly those describing dimensions or physical properties.
    *   *breed* (wide) + *-te* → **breedte** (width)
    *   *diep* (deep) + *-te* → **diepte** (depth)
    *   *snel* (fast) + *-te* → **snelheid** (Note: *snelheid* uses *-heid*, showing variation, but *-te* is common for physical dimensions).
    *   *lang* (long) + *-te* → **lengte** (length)

---

### 3. Morphophonological Notes
When analyzing the transition from root to derived word, linguists look at how sounds interact at the boundary.

*   **Vowel Length:** The root *hoog* contains a long vowel (/oː/). In the derived form *hoogte*, the vowel length is preserved. This contrasts with the word *lang* (long), which becomes *lengte* (length), where the vowel changes quality and shortens due to historical umlaut processes. *Hoogte* does not undergo this vowel mutation.
*   **Consonant Cluster:** The root ends in /ɣ/ and the suffix begins with /t/. In Dutch phonotactics, this cluster /ɣt/ is permissible, so no epenthetic (inserted) vowel is needed between them.

### 4. Summary
Linguistically, **`hoogte`** is a **derived noun**.
1.  It starts with the adjective **`hoog`** (high).
2.  It applies the nominalizing suffix **`-te`** (equivalent to English *-th*).
3.  The result is an abstract noun representing the **measure of the quality** of being high.

This structure highlights the shared Germanic heritage between Dutch and English, where the same Proto-Germanic roots and suffixes evolved into parallel words (*hoogte* / *height*).
