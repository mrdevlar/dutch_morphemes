From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`dekbed`** is a **compound noun**. It is formed by combining two free morphemes.

Here is the morphological breakdown and analysis:

### 1. Segmentation
The word can be segmented into two distinct morphemes:
> **`dek`** + **`bed`**

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### **Morpheme 1: `dek`**
*   **Type:** Free morpheme (can stand alone).
*   **Origin:** Derived from the verb **`dekken`** (to cover).
*   **Part of Speech:** In this compound, it functions as a **verbal stem** acting as a noun modifier.
*   **Meaning:** "Cover," "lid," or "to cover."
*   **Etymology:** From Middle Dutch *dek*, related to Old High German *decka* and Proto-Germanic *\*þaką* (which is also the ancestor of the English word *thatch*).
*   **Function:** It acts as the **modifier** (or determiner) of the compound. It specifies the function or nature of the head noun.

#### **Morpheme 2: `bed`**
*   **Type:** Free morpheme (can stand alone).
*   **Origin:** From Old Dutch *bed*.
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun.
*   **Meaning:** "Bed."
*   **Etymology:** From Proto-Germanic *\*badją*, related to English *bed* and German *Bett*.
*   **Function:** It acts as the **head** of the compound. In Dutch morphology, the head of a compound usually appears at the end and determines the grammatical category (noun) and the general semantic category of the whole word.

### 3. Compound Structure & Semantics

*   **Compound Type:** **Endocentric Compound**.
    *   This means the meaning of the whole word is a specific subtype of the head. A *dekbed* is a type of *bedding* (associated with a *bed*).
*   **Literal Translation:** "Cover-bed" or "Bed-cover."
*   **Semantic Shift:** While the literal structure suggests a bed that covers something, the actual meaning has narrowed through usage. It refers specifically to the **duvet** or **comforter** (the soft, filled covering placed *on top* of a person in a bed), rather than the bed frame or mattress itself.
*   **Stress Pattern:** In Dutch compounds, primary stress typically falls on the first element.
    *   Pronunciation: **`DÉK`**bed (not dek`BED`).

### 4. Inflectional Morphology
While `dekbed` itself contains no inflectional morphemes in its singular form, it interacts with them when pluralized.

*   **Singular:** `dekbed`
*   **Plural:** `dekbed` + **`den`** (or `en`) $\rightarrow$ **`dekbedden`**
    *   The plural suffix **-en** is added to the end of the entire compound, not just the head.
    *   Because the head `bed` ends in a consonant cluster, a linking consonant **-d-** is often inserted for phonological ease (epenthesis), resulting in *bedden*.

**Linguistic Takeaway:**
`Dekbed` is a transparent compound where the meaning is largely compositional (Cover + Bed = Duvet). It illustrates the Germanic tendency to create new vocabulary by combining existing roots rather than borrowing from Latin or French (unlike the English word *duvet*, which is a loanword from French).
