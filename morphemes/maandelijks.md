From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`maandelijks`** (meaning "monthly") can be analyzed through **morphology**, the study of the internal structure of words and the rules by which they are formed.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes, their functions, and the derivation process.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **`maand`** + **`-elijk`** + **`-s`**

---

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. Root Morpheme: `maand`
*   **Type:** Free morpheme (Root).
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun.
*   **Meaning:** "Month".
*   **Function:** This is the lexical base of the word. It carries the core semantic meaning.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *maent*, Old Dutch *mānōt*, and Proto-Germanic *\*mēnōþs*. It is a cognate with the English word "month."
*   **Phonology:** Ends in a voiced alveolar stop /d/.

#### B. Derivational Suffix: `-elijk`
*   **Type:** Bound morpheme (Derivational Suffix).
*   **Part of Speech:** Adjectivalizer (Noun $\rightarrow$ Adjective).
*   **Meaning:** "Pertaining to," "characterized by," or "-ly" (in English).
*   **Function:** This suffix changes the grammatical category of the root from a noun to an adjective. It creates the concept of "having the quality of a month" or "occurring every month."
*   **Linguistic Note (The Epenthetic -e-):**
    *   Historically and synchronically, the core suffix is often considered **`-lijk`** (cognate with English *-ly* and German *-lich*).
    *   However, when attached to a stem ending in a consonant (like *maand*), an epenthetic vowel **`-e-`** is inserted to break up the consonant cluster (*d* + *l* is difficult to pronounce).
    *   Therefore, while the functional unit is *-lijk*, in modern Dutch morphology, **`-elijk`** is often treated as the allomorph used after consonant-final stems.
*   **Intermediate Form:** *maand* + *elijk* = **`maandelijk`** (Adjective: "monthly").

#### C. Inflectional Suffix: `-s`
*   **Type:** Bound morpheme (Inflectional Suffix).
*   **Part of Speech:** Adverbializer / Adjectival Inflection.
*   **Meaning:** Marks the word as an adverb or a neuter adjective.
*   **Function:**
    1.  **Adverbial:** In Dutch, many adjectives derived with *-lijk* take an *-s* to function as an adverb (e.g., *maandelijks* = "monthly" as in "I pay monthly").
    2.  **Neuter Adjective:** It is also the uninflected form used when modifying a neuter noun in the indefinite singular (e.g., *een maandelijks abonnement* = "a monthly subscription").
*   **Contrast:** If the word modifies a common gender noun or is plural, the *-s* is dropped and replaced by *-e* (e.g., *de maandelijkse betaling* = "the monthly payment").

---

### 3. Summary of Derivation Process

The word formation follows a step-by-step process:

1.  **Base:** `maand` (Noun: Month)
2.  **Derivation:** `maand` + `-elijk` $\rightarrow$ `maandelijk` (Adjective: Monthly)
3.  **Inflection:** `maandelijk` + `-s` $\rightarrow$ `maandelijks` (Adverb / Neuter Adjective)

### 4. Comparison with English
The structure is remarkably similar to English, highlighting the shared Germanic heritage:

| Dutch Morpheme | English Equivalent | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`maand`** | **`month`** | month |
| **`-elijk`** | **`-ly`** | month**ly** |
| **`-s`** | *(None / Adverbial)* | (monthly) |

*Note: In English, "monthly" functions as both adjective and adverb without an extra suffix. In Dutch, the distinction is often marked by the final *-s* for the adverbial form.*

### 5. Syntactic Usage Examples
To understand the morphemes in context:

*   **Adverbial (with `-s`):**
    *   *Ik betaal **maandelijks**.* (I pay **monthly**.)
    *   Here, `-s` marks the adverbial function.
*   **Adjectival, Neuter (with `-s`):**
    *   *Dit is een **maandelijks** ritme.* (This is a **monthly** rhythm.)
    *   Here, `-s` is the default ending for a neuter noun (*ritme*).
*   **Adjectival, Common/Plural (without `-s`, with `-e`):**
    *   *De **maandelijkse** kosten.* (The **monthly** costs.)
    *   Here, the `-s` is replaced by `-e` due to Dutch adjective inflection rules (common gender/plural).

### Conclusion
The word **`maandelijks`** is a complex word formed by a noun root, a derivational suffix that creates an adjective, and an inflectional suffix that marks the adverbial or neuter adjectival form. It is a classic example of Dutch **agglutinative** tendencies within a Germanic inflectional framework.
