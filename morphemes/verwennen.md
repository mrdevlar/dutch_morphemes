From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch verb **`verwennen`** (meaning "to spoil" or "to pamper") can be segmented into three distinct morphemes.

Here is the breakdown:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
**`ver-`** + **`wenn-`** + **`-en`**

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Prefix: `ver-`
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix.
*   **Function:** In Dutch morphology, the prefix *ver-* is highly productive and versatile. It often indicates a change of state, a completion of an action, or a transformation.
*   **Semantic Nuance in this Context:** In the specific case of *verwennen*, *ver-* carries a nuance of **"excess"** or **"going too far."** It modifies the root to imply that the action is being done to a degree that creates a negative or dependent outcome.
    *   *Comparison:* It functions similarly to the English prefix *over-* (as in *overindulge*) or *mis-* (as in *mislead*), though *ver-* is more neutral in other contexts (e.g., *veranderen* = to change).

#### B. The Root: `wenn-`
*   **Type:** Lexical Root (Stem).
*   **Base Verb:** *wennen* (to get used to / to accustom).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *wennian*, related to Old High German *wennan*. It is cognate with the English word *win* (in the sense of gaining a habit) and the German *gewöhnen*.
*   **Core Meaning:** The fundamental meaning of this root is **habituation**. It refers to the process of becoming accustomed to something.
*   **Related Words:**
    *   *Gewoonte* (Habit/Custom) – shares the same Indo-European root regarding "becoming."
    *   *Gewend* (Accustomed) – the past participle of *wennen*.

#### C. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix.
*   **Function:** This is the standard marker for the **infinitive** form of weak (regular) verbs in Dutch.
*   **Grammar:** It indicates that the word is a verb in its base form (e.g., "to spoil"). In conjugated forms, this changes (e.g., *ik verwen* [I spoil], *hij verwent* [he spoils]).

---

### 3. Semantic Shift (How the meaning evolved)

The linguistic interest in *verwennen* lies in how the combination of the prefix and root creates a specific meaning that differs from the sum of its parts.

1.  **`wennen`** = To accustom someone to something (Neutral).
    *   *Example:* "Ik moet mij **wennen** aan het nieuwe werk." (I need to get used to the new job.)
2.  **`ver-`** + **`wennen`** = To accustom someone *excessively*.
    *   When you *verwennen* someone (usually a child or pet), you are allowing them to get used to comfort, luxury, or lack of discipline to such a degree that it hinders their development or creates entitlement.
    *   Therefore, the semantic shift moves from **"habituation"** $\rightarrow$ **"indulgent habituation"** $\rightarrow$ **"spoiling."**

### 4. Summary Table

| Morpheme | Type | Meaning/Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **ver-** | Derivational Prefix | Indicates excess, change of state, or intensification. |
| **wenn-** | Root | To accustom, to habituate. |
| **-en** | Inflectional Suffix | Marks the infinitive verb form. |
| **verwennen** | **Whole Word** | **To spoil / To pamper.** |

### 5. Cross-Linguistic Comparison
*   **German:** *verwöhnen* (Almost identical structure and meaning).
*   **English:** There is no direct morphological equivalent. English uses the Germanic root *win* (as in *winning* a habit) but the specific construction *verwennen* does not exist. The closest semantic equivalent is **"to spoil"** or **"to pamper."**
*   **French:** *gâter* (to spoil).

In summary, *verwennen* is a classic example of Dutch **prefixation**, where a derivational prefix (*ver-*) alters the valency and semantic direction of a base verb (*wennen*) to create a new lexical item with a specific cultural connotation regarding child-rearing and indulgence.
