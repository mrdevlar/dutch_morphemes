From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`tekening`** (meaning "drawing" or "sketch") is a **derived noun**. It is formed through a process of **nominalization**, where a verb is converted into a noun.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Visual Breakdown
The word consists of two distinct morphemes:

> **teken** + **ing**

---

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### **Morpheme 1: `teken` (Root / Stem)**
*   **Type:** Free morpheme (can stand alone as a word).
*   **Category:** Verbal Stem.
*   **Source:** This is the stem of the Dutch verb **`tekenen`** (to draw).
    *   *Infinitive:* `teken` + `en` = `tekenen`
    *   *Stem:* `teken`
*   **Meaning:** The core semantic meaning relates to marking, signing, or drawing.
*   **Etymology:** It stems from the Proto-Germanic root **\*taikną**, meaning "sign" or "token."
    *   *Cognates:* English **token**, German **Zeichen**, Old English **tacen**.
*   **Function:** It provides the lexical meaning (the "what") of the word.

#### **Morpheme 2: `-ing` (Suffix)**
*   **Type:** Bound morpheme (cannot stand alone).
*   **Category:** Derivational Suffix.
*   **Function:** **Nominalizer**. It changes the part of speech from a verb (action) to a noun (entity).
*   **Semantic Role:** In Dutch, the suffix *-ing* typically indicates the **result** or **product** of the action denoted by the verb, though it can sometimes refer to the process itself.
    *   In *tekening*, it refers to the **result** (the physical drawing on paper).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from the Proto-Germanic suffix **\*-ingō**.
    *   *Cognates:* German **-ung** (e.g., *Zeichnung*), English **-ing** (e.g., *building*, *painting* — though English *-ing* is more often a gerund, the Dutch *-ing* is more often a concrete noun).

---

### 3. Morphological Process: Derivation
The formation of this word is an example of **derivational morphology**.

1.  **Base:** The verb *tekenen* (to draw).
2.  **Stem Extraction:** Remove the infinitive ending *-en* to get the stem *teken*.
3.  **Suffixation:** Add the nominalizing suffix *-ing*.
4.  **Result:** *tekening* (a drawing).

**Note on Spelling:**
Unlike some Dutch derivations, there is no vowel shortening or consonant doubling here. The stem *teken* retains its long vowel sound, and the suffix attaches directly.

---

### 4. Grammatical Context
Once derived, the word functions as a **Common Gender Noun** (*de-woord*).

*   **Singular:** *de tekening* (the drawing)
*   **Plural:** *de tekeningen* (the drawings)
    *   *Note:* The plural is formed by adding the standard Dutch plural suffix **-en** to the derived noun (*tekening* + *en*).
