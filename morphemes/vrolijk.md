From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`vrolijk`** (meaning "cheerful," "merry," or "jolly") is a **derived adjective**. It consists of two distinct morphemes: a bound root and a derivational suffix.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Morphological Structure

| Morpheme | Form | Type | Meaning/Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Root** | `vrool-` | Bound Root | "Joy," "happiness," "mirth" |
| **Suffix** | `-lijk` | Derivational | "Having the nature of," "-like" |
| **Total** | `vrolijk` | Adjective | "Having the nature of joy" |

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis

#### A. The Root: `vrool-`
*   **Status:** **Bound Root.** In modern Dutch, this root cannot stand alone as an independent word. You cannot say *"Ik ben vrool"* to mean "I am joyful."
*   **Etymology:** This root is historically related to the Middle Dutch word *vrool* (joy/happiness) and is cognate with the Old High German *froh* (happy). It is also etymologically linked to the modern Dutch noun **`vreugde`** (joy).
*   **Phonology:** Historically, the root contained a long vowel sound. However, in the derived form *vrolijk*, the vowel is shortened (from a long /oː/ to a short /ɔ/). This is a common phonological process in Dutch morphology where vowel length changes during derivation.
*   **Orthography:** The spelling reflects a simplification. The root ends in an *l* (`vrool`), and the suffix begins with an *l* (`-lijk`). When combined, the double *l* is simplified to a single *l* in modern spelling (*vroollijk* → *vrolijk*).

#### B. The Suffix: `-lijk`
*   **Status:** **Free/Productive Suffix.** This is one of the most common and productive suffixes in the Dutch language.
*   **Function:** It is a **derivational suffix** that turns a noun or a root into an adjective.
*   **Meaning:** It conveys the meaning "characterized by," "having the quality of," or "resembling."
*   **Cognates:**
    *   **English:** *-ly* (e.g., *friend* → *friend**ly***).
    *   **German:** *-lich* (e.g., *fröh**lich***).
    *   **Frisian:** *-lik*.
*   **Examples:**
    *   *vriend* (friend) + *-lijk* → *vriendelijk* (friendly).
    *   *mooi* (beautiful) + *-lijk* → *mooilijk* (rare, but follows the pattern).
    *   *mens* (human) + *-lijk* → *menselijk* (human/human-like).

---

### 3. Semantic Composition
The meaning of the word is compositional, meaning it is derived from the sum of its parts:

1.  **`vrool-`** (Joy/Happiness)
2.  **`-lijk`** (Having the nature of)
3.  **`vrolijk`** = "Having the nature of joy" → **Cheerful.**

### 4. Related Derivatives
Because the suffix `-lijk` is productive, it allows for further derivation, creating a "word family":

*   **`vrolijkheid`** (Noun): *vrolijk* + *-heid* (suffix for abstract nouns, like English *-hood* or *-ness*). Meaning: "Cheerfulness."
*   **`vervrolijken`** (Verb): *ver-* (prefix indicating change of state) + *vrolijk* + *-en* (verb ending). Meaning: "To cheer up" or "to make cheerful."

### 5. Linguistic Note: Synchronic vs. Diachronic
*   **Diachronic (Historical):** A linguist analyzing the history of the word sees two clear morphemes: *vrool* + *lijk*.
*   **Synchronic (Modern):** For a contemporary Dutch speaker, the root *vrool-* is opaque (unrecognizable). Therefore, many speakers might subconsciously treat *vrolijk* as a single, unanalyzable morpheme (a "fossilized" word), even though the suffix *-lijk* is still recognized in other words (like *vriendelijk*). However, morphological analysis still classifies it as a complex word.
