From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`tegenvallen`** is a compound verb consisting of three distinct morphemes. It is classified as a **separable verb** (*scheidbaar werkwoord*).

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Morphemic Segmentation
The word can be segmented into the following morphemes:

> **tegen** + **val** + **-en**

| Morpheme | Type | Status | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **tegen** | Prefix / Particle | Free | Adds semantic opposition (against). |
| **val** | Root / Stem | Free | Carries the core lexical meaning (fall). |
| **-en** | Suffix | Bound | Grammatical marker (Infinitive). |

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. `tegen` (The Prefix/Particle)
*   **Category:** In this context, `tegen` functions as a **separable verbal prefix**. While it originates as a preposition (meaning "against"), when attached to a verb, it acts as a particle that modifies the verb's meaning.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *tegen*, Old Dutch *thegana*. It is cognate with the English preposition *against* (though English *against* evolved from *on-gegn* + *tō*, while Dutch *tegen* comes from the same Germanic root *ga-* + *anti-* conceptually).
*   **Semantic Contribution:** It introduces a sense of **opposition**, **counter-action**, or **resistance**.
*   **Syntactic Behavior:** Because it is a separable prefix, it detaches from the root in main clauses.
    *   *Infinitive:* `tegenvallen`
    *   *Main Clause:* "Het **viel** erg **tegen**." (It fell very against.)

#### B. `val` (The Root/Stem)
*   **Category:** Lexical Root.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Proto-Germanic *\*fallaną*. It is a cognate with the English verb *to fall* and the German *fallen*.
*   **Phonology/Orthography:** The root vowel is short (/ɑ/). In Dutch orthography, a short vowel in an open syllable must be followed by a double consonant to maintain the short sound. Hence, the root is spelled `val`, but the infinitive is `val` + `len` = `vallen`.
*   **Semantic Contribution:** The core action of descending, dropping, or collapsing.
*   **Verb Class:** `Vallen` is a **strong verb** (Class 3).
    *   Present: *val*
    *   Past: *viel*
    *   Past Participle: *gevallen*

#### C. `-en` (The Suffix)
*   **Category:** Inflectional Suffix.
*   **Function:** This is the standard marker for the **infinitive** form of Dutch verbs. It is a bound morpheme (it cannot stand alone).
*   **Note:** In the past tense or participle forms, this suffix changes or disappears (e.g., *viel*, *gevallen*), but in the dictionary form (`tegenvallen`), `-en` is present.

---

### 3. Semantic Composition (How the meaning is built)

The meaning of `tegenvallen` is not simply the sum of its parts ("to fall against"), but rather an idiomatic compound:

1.  **Literal Image:** Imagine an object that is expected to land in a specific spot (success/expectation), but instead, it falls *against* something or falls short of the mark.
2.  **Idiomatic Meaning:** To disappoint; to fail to meet expectations.
    *   *Example:* "De film viel tegen." (The movie disappointed / fell short.)

### 4. Linguistic Classification Summary

*   **Word Class:** Verb (*Werkwoord*).
*   **Morphological Type:** **Compound Verb** (*Samenstelling*). Specifically, a Verb + Preposition/Adverb compound.
*   **Syntactic Type:** **Separable Verb** (*Scheidbaar werkwoord*).
    *   In a subordinate clause, the prefix remains attached: *"...omdat het teleurstellend **tegenviel**."*
    *   In a main clause, the prefix separates: *"Het **viel** teleurstellend **tegen**."*
*   **Transitivity:** Intransitive (it does not take a direct object).

### 5. Cognate Comparison
To understand the Germanic roots, compare with English and German:

*   **Dutch:** `tegen` (against) + `vallen` (fall) = `tegenvallen` (disappoint).
*   **German:** `gegen` (against) + `fallen` (fall) = `enttäuschen` (disappoint - *Note: German uses a different verb for this idiom, though `gegenfallen` exists in dialects/archaic usage*).
*   **English:** `against` + `fall` = No direct equivalent idiom, though "to fall short" is semantically similar.

### Summary
**`Tegenvallen`** is a morphologically transparent compound where the prefix **`tegen-`** modifies the strong verb root **`val-`** (fall), creating a new semantic concept of "failing to meet expectations," marked grammatically as an infinitive by the suffix **`-en`**.
