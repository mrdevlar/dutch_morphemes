From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`wandeling`** (meaning "a walk," "a stroll," or "a hike") can be analyzed through **morphology**, the study of the internal structure of words.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes, their functions, and the morphological process involved.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word consists of two distinct morphemes:

> **`wandel-`** + **`-ing`**

### 2. Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Root/Stem: `wandel-`
*   **Type:** Bound Root / Verbal Stem.
*   **Source:** This morpheme is derived from the infinitive verb **`wandelen`** (to walk).
*   **Function:** It carries the core lexical meaning of the word (the concept of walking).
*   **Morphological Note:** In Dutch, to create a stem from a regular verb ending in *-en*, you remove the *-en*.
    *   *Infinitive:* `wandelen`
    *   *Stem:* `wandel-`
*   **Status:** While `wandel` contains the meaning, it cannot stand alone as a word in modern Dutch (you cannot say "Ik ga wandel"). Therefore, in this specific context, it functions as a **bound root** that requires affixation to become a complete word.

#### B. The Suffix: `-ing`
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix.
*   **Function:** **Nominalization**. This suffix changes the grammatical category of the word from a **Verb** to a **Noun**.
*   **Semantic Role:** It indicates the **action** or the **event** associated with the root. It transforms the activity of "walking" into the concept of "a walk."
*   **Cognates:** This is a Germanic suffix cognate with:
    *   English: *-ing* (e.g., *walk* $\rightarrow$ *walking*)
    *   German: *-ung* (e.g., *wandeln* $\rightarrow$ *Wandlung* / *Wanderung*)
    *   Frisian: *-ing*

### 3. The Morphological Process
The formation of `wandeling` is an example of **Derivation**.

*   **Input:** Verb (`wandelen`)
*   **Process:** Remove infinitive ending (`-en`) + Add nominalizing suffix (`-ing`).
*   **Output:** Noun (`wandeling`)
*   **Change in Category:** Verb $\rightarrow$ Noun.

### 4. Phonological and Orthographic Notes
*   **Stress:** In Dutch, the primary stress typically falls on the first syllable of the root.
    *   Pronunciation: **`WAN`**-de-ling (/ˈwɑn.də.lɪŋ/)
*   **Spelling:** There is no vowel change (umlaut) or consonant doubling required when adding `-ing` to the stem `wandel-`. The spelling remains transparent.
*   **Pluralization:** Because `-ing` creates a noun, the word can be pluralized using the standard Dutch plural suffix `-en`.
    *   Singular: `wandeling`
    *   Plural: `wandeling` + `en` = `wandelingen`

### Linguistic Context
In Dutch linguistics, words ending in *-ing* are often referred to as **`-ing`-nouns**. They are highly productive, meaning speakers can frequently create new nouns from verbs using this suffix (e.g., *lezen* $\rightarrow$ *lezing* [reading/lecture], *werken* $\rightarrow$ *werking* [functioning/effect]). `Wandeling` is a standard, lexicalized example of this pattern.
