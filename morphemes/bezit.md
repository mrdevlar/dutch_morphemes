From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`bezit`** (meaning "possession," "property," or "assets") is a deverbal noun. It is formed through a process of derivation involving a prefix and a verbal root.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into two primary morphemes:

> **`be-`** + **`zit`**

*   **`be-`**: A derivational prefix.
*   **`zit`**: The verbal root (stem).

*(Note: While the word ends in a `t`, this `t` is phonologically part of the root `zit`, not a separate nominalizing suffix in this specific case. The noun form is identical to the stem of the related verb.)*

---

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Prefix: `be-`
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix.
*   **Function:** In Dutch (and Germanic languages generally), the prefix **`be-`** often serves to **transitivize** an intransitive verb.
    *   The base verb *zitten* ("to sit") is intransitive (you sit *somewhere*, but you don't "sit" an object).
    *   By adding **`be-`**, the verb becomes *bezitten* ("to possess" or "to occupy"). It implies acting upon an object or covering/enclosing something.
*   **Semantic Contribution:** It adds a sense of "affecting the object" or "bringing something into a state." In this context, it shifts the meaning from the physical act of sitting to the state of holding or controlling something.

#### B. The Root: `zit`
*   **Source:** The verb **`zitten`** (to sit).
*   **Etymology:** This root traces back to the Proto-Germanic **`*sitjaną`** and ultimately the Proto-Indo-European root **`*sed-`** (to sit).
*   **Cognates:**
    *   English: *sit*, *seat*, *settle*.
    *   German: *sitzen*, *Sitz*.
*   **Semantic Shift (Metaphor):** This is the most interesting linguistic aspect of the word.
    *   **Literal:** To sit on something.
    *   **Metaphorical:** To have something under your physical control or "on your lap."
    *   **Abstract:** To own or possess.
    *   This is a common semantic evolution in Germanic languages (similar to the English phrase "to sit on a property" or the legal concept of "seisin," related to sitting).

---

### 3. Grammatical Context & Related Forms

To fully understand the morphology, it helps to look at the word family:

1.  **`zitten`** (Verb): To sit. (Intransitive)
2.  **`bezitten`** (Verb): To possess. (Transitive, derived via `be-`)
3.  **`bezit`** (Noun): Possession/Property. (Deverbal noun, derived from the stem of *bezitten*).
4.  **`bezitting`** (Noun): An asset/possession (concrete).
    *   *Morphology:* `be-` + `zit` + `-ing` (suffix).
    *   *Difference:* While *bezit* is the abstract concept of possession, *bezitting* refers to the specific items owned.

### 4. Etymological History
*   **Old Dutch:** *bisitan*
*   **Middle Dutch:** *bezetten* (verb), *bezet* (noun)
*   **Modern Dutch:** *bezitten* / *bezit*

Over time, the vowel shifted (umlaut) and the consonant cluster simplified, but the structure remained consistent. The semantic shift from "sitting" to "owning" was already present in Old High German (*bisitan* meant "to dwell in" or "to possess").

### Conclusion
Linguistically, **`bezit`** is a classic example of **prefixation** combined with **semantic shift**. It takes the physical action of sitting (`zit`), makes it transitive and controlling via the prefix (`be-`), and nominalizes the result to describe the state of ownership. It illustrates how abstract legal and economic concepts (possession) are often built upon concrete physical metaphors (sitting/occupying).
