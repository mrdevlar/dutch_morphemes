From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`biologisch`** (meaning "biological" or "organic") is a complex word formed through derivation. It can be segmented into three distinct morphemes based on its etymological roots and Dutch morphological rules.

Here is the breakdown and explanation of the morphemes.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation

The word can be segmented as follows:

> **`bio`** + **`log`** + **`isch`**

Alternatively, from a synchronic (current usage) perspective, it is often viewed as a derivation from the noun *biologie*:

> **`biolog-`** (Stem) + **`-isch`** (Suffix)

Below is the detailed analysis of the three constituent morphemes.

---

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. `bio-` (Prefix / Combining Form)
*   **Type:** Bound Root (Combining Form)
*   **Origin:** Ancient Greek *bios* (βίος), meaning "life."
*   **Function:** It acts as a prefix indicating the subject matter relates to living organisms.
*   **Status in Dutch:** It is a **bound morpheme**, meaning it cannot stand alone as an independent word in modern Dutch (you cannot say *"Ik ga bio"*). It must attach to another root (e.g., *biologie*, *biotoop*, *biomassa*).
*   **Meaning:** Life, living.

#### B. `-log-` (Root / Combining Form)
*   **Type:** Bound Root
*   **Origin:** Ancient Greek *logos* (λόγος), meaning "word," "reason," "study," or "discourse."
*   **Function:** In scientific terminology, this root denotes a field of study or science.
*   **Status in Dutch:** Like *bio-*, this is a **bound morpheme**. It does not exist as a standalone word in Dutch. It appears in words like *biologie* (biology), *psychologie* (psychology), *geologie* (geology).
*   **Meaning:** Study of, science of, or discourse about.
*   **Note on Spelling:** In the word *biologisch*, the *g* is the final consonant of this root.

#### C. `-isch` (Derivational Suffix)
*   **Type:** Adjectival Suffix
*   **Origin:** Greek *-ikos* → Latin *-icus* → Germanic *-isch*.
*   **Function:** This is a productive suffix in Dutch used to turn nouns (often of Greek or Latin origin) into adjectives.
*   **Meaning:** "Pertaining to," "related to," or "characteristic of."
*   **Pronunciation:** In standard Dutch, the *-isch* ending is pronounced /ɪs/ (rhyming with *is*). The *sch* is not pronounced as a "sh" sound (/ʃ/) in this context, but as /s/.
*   **Orthography:** When attaching to a stem ending in a vowel (like *biologie*), the final *-ie* is dropped before adding *-isch*.
    *   *Biologie* (Noun) → Stem: *biolog-* → *biologisch* (Adjective).

---

### 3. Derivational Process (Word Formation)

To understand how these morphemes fit together, we must look at the word formation process:

1.  **Base Noun:** The starting point is the noun **`biologie`** (Biology).
    *   Structure: `bio` + `log` + `ie` (nominalizing suffix).
2.  **Stem Extraction:** To form the adjective, the nominal suffix *-ie* is removed to reveal the stem **`biolog-`**.
3.  **Adjectival Suffixation:** The adjectival suffix **`-isch`** is attached to the stem.
4.  **Result:** **`biologisch`**.

**Linguistic Note:** This is an example of **stem modification**. The word does not simply add a suffix to the full noun (*biologie* + *isch* would be incorrect); it modifies the stem (*biolog-* + *isch*).

---

### 4. Semantic Nuance (Meaning)

While the morphemes strictly define "pertaining to the study of life," the usage of *biologisch* in Dutch has two distinct meanings depending on context:

1.  **Scientific:** Relating to the science of biology.
    *   *Example:* *Een biologisch proces* (A biological process).
2.  **Agricultural/Commercial:** Equivalent to the English "organic" (regarding food/farming).
    *   *Example:* *Biologische appels* (Organic apples).
    *   *Note:* In English, "biological" rarely means "organic" in a farming context, but in Dutch, *biologisch* is the standard term for organic produce.

### 5. Cognates

Because the morphemes are of Greek origin and entered Dutch via Latin and French, they are shared across many Indo-European languages:

*   **English:** *Biological* (`bio` + `log` + `ical`).
*   **German:** *Biologisch* (`bio` + `log` + `isch`).
*   **French:** *Biologique* (`bio` + `log` + `ique`).
*   **Spanish:** *Biológico* (`bio` + `log` + `ico`).
