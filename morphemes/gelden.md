From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`gelden`** can be segmented into two distinct morphemes. However, because Dutch is a language with significant homography (words spelled the same but with different meanings/grammatical functions), the analysis depends slightly on whether the word is functioning as a **verb** or a **noun**.

In modern usage, it is most frequently encountered as a **verb**. Below is the morphological breakdown for the verb, followed by a note on the noun.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word is segmented as follows:

> **`geld`** + **`-en`**

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Root: `geld`
*   **Type:** Free Morpheme (Lexical Root)
*   **Function:** This is the **stem** of the word. It carries the core semantic meaning (lexical content).
*   **Etymology:** This root comes from Proto-Germanic **`*geldaną`**, meaning "to pay" or "to compensate."
    *   **Cognates:** It is related to the German verb *gelten* (to be valid) and the English noun *yield* (originally meaning payment or compensation) and the Old English *gieldan* (to pay).
*   **Semantic Shift:** In modern Dutch, the meaning has shifted from the original "to pay" to "to be valid," "to apply," or "to cost."
    *   *Example:* "Dit **geldt** voor iedereen" (This **applies** to everyone).
    *   *Example:* "Hoeveel **geldt** dit?" (How much **does this cost**? / How much is this **worth**?)
*   **Relationship to Noun:** Interestingly, the Dutch noun for money, **`geld`**, is derived from this same verbal root. Historically, "money" was conceptualized as "that which is paid."

#### B. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Type:** Bound Morpheme (Inflectional Suffix)
*   **Function:** This suffix marks the grammatical form of the word.
*   **Grammatical Category:**
    1.  **As a Verb (Infinitive):** In Dutch, the suffix `-en` is the standard marker for the **infinitive** form of a verb (e.g., *lopen*, *werken*, *gelden*). It indicates the action in its un-conjugated state.
    2.  **As a Noun (Plural):** If the word is interpreted as a noun, `-en` acts as a **plural suffix**. While the noun *geld* (money) is usually uncountable, the plural form *gelden* exists to mean "funds," "sums of money," or "monies" (e.g., *publieke gelden* - public funds).
*   **Phonology:** In rapid speech, the final `-n` is often dropped (apocope), resulting in a pronunciation closer to `/ˈɣɛldə/`, though the spelling retains the `-n`.

### 4. Linguistic Note on Homography
It is important to note that **`gelden`** is a **homograph**.
1.  **Verb:** "Ik hoop dat dit **geldt**." (I hope this **applies**.) -> Root `geld` + Infinitive `en`.
2.  **Noun:** "De **gelden** zijn ontvangen." (The **funds** have been received.) -> Root `geld` + Plural `en`.

While the segmentation is identical (`geld` + `en`), the syntactic category changes the grammatical function of the suffix. In a linguistic corpus, context determines whether the suffix is analyzed as an **infinitive marker** or a **plural marker**.
