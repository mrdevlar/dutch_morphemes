From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`natuurlijk`** is a classic example of **derivational morphology**, where a base word is modified by a suffix to create a new word with a related but distinct meaning and grammatical category.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into two distinct morphemes:

> **`natuur`** + **`-lijk`**

---

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Root: `natuur`
*   **Type:** Free Morpheme (Lexical Root)
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun (*de-noun*)
*   **Meaning:** "Nature" (the physical world, inherent quality, or environment).
*   **Etymology:** This is a **loanword** from Latin. It entered the Dutch language via Old French (*nature*) and Middle Dutch (*natuer*).
    *   *Latin:* *natura* (birth, constitution, nature).
*   **Phonology:** Pronounced /naːtyːr/. The *uu* represents a long close front rounded vowel /yː/.

#### B. The Suffix: `-lijk`
*   **Type:** Bound Morpheme (Derivational Suffix)
*   **Function:** Derives an **Adjective** (and subsequently an Adverb) from a Noun.
*   **Meaning:** "Like," "characterized by," or "having the quality of."
*   **Etymology:** This is a **native Germanic** element. It is cognate with the English suffix *-ly* and the German suffix *-lich*.
    *   *Proto-Germanic:* *\*-līkaz* (originally related to the word for "body" or "form," similar to Dutch *lichaam*). Over time, the meaning shifted from "having the body of" to "having the form/quality of."
*   **Phonology:** Pronounced /lɛi̯k/. The *ij* is a diphthong /ɛi/.

---

### 3. Semantic Composition
When combined, the morphemes create a literal meaning that has undergone semantic broadening:

1.  **Literal Meaning:** "Nature-like" or "having the quality of nature."
    *   *Example:* *natuurlijk materiaal* (natural material).
2.  **Adverbial Meaning:** "In a natural manner."
    *   *Example:* *Hij reageerde natuurlijk.* (He reacted naturally.)
3.  **Pragmatic/Idiomatic Meaning:** "Of course" or "Obviously."
    *   *Example:* *Natuurlijk kom ik!* (Of course I'm coming!)
    *   *Linguistic Note:* This usage relies on the implication that the action follows "naturally" from the situation, making it an obvious truth.

---

### 4. Cross-Linguistic Comparison (Cognates)
Because Dutch and English are both West Germanic languages, the structure of this word is nearly identical to English, making it a transparent cognate:

| Dutch | English | Morpheme Equivalent |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **natuur** | **nature** | Latin root *natura* |
| **-lijk** | **-ly** | Germanic suffix *-līkaz* |
| **natuurlijk** | **natural / naturally** | Resulting Adjective/Adverb |

*Note: In English, the suffix *-al* is often used to turn the noun into an adjective (*nature* $\to$ *natural*), whereas Dutch uses the Germanic *-lijk* directly on the noun stem.*

### 5. Inflectional Note
While the word provided is **`natuurlijk`**, in Dutch grammar, adjectives often take an inflectional ending when used attributively (before a noun).
*   **Base Form:** *natuurlijk* (e.g., *het is natuurlijk* - it is natural).
*   **Inflected Form:** *natuurlijk**e*** (e.g., *een natuurlijke man* - a natural man).
*   **Morphological Structure:** `natuur` + `-lijk` + `-e` (Inflectional suffix for gender/number agreement).

### Summary
**`Natuurlijk`** is a compound of a **Romance loan root** (*natuur*) and a **Germanic derivational suffix** (*-lijk*). It functions primarily as an adjective or adverb, meaning "natural" or "naturally," but frequently serves as a discourse marker meaning "of course."
