From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`hoofdrol`** is a **compound noun** (*samenstelling*). It consists of two distinct morphemes joined together to create a new lexical unit.

Here is the breakdown and explanation of the morphemes:

### 1. Segmentation
The word can be segmented as follows:
> **hoofd** + **rol**

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### Morpheme 1: `hoofd`
*   **Category:** Noun (Free Morpheme).
*   **Literal Meaning:** "Head" (the body part).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *hoofd*, from Proto-Germanic *\*haubidą*, and ultimately from Proto-Indo-European *\*kaput-* (cognate with English *head* and German *Haupt*).
*   **Semantic Function in Compound:** In this context, `hoofd` undergoes a **semantic shift** (metonymy/metaphor). It no longer refers to the physical body part but functions as an intensifier or classifier meaning "main," "primary," "chief," or "principal."
    *   *Linguistic Note:* This is a productive pattern in Dutch. Just as the head is the most important part of the body, `hoofd-` denotes the most important instance of the following noun (e.g., *hoofdstad* = capital city, *hoofdpijn* = headache, *hoofdprijs* = grand prize).

#### Morpheme 2: `rol`
*   **Category:** Noun (Free Morpheme).
*   **Meaning:** "Role" (specifically in theater, film, or literature).
*   **Etymology:** Borrowed from Middle Dutch *rolle*, from Old French *rolle*, from Latin *rotulus* (a diminutive of *rota*, meaning "wheel"). Originally referred to a scroll or a list of names written on a roll.
*   **Semantic Function in Compound:** This is the **head** of the compound. In Dutch morphology, the rightmost element usually determines the grammatical category and the core meaning of the new word. A *hoofdrol* is a type of *rol*.

### 3. Morphological Structure

*   **Type:** **Endocentric Compound**.
    *   The meaning of the whole word is a hyponym (a specific subtype) of the head. A *hoofdrol* is a specific kind of *rol*.
*   **Head-Modifier Relationship:**
    *   **Head:** `rol` (The core concept).
    *   **Modifier:** `hoofd` (Specifies the type/quality of the role).
*   **Stress Pattern:** In Dutch compounds, primary stress typically falls on the **first** element.
    *   Pronunciation: **HOOFD**-rol (not hoofd-ROL).
*   **Pluralization:** When pluralized, the inflectional suffix attaches to the **head** (the second element).
    *   Singular: *hoofdrol*
    *   Plural: *hoofdrollen* (not *hoofdenrol*).

### 4. Comparison with English
*   **Dutch:** *hoofdrol* (Literal: "head-role")
*   **English:** *lead role* or *main role*
*   **Observation:** While English uses "head" in compounds like "headquarters" or "headmaster," it does not use "head role." This highlights how specific semantic extensions of the word "head" vary between Germanic languages.
