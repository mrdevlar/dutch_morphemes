From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`wijzen`** is a **homograph**. This means it is spelled the same way but can represent two distinct words with different morphological structures and meanings depending on the context.

1.  **The Verb:** *wijzen* (to point, to indicate).
2.  **The Noun:** *wijzen* (plural of *wijs* — ways, methods, or manners).

Below is the morphological breakdown for both interpretations.

---

### 1. Analysis as a Verb (Infinitive)
**Meaning:** To point, to show, to indicate.
**Grammatical Category:** Verb (Infinitive form).

| Morpheme | Type | Function | Explanation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`wijz-`** | **Bound Root** | Lexical | This is the core meaning carrier. It cannot stand alone as a word in modern Dutch (you cannot say *"Ik wijz"*). It carries the semantic content of "pointing" or "indicating." |
| **`-en`** | **Suffix** | Grammatical | This is the **infinitive marker**. In Dutch, adding *-en* (or sometimes *-n*) to a verb stem creates the dictionary form (e.g., *lopen*, *werken*, *wijzen*). |

**Morphophonology Note:**
The root appears as **`wijz-`** here because it is in a voiced environment (followed by the vowel *e* in the suffix). In Dutch, consonants are often devoiced at the end of a word (final-obstruent devoicing), but inside a word, they retain their voicing.

---

### 2. Analysis as a Noun (Plural)
**Meaning:** Ways, methods, manners (singular: *de wijs*).
**Grammatical Category:** Noun (Plural form).

| Morpheme | Type | Function | Explanation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`wijs-`** | **Free Root** | Lexical | This is the singular noun stem meaning "way," "method," or "manner." Unlike the verb root, this *can* stand alone as a word (*de wijs*). |
| **`-en`** | **Suffix** | Grammatical | This is the **plural marker**. It indicates that there is more than one "way" or "method." |

**Morphophonology Note:**
Here, the root appears as **`wijs-`** (with an *s*) rather than *wijz-*. This is due to **final devoicing**. When the root stands alone or ends the word (before the plural suffix is added in the underlying form), the /z/ sound devoices to /s/. However, in the plural *wijzen*, the *z* is often pronounced voiced because it is followed by a vowel, though the spelling retains the *s* from the singular form *wijs*.
*Correction on Spelling:* Actually, in standard Dutch orthography, the plural of *wijs* is spelled **`wijzen`** (with a *z*).
*   Singular: *wijs* (ends in *s* due to devoicing).
*   Plural: *wijzen* (the *z* is voiced because it is no longer in final position).
*   *Linguistic nuance:* The underlying root is /wɛis/. In the singular, it surfaces as [s]. In the plural, it surfaces as [z].

---

### 3. Etymological Connection (The "Root" Family)
To fully understand the morphology, one must look at the **Proto-Germanic** and **Old Dutch** history, which links these morphemes to the concept of "wisdom."

*   **Proto-Germanic:** *\*wīsaz* (wise).
*   **Old Dutch:** *wīs* (wise).
*   **Semantic Shift:**
    1.  **Wise** (*wijs*): Having knowledge.
    2.  **Way/Manner** (*wijs*): A "wise" way of doing something became a "method" or "way."
    3.  **To Point** (*wijzen*): To show someone the "way" or "method" (to point).

Therefore, the lexical morpheme **`wijz-/wijs-`** is historically related to the English word **`wise`** and **`way`**.

### Conclusion
Linguistically, **`wijzen`** is a **complex word** composed of two morphemes in both instances. The distinction lies in the **lexical root** (whether it is the bound verb stem *wijz-* or the free noun stem *wijs-*) and the **grammatical function** of the suffix *-en* (infinitive vs. plural). Despite the different underlying structures, they share the same phonological realization and etymological origin.
