From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`massaal`** is a derived adjective formed through the combination of a lexical root and a derivational suffix.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into two distinct morphemes:

> **`massa`** + **`-aal`**

### 2. Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Root: `massa`
*   **Type:** Free morpheme (Lexical root).
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun (*de massa*).
*   **Meaning:** "Mass," "bulk," "crowd," or "large quantity."
*   **Etymology:** Borrowed from Latin *massa* (meaning "lump," "dough," or "bulk"), which entered Dutch via French (*masse*) and Middle Dutch.
*   **Function:** It provides the core semantic content of the word. In Dutch, *massa* can also be used colloquially to mean "a lot" (e.g., *Ik heb er massa's van* — "I have tons of it").

#### B. The Suffix: `-aal`
*   **Type:** Bound morpheme (Derivational suffix).
*   **Part of Speech:** Adjectival suffix.
*   **Meaning/Function:** It indicates "relating to," "characterized by," or "pertaining to." It converts a noun into an adjective.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from the Latin adjectival suffix *-alis* (via French *-al*).
*   **Productivity:** This is a highly productive suffix in Dutch, used to form adjectives from nouns of Latin or Romance origin (e.g., *nationaal* from *natie*, *cultureel* from *cultuur*, *lokaal* from *locatie*).

### 3. Orthographic and Phonological Notes
*   **Vowel Length:** In Dutch orthography, when the suffix *-aal* is added to a root ending in the vowel *a*, the vowels merge to form a long vowel sound represented by the digraph **`aa`**.
    *   *Massa* (ends in /a/) + *-aal* (starts with /aː/) → **`massaal`**.
    *   This preserves the pronunciation of the root while integrating the suffix.
*   **Stress:** The primary stress in *massaal* typically falls on the first syllable (**`MAS`**-saal), though in some contexts, secondary stress may fall on the suffix.

### 4. Semantic Composition
The meaning of the compound is compositional but has undergone slight semantic broadening:

*   **Literal:** "Relating to a mass."
*   **Actual Usage:** "On a large scale," "widespread," "in large numbers," or "collective."
*   **Nuance:** Unlike the English cognate *massive* (which often implies weight, solidity, or size of a single object, derived from French *massif*), the Dutch *massaal* specifically emphasizes **quantity** and **scale of participation**.
    *   *Example:* *Een massaal protest* (A mass protest) implies many people participated, not necessarily that the protest was physically heavy.
