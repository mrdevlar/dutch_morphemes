From a linguistic perspective, the analysis of the Dutch word **`borrel`** depends on whether you are looking at it **synchronically** (as it exists in modern Dutch) or **diachronically** (its historical development).

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes.

### 1. Synchronic Analysis (Modern Dutch)
In contemporary Dutch morphology, **`borrel`** is considered a **monomorphemic word**.

*   **Morpheme:** `borrel`
*   **Type:** Free Root Morpheme (Lexeme)
*   **Category:** Noun (primarily), though it can function as a verb stem.
*   **Explanation:** There are no separable prefixes or suffixes within the word itself that carry grammatical meaning in modern usage. You cannot split it into `bor` + `rel` where both parts have independent meaning for a modern speaker.

**Evidence of Monomorphemic Status:**
We know it is a single root because it serves as the base for other words through the addition of *new* morphemes:
*   **`borrel` + `-tje`** (Diminutive suffix) $\rightarrow$ `borreltje` (a small drink / a casual drink).
*   **`borrel` + `-en`** (Infinitive verb suffix) $\rightarrow$ `borrelen` (to drink socially / to bubble).
*   **`borrel` + `-s`** (Plural suffix) $\rightarrow$ `borrels` (multiple drinks).

### 2. Diachronic Analysis (Etymology & History)
If we look at the history of the word (Middle Dutch and Old Low Franconian), linguists often analyze the internal structure differently.

*   **Origin:** Middle Dutch *borrele*.
*   **Root:** Likely related to the verb *bollen* (to swell, to bubble) or *boren* (to bore/drill, in the sense of bubbling up).
*   **Potential Historical Suffix:** The ending **`-el`** may historically function as a **frequentative** or **nominalizing suffix**.
    *   In Germanic languages, the suffix *-el* often turns a verb into a noun indicating an instrument or a repeated action (e.g., *spiegel* from *spieken*, *kavel* from *kaven*).
    *   Therefore, historically, it could be segmented as: **`borr`** (root: bubble/swell) + **`-el`** (suffix: noun of action/object).
*   **Current Status:** This segmentation is no longer productive. Modern speakers do not perceive `-el` as a suffix; they perceive `borrel` as a whole unit.

### 3. Semantic Morphology (Meaning Shift)
The word `borrel` is a classic example of **semantic broadening** and **metonymy**.

1.  **Original Meaning:** A "bubble" or a "boil" (a swelling on the skin). This connects to the root *bollen* (to swell).
2.  **Shift 1 (Metonymy):** A drink that bubbles (beer or ale).
3.  **Shift 2 (Generalization):** Any alcoholic drink (even non-bubbly ones like jenever or wine).
4.  **Shift 3 (Metonymy):** The social event surrounding the drink.
    *   *Example:* "Ik ga naar de borrel" (I am going to the [drinks] party).
