From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`aankondigen`** (to announce) is a complex verb formed through derivation and compounding. It is a classic example of Germanic morphology, featuring a separable prefix, a bound verbal stem, and an infinitive suffix.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation.

### 1. Visual Segmentation
In modern synchronic Dutch morphology (how the word is analyzed today), the word is segmented as follows:

> **`aan`** + **`kondig`** + **`en`**

However, to understand the full linguistic history, we must look at the internal structure of the stem **`kondig`**. A diachronic (historical) breakdown reveals:

> **`aan`** + **`kond`** + **`ig`** + **`en`**

---

### 2. Morpheme-by-Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Prefix: `aan-`
*   **Type:** Separable Prefix (*scheidbaar voorvoegsel*).
*   **Function:** Directional / Aspectual.
*   **Meaning:** Literally "on," "at," or "to." In verbal compounds, it often implies directing an action toward a target or making something manifest.
*   **Syntactic Behavior:** Because it is a separable prefix, in a main clause, it detaches from the verb and moves to the end of the sentence.
    *   *Infinitive:* Ik wil het **aankondigen**. (I want to announce it.)
    *   *Main Clause:* Ik **kondig** het **aan**. (I announce it.)
*   **Semantic Contribution:** It adds the nuance of "making known **outwardly**" or "declaring **to** someone."

#### B. The Stem: `kondig`
*   **Type:** Bound Verbal Stem.
*   **Status:** In modern Dutch, `kondig` is not a free morpheme (you cannot use it as a standalone adjective like "kondig" in the way you can use "mooi" or "groot"). It exists almost exclusively within derived verbs like *aankondigen* or *bekendmaken* (related concept).
*   **Internal Structure (Historical):**
    *   **Root (`kond-`):** Derived from the Proto-Germanic root **`*kunþ-`**, meaning "known" or "knowledge." This is cognate with the English word **`cunning`** (originally "having knowledge") and the Dutch noun **`kennis`** (knowledge).
    *   **Adjectival Suffix (`-ig`):** This suffix turns the root into an adjective (similar to English *-y* or *-ish*). Historically, *kondig* meant "known" or "made known."
    *   **Verbalizer:** The transition from the adjective *kondig* to the verb *kondigen* implies "to make known."

#### C. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix (Infinitive Marker).
*   **Function:** Marks the word as a verb in its base form (the infinitive).
*   **Grammar:** This is the standard ending for Dutch verbs in the dictionary form (e.g., *lopen*, *werken*, *zien* → *zien* is irregular, but *aankondigen* follows the regular weak conjugation pattern).

---

### 3. Etymological & Semantic Composition

When you combine these morphemes, the literal semantic composition is:

1.  **`kond`** (known/knowledge)
2.  **`-ig`** (state of being) $\rightarrow$ *kondig* (known/making known)
3.  **`-en`** (to do) $\rightarrow$ *kondigen* (to make known)
4.  **`aan-`** (outward/towards) $\rightarrow$ *aankondigen* (to make known outwardly/declare)

**English Cognates:**
*   **`Cunning`**: Shares the same Proto-Germanic root **`*kunþ-`**. Originally meant "knowledgeable," shifted to "sly" (knowledge used for trickery).
*   **`Kind`**: Also related (originally "of the same kin/knowledge").
*   **German `ankündigen`**: A direct cognate with the exact same morphological structure.

### 5. Linguistic Note on "Separability"
From a syntactic perspective, `aankondigen` is a **separable verb** (*scheidbaar werkwoord*). This is a crucial feature of Dutch and Germanic linguistics. The prefix `aan-` carries stress (aankóndigen), whereas the stem `kondig` carries the primary stress in the infinitive form. When the verb is conjugated in a main clause, the prefix separates and moves to the end of the clause, creating a "bracket" structure around the sentence elements.

*   **Infinitive:** *Hij gaat het nieuws **aankondigen**.*
*   **Conjugated:** *Hij **kondigt** het nieuws **aan**.*
