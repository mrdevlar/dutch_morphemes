From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`wetenschappelijk`** (meaning "scientific") is a complex derived word. It is formed through a process of **derivational morphology**, where new words are created by adding affixes to a root.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes, their functions, and the phonological/orthographic adjustments that occur.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation

The word can be segmented into three primary morphological units:

> **weten** + **schap** + **elijk**

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Root: `weten`
*   **Form:** Verb (Infinitive) / Noun
*   **Meaning:** "To know" / "Knowledge"
*   **Function:** This is the lexical root of the word. It carries the core semantic meaning.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Proto-Germanic **`*witaną`*.
*   **Cognates:**
    *   English: *wit*, *wise*, *witness*
    *   German: *wissen* (to know)
    *   Old English: *witan*

#### B. The First Suffix: `-schap`
*   **Form:** Derivational Suffix (Nominalizer)
*   **Meaning:** Indicates a state, condition, quality, or collective body.
*   **Function:** It turns the concept of "knowing" (*weten*) into a noun representing a system or collective body of knowledge.
*   **Resulting Word:** *Wetenschap* (Science).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Proto-Germanic **`*-skapą`*.
*   **Cognates:**
    *   English: *-ship* (as in *friendship*, *hardship*)
    *   German: *-schaft* (as in *Freundschaft*)
*   **Note:** In this specific compound, the root *weten* functions as a noun stem ("knowledge") rather than just a verb.

#### C. The Second Suffix: `-lijk` (Allomorph: `-elijk`)
*   **Form:** Derivational Suffix (Adjectivalizer)
*   **Meaning:** "Like," "characterized by," or "pertaining to."
*   **Function:** It turns the noun *wetenschap* into an adjective.
*   **Resulting Word:** *Wetenschappelijk* (Scientific).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Proto-Germanic **`*-līkaz`* (body/form).
*   **Cognates:**
    *   English: *-ly* (as in *friendly*, *manly*)
    *   German: *-lich* (as in *männlich*)

### 3. Orthographic and Phonological Adjustments

You may notice that the spelling **`wetenschappelijk`** does not perfectly match the simple concatenation of *weten* + *schap* + *lijk*. Two specific linguistic rules apply here:

#### 1. The Epenthetic 'e' (`-lijk` → `-elijk`)
While the standard adjectival suffix is **`-lijk`**, when it is attached to nouns ending in **`-schap`**, **`-heid`**, or **`-ing`**, an epenthetic vowel **`e`** is inserted.
*   *Standard:* *vriend* + *lijk* = *vriendelijk* (friendly)
*   *Standard:* *wetenschap* + *lijk* = *wetenschappelijk*
*   **Reason:** This insertion facilitates pronunciation and maintains the rhythmic structure of the word (avoiding a heavy consonant cluster between the stem and the suffix). In linguistic terms, **`-elijk`** is an **allomorph** of **`-lijk`**.

#### 2. Consonant Doubling (`p` → `pp`)
The word is spelled with a double **`p`** (*schappelijk*).
*   **Rule:** In Dutch orthography, a single consonant is doubled after a short vowel when a suffix starting with a vowel is added. This ensures the preceding vowel remains short.
*   **Application:** The syllable *schap* contains a short **`a`**. If we wrote *wetenschappelijk* with a single `p` (*schappelijk*), the `a` might be misread as a long vowel (as in an open syllable).
*   **Result:** The `p` is doubled to "close" the syllable and preserve the short vowel sound: *schap-pelijk*.

### 4. Semantic Composition (Meaning Building)

The meaning of the word is built hierarchically:

1.  **`weten`** (To know)
    *   *Concept:* The act of knowing.
2.  **`weten` + `schap`** = **`wetenschap`** (Science)
    *   *Concept:* The organized state or system of knowledge.
3.  **`wetenschap` + `elijk`** = **`wetenschappelijk`** (Scientific)
    *   *Concept:* Pertaining to the system of knowledge; characterized by science.
