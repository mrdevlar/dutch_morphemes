From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`eenmaal`** is a **compound word** consisting of two distinct morphemes. It functions primarily as an **adverb** of frequency or quantity.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented as follows:
> **`een`** + **`maal`**

*   **Type of Word:** Endocentric Compound (specifically, a numeral-noun compound functioning as an adverb).
*   **Stress Pattern:** Primary stress falls on the first syllable: **`EEN`**-maal.

---

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### Morpheme 1: `een`
*   **Form:** *een*
*   **Category:** Numeral (Cardinal Number) / Indefinite Article.
*   **Meaning:** "One" (the number 1).
*   **Function in Compound:** In this specific context, it functions as the **numeral**, not the indefinite article ("a/an"). It specifies the quantity of the occurrence.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *een*, Old Dutch *ēn*, Proto-Germanic *\*ainaz*, and ultimately Proto-Indo-European *\*oi-no-*.
*   **Linguistic Note:** While *een* is the most common indefinite article in Dutch (e.g., *een man* = a man), in *eenmaal*, it retains its numerical value.

#### Morpheme 2: `maal`
*   **Form:** *maal*
*   **Category:** Noun (historically), functioning as a bound morpheme in this context.
*   **Meaning:** "Time" (in the sense of an instance, occurrence, or turn).
*   **Function in Compound:** It acts as the **head** of the compound, defining the unit of measurement. It turns the number into a frequency adverb.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *male*, Old Dutch *māl*, Proto-Germanic *\*mēlą* (time, measure, occasion). It is cognate with the German word *Mal*.
*   **Linguistic Note:** In modern spoken Dutch, the free noun for "time/instance" is more commonly *keer* (e.g., *een keer*). However, *maal* survives robustly in **compounds** (e.g., *tweemaal*, *driemaal*) and formal contexts.

---

### 3. Morphosyntactic Combination

**Process:** **Compounding** (N + N $\rightarrow$ Adv)
The two morphemes combine to form a new lexical unit.

1.  **Semantic Composition:** The meaning is compositional (transparent).
    *   *een* (one) + *maal* (time/instance) = "one time".
2.  **Adverbialization:** Although *maal* is a noun, the resulting compound functions syntactically as an **adverb**. It modifies verbs to indicate frequency.
    *   *Example:* "Ik heb het **eenmaal** gedaan." (I have done it **once**.)
3.  **Orthography:** In Dutch, this compound is written as one word (*eenmaal*), unlike some English equivalents which might be spaced ("one time").

---

### 4. Semantic Nuances and Usage

To fully understand the morphology, one must look at how the meaning distinguishes it from synonyms:

*   **`Eenmaal` vs. `Een keer`:**
    *   *Eenmaal* is slightly more formal or precise. It is often used in instructions, mathematics, or formal writing (e.g., *eenmaal per dag* = once a day).
    *   *Een keer* is the colloquial standard for "once."
*   **`Eenmaal` vs. `Eens`:**
    *   *Eenmaal* refers to **quantity** (exactly one time).
    *   *Eens* refers to **indefinite time** (sometime, once upon a time).
    *   *Example:* "Ik heb het **eenmaal** gezien" (I saw it exactly one time) vs. "Ik heb het **eens** gezien" (I saw it at some point in the past).
*   **Mathematical Usage:**
    *   In arithmetic, *maal* functions as the operator for multiplication ("times").
    *   *Example:* "Eén **maal** één is één" (One **times** one is one).
