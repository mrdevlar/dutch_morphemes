From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`fotograaf`** (photographer) is a **neo-classical compound**. It is formed by combining two morphemes derived from Ancient Greek, which have been fully integrated into the Dutch lexicon.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Morphemic Segmentation
The word can be segmented into two distinct morphemes:

> **`foto`** + **`graaf`**

*   **Total Morphemes:** 2
*   **Word Class:** Noun (Common gender / *de*-word)
*   **Structure:** Compound (Root + Root)

---

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### Morpheme 1: `foto-`
*   **Type:** Free Root / Combining Form
*   **Etymology:** Derived from the Ancient Greek word **φῶς** (*phōs*), meaning "light" (genitive *phōtos*).
*   **Meaning in Dutch:** In modern Dutch, *foto* functions as a standalone noun meaning "photograph." In this compound, it acts as the modifier, indicating the medium or subject matter (light/image).
*   **Status:** In contemporary Dutch, this is a **free morpheme** because it can stand alone as a word (*Ik heb een foto gemaakt* / "I made a photo"). However, in the context of this specific compound, it functions as the first element of a compound noun.

#### Morpheme 2: `-graaf`
*   **Type:** Bound Root / Agentive Suffix
*   **Etymology:** Derived from the Ancient Greek verb **γράφειν** (*graphein*), meaning "to write" or "to draw," and the agent noun suffix **-γραφος** (*-graphos*), meaning "one who writes/draws."
*   **Meaning in Dutch:** It denotes the **agent** (the person performing the action). It implies "one who records" or "one who draws with light."
*   **Status:** This is a **bound morpheme** in this context. While the string of letters *graaf* exists as a standalone Dutch word, it has a completely different etymology and meaning in that case (see below). You cannot use *-graaf* to mean "writer" in other contexts (e.g., you do not say *boekgraaf* for "author").
*   **Homophony Warning:** It is crucial to distinguish this morpheme from the native Dutch word **`graaf`** (meaning "Count," a noble title, or "digger," from the verb *graven*). The *fotograaf* morpheme is a loan morpheme; the *graaf* (Count) morpheme is Germanic. They are homographs and homophones but etymologically unrelated.

---

### 3. Morphological Classification

**Neo-Classical Compound:**
Linguists classify *fotograaf* as a neo-classical compound. These are words formed from Greek or Latin roots that were coined in modern times (post-classical antiquity) to describe new concepts (in this case, photography, invented in the 19th century).

*   **Synchronic Analysis (Current Dutch):** A compound of two nouns (*foto* + *graaf*).
*   **Diachronic Analysis (Historical):** A calque (loan translation) of the Greek *photographos* (light-writer).

---

### 4. Inflectional Morphology (Derivations)

The morphemes behave differently when the word is inflected or derived into other parts of speech.

*   **Plural:** *fotografen*
    *   **Morphology:** `fotograaf` + `-en`
    *   **Morphophonology:** Notice the vowel change. The long vowel **`aa`** in the singular shortens to **`a`** in the plural (*fotograaf* → *fotografen*). This is a standard Dutch morphophonological rule where long vowels shorten in open syllables during inflection.
*   **Verb:** *fotograferen* (to photograph)
    *   **Morphology:** `foto` + `graaf` + `-er` + `-en`
    *   Here, the agentive noun is converted into a verb. The suffix *-er-* is inserted to create the verbal stem, followed by the infinitive ending *-en*.
*   **Adjective:** *fotografisch* (photographic)
    *   **Morphology:** `fotograaf` + `-isch`
    *   The suffix *-isch* turns the noun into an adjective.
