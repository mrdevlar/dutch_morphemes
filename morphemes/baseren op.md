From a linguistic perspective, it is important to clarify first that **`baseren op`** is not a single word, but rather a **prepositional verb** (in Dutch: *voorzetselwerkwoord*). It consists of two distinct lexical items: the verb *baseren* and the preposition *op*.

Here is the morphological and syntactic breakdown of the phrase.

---

### 1. The Verb: *baseren*
This is a loan verb derived from French (*baser*) or Latin (*basis*). In Dutch morphology, it is analyzed as follows:

| Morpheme | Form | Type | Function/Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`baser`** | Stem | **Bound Root / Lexical Stem** | Carries the core lexical meaning ("to base"). It is derived from the noun *basis*. |
| **`-en`** | Suffix | **Inflectional Suffix** | Marks the verb as the **infinitive** form (the dictionary form). |

*   **Etymological Note:** The stem `baser` itself contains the root `base` (from Latin *basis* / French *base*) and the verbalizing element `-r-` (from the French infinitive ending *-er*). However, in modern Dutch morphological analysis, `baser` is treated as the indivisible stem to which the Dutch infinitive ending `-en` is attached.
*   **Pronunciation:** The stress falls on the first syllable (*BA-seren*), which is typical for Dutch verbs ending in *-eren*.

### 2. The Preposition: *op*
This is a separate word that functions grammatically to connect the verb to its object.

| Morpheme | Form | Type | Function/Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`op`** | Word | **Free Morpheme** | A preposition. Literally means "on" or "upon." |

*   **Semantic Shift:** In this specific construction, *op* does not indicate physical location (e.g., *op de tafel* / on the table). Instead, it indicates a **metaphorical foundation** or **source**. It governs the object that provides the basis for the action.

---

### 3. Syntactic and Semantic Integration
While they are two separate words morphologically, they function as a single semantic unit.

#### A. Valency (Government)
The verb *baseren* is **transitive** but requires a prepositional complement to complete its meaning. It cannot stand alone with just a direct object.
*   *Incorrect:* `Ik baseer het.` (I base it.)
*   *Correct:* `Ik baseer het **op** feiten.` (I base it **on** facts.)

#### B. Separability (Scheidbaarheid)
In Dutch grammar, *baseren op* is a **separable verb**.
*   **Main Clause:** The preposition *op* separates from the verb and moves to the end of the clause.
    *   *Example:* "Ik **baseer** mijn theorie **op** bewijs." (I base my theory on evidence.)
*   **Subordinate Clause:** The verb and preposition stay together (though the verb moves to the end).
    *   *Example:* "...dat ik mijn theorie **op bewijs baseer**." (...that I base my theory on evidence.)

#### C. Meaning
*   **Literal:** To place something on top of something else.
*   **Figurative (Current Usage):** To derive, ground, or justify something using a specific source or foundation.
    *   *English Equivalent:* "To base [something] on [something]."

Linguistically, **`baseren op`** is a **bi-morphemic verb** (`baser` + `-en`) combined with a **monomorphemic preposition** (`op`). Together, they form a **prepositional verb complex** where the preposition is an obligatory part of the verb's valency, changing the meaning from a physical action to an abstract relationship of foundation.
