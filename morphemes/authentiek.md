From a linguistic perspective, analyzing the Dutch word **`authentiek`** requires distinguishing between its **synchronic** structure (how it functions in modern Dutch) and its **diachronic** structure (its historical etymological composition).

Here is the breakdown and explanation of the morphemes.

### 1. Synchronic Analysis (Modern Dutch)
In the context of the modern Dutch language, **`authentiek`** is treated as a **single, unanalyzable root morpheme**.

*   **Word:** `authentiek`
*   **Part of Speech:** Adjective (bijvoeglijk naamwoord)
*   **Morphological Status:** Free Morpheme (Loanword / *Leenwoord*)
*   **Explanation:** A native Dutch speaker does not intuitively break this word down into smaller meaningful parts. You cannot use the stem `authent` on its own, nor does the suffix `-iek` carry a specific meaning independent of this specific root in the speaker's mind. It functions as a lexical unit.

**Inflectional Morphology:**
When used in a sentence, Dutch adjectives often take an inflectional suffix.
*   **Base form:** `authentiek` (e.g., *Dit is authentiek* — "This is authentic")
*   **Inflected form:** `authentiek` + **`-e`** (e.g., *Een authentieke foto* — "An authentic photo")
    *   **Morpheme:** `-e` (Inflectional suffix indicating attributive position).

### 2. Diachronic Analysis (Etymological Morphology)
While it is one morpheme in modern Dutch, the word is a loanword (from French *authentique*, from Latin *authenticus*, from Greek *authentēs*). If we look at its historical construction, we can identify the following morphological components:

#### A. The Root: `auto-` (Greek: *autós*)
*   **Meaning:** "Self" (zelf).
*   **Function:** This is the core semantic root. It implies something that originates from the source itself, rather than being copied or imitated.
*   **Cognates:** *Autobiografie*, *autonoom*, *automatisch*.

#### B. The Agentive Suffix: `-ent-` (Greek/Latin: *-entes* / *-ent-*)
*   **Meaning:** "Doer" or "Agent" (doener).
*   **Function:** In the original Greek *authentēs*, this suffix turned the concept of "self" into "one who acts on their own authority" (an author or perpetrator).
*   **Evolution:** In Latin (*authenticus*), this became part of the adjectival stem.

#### C. The Adjectival Suffix: `-iek` (Latin: *-icus* / French: *-ique*)
*   **Meaning:** "Pertaining to" or "Characterized by."
*   **Function:** This suffix turns the noun/stem into an adjective. In Dutch, this is a productive suffix for loanwords of Latin/French origin.
*   **Examples:** *Katholiek* (Catholic), *Dramatiek* (Dramatic - though often a noun), *Energetiek* (Energetic).
*   **Note:** In Dutch orthography, the Latin *-icus* often becomes *-iek* (pronounced /iːk/).

### 3. Derivational Morphology (Word Family)
Because `authentiek` is a loan root, it can form new words in Dutch by adding Dutch derivational suffixes.

1.  **`Authentiek` + `-iteit`** $\rightarrow$ **`Authenticiteit`**
    *   **Morphemes:** `authentiek` (Root) + `-iteit` (Nominalizing suffix).
    *   **Meaning:** Authenticity (the quality of being authentic).
    *   **Note:** The `-e-` is dropped before adding `-iteit` (a common phonological adjustment in Dutch).

2.  **`Authentiek` + `-iseren`** $\rightarrow$ **`Authentiseren`**
    *   **Morphemes:** `authentiek` (Root) + `-iseren` (Verbalizing suffix).
    *   **Meaning:** To authenticate (to make authentic or verify authenticity).
    *   **Note:** This is a less common verb than the English "to authenticate," but it exists in technical/legal contexts.

### Linguistic Conclusion
From a strict morphological standpoint, **`authentiek`** is a **monomorphemic loanword** in contemporary Dutch. It is a single lexical unit that cannot be segmented by a speaker without knowledge of its etymology. However, etymologically, it is a compound of the Greek root for "self" (*auto-*) and an agentive/adjectival structure (*-ent-icus*), adapted into Dutch via French orthography and phonology.
