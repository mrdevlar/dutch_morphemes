#### Component B: `reductie`
This component is morphologically more complex. It is a loanword from Latin (via French), but it is treated as a single noun in modern Dutch. However, a linguistic breakdown reveals its internal structure:

**Breakdown:** `re-` + `duc` + `-tie`

1.  **Prefix: `re-`**
    *   **Type:** Bound Morpheme (Prefix)
    *   **Origin:** Latin (*re-* meaning "back" or "again").
    *   **Function:** In this context, it implies a reversal or a movement "back" or "down."
    *   **Note:** In Dutch, this prefix is not productive on its own (you cannot attach `re-` to a random Dutch verb to mean "undo"), but it is fossilized within many loanwords (e.g., *reconstructie*, *revisie*).

2.  **Root: `duc` (or `duct`)**
    *   **Type:** Bound Morpheme (Root)
    *   **Origin:** Latin verb *ducere* ("to lead" or "to bring").
    *   **Function:** The core semantic meaning of "leading" or "bringing."
    *   **Note:** This root does not exist as a standalone word in Dutch. It only appears in derived forms like *productie* (leading forth), *eductie* (leading out), and *reductie* (leading back/down).

3.  **Suffix: `-tie`**
    *   **Type:** Derivational Suffix
    *   **Origin:** Latin *-tio* (via French *-tion*).
    *   **Function:** **Nominalization.** It converts the verbal concept (to lead back) into an abstract noun (the process of leading back).
    *   **Dutch Equivalent:** This corresponds to the Dutch suffix `-ing` (e.g., *reduceren* $\rightarrow$ *reductie* is similar to *reduceren* $\rightarrow$ *reductie* / *reductie* is the noun form of the verb *reduceren*).
