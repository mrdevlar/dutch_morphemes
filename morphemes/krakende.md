From a morphological perspective, the Dutch word **`krakende`** can be segmented into three distinct morphemes. It is a derived form of a verb functioning as an inflected adjective (specifically, a present participle).

Here is the breakdown:

### 1. Segmentation
**`krak`** + **`-end`** + **`-e`**

---

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### **A. `krak` (Root / Lexical Stem)**
*   **Type:** Free lexical morpheme (Root).
*   **Source:** Derived from the infinitive verb **`kraken`** (to crack, to creak, to crunch, or to break).
*   **Formation:** To find the stem of a Dutch verb, the infinitive ending **`-en`** is removed.
    *   *Infinitive:* `kraken`
    *   *Stem:* `krak`
*   **Semantics:** This root is **onomatopoeic**. It imitates the sound of something breaking, wood creaking, or joints popping. It is cognate with the English word *crack* and the German *krachen*.
*   **Function:** It carries the core semantic meaning of the word.

#### **B. `-end` (Derivational Suffix)**
*   **Type:** Derivational suffix.
*   **Function:** This suffix creates a **present participle** (*tegenwoordig deelwoord*) from the verb stem.
*   **Grammatical Shift:** It transforms the verb (`kraken`) into a verbal adjective (`krakend`).
*   **Semantics:** It indicates an ongoing action or a state of being in progress (similar to the English suffix *-ing*).
*   **Intermediate Form:** `krak` + `-end` = **`krakend`** (cracking/creaking).

#### **C. `-e` (Inflectional Suffix)**
*   **Type:** Inflectional suffix.
*   **Function:** This is an **adjectival inflection** (specifically, the *sterke* or *zwakke* declension ending depending on context, though here it functions as the standard attributive ending).
*   **Grammar Rule:** In Dutch, when a participle or adjective is used **attributively** (placed before a noun), it usually takes an **`-e`** ending if:
    1.  The noun is plural (e.g., *de krakende deuren*).
    2.  The noun is a *de*-word (common gender) in the singular (e.g., *de krakende deur*).
    3.  The noun is a *het*-word (neuter gender) in the plural (e.g., *de krakende vloeren*).
    *   *Note:* If it were a singular *het*-word used attributively without an article (e.g., *een krakend geluid*), the `-e` would be dropped.
*   **Semantics:** This morpheme carries no lexical meaning; it serves purely a grammatical function to ensure agreement with the noun it modifies.

---

### 3. Summary of Construction

| Morpheme | Form | Type | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Root** | `krak` | Lexical | Core meaning (to crack/creak) |
| **Derivational** | `-end` | Suffix | Creates present participle (ongoing action) |
| **Inflectional** | `-e` | Suffix | Adjectival agreement (attributive position) |

### 4. Usage Example
> *"De **krakende** vloer verried zijn aanwezigheid."*
> (The **creaking** floor betrayed his presence.)

*   **`krakende`** modifies **`vloer`** (floor).
*   **`vloer`** is a *de*-word (singular).
*   Therefore, the adjective requires the inflectional **`-e`**.

### 5. Linguistic Note on Phonology
While not strictly morphology, it is worth noting that the root `krak` ends in a voiceless velar plosive /k/. When adding `-end`, the pronunciation remains distinct. However, in rapid speech, the final /d/ of `-end` and the /e/ of `-e` may undergo liaison or elision depending on the dialect, though in standard Dutch, they are pronounced distinctly as /krɑ.kən.də/.
