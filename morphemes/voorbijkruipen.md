### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### **`voor`** (Prefix)
*   **Category:** This is originally a preposition (*voor* = before/in front of). In this context, it functions as a **separable verbal prefix**.
*   **Semantic Role:** It indicates direction or position relative to the action. It suggests movement towards a point that is "ahead" or "past."
*   **Linguistic Note:** In Dutch, prepositions often become verbal prefixes (e.g., *opstaan*, *uitgaan*).

#### **`bij`** (Prefix)
*   **Category:** Also originally a preposition (*bij* = by/near). Like `voor`, it functions here as a **separable verbal prefix**.
*   **Semantic Role:** It indicates proximity.
*   **Combination:** When `voor` and `bij` are combined (`voorbij`), they form a complex directional unit meaning "past" or "by." While they are two distinct morphemes, they function semantically as a single unit modifying the verb.

#### **`kruip`** (Root/Stem)
*   **Category:** This is the lexical root of the verb.
*   **Base Form:** The infinitive of this root is *kruipen*.
*   **Meaning:** To move on hands and knees, or to move slowly and quietly (to creep).
*   **Conjugation Class:** It is a **strong verb** (*sterk werkwoord*).
    *   Present: *kruip*
    *   Past: *kroop*
    *   Past Participle: *gekropen*
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *kriupan*, related to the German *kriechen* and the English *creep*.

#### **`-en`** (Suffix)
*   **Category:** Inflectional suffix.
*   **Function:** This is the standard marker for the **infinitive** form of Dutch verbs.
*   **Phonology:** In standard pronunciation, this is often realized as a schwa sound /ə/ or is elided in rapid speech.

### 3. Syntactic Behavior: Separability
A crucial linguistic feature of this word is that it is a **separable verb**.

*   **In the Infinitive:** The parts are joined together (`voorbijkruipen`).
    *   *Example:* "Hij probeerde **voorbijkruipen**." (He tried to crawl past.)
*   **In a Main Clause:** The prefixes (`voor` and `bij`) detach and move to the end of the clause, while the root (`kruip`) is conjugated and stays in the second position.
    *   *Example:* "Hij **kroop** langzaam **voorbij**." (He crawled slowly past.)
*   **In the Past Participle:** The prefix `ge-` is inserted **between** the separable prefix and the root.
    *   *Structure:* `voor` + `bij` + `ge` + `kropen`
    *   *Result:* **`voorbijgekropen`** (not *gevoorbijkropen*).

The meaning of the compound is compositional, meaning it is derived from the sum of its parts, though with a specific idiomatic nuance.

1.  **`kruipen`**: The mode of movement (crawling).
2.  **`voorbij`**: The trajectory (passing by something).
3.  **Combined Meaning:** To crawl past something or someone.
    *   *Literal:* A person crawling on the floor past a piece of furniture.
    *   *Metaphorical:* Often used for time passing slowly ("De tijd **kroop voorbij**" - Time crawled by).
