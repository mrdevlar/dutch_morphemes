From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`ondernemen`** (meaning "to undertake," "to do," or "to start") is a complex verb formed through derivation. It is a perfect cognate of the English word *undertake*.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation of its components.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
We can segment the word into three distinct morphemes:

> **`onder-`** + **`nem`** + **`-en`**

### 2. Detailed Explanation of Morphemes

#### A. The Prefix: `onder-`
*   **Type:** Inseparable Prefix (*Onscheidbaar Voorvoegsel*).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from the preposition *onder* (meaning "under"). It traces back to Proto-Germanic **\*undar***.
*   **Function:** In this specific compound, the prefix is **inseparable**. This is a crucial grammatical distinction in Dutch.
    *   *Evidence of Inseparability:* In the past tense, the prefix does not detach.
        *   Correct: *Hij **ondernam** het project.* (He undertook the project).
        *   Incorrect: *Hij nam het project **onder**.*
    *   *Stress Pattern:* Because it is inseparable, the stress falls on the root, not the prefix.
        *   Pronunciation: *on-der-**NE**-men* (Stress on *NE*).
*   **Semantic Shift:** While the preposition *onder* usually means "under" (spatially), in this verbal compound, it carries an archaic sense similar to the English "under" in *undertake*. It implies taking something **upon oneself** or taking charge of something. It does not mean "to take under" in a physical sense.

#### B. The Root: `nem`
*   **Type:** Verbal Root (*Wortel*).
*   **Meaning:** "To take."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Proto-Germanic **\*namjaną*** and Proto-Indo-European **\*nem-*** (to take, distribute, allot).
*   **Conjugation Class:** This is a **strong verb** (*sterk werkwoord*). It changes its vowel to indicate tense (ablaut).
    *   Infinitive: *nemen* (vowel *e*)
    *   Past Singular: *nam* (vowel *a*)
    *   Past Participle: *genomen* (vowel *o*)
*   **Cognates:**
    *   English: *nimble* (originally "quick to take"), *name* (originally "to take/distribute").
    *   German: *nehmen* (to take).
    *   Latin: *nomen* (name).

#### C. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Type:** Infinitive Suffix (*Infinitiefuitgang*).
*   **Function:** This is the standard ending for Dutch verbs in the infinitive form (equivalent to the English "to" in "to take").
*   **Note:** In the segmentation `onder-` + `nem` + `-en`, the `-en` is attached to the root `nem`. However, in standard lexicography, the base verb is often cited as the unit **`nemen`**.

### 3. Syntactic and Semantic Behavior

**1. Inseparability:**
As noted above, `ondernemen` is an inseparable verb. This affects how it is conjugated and how the past participle is formed.
*   **Past Participle:** *ondernomen* (Note: No *ge-* prefix is added because inseparable verbs do not take *ge-*).
*   **Comparison:** Contrast with the separable verb *opnemen* (to record/take up), which becomes *opgenomen* (with *ge-* inserted).

**2. Semantic Derivation:**
The literal meaning "to take under" evolved into the figurative meaning "to take upon oneself."
*   **Literal:** *Onder* (under) + *nemen* (take).
*   **Figurative:** To assume responsibility for a task or business.

### 4. Related Derivatives
The morphological structure of `ondernemen` generates several common Dutch words:

1.  **`De Ondernemer`** (The Entrepreneur):
    *   `onder-` + `nem` + `-er` (agentive suffix).
    *   Literally: "The one who undertakes."
2.  **`De Onderneming`** (The Enterprise/Company):
    *   `onder-` + `nem` + `-ing` (nominalization suffix).
    *   Literally: "The undertaking."

### Summary
The word **`ondernemen`** is a morphologically transparent compound consisting of the inseparable prefix **`onder-`** (indicating assumption of responsibility) and the strong verb root **`nem-`** (to take), finished with the infinitive suffix **`-en`**. Its stress pattern (*onderNEmen*) and lack of the *ge-* prefix in the participle (*ondernomen*) are the primary linguistic markers that identify it as an inseparable verb.
