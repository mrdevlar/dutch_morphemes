From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`corruptie`** is a loanword (leenwoord) derived from Latin, likely mediated through French. To fully understand its morphology, we must look at it on two levels:

1.  **Synchronic Analysis:** How the word is structured in modern Dutch.
2.  **Diachronic Analysis:** The historical etymological breakdown of the roots.

Here is the morphological breakdown.

---

### 1. Synchronic Analysis (Modern Dutch)
In contemporary Dutch morphology, the word is analyzed as a **stem** plus a **nominalizing suffix**.

| Morpheme | Form | Type | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`corrupt`** | Stem | Adjective/Root | Carries the core lexical meaning ("spoiled," "bribed," "dishonest"). |
| **`-ie`** | Suffix | Derivational | Turns the adjective/verb root into an abstract noun (state or process). |

*   **`corrupt`**: This exists independently in Dutch as an adjective (e.g., *"een corrupte ambtenaar"*).
*   **`-ie`**: This is a highly productive suffix in Dutch for creating nouns from Latin/French roots (e.g., *actie*, *operatie*, *creatie*). It indicates the **state** or **result** of the action.

**Synchronic Structure:** `corrupt` + `ie` = **corruptie**

---

### 2. Diachronic Analysis (Etymological/Latin Roots)
If we dig deeper into the history of the word (which is standard in linguistic morphology for loanwords), we can break the stem `corrupt` down further into its Latin components. The word comes from the Latin noun *corruptio*, from the verb *corrumpere*.

| Morpheme | Form | Origin | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`cor-`** | Prefix | Latin *com-* | "Together," "thoroughly," or "intensively." (Assimilated to *cor-* before 'r'). |
| **`rupt`** | Root | Latin *rumpere* | "To break," "to burst." |
| **`-ie`** | Suffix | Latin *-io* | Noun-forming suffix indicating action or result. |

**Etymological Structure:** `com-` + `rumpere` + `-io` → **corruptie**

#### Semantic Shift
*   **Literal Meaning:** "To break thoroughly" or "to destroy."
*   **Figurative Meaning:** In Latin, *corrumpere* evolved to mean "to bribe" (literally "to break someone's integrity") or "to spoil/decay."
*   **Dutch Meaning:** The word retains this figurative sense, referring to dishonesty, bribery, or moral decay within an organization or system.

### 3. Related Morphological Families
To understand the morphemes better, it helps to look at the word family in Dutch, which shares the same roots:

*   **`corrupt`** (Adjective): *Hij is corrupt.* (He is corrupt.)
*   **`corrumperen`** (Verb): *Het systeem werd gecorrumpeerd.* (The system was corrupted.)

*   **`corruptie`** (Noun): *Er is sprake van corruptie.* (There is corruption.)

    *   Stress is on the **`rupt`** syllable.

### Summary
Linguistically, **`corruptie`** is a **derived noun**.
1.  **Surface Level:** It is the adjective **`corrupt`** + the noun suffix **`-ie`**.
2.  **Deep Level:** It is the Latin prefix **`com-`** (intensive) + the root **`rumpere`** (to break) + the nominal suffix **`-io`**.

This structure highlights how Dutch morphology often preserves the internal structure of its Latin loanwords, allowing speakers to intuitively understand the relationship between *corrupt* (the state of being) and *corruptie* (the phenomenon).
