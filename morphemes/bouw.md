From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`bouw`** is analyzed as a **monomorphemic** word. This means it consists of a single morpheme.

Here is the detailed breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Morphemic Segmentation
*   **Word:** `bouw`
*   **Morphemes:** 1
*   **Segmentation:** `[bouw]`

### 2. Morpheme Analysis
*   **Morpheme:** `bouw`
*   **Type:** **Root** (or Lexeme)
*   **Status:** **Free Morpheme**
    *   It is a *free* morpheme because it can stand alone as a complete word (e.g., *"De bouw is gestopt"* / "The construction has stopped").
    *   It is a *root* because it carries the core lexical meaning ("to build" or "construction") and cannot be broken down into smaller meaningful units.

### 3. Grammatical Function (Polysemy)
While there is only one morpheme, this single form functions in two different grammatical categories depending on context:

1.  **As a Noun (Substantive):**
    *   **Meaning:** Construction, building process, or the construction industry.
    *   **Example:** *"De **bouw** van de brug duurt lang."* (The **construction** of the bridge takes a long time.)
    *   **Article:** *de* (common gender).
2.  **As a Verb Stem:**
    *   **Meaning:** To build.
    *   **Context:** It is the stem of the verb *bouwen*. In the present tense (1st/3rd person singular) or the imperative, the suffix is dropped, leaving just the stem.
    *   **Example:** *"Ik **bouw** een huis."* (I **build** a house.)
    *   **Example:** *"**Bouw** dat!"* (**Build** that!)

### 4. Derivational Context
Although `bouw` itself has no affixes (prefixes or suffixes), it is the base for many other words in Dutch. Understanding these helps clarify why `bouw` is the root:

*   **Inflection (Verb):** `bouw` + `-en` = **bouwen** (to build - infinitive)
*   **Derivation (Noun):** `ge-` + `bouw` + `-` = **gebouw** (building - a physical structure)
    *   *Note:* In `gebouw`, the prefix `ge-` and the root `bouw` combine, but the vowel changes slightly in pronunciation compared to the verb stem.
*   **Compounding:** `bouw` + `vak` = **bouwvak** (construction trade)

### 5. Etymology and Cognates
*   **Origin:** Middle Dutch *bouwen*, from Old Dutch *bouwan*, from Proto-Germanic **\*būaną*.
*   **Cognates:**
    *   **English:** *build* (Note the sound shift: Germanic *b* remained *b*, but the vowel and consonant cluster shifted differently in English vs. Dutch).
    *   **German:** *bauen* (to build).
    *   **Frisian:** *bouwe*.

### Summary
In the word **`bouw`**, there are no prefixes or suffixes to separate. It is a **single, free root morpheme** that serves as the lexical core for the concept of building, functioning either as a noun or as the stem of a verb.
