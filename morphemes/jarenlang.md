From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`jarenlang`** is a **compound word** (*samenstelling*). It is formed by combining a noun and an adjective to create a new lexical item that functions as either an adjective or an adverb.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation of the morphemes:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **`jaar`** + **`-en`** + **`lang`**

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### **A. `jaar` (Root / Lexeme)**
*   **Category:** Noun (Substantive).
*   **Meaning:** "Year."
*   **Function:** This is the semantic core of the first part of the compound. It establishes the unit of time being measured.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *jaer*, Old Dutch *jār*, and Proto-Germanic *\*jērą*. It is cognate with the English word "year."

#### **B. `-en` (Inflectional Suffix)**
*   **Category:** Plural Marker.
*   **Function:** This suffix marks the noun `jaar` as plural (`jaren` = "years").
*   **Linguistic Note:** In Dutch compounding, it is common for the first constituent (the noun) to appear in its **plural form** when indicating a duration or quantity. This is distinct from English, which often uses the singular in similar compounds (e.g., "year-long" vs. Dutch "jarenlang").
*   **Role in Compound:** While `-en` is technically an inflectional suffix (usually indicating number), in this specific compound structure, it acts as a **linking element** that connects the noun to the adjective. It implies "a period consisting of multiple years."

#### **C. `lang` (Root / Lexeme)**
*   **Category:** Adjective / Adverb.
*   **Meaning:** "Long."
*   **Function:** This is the head of the compound in terms of grammatical category. It modifies the noun phrase to describe the *duration* of the time.
*   **Syntactic Shift:** When combined with the time noun, `lang` shifts from describing physical length to describing **temporal duration**.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *lang*, Old Dutch *lang*, and Proto-Germanic *\*langaz*. It is cognate with the English word "long."

### 3. Morphological Structure & Type
*   **Type:** Endocentric Compound (specifically a **Noun-Adjective Compound**).
*   **Structure:** `[Noun-Plural] + [Adjective]`
*   **Word Class of Result:** The resulting word `jarenlang` can function as:
    1.  **An Adjective:** *Een **jarenlang** proces* (A years-long process).
    2.  **An Adverb:** *Hij heeft **jarenlang** gewerkt* (He worked for years / for a long time).

### 4. Semantic Composition
The meaning is compositional but idiomatic:
*   **Literal:** "Years" + "Long."
*   **Actual Meaning:** "Lasting for a period of years" or "For many years."
*   **Nuance:** The use of the plural `-en` is crucial. If one were to say `jaarleng` (singular), it would imply a single year's length, which is not a standard construction in Dutch. The plural `jaren` emphasizes the **continuity** and **repetition** of the years over a span of time.

### Summary
In the word **`jarenlang`**, the morpheme **`jaar`** provides the time unit, **`-en`** pluralizes it to indicate a span of time, and **`lang`** defines the nature of that span as a duration. Together, they form a lexicalized compound meaning "lasting for years."
