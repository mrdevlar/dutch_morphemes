Here is the morphological breakdown and linguistic explanation.

### 1. The Verb: `omgaan`
This is a **separable compound verb** (*scheidbaar samengesteld werkwoord*). It consists of two distinct morphemes.

#### A. `om` (Particle / Separable Prefix)
*   **Morphological Type:** Free morpheme functioning as a verbal particle (or separable prefix).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from the preposition/adverb *om* (around, about, over).
*   **Function:** In this context, it acts as a **derivational morpheme**. It modifies the meaning of the root verb `gaan`.
*   **Semantic Shift:**
    *   *Literal:* "Around" (spatial).
    *   *Figurative:* In `omgaan`, it implies interaction, circulation, or handling. It transforms the physical act of "going" into a metaphorical act of "dealing" or "associating."
*   **Syntactic Behavior:** Because it is a separable verb, in a main clause, `om` detaches from `gaan` and moves to the end of the sentence (e.g., *Hij gaat goed met stress **om***).

#### B. `gaan` (Root Verb)
*   **Morphological Type:** Free morpheme (Lexical Root).
*   **Etymology:** Proto-Germanic *\*gangan* (to walk/go).
*   **Function:** This is the semantic core of the verb.
*   **Meaning:** Literally "to go."
*   **Morphology:** In the infinitive form provided (`omgaan`), it appears in its base form. If conjugated (e.g., *hij gaat*), it undergoes vowel change (ablaut) typical of strong verbs in Germanic languages.

**Combined Meaning of `omgaan`:**
Literally "to go around." Idiomatically, it means "to handle," "to deal with," or "to associate with."

### 2. The Preposition: `met`
*   **Morphological Type:** Free morpheme (Preposition).
*   **Etymology:** Middle Dutch *met*, Old Dutch *metti*, Proto-Germanic *\*miti*.
*   **Function:** It introduces the **complement** (the object) of the verb.
*   **Valency:** The verb `omgaan` is **intransitive** on its own; it requires a prepositional object to complete its meaning. `Met` is the required preposition that licenses this object.
*   **Meaning:** "With."

### 3. The Phrase: `omgaan met` (Lexical Unit)
While `omgaan` and `met` are separate words, linguistically they form a **complex predicate** or a **collocation**.

*   **Compositionality:** The phrase is **semi-compositional**. You can guess the meaning ("to go with"), but the specific idiomatic meaning ("to deal with") is not fully transparent from the sum of the parts without cultural knowledge.
*   **Argument Structure:**

    *   **Verb:** `omgaan` (handling/interacting).
    *   **Prepositional Object:** Introduced by `met` (the thing/person being dealt with).
