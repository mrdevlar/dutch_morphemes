*   **`student`**: A noun meaning "student". It functions as the **head** (the core meaning of the word; the person is a student).

### 4. Linguistic Commentary

#### A. Endocentric Compounding
The word is an **endocentric compound**. This means the meaning of the whole word is a hyponym (a specific type) of the head.
*   **Head:** `student` (A student).
*   **Modifier:** `geneeskunde` (Of medicine).
*   **Result:** A student *of medicine*.

#### B. The Linking Element (`-s-`)
In Dutch morphology, the `s` inside `geneeskunde` is a classic example of a **linking element** (*tussenklank*).
*   When a verb stem (like `genees-`) combines with a noun suffix (like `-kunde`), an `s` is often inserted to facilitate pronunciation and mark the relationship.
*   Note: There is **no** linking `s` between `geneeskunde` and `student`. When a noun ending in `-e` compounds with another noun, Dutch typically concatenates them directly (e.g., *taalkundestudent*, *biologiestudent*).

#### C. Lexicalization
While `geneeskunde` can be analyzed morphologically as `genees` + `kunde`, in modern Dutch, it is often treated as a **lexicalized unit**. Most speakers perceive `geneeskunde` as a single word meaning "medicine" rather than actively analyzing "heal-science" every time they use it. However, for linguistic analysis, the internal structure remains visible.

#### D. Loanword Integration
The morpheme **`student`** is a loanword from Latin (*studens*, present participle of *studere*, "to study"). It has been fully integrated into Dutch phonology and morphology (it takes Dutch plural suffixes like *studenten*), but it retains its original root structure without internal Dutch morphemes.
