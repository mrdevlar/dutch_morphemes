From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`zelfvertrouwen`** (meaning "self-confidence") is a **compound noun** formed through derivational morphology. It can be analyzed at two levels: the macro level (the compound itself) and the micro level (the internal structure of the second component).

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation.

### 1. Macro Segmentation (The Compound)
At the highest level, the word is a combination of two independent lexical units:

> **`zelf`** + **`vertrouwen`**

*   **Type:** Endocentric Compound (specifically a Noun-Noun compound).
*   **Head:** The head of the compound is *vertrouwen* (confidence/trust). The word refers to a type of *vertrouwen*.
*   **Modifier:** *zelf* modifies the head, specifying *whose* trust it is.

---

### 2. Micro Segmentation (Internal Morphology)
To understand the full linguistic depth, we must break down the second component, *vertrouwen*, into its constituent morphemes.

> **`ver-`** + **`trouw`** + **`-en`**

#### A. Morpheme: `zelf`
*   **Category:** Free Morpheme (Pronoun/Noun).
*   **Meaning:** "Self."
*   **Function:** In this compound, it functions as a nominal modifier. It specifies the locus of the action/state.
*   **Etymology:** From Middle Dutch *selve*, Old Dutch *selbo*. Cognate with English *self* and German *selbst*.
*   **Phonology:** /zɛlf/

#### B. Morpheme: `ver-`
*   **Category:** Bound Derivational Prefix.
*   **Meaning:** This is a highly productive prefix in Germanic languages. It often indicates a change of state, a process, or intensification. In the context of *vertrouwen*, it transforms the concept of "loyalty" into the **act** or **state** of placing that loyalty in someone/something.
*   **Etymology:** From Proto-Germanic *\*far-*. Cognate with English *for-* (as in *forbear*) and German *ver-*.
*   **Function:** It nominalizes/verbalizes the root *trouw*.

#### C. Morpheme: `trouw`
*   **Category:** Root Morpheme (Lexeme).
*   **Meaning:** "Faith," "Loyalty," "Fidelity," or "Truth."
*   **Function:** This is the semantic core of the word.
*   **Etymology:** From Middle Dutch *trouwe*, Old Dutch *trouwa*.
*   **Cognates:**
    *   English: *True*, *Troth* (as in "plight one's troth").
    *   German: *Treue* (loyalty).
    *   *Note:* In modern Dutch, the verb *trouwen* exists but means "to marry" (literally "to make faithful"). However, in *vertrouwen*, the root retains the meaning of "faith/trust."
*   **Phonology:** /trʌu̯/

#### D. Morpheme: `-en`
*   **Category:** Derivational Suffix (Nominalizer).
*   **Meaning:** This is the infinitive ending of the verb, which has been **substantivized** (turned into a noun).
*   **Function:** It turns the verbal concept (*vertrouwen* = to trust) into an abstract noun (*vertrouwen* = trust/confidence).
*   **Grammar:** In Dutch, infinitives can easily function as neuter nouns (e.g., *het eten* = the eating/food). Here, it functions as a mass noun.

---

### 3. Synthesis and Semantic Shift

When combined, the morphemes create a specific semantic chain:

1.  **`trouw`** (Loyalty/Faith)
2.  **`ver-` + `trouw` + `-en`** $\rightarrow$ **`vertrouwen`** (The act/state of having faith/trust).
3.  **`zelf` + `vertrouwen`** $\rightarrow$ **`zelfvertrouwen`** (Trust placed in oneself).

**Linguistic Notes on the Compound:**
*   **Linking Elements:** Dutch compounds often use linking sounds (like *-s-* or *-en-*), but *zelfvertrouwen* requires none. The final /f/ of *zelf* and the initial /v/ of *ver* do not create a phonological clash that necessitates a buffer.
*   **Stress Pattern:** In Dutch compounds, the primary stress usually falls on the **first** element.
    *   Pronunciation: **`ZÉLF`**vertrouwen.
    *   This distinguishes it from a phrase like *zelf vertrouwen* (trusting oneself as a verb phrase), though the pronunciation is often similar, the stress on *zelf* solidifies it as a single lexical unit.
*   **Orthography:** It is written as one word (closed compound), which is standard for Dutch nouns formed this way.
