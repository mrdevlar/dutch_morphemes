From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch verb **`isoleren`** (to isolate) can be broken down into three distinct morphemes. This word is a classic example of a **loanword** that has been fully integrated into Dutch morphology.

Here is the morphological segmentation and explanation:

### 1. Segmentation
**`isol`** + **`er`** + **`en`**

---

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. `isol-` (Bound Root)
*   **Type:** Bound Lexical Root (Free morpheme in Latin/French, bound in Dutch).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from the Latin word ***insula*** (meaning "island"). Through the French verb *isoler* (from *isolé*, past participle of *isoler*), it entered Dutch.
*   **Meaning:** The core semantic concept is "separation" or "setting apart," metaphorically likening the object to an island surrounded by water.
*   **Linguistic Note:** In Dutch, this root cannot stand alone as a word (you cannot say *"Ik isole"*). It is a **bound root**.
*   **Crucial Distinction:** Do not confuse this with the Greek prefix **`iso-`** (meaning "equal" or "same," as in *isotherm* or *isobar*). In *isoleren*, the `iso-` sequence is part of the root *isol-*, not the Greek prefix.

#### B. `-er-` (Derivational Suffix / Verbalizer)
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix.
*   **Origin:** This corresponds to the French infinitive ending ***-er*** (which itself comes from the Latin first conjugation ***-are***).
*   **Function:** It converts the noun/root concept into a verb.
*   **Dutch Morphology:** In Dutch, verbs borrowed from French or Latin that end in *-er* in the source language typically adopt the suffix **`-eren`**.
    *   *Examples:* *fotograferen* (to photograph), *repareren* (to repair), *organiseren* (to organize).
    *   While often analyzed as a single unit **`-eren`** in Dutch grammar, etymologically and morphologically, it can be split into the verbalizer **`-er`** and the Dutch infinitive marker **`-en`**.

#### C. `-en` (Inflectional Suffix)
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix.
*   **Function:** Marks the **infinitive** form of the verb.
*   **Productivity:** This is the standard, highly productive infinitive ending for all Dutch verbs (e.g., *loop**en***, *werk**en***, *zi**en***).
*   **Grammar:** It distinguishes the dictionary form from the stem (*isoler*) used in conjugation (e.g., *ik isoleer*, *jij isoleert*).

---

### 3. Morphological Structure Summary

| Morpheme | Form | Type | Origin | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Root** | `isol-` | Bound Root | Latin (*insula*) | Carries the core meaning ("island/separate"). |
| **Derivational** | `-er-` | Verbalizer | French (*-er*) | Turns the root into a verb stem. |
| **Inflectional** | `-en` | Infinitive Marker | Proto-Germanic | Marks the verb as an infinitive. |

### 4. Related Morphological Forms
Understanding the morphemes helps explain related words in the Dutch lexicon:

*   **`isolatie`** (noun): `isol` (root) + `-atie` (nominalizing suffix from Latin *-atio*).
*   **`geïsoleerd`** (adjective/participle): `ge-` (prefix for past participle) + `isol` (root) + `-eer` (stem) + `-d` (past participle ending).
*   **`isoleer`** (imperative/stem): `isol` + `er` (The `-en` is dropped for the imperative or present tense stem).

### 5. Phonological Note
The stress pattern in Dutch for this word falls on the syllable containing the verbalizer:
**iso-LER-en**
This confirms that `-er-` is part of the stressed stem, distinguishing it from the unstressed grammatical ending `-en`.
