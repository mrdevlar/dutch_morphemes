From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`overslaan`** is a compound verb formed through derivational morphology. It consists of two distinct morphemes: a separable prefix and a verbal root.

Here is the breakdown and explanation of the morphemes.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented as follows:
> **over-** + **-slaan**

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Prefix: `over-`
*   **Type:** Separable Prefix (*scheidbaar voorvoegsel*).
*   **Origin:** Preposition/Adverb.
*   **Literal Meaning:** "Over," "across," "beyond," or "above."
*   **Function:** In this context, it acts as a **directional** or **spatial** modifier. It indicates movement across a boundary or passing something by.
*   **Linguistic Note:** In Dutch, when *over-* is used as a prefix to create a new verb meaning "to pass over" or "to skip," it is **separable**. This means in a main clause, the prefix moves to the end of the sentence (e.g., *Ik sla het over* — "I skip it").

#### B. The Root: `-slaan`
*   **Type:** Free Morpheme (Verbal Stem).
*   **Origin:** Old Dutch *slaan*.
*   **Literal Meaning:** "To hit," "to strike," or "to beat."
*   **Cognates:**
    *   English: *Slay* (archaic/strong verb form related to striking/killing).
    *   German: *Schlagen* (to hit/beat).
    *   Swedish: *Slå*.
*   **Function:** This provides the core action of the verb.

### 3. Semantic Composition (The "Why")
How do "over" + "hit" result in "skip"? This is an example of **semantic shift** via metaphor.

*   **Literal Construction:** "To hit over."
*   **Metaphorical Evolution:**
    1.  **Physical Motion:** Historically, the concept likely relates to the motion of skipping a stone across water (striking the surface and moving *over* it) or skipping a rope (the rope hits the ground and moves *over* the feet).
    2.  **Abstract Motion:** The meaning generalized to "moving past a point without stopping." If you "hit over" a page in a book, you do not read it; you pass it. If you "hit over" a beat in music, you do not play it; you move to the next one.
*   **Current Meanings:**
    *   To skip (a page, a chapter).
    *   To omit (a detail, a step).
    *   To overlook (a mistake).
    *   To miss (a meal: *een maaltijd overslaan*).

### 4. Grammatical Behavior
Because `over-` is a separable prefix in this specific word, the morphology changes depending on the sentence structure.

*   **Infinitive:** *overslaan* (to skip)
*   **Present Tense (Main Clause):** *Ik **sla** de pagina **over**.* (I skip the page.)
    *   *Note:* The root *sla* is conjugated, and the prefix *over* is displaced to the end.
*   **Past Participle:** *overgeslagen*
    *   *Note:* The ge- prefix is inserted between the separable prefix and the root (*over-ge-slaan*).

### 5. Comparison with Inseparable `over-`
It is important to distinguish this from the inseparable prefix *over-*, which usually implies "too much" or "excessively" (e.g., *overleven* - to survive, *overstappen* - to transfer/change trains).

*   **Separable (*overslaan*):** Focuses on spatial movement "over" something (Skip).
*   **Inseparable (*overkomen*):** Focuses on an event happening to someone (Happen/Occur).
