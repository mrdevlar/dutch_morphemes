From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`levensgeluk`** is a **compound noun** (*samenstelling*). It is formed by combining two independent lexical roots with a linking element.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **`leven`** + **`s`** + **`geluk`**

---

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### A. `leven` (Root / Stem)
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun (*zelfstandig naamwoord*).
*   **Meaning:** "Life" or "Living."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *leven*, Old Dutch *lebon*, and Proto-Germanic *\*libaną* (to live). It is cognate with the English verb *to live* and the noun *life* (though English *life* comes from the noun form *\*libaz*).
*   **Function:** In this compound, it acts as the **modifier** (or determiner). It specifies the *type* of happiness being discussed.

#### B. `s` (Linking Element / Fugue)
*   **Part of Speech:** Interfix / Linking Morpheme (*verbindingsklank*).
*   **Meaning:** None (grammatical function only).
*   **Linguistic Function:** This is a **genitive linking element** (*genitief-s*). Historically, this *s* marked the genitive case (possessive), indicating "of" or "'s".
    *   *Example:* *Levens* = "Life's".
    *   In modern Dutch morphology, this *s* is often called a **Fugue** (*voegklank*). It connects the two nouns to ensure phonological flow and mark the compound relationship. It is not a plural marker here (though it looks identical to the plural *s* in *levens* as in "many lives").
*   **Pronunciation:** It is pronounced as a voiceless alveolar fricative /s/.

#### C. `geluk` (Root / Head)
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun (*zelfstandig naamwoord*).
*   **Meaning:** "Luck," "Fortune," or "Happiness."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *gelucke*, Old Dutch *gilukki*. It is cognate with the English word *luck*.
    *   *Note on Semantics:* In Dutch, *geluk* covers both "luck" (chance) and "happiness" (state of being). Context determines the meaning. In *levensgeluk*, it leans heavily toward "happiness" or "contentment."
*   **Function:** This is the **head** of the compound (*hoofdwoord*). In Dutch endocentric compounds, the rightmost element determines the grammatical category and the core meaning of the whole word. Therefore, *levensgeluk* is a type of *geluk*.

---

### 3. Syntactic and Semantic Structure

*   **Compound Type:** **Endocentric Compound**.
    *   The meaning of the whole word is a hyponym of the head. A *levensgeluk* is a specific kind of *geluk*.
*   **Semantic Relationship:** **Possessive / Descriptive**.
    *   The relationship between the modifier and the head is roughly equivalent to "Happiness **of** life" or "Life's happiness."
*   **Stress Pattern:**
    *   Dutch compounds typically carry primary stress on the **first element**.
    *   Pronunciation: **`LE`**-vens-ge-luk.

### 5. Translation & Usage
*   **Literal:** "Life-happiness."
*   **Idiomatic:** "Happiness in life," "Contentment," or "Life satisfaction."
*   **Context:** It is often used in philosophical or psychological contexts (e.g., *het zoeken naar levensgeluk* — "the search for happiness in life").
