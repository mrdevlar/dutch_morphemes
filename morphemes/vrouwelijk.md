From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`vrouwelijk`** (meaning "feminine" or "female") can be analyzed through its morphological structure, etymology, and semantic derivation.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word consists of two primary morphological units:

> **`vrouw`** + **`-elijk`**

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. Root Morpheme: `vrouw`
*   **Type:** Free morpheme (can stand alone as a word).
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun.
*   **Meaning:** "Woman."
*   **Function:** This is the lexical root that provides the core semantic content of the word.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *vrouwe*, Old Dutch *vrouwa*, and Proto-Germanic **\*frawjōn* (mistress, lady). It is cognate with the German *Frau* and related to the English word *free* (in the sense of a noblewoman vs. a slave, though this etymology is debated).

#### B. Derivational Suffix: `-elijk`
*   **Type:** Bound morpheme (cannot stand alone).
*   **Part of Speech:** Adjectival suffix.
*   **Meaning:** "Pertaining to," "characteristic of," or "having the nature of."
*   **Function:** This is a **derivational suffix**. It changes the part of speech from a noun (*vrouw*) to an adjective (*vrouwelijk*).
*   **Allomorphy (The `-e-`):**
    *   The core suffix in Dutch is **`-lijk`** (cognate with English *-ly* and German *-lich*).
    *   However, in Dutch phonology, when `-lijk` is attached to certain stems (often those ending in vowels or specific consonant clusters), an epenthetic (inserted) schwa vowel **`-e-`** is added to facilitate pronunciation.
    *   Therefore, linguistically, this can be viewed as **`-e-`** (linking vowel) + **`-lijk`** (suffix), but in modern morphological analysis, **`-elijk`** is often treated as a single allomorph of the adjectival suffix.
    *   *Pronunciation:* /ˈvrʌu.ə.lək/ (The *-e-* creates a syllable break).

### 3. Morphological Process
*   **Process:** **Derivation** (specifically, denominal derivation).
*   **Direction:** Noun $\rightarrow$ Adjective.
*   **Result:** The word shifts from denoting a specific entity (a woman) to denoting a quality or category associated with that entity (feminine).

### 4. Semantic Nuance
While the literal translation is "woman-like," the usage of *vrouwelijk* covers three main domains:
1.  **Biological:** Female (e.g., *vrouwelijk geslacht* = female sex).
2.  **Grammatical:** Feminine gender (e.g., *het vrouwelijk geslacht* = the feminine gender in grammar).
3.  **Qualitative:** Possessing qualities traditionally associated with women (e.g., *vrouwelijke kleding* = women's clothing).
