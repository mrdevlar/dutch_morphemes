From a linguistic (morphological) perspective, the Dutch word **`komen`** ("to come") is a **verb** in its **infinitive** form.

Here is the breakdown of its morphemes, followed by a detailed explanation of each component.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into two distinct morphemes:

> **`kom`** + **`-en`**

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Stem: `kom-`
*   **Type:** Lexical Root / Verbal Stem
*   **Status:** **Free Morpheme** (in specific contexts) / **Bound Root** (in this context)
*   **Meaning:** This carries the core semantic content of the word: the action of "coming" or "arriving."
*   **Linguistic Note:**
    *   In the infinitive form `komen`, this stem is technically **bound** because it cannot stand alone as a grammatical word in this specific form without the suffix.
    *   However, `kom` is a **free morpheme** in other contexts, specifically the **imperative singular** (e.g., *"Kom hier!"* / "Come here!").
    *   **Allomorphy:** In Dutch morphology, this stem undergoes vowel alternation (ablaut) in the past tense. While the stem is `kom-` in the present/infinitive, it changes to `kwam-` in the simple past (*ik kwam*). This makes it a **strong verb** (irregular verb) historically, though it behaves regularly in the present tense.

#### B. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix
*   **Status:** **Bound Morpheme**
*   **Meaning:** This morpheme carries grammatical information rather than lexical meaning.
*   **Function:** It serves a dual function in Dutch morphology:
    1.  **Infinitive Marker:** It marks the verb as the dictionary form (e.g., *Ik wil **komen*** / "I want to come").
    2.  **Plural Present Marker:** It marks the 1st, 2nd, and 3rd person plural in the present tense (e.g., *Wij **komen*** / "We come").
*   **Linguistic Note:** This is an example of **cumulative exponence**, where a single morpheme (`-en`) realizes multiple grammatical features (non-finite mood + plural number, depending on context).

### 3. Morphological Classification

*   **Word Formation Process:** **Inflection**.
    *   The word is not created by adding a prefix or changing the meaning (derivation); rather, it is the base form of the verb modified for grammatical usage.
*   **Morphological Type:** Dutch is generally considered an **inflectional language** (specifically fusional), though this specific word shows **agglutinative** tendencies (a clear stem + a clear suffix).
*   **Phonology:**
    *   **`kom`**: /kɔm/ (Short vowel in standard Dutch, though often lengthened in open syllables depending on dialect).
    *   **`-en`**: /ə(n)/ (Schwa sound, often elided in rapid speech, becoming /kɔm/).
