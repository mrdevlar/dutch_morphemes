From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`afdeling`** (meaning "department," "section," or "division") is a **derived noun**. It is formed through a process of derivation where affixes are added to a root to create a new word with a related meaning.

Here is the morphological breakdown of the word:

### 1. The Morpheme Breakdown

The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **`af-`** + **`del`** + **`-ing`**

| Morpheme | Type | Meaning/Function | Origin |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`af-`** | **Prefix** | "Off," "away," "down," or "separation." | Proto-Germanic *\*af-* |
| **`del`** | **Root** | "Divide," "share," or "part." | Proto-Germanic *\*dailōną* |
| **`-ing`** | **Suffix** | Nominalizer (turns a verb into a noun). | Proto-Germanic *\*-ingaz* |

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Each Morpheme

#### **A. The Prefix: `af-`**
*   **Function:** This is a separable prefix in Dutch verbs (e.g., *afgeven*, *afmaken*). In this noun, it indicates **separation** or **detachment**.
*   **Semantics:** It implies taking something away from a whole or setting something apart.
*   **Etymology:** Cognate with the English prefix *off-* (as in *offshoot* or *offset*) and the German *ab-*.

#### **B. The Root: `del`**
*   **Function:** This is the lexical core of the word. It comes from the verb **`delen`** (to divide, to share, to distribute).
*   **Phonological Note:** In the infinitive verb *delen*, the vowel is long (spelled *ee*, pronounced /eː/) because the syllable is open (*de-len*). However, in *afdeling*, the syllable is closed by the suffix *-ing*, causing the vowel to shorten (spelled *e*, pronounced /ɛ/). This is a standard Dutch phonological rule known as **syllable closure shortening**.
*   **Semantics:** It refers to the act of cutting, splitting, or allocating parts.

#### **C. The Suffix: `-ing`**
*   **Function:** This is a **deverbal nominalizer**. It attaches to a verb stem to create a noun.
*   **Semantics:** It typically denotes either:
    1.  **The process:** The act of doing the verb (e.g., *het bouwen* vs. *de bouw*).
    2.  **The result:** The object or entity that results from the action.
*   **In this context:** In *afdeling*, it denotes the **result** of the action. It is not the act of dividing off, but the **section** that has been divided off.

---

### 3. Syntactic and Semantic Construction

To understand how these morphemes combine, we look at the underlying verb:

1.  **Base Verb:** *delen* (to divide).
2.  **Derived Verb:** *afdel*en (to divide off / to allocate).
    *   *Note: While `afdel`en is a valid verb, it is less common in daily speech than `verdelen` (to distribute), but it is the direct source of the noun.*
3.  **Nominalization:** *afdel* + *-ing* → *afdeling*.

**Literal Translation:** "The thing resulting from dividing off."
**Actual Meaning:** A department, branch, or section of an organization.

### 4. Related Words (Cognates and Derivatives)

Understanding the morphemes helps in recognizing related vocabulary:

*   **`Delen`**: To divide/share (Root only).
*   **`Verdeling`**: Division/Distribution (Prefix *ver-* + Root *del* + Suffix *-ing*).
*   **`Afdelen`**: To divide off (Prefix *af-* + Root *del* + Verb ending *-en*).
*   **`Deel`**: Part/Share (The noun form of the root).
*   **German Cognate:** *Abteilung* (Prefix *ab-* + Root *teil* + Suffix *-ung*).
*   **English Cognate:** *Department* (Latin *de-* + *partire* "to divide").

### Summary
The word **`afdeling`** is a classic example of Dutch derivational morphology. It takes the concept of **division** (`del`), applies a sense of **separation** (`af-`), and nominalizes the result into a concrete entity (`-ing`) representing a specific **section** of a larger whole.
