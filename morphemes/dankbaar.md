From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`dankbaar`** (meaning "grateful" or "thankful") is a **derived adjective**. It is formed through a process of **suffixation**, where a derivational morpheme is added to a root morpheme.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into two distinct morphemes:

> **`dank`** + **`-baar`**

*   **`dank`**: Root / Stem (Free Morpheme)
*   **`-baar`**: Suffix (Bound Morpheme)

---

### 2. Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Root: `dank`
*   **Category:** Noun / Verb Stem
*   **Meaning:** "Thanks," "gratitude," or the act of thanking.
*   **Etymology:**
    *   **Middle Dutch:** *danc*
    *   **Old Dutch:** *dank*
    *   **Proto-Germanic:** *\*thankaz* (meaning "thought" or "gratitude").
    *   **Proto-Indo-European (PIE):** *\*teng-* (meaning "to think").
*   **Cognates:** This root is shared across Germanic languages.
    *   English: *thank*, *thanks*
    *   German: *Dank*
    *   Swedish: *tack*
*   **Semantic Note:** Historically, the concept of "thanks" was closely linked to "thought" or "mind" (i.e., holding someone in good thought). Over time, the semantic field narrowed to specifically mean "gratitude."

#### B. The Suffix: `-baar`
*   **Category:** Derivational Suffix
*   **Function:** Converts a noun or verb stem into an adjective.
*   **Meaning:** It generally indicates "capable of being [verb]" or "worthy of [noun]." It implies a passive potential or a characteristic quality.
*   **Etymology:**
    *   **Middle Dutch:** *-bare*
    *   **Old Dutch:** *-baro*
    *   **Proto-Germanic:** *\*-baz*
    *   **Origin:** Derived from the verb *baren* (to bear, to carry, to bring forth).
*   **Cognates:**
    *   English: *-able* / *-ible* (e.g., *readable*, *visible*)
    *   German: *-bar* (e.g., *dankbar*)
*   **Linguistic Nuance:** While *-baar* usually corresponds to the English *-able* (passive capability), in the specific lexicalization of *dankbaar*, it functions more similarly to the English suffix *-ful* (possessing a quality).
    *   *Literal construction:* "Thank-bearing" or "worthy of thanks."
    *   *Actual meaning:* "Feeling gratitude."

---

### 3. Morphological Process & Semantics

**Process:** **Derivation**
The word is created by adding a derivational suffix to a base. This changes the part of speech (from a noun/verb concept to an adjective) and adds specific semantic content.

**Semantic Composition:**
1.  **`dank`** provides the semantic core: the concept of gratitude.
2.  **`-baar`** provides the adjectival quality: the state of possessing or being characterized by that concept.

**Comparison with English:**
*   **Dutch:** *dank* + *-baar* (Literally: "thank-able")
*   **English:** *thank* + *-ful* (Literally: "thank-full")
*   **German:** *dank* + *-bar* (Identical to Dutch)

*Note:* While English has the word *thankable*, it is archaic or rare. Dutch (and German) standardized the *-baar/-bar* suffix for this concept, whereas English standardized *-ful*.

### 4. Orthographic Note
In Dutch orthography, the vowel length is indicated by the spelling:
*   **`dank`**: The single vowel 'a' followed by a consonant cluster indicates a **short** vowel sound (/ɑ/).
*   **`baar`**: The double vowel 'aa' indicates a **long** vowel sound (/aː/).
*   This spelling rule ensures the pronunciation remains distinct between the root and the suffix.
