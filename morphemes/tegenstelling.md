From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`tegenstelling`** is a complex word formed through **derivational morphology**. It is a deverbal noun (a noun derived from a verb) that combines a prefix, a verbal root, and a nominalizing suffix.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **tegen** + **stel** + **ling**
> *(Prefix) + (Root) + (Suffix)*

*Note: While visually it looks like `tegen` + `stelling`, linguistically `stelling` is itself composed of the root `stel` and the suffix `-ing`. Therefore, the deepest level of analysis reveals three morphemes.*

---

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

| Morpheme | Type | Origin/Source | Meaning & Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **tegen-** | **Prefix** | Preposition *tegen* | **Meaning:** "Against," "opposite," or "counter."<br>**Function:** Adds the semantic notion of opposition to the root action. It turns the neutral act of "placing" into an act of "placing against." |
| **stel-** | **Root** | Verb *stellen* | **Meaning:** "To place," "to set," or "to put."<br>**Function:** Provides the core lexical meaning (the action). This is the stem of the verb *stellen*. |
| **-ing** | **Suffix** | Nominalizer | **Meaning:** Indicates an action, process, or result.<br>**Function:** Converts the verb (*tegenstellen*) into a noun (*tegenstelling*). It is equivalent to the English suffix *-ing* (as in *setting*) or *-tion* (as in *opposition*). |

---

### 3. Morphophonology and Orthography
A key linguistic feature of this word is the spelling change regarding the letter **'l'**.

*   **The Verb:** The infinitive is *tegen**stel**len*. The stem is *stel*.
*   **The Noun:** When the suffix *-ing* is added to a Dutch verb stem with a short vowel (like the *e* in *stel*), the final consonant is doubled to preserve the short vowel sound.
    *   *Stel* + *ing* $\rightarrow$ **Stelling** (not *Steling*).
    *   Therefore: *Tegen* + *stelling* $\rightarrow$ **Tegenstelling**.

This doubling is a rule of Dutch orthography known as the **short vowel rule** (korte klinkerregel).

---

### 4. Semantic Composition (How the meaning is built)
The meaning of the word is compositional, meaning it is built logically from the parts:

1.  **Stel:** To place something.
2.  **Tegen-stel:** To place something *against* something else (to oppose).
3.  **Tegen-stel-ling:** The **state**, **act**, or **result** of placing things against one another.

**Resulting Meaning:** Contrast, opposition, antithesis, or juxtaposition.

---

### 5. Etymological Context
All three morphemes share Germanic roots, showing the close relationship between Dutch, German, and English:

*   **tegen-**: Cognate with German *gegen* and English *against*.
*   **stel-**: Cognate with German *stellen* and English *set* (from Proto-Germanic *\*stalljan*).
*   **-ing**: Cognate with German *-ung* and English *-ing* (from Proto-Germanic *\*-ingaz*).

### Summary
**`Tegenstelling`** is a **deverbal noun** created by taking the root **`stel`** (to place), adding the prefix **`tegen-`** (against) to create the verb *to oppose*, and finally adding the suffix **`-ing`** to nominalize the concept into "the state of opposition."
