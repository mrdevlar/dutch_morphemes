From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`gevaarlijk`** (meaning "dangerous") can be analyzed through **derivational morphology**. It is formed by combining a noun root with an adjectival suffix.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes:

### 1. Synchronic Morphological Breakdown
In modern Dutch (synchronic analysis), the word is divided into two distinct morphemes:

> **`gevaar`** + **`-lijk`**

#### **Morpheme 1: `gevaar`**
*   **Type:** Free morpheme (Root).
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun.
*   **Meaning:** "Danger," "peril," or "risk."
*   **Function:** This serves as the semantic base of the word. It carries the core meaning.
*   **Note on Internal Structure:** While it looks like it might contain the prefix *ge-*, in modern Dutch, *gevaar* is treated as a single, unanalyzable lexical unit. The *ge-* is not the productive verbal prefix used to form past participles (e.g., *gezien*, *gemaakt*).

#### **Morpheme 2: `-lijk`**
*   **Type:** Bound morpheme (Derivational Suffix).
*   **Part of Speech:** Adjective-forming suffix.
*   **Meaning:** "Having the quality of," "characterized by," or "like."
*   **Function:** It changes the part of speech from a **Noun** to an **Adjective**.
*   **Phonology:** In this context, it is pronounced as /lək/ (schwa + voiceless velar stop).
*   **Cognates:** This is cognate with the English suffix **-ly** (as in *friend-ly*, *man-ly*) and the German suffix **-lich** (as in *gefähr-lich*).

---

### 2. Diachronic (Historical) Perspective
To fully understand the word linguistically, it is helpful to look at its etymology (diachronic analysis), which reveals a deeper layer of morphology that is no longer productive in modern Dutch.

*   **The Root `vaar`:** The core of the word *gevaar* comes from the Old Dutch verb **`varen`** (to travel, to go, to fare).
*   **The Prefix `ge-`:** In Old and Middle Dutch, the prefix *ge-* was often used to form abstract nouns or collective nouns from verbs.
    *   *Etymological construction:* `ge-` (abstract/collective) + `vaar` (travel/go) $\rightarrow$ *gevaar*.
    *   *Original sense:* Literally "a going together" or "an encounter." Over time, the meaning shifted from "meeting" to "encounter with risk" to simply "danger."
*   **Why this matters:** While a modern speaker sees *gevaar* as one block, historically it was a compound. However, for the purpose of analyzing *gevaarlijk* today, we treat *gevaar* as the base noun.

---

### 3. Morphological Process
The formation of this word is an example of **Suffixation** (specifically, **Adjectivalization**).

1.  **Base:** *gevaar* (Noun: Danger)
2.  **Process:** Add suffix *-lijk*
3.  **Result:** *gevaarlijk* (Adjective: Dangerous)

This is a regular pattern in Dutch. You can apply this to many other nouns:
*   *vriend* (friend) $\rightarrow$ *vriend**lijk*** (friendly)
*   *mooi* (beautiful - adjective) $\rightarrow$ *mooi**lijk*** (rare, but follows the rule)
*   *zeker* (certain) $\rightarrow$ *zeker**lijk*** (certainly)
*   *moed* (courage) $\rightarrow$ *moed**ig*** (Note: *-ig* is a variant of *-lijk* used with certain roots).
