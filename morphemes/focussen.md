From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`focussen`** can be segmented into three distinct morphemes. It is a loanword (Anglicism) that has been adapted into Dutch morphology.

Here is the breakdown:

### 1. Visual Segmentation
**`focus`** + **`s`** + **`en`**

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### A. Root (Lexeme): `focus`
*   **Type:** Free Morpheme / Root.
*   **Origin:** Latin (*focus* = "hearth" or "fireplace"), borrowed into Dutch via English.
*   **Function:** This carries the core lexical meaning of the word (the center of attention, an optical point, or the act of concentrating).
*   **Status:** In this context, it functions as the verbal stem. While *focus* exists as a noun in Dutch (*de focus*), here it serves as the base for the verb.

#### B. Linking Element (Interfix): `s`
*   **Type:** Bound Morpheme (specifically a **voegklank** or linking sound).
*   **Function:** This is an epenthetic consonant inserted between the stem and the suffix.
*   **Linguistic Explanation:**
    *   **Phonological:** It prevents a vowel clash or clarifies the boundary between the stem and the suffix.
    *   **Morphological:** In Dutch, when a verb is derived from a noun stem ending in a voiceless fricative (like *s*, *f*, *ch*), an extra *-s-* is often inserted before the infinitive ending *-en* to maintain the pronunciation of the stem's final sound.
    *   **Analogy:** This is similar to how compound words use a linking *-s-* (e.g., *liefde* + *s* + *verklaring* = *liefdesverklaring*). In the case of *focussen*, it signals that the stem ends in an *s* sound before the schwa vowel of the suffix begins.
    *   *Note:* This *-s-* is also visible in the past tense (*focusseerde*), confirming it is treated as part of the verbal stem structure in Dutch conjugation.

#### C. Inflectional Suffix: `-en`
*   **Type:** Bound Morpheme / Inflectional Suffix.
*   **Function:** Marks the **infinitive** form of the verb.
*   **Grammar:** In Dutch, the standard ending for the infinitive of weak verbs is *-en* (pronounced as a schwa + nasal, /ə(n)/).
*   **Example:** *lopen* (to walk), *werken* (to work), *focussen* (to focus).

### 3. Linguistic Context & Conjugation

To understand why the morphemes break down this way, it helps to look at how the word behaves in a sentence (conjugation). This proves where the stem ends and the inflection begins.

*   **Infinitive:** *focuss**en*** (Root + Linker + Suffix)
*   **Present Tense (Ik):** *ik **focus*** (Root only; the linker and suffix are dropped)
*   **Present Tense (Jij):** *jij **focust*** (Root + Linker + *-t* suffix)
*   **Past Tense:** *hij **focusseerde*** (Root + Linker + *-eer-* + *-de*)

**Summary:**
The word is a **Dutchified loan verb**. While the English source is simply "to focus," Dutch morphology requires an infinitive ending (*-en*). Because the stem ends in *s*, Dutch orthography and morphology insert a linking *s* to ensure the stem is clearly distinguished from the suffix, resulting in the structure **focus-s-en**.
