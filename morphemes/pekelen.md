From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`pekelen`** can be analyzed through its morphology (structure), semantics (meaning), and etymology (history).

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes:

### 1. Morphological Breakdown

The word consists of two distinct morphemes:

$$ \text{pekel} + \text{-en} $$

| Morpheme | Type | Category | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`pekel`** | Free Morpheme | Noun (Root) | Lexical meaning (Brine/Pickle) |
| **`-en`** | Bound Morpheme | Suffix | Verbalizer & Infinitive Marker |

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Root: `pekel`
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun (*het pekel* or *de pekel*).
*   **Meaning:** Brine, salt water, or the liquid used for preserving food.
*   **Morphological Status:** This is a **free morpheme**, meaning it can stand alone as a word.
*   **Semantic Role:** It provides the core semantic content of the word. In the derived verb, it indicates the *instrument* or *medium* used in the action (i.e., using brine to preserve).

#### B. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Part of Speech:** Verbal Suffix.
*   **Function:** This suffix serves a dual purpose in Dutch morphology:
    1.  **Derivational (Verbalization):** It converts the noun *pekel* into a verb. This process is known as **denominalization** (creating a verb from a noun). Similar examples in Dutch include *boter* (butter) $\rightarrow$ *boteren* (to butter) or *koffie* (coffee) $\rightarrow$ *koffie zetten* (though *koffien* is not standard, the pattern exists).
    2.  **Inflectional (Infinitive):** In Dutch, *-en* is the standard ending for the **infinitive** form of weak verbs (e.g., *lopen*, *werken*, *pekel**en***).
*   **Pronunciation:** It is typically pronounced as a schwa sound /ə/ followed by a nasal /n/, often elided in rapid speech (e.g., *pekel'n*).

---

### 3. Morphological Process: Denominalization

The formation of *pekelen* is a classic example of **zero-derivation** combined with suffixation.

1.  **Base:** The noun *pekel* exists independently.
2.  **Conversion:** The noun is re-categorized as a verbal stem (*pekel-*).
3.  **Inflection:** The infinitive suffix *-en* is attached to mark the verb form.

**Syntactic Usage:**
*   **Transitive:** *Ik pekel de haring.* (I pickle the herring.)
*   **Passive:** *De haring wordt gepekel.* (The herring is being pickled.)
    *   *Note:* The past participle adds the prefix *ge-* and the suffix *-t* (gepekel**t**), following the standard weak verb conjugation pattern.

### 4. Etymology and Cognates

From a historical linguistics perspective, *pekelen* reveals interesting connections between Germanic languages.

*   **Origin:** Middle Dutch *pekel*.
*   **Proto-Germanic:** Likely related to the root *\*pēk-* or *\*pōk-*, associated with soaking or brining.
*   **Cognates:**
    *   **English:** *Pickle*. The English word was borrowed from Middle Dutch or Low German *pekel* in the 16th century.
    *   **German:** *Pökel* (brine) or *pökeln* (to cure/pickle).
    *   **Frisian:** *pekkel*.
*   **Semantic Shift:** In English, *pickle* has broadened to refer to the food item itself (a pickled cucumber), whereas in Dutch, *pekel* refers strictly to the liquid (brine), and *pekelen* is the action. The food item in Dutch is usually referred to as *zuur* (sour) or *inleg* (preserved item).

### Summary
Linguistically, **`pekelen`** is a **denominal weak verb**. It is constructed by taking the noun root **`pekel`** (brine) and attaching the infinitive/verbalizing suffix **`-en`**, signifying the action of treating something with brine. It shares a direct etymological lineage with the English word *pickle*.
