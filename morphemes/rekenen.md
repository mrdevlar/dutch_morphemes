From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`rekenen`** is a regular verb in its infinitive form. It can be analyzed through **morphology** (the structure of words) and **etymology** (the history of the word).

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes:

### 1. Morphological Breakdown

The word consists of two distinct morphemes:

| Morpheme | Type | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`reken-`** | **Root / Stem** | Lexical morpheme (carries the core semantic meaning). |
| **`-en`** | **Suffix** | Inflectional morpheme (marks the infinitive form of the verb). |

---

### 2. Detailed Explanation of Morphemes

#### A. The Root: `reken-`
*   **Category:** Free morpheme (in the sense that it is the lexical base, though it cannot stand alone as a complete word in modern Dutch without inflection).
*   **Meaning:** The core semantic field involves **counting, calculating, arranging, or ordering.**
*   **Etymology:**
    *   It derives from the Old Dutch *rekenen*.
    *   It is cognate with the Old High German *rechen* and the Old English *ræcnian*.
    *   **English Cognate:** The direct English cognate is **"reckon."**
    *   **Proto-Germanic:** Reconstructed as *\*raknōną* (to reckon, count).
    *   **Proto-Indo-European (PIE):** Traces back to the root **\*reg-**, meaning "to move in a straight line," "to rule," or "to straighten." This is the same root found in English words like *right*, *rectify*, *regal*, and *correct*.
    *   **Semantic Shift:** The original sense of "straightening" or "ordering" evolved into "counting" (ordering numbers) and eventually "calculating."

#### B. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Category:** Bound morpheme (cannot stand alone).
*   **Function:** **Infinitive Marker.**
*   **Grammar:** In Dutch, the suffix *-en* is the standard ending used to form the dictionary form (infinitive) of the vast majority of verbs.
    *   *Example:* *lopen* (to walk), *werken* (to work), *rekenen* (to calculate).
*   **Note:** In some dialects or older forms, this might appear as *-n* (e.g., *reken*), but in Standard Dutch, the full *-en* is required for the infinitive.

---

### 3. Derivational Morphology (Word Family)
The root `reken-` is highly productive in Dutch. By adding different derivational morphemes to this root, new words are created:

1.  **`reken` + `-ing` = `rekening`**
    *   *Morphology:* Root + Nominalizing Suffix.
    *   *Meaning:* "Calculation" $\rightarrow$ "Bill," "Account," or "Invoice."
2.  **`reken` + `-aar` = `rekenaar`**
    *   *Morphology:* Root + Agent Noun Suffix.
    *   *Meaning:* "Calculator" (the person or the device).
3.  **`reken` + `-machine` = `rekenmachine`**
    *   *Morphology:* Compound Word (Root + Noun).
    *   *Meaning:* "Calculator" (the device).
4.  **`op` + `rekenen` = `oprekenen`**
    *   *Morphology:* Separable Prefix Verb.
    *   *Meaning:* "To charge to someone" or "to hold against someone."

---

### 4. Semantic Nuances
While the morphological structure is simple, the semantics of `rekenen` cover three main areas, reflecting the historical evolution of the root:

1.  **Mathematical:** To perform arithmetic (*Ik moet de som rekenen* - I must calculate the sum).
2.  **Expectation:** To count on or expect (*Ik reken op je* - I am counting on you / I expect you to be there). This mirrors the English usage of "reckon."
3.  **Attribution:** To consider or regard (*Hij rekent zich tot de vrienden* - He counts himself among the friends).

### Summary
Linguistically, **`rekenen`** is a **monomorphemic root** (`reken-`) combined with a **verbal inflectional suffix** (`-en`). It is a classic example of a Germanic weak verb that retains a clear connection to its Proto-Indo-European root regarding "straightening" and "ordering," which evolved into the modern concept of "calculating."
