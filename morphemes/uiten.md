From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`uiten`** (meaning "to express," "to voice," or "to utter") can be analyzed by breaking it down into its constituent morphemes.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word consists of two morphemes:
> **`uit`** + **`-en`**

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Root: `uit`
*   **Type:** Free Morpheme / Lexical Root
*   **Original Part of Speech:** Preposition / Adverb
*   **Meaning:** "Out," "outside," "from within."
*   **Linguistic Process (Conversion/Zero-Derivation):**
    Historically, *uit* is a preposition (e.g., *Ik kom uit huis* — "I come out of the house"). In the verb *uiten*, this preposition has undergone **functional shift** (or conversion). It has been verbalized to become the lexical stem of the verb.
*   **Semantic Contribution:**
    The core meaning of the verb relies on the spatial metaphor of the preposition. It implies moving something from an internal state (inside the mind or body) to an external state (outside, into the open).
    *   *Internal:* Thoughts, feelings, opinions.
    *   *External:* Speech, writing, action.
    *   *Result:* To "bring out" one's thoughts = to express them.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Proto-Germanic **\*ut**, cognate with English *out* and German *aus*.

#### B. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Type:** Bound Morpheme / Inflectional Suffix
*   **Function:** Infinitive Marker
*   **Meaning:** It carries no lexical meaning of its own; its sole function is grammatical.
*   **Grammar:** In Dutch, the suffix *-en* (or sometimes *-n*) is the standard marker for the **infinitive form** of a verb. It indicates the base form of the verb, which is used after modal verbs (e.g., *kunnen*, *willen*) or as a noun (verbal noun).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Proto-Germanic infinitive ending **\*-aną**.

### 3. Synthesis and Grammatical Behavior

When combined, the morphemes create a **weak verb** (regular verb).

*   **Stem:** Because *uit* is the root, the stem of the verb is **`uit`**.
*   **Conjugation:**
    *   *Infinitive:* uiten
    *   *Present (1st person singular):* ik **uit** (The suffix *-en* is dropped for the singular present tense).
    *   *Past Tense:* ik **uitte** (Regular weak verb past tense suffix *-te* added to the stem).
    *   *Past Participle:* **uitgeuit** (Though rare in modern usage, morphologically it follows the pattern *ge-* + stem + *-t*).

### 4. Comparison with Related Words
To understand the morphology of *uiten* fully, it helps to compare it with the verb **`uitdrukken`** (to express):

*   **`uitdrukken`**:
    *   Morphemes: `uit` (Separable Prefix) + `druk` (Root: press) + `-en` (Suffix).
    *   Here, *uit* modifies the action of *drukken*.
*   **`uiten`**:
    *   Morphemes: `uit` (Root) + `-en` (Suffix).
    *   Here, *uit* **is** the action. There is no secondary root.
