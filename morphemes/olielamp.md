### 1. Morphological Segmentation

### 2. Analysis of Individual Morphemes

#### Morpheme B: `lamp`

*   **Meaning:** "Lamp" (a device for producing light).

*   **Function in Compound:** **Head (Determiner).**
    *   This is the **right-hand head** of the compound. In Dutch (and Germanic languages generally), the rightmost element determines the grammatical category and the core semantic class of the new word.

### 3. Linguistic Features of the Compound

#### A. Right-Headedness
Dutch compounds are **right-headed**.
*   **Structure:** [Modifier] + [Head]
*   **Implication:** The grammatical properties (gender, pluralization) of the whole word are determined by the head (*lamp*).
    *   *Example:* If you make it plural, you pluralize the head: *olielampen* (not *olieslampen*).

#### B. Linking Elements (Fugenelementen)

*   **In `olielamp`:** There is **zero linking element**. The morphemes are juxtaposed directly.
*   **Reasoning:** This often depends on the phonological ending of the first word and historical usage. Since *olie* ends in a vowel and *lamp* begins with a consonant, no linking consonant is required for phonotactic reasons in standard Dutch.

#### C. Phonological Stress
A key linguistic marker distinguishing a compound word from a noun phrase in Dutch is **stress placement**.
*   **Compound (`olielamp`):** Primary stress falls on the **first** element.

*   **Phrase (`olie lamp`):** If one were to say "oil lamp" as two separate words (e.g., "This is an oil lamp"), the stress would typically shift to the second element or be distributed differently.
*   **Function:** The initial stress signals to the listener that this is a single lexical unit.

#### D. Semantic Compositionality
The meaning of *olielamp* is **compositional**.
