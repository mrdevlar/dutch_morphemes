*   **Origin:** Latin *salarium* (money allowance).
*   **Root:** `sal-` (Latin for "salt").
*   **Suffix:** `-arium` (Latin suffix indicating a place or thing associated with).

**Historical Breakdown:**
1.  **`sal-`**: Salt. In ancient Rome, soldiers were sometimes paid in salt or given an allowance (*salarium*) to purchase salt, which was a valuable preservative and commodity.
2.  **`-arium`**: A suffix denoting a connection to the root.
3.  **Evolution:** Latin *salarium* $\rightarrow$ Old French *salaire* $\rightarrow$ Middle Dutch *salaris*.

**Why this matters linguistically:**
This is an example of **morphological opacity**. While the word *originated* from multiple morphemes, the semantic connection between "salt" and "salary" has been lost in the Dutch lexicon. Modern speakers do not associate `salaris` with `zout` (salt).
