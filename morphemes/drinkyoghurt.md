From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`drinkyoghurt`** is a **compound noun** (*samengesteld zelfstandig naamwoord*). It is formed by combining two distinct morphemes to create a new lexical item with a specific meaning.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into two morphemes:

> **`drink`** + **`yoghurt`**

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### Morpheme 1: `drink`
*   **Category:** Verb Stem (*werkwoordstam*).
*   **Source:** Derived from the infinitive verb **`drinken`** (to drink).
*   **Form:** In Dutch compounding, verbs typically lose their infinitive suffix *-en* to become the stem.
    *   *Infinitive:* drink**en**
    *   *Stem:* **drink**
*   **Function:** **Modifier.** It modifies the head noun by specifying the *function* or *intended use* of the yogurt. It indicates that this yogurt is meant to be consumed as a liquid (drunk) rather than eaten with a spoon.
*   **Note:** While `drink` looks identical to the English noun "drink," in this Dutch context, it is strictly the stem of the verb *drinken*. The Dutch noun for "a drink" is *drank*.

#### Morpheme 2: `yoghurt`
*   **Category:** Noun (*zelfstandig naamwoord*).
*   **Source:** A loanword (ultimately from Turkish/Persian *yoğurt*), fully integrated into the Dutch lexicon.
*   **Function:** **Head.** In Dutch compounds, the rightmost element is the "head." This means `yoghurt` determines the grammatical category of the whole word (it is a noun) and the core semantic meaning (it is a type of yogurt).
*   **Internal Structure:** This is a **monomorphemic** unit in Dutch. It does not break down further into meaningful Dutch parts (e.g., you cannot separate `yogh` and `urt`).

### 3. Morphological Process: Compounding
The process used here is **Noun-Verb Stem Compounding** (specifically, a Verb-Stem + Noun compound).

*   **Type:** Endocentric Compound. The meaning of the whole word is a hyponym of the head. (A `drinkyoghurt` *is a type of* `yoghurt`).
*   **Linking Element:** There is **no linking morpheme** (such as *-s-* or *-e-*) inserted between the two parts.
    *   *Correct:* `drink` + `yoghurt` = `drinkyoghurt`
    *   *Incorrect:* `drinksyoghurt` (The linking *-s-* is not used here).
*   **Orthography:** In standard Dutch orthography, compound words are written as a single word without spaces.

### 4. Semantic Interpretation
*   **Literal Meaning:** "Drink-yogurt."
*   **Functional Meaning:** Yogurt that has a liquid consistency suitable for drinking from a bottle or cup, as opposed to "spoonable" yogurt (*eet-yoghurt* or *dikke yoghurt*).
*   **Syntactic Structure:** [Modifier: `drink`] [Head: `yoghurt`]

### Linguistic Note on Spelling
While `drinkyoghurt` is the standard spelling, you may occasionally see variations like *drinkyoghurt* (same spelling) or *yoghurtdrank* (yogurt-drink). The spelling `drinkyoghurt` follows the standard Dutch rule of combining the verb stem directly with the noun without a space or hyphen.
