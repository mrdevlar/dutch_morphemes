From a linguistic perspective, the analysis of the Dutch word **`avontuur`** (meaning "adventure") requires distinguishing between its **synchronic** structure (how it functions in modern Dutch) and its **diachronic** structure (its historical etymology).

Here is the breakdown:

### 1. Synchronic Analysis (Modern Dutch)
In the context of modern Dutch morphology, **`avontuur`** is considered a **monomorphemic loanword**.

*   **Morpheme Count:** 1
*   **Structure:** `[avontuur]` (Root)
*   **Explanation:** To a contemporary Dutch speaker, the word is an indivisible unit.
    *   There is no productive prefix *avon-*.
    *   There is no productive suffix *-tuur* that can be easily attached to other Dutch roots to create new words (unlike *-heid* or *-ing*).
    *   The segment *avont* does not exist as an independent word in Dutch (unlike *avond*, meaning "evening," which is a false cognate in this context).
    *   Therefore, linguistically, it is treated as a single lexical item borrowed from another language.

### 2. Diachronic Analysis (Etymological Morphology)
While it functions as one morpheme today, the word retains the internal structure of its Latin ancestor. If we analyze it **etymologically**, we can break it down into its original morphological components.

**Origin:** Latin *adventūra* (via Old French *aventure*).

**Etymological Breakdown:**
1.  **`ad-`** (Prefix)
    *   **Meaning:** "to" or "toward."
    *   **Function:** Directional prefix.
2.  **`ven-`** (Root)
    *   **Source:** Latin *venīre* ("to come").
    *   **Meaning:** "come."
3.  **`-tūra`** (Suffix)
    *   **Source:** Latin *-tura* (forming nouns of action or result).
    *   **Meaning:** Indicates the action or result of the verb.
    *   **Evolution:** In French, this became *-ture*. In Dutch, it adapted to *-tuur*.

**Literal Etymological Meaning:** "The act of coming to" or "that which happens when one arrives." (Originally referred to things that happened to someone by chance or fate).

### 3. Phonological and Orthographic Adaptation
When the word moved from Latin $\rightarrow$ Old French $\rightarrow$ Middle Dutch $\rightarrow$ Modern Dutch, the morphemes underwent changes:

*   **Latin:** *adventūra*
*   **Old French:** *aventure* (The *d* was dropped, a common phonetic shift).
*   **Middle Dutch:** *avonture* (Adapted to Dutch phonology).
*   **Modern Dutch:** *avontuur*
    *   The final *-e* was dropped.
    *   The *-u-* was lengthened/modified to *-uu-* to fit Dutch vowel spelling conventions for long vowels.

### 4. Important Distinction: False Cognate
It is crucial to note that **`avontuur`** is **not** related to the Dutch word **`avond`** (evening).
*   **`Avond`** comes from Old Dutch *afund* (related to "off" + "end").
*   **`Avontuur`** comes from Latin *advenīre* (to arrive).
*   They look similar due to the Latin *ad-* becoming *av-* in Dutch spelling, but they have different roots.

While you can trace the history of the word back to three Latin morphemes (*ad-ven-tura*), in the current Dutch language system, **`avontuur`** is a single, unanalyzable morpheme.
