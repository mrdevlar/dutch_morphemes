From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`tentoonstelling`** (meaning "exhibition" or "display") is a complex word formed through **derivational morphology**. It is derived from a separable verb.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes, followed by a detailed explanation of each component.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The most granular morphological breakdown is:

> **`tentoon`** + **`stel`** + **`-ing`**

*(Note: Due to Dutch spelling rules regarding vowel length, the `l` is doubled in the written form, resulting in `stelling`.)*

---

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. `tentoon` (Adverbial Particle)
*   **Type:** Bound morpheme (specifically, a separable particle).
*   **Function:** It modifies the meaning of the verb root. In this context, it indicates direction or state ("out," "forth," or "on display").
*   **Syntactic Role:** In the verb form (*tentoonstellen*), this is a **separable prefix** (*scheidbaar voorvoegsel*). In the noun form (*tentoonstelling*), it fuses with the root to form a single lexical unit.
*   **Etymology:** Historically, this is a contraction of two older morphemes: **`te`** (to) + **`tonen`** (to show). Over centuries, *te tonen* fused phonetically into *tentoon*. While synchronically (in modern Dutch) it is treated as one unit, diachronically (historically) it contains two roots.

#### B. `stel` (Verbal Root)
*   **Type:** Free morpheme (Root).
*   **Source:** The verb **`stellen`** (to place, to set, to put).
*   **Meaning:** The core action of placing or positioning something.
*   **Phonology:** This is the underlying stem. In the infinitive verb *stellen*, the vowel is short.

#### C. `-ing` (Derivational Suffix)
*   **Type:** Bound morpheme (Suffix).
*   **Function:** **Nominalizer**. It converts the verbal action into a noun (specifically, a deverbal noun).
*   **Meaning:** It indicates the process, result, or instance of the action.
*   **English Equivalent:** Similar to the English suffix *-ing* (as in *building* from *build*) or *-tion* (as in *construction* from *construct*).

---

### 3. Orthographic and Phonological Note (The Double 'L')
You may notice the spelling is `stell`ing, not `stel`ing. This is due to a specific Dutch orthographic rule regarding **vowel length**.

*   **The Rule:** In Dutch, a short vowel sound in a stressed syllable must be followed by a double consonant when a suffix is added.
*   **Application:** The root is `stel` (short 'e'). When the suffix `-ing` is added, the consonant `l` is doubled to `ll` to ensure the 'e' remains short.
*   **Result:** `stel` + `-ing` → **`stelling`**.
*   **Linguistic Term:** This is known as **consonant gemination** (orthographic doubling).

### 4. Syntactic Context: Nominalization of a Separable Verb
To fully understand the word, one must look at the verb it comes from: **`tentoonstellen`** (to exhibit).

1.  **Verb:** *Ik **stel** de schilderijen **tentoon**.* (I exhibit the paintings.)
    *   Here, `tentoon` separates from `stel`.
2.  **Noun:** *De **tentoonstelling** is mooi.* (The exhibition is beautiful.)
    *   Here, the verb is nominalized. The particle `tentoon` and the root `stel` fuse together, and the suffix `-ing` is attached to the end.
