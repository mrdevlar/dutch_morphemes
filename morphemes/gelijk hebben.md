From a linguistic perspective, it is important to first clarify that **`gelijk hebben`** is not a single compound word, but rather a **verb phrase** (specifically an idiomatic expression). In Dutch grammar, this is often classified as a **light verb construction** or a **fixed phrase** where a noun/adjective combines with a verb to create a specific meaning.

To analyze this linguistically, we must break it down into its constituent words, and then analyze the morphemes within those words.

### 1. Word 1: `gelijk`
**Part of Speech:** Adjective / Nominalized Noun
**Meaning:** Equal, same, right, justice.

| Morpheme | Type | Origin/Function | Explanation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`ge-`** | Prefix | Old Dutch / Proto-Germanic | This is a derivational prefix. In this context, it does not indicate a past participle (like in *gehad*). Instead, it forms an adjective indicating a state or quality. It is cognate with the English prefix *a-* (as in *alike*) or the German *ge-*. |
| **`lijk`** | Root | Proto-Germanic *\*līkō* | This root relates to "body," "form," or "likeness." It is cognate with the English word **like** (in the sense of similarity) and the German *gleich*. <br>*(Note: Do not confuse this with the modern Dutch noun `lijk` meaning "corpse," which shares the same etymological root but diverged semantically.)* |

*   **Morphological Status:** While analyzable, `gelijk` is highly **lexicalized**. Modern speakers rarely perceive the `ge-` as an active prefix; they view `gelijk` as a single unit meaning "equal" or "right."
*   **Syntactic Role:** In the phrase `gelijk hebben`, `gelijk` functions as a **nominalized adjective**. It acts as the direct object of the verb (literally "to have the equality").

### 2. Word 2: `hebben`
**Part of Speech:** Verb
**Meaning:** To have.

| Morpheme | Type | Origin/Function | Explanation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`heb-`** | Root | Proto-Germanic *\*habjaną* | The lexical core of the verb. It carries the primary semantic meaning of possession or holding. |
| **`-en`** | Suffix | Infinitive Marker | This is a bound morpheme that marks the verb as an **infinitive** (the dictionary form). It also serves as the plural present tense marker (e.g., *wij hebben*). |

*   **Morphological Status:** `Hebben` is a **strong verb** historically, though it conjugates somewhat irregularly in modern Dutch (e.g., *ik heb*, *jij hebt*, *hij heeft*, *wij hebben*).

### 3. The Phrase Level: `gelijk hebben`
**Linguistic Classification:** Idiomatic Predicate / Light Verb Construction

When combined, the morphemes of the individual words interact to create a meaning that is not simply the sum of its parts.

*   **Literal Semantics:** "To have equality."
*   **Idiomatic Semantics:** "To be right" (in an argument or factual context).
*   **Syntactic Structure:**
    *   **Verb:** `hebben` (The "light verb" that carries the tense and agreement).
    *   **Object:** `gelijk` (The semantic core that carries the specific meaning of "rightness").

**Why is this linguistic distinction important?**
In Dutch, you cannot translate "to be right" using the verb *zijn* (to be) as in English.
*   English: *I **am** right.* (Verb: *to be*)
*   Dutch: *Ik **heb** gelijk.* (Verb: *to have*)

This is a classic example of a **calque** or a specific **collocation** where the verb choice is fixed by the idiom. If you change the verb (e.g., *Ik ben gelijk*), the sentence becomes grammatically incorrect or changes meaning entirely (implying "I am equal").

### Summary of Morphological Breakdown

1.  **`ge-`** (Prefix) + **`lijk`** (Root) $\rightarrow$ **`gelijk`** (Adjective/Noun: "Equality/Rightness")
2.  **`heb-`** (Root) + **`-en`** (Infinitive Suffix) $\rightarrow$ **`hebben`** (Verb: "To have")
3.  **`gelijk`** + **`hebben`** $\rightarrow$ **`gelijk hebben`** (Idiomatic Verb Phrase: "To be right")

### Conjugation Example
Because `hebben` is the finite verb, it carries the grammatical information (person, number, tense), while `gelijk` remains invariant (it does not change form).

*   *Ik **heb** gelijk.* (I have right / I am right)
*   *Jij **hebt** gelijk.* (You have right)
*   *Wij **hebben** gelijk.* (We have right)
*   *Ik **had** gelijk.* (I had right / I was right) — *Note: `hebben` is conjugated to past tense `had`, `gelijk` stays the same.*
