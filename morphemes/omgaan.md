From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`omgaan`** is a classic example of a **separable compound verb** (*scheidbaar werkwoord*).

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation of its components.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word consists of two distinct morphemes:

> **`om`** + **`gaan`**

*   **`om`**: A separable verbal particle (prefix/adverb).
*   **`gaan`**: The lexical root (verb stem).

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Particle: `om`
*   **Morphological Type:** Separable Prefix / Adverbial Particle.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *umbi*, cognate with German *um* and English *on/around*.
*   **Core Semantics:** Spatially, it denotes "around," "about," or "surrounding."
*   **Function:** In this specific verb, `om` modifies the direction or nature of the action. It transforms the physical act of moving into a metaphorical interaction.
    *   *Note:* In Dutch grammar, `om` is not a derivational prefix (like *be-* or *ver-*) that creates a new lexical category; it is a particle that retains its own stress and can detach from the verb in certain syntactic contexts.

#### B. The Root: `gaan`
*   **Morphological Type:** Verb Stem (Infinitive).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Proto-Germanic *\*gangan*, cognate with English *go* and German *gehen*.
*   **Conjugation Class:** Strong Verb (Irregular).
    *   Present: *gaan*
    *   Past: *ging*
    *   Past Participle: *gegaan*
*   **Core Semantics:** To move, to walk, to proceed, or to function.

---

### 3. Syntactic Behavior (Separability)
From a morphosyntactic perspective, `omgaan` is a **separable verb**. This dictates how the morphemes behave in a sentence structure.

*   **Infinitive / Subordinate Clause:** The morphemes are joined.
    *   *Ik probeer dit probleem goed **om te gaan**.* (I try to deal with this problem well.)
    *   *Ik wil met hem **omgaan**.* (I want to associate with him.)
*   **Main Clause (Finite Verb):** The particle `om` separates and moves to the end of the clause (V2 word order).
    *   *Ik **ga** met mijn vrienden **om**.* (I associate with my friends.)
    *   *Hij **ging** het probleem **om**.* (He dealt with the problem.)

**Morphological Note on the Past Participle:**
When forming the past participle, the standard Dutch prefix `ge-` is inserted **between** the particle and the root.
*   *om* + *ge* + *gaan* = **`omgegaan`**.
*   This confirms that `om` is treated as a separable particle rather than a fixed prefix (compare with fixed prefix verbs like *begrijpen* $\rightarrow$ *begrepen*, where no *ge-* is inserted).

---

### 4. Semantic Composition (Meaning)
The meaning of `omgaan` is **non-compositional** to a degree; it is a lexicalized unit.

1.  **Literal Meaning:** "To go around."
    *   *Example:* *De zon gaat om de aarde.* (The sun goes around the earth.)
2.  **Figurative Meaning (Most Common):** "To deal with," "to handle," or "to associate with."
    *   *Example:* *Hoe ga je om met stress?* (How do you deal with stress?)
    *   *Example:* *Ze gaan met elkaar om.* (They are dating/associating with each other.)

**Linguistic Explanation:**
The semantic shift occurs through **metaphorization**. The physical act of moving *around* something (circumventing or surrounding it) evolves into the abstract act of managing a situation or interacting socially with a person.

### 5. Summary Table

| Morpheme | Type | Meaning | Etymology |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **om** | Particle | Around / About | Old Dutch *umbi* |
| **gaan** | Root Verb | To go / To move | Proto-Germanic *\*gangan* |
| **omgaan** | Compound Verb | To deal with / Associate | Lexicalized Phrasal Verb |

### Conclusion
Linguistically, **`omgaan`** is a **separable phrasal verb** composed of a directional particle (`om`) and a motion verb (`gaan`). Its complexity lies not just in its two parts, but in its syntactic behavior (separation in main clauses, insertion of *ge-* in participles) and its semantic shift from physical movement to social or psychological interaction.
