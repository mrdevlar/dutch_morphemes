To fully understand its morphology, we must analyze it on two levels: the **compound level** (how the two main parts join) and the **derivational level** (the internal history of the second part).

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes:

### 1. Primary Morphological Structure (The Compound)

| Morpheme | Type | Origin | Meaning/Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`audio`** | Modifier (Attributive Noun) | Latin (*audire*) | "Sound" or "Hearing." In this compound, it specifies the *type* of file. |
| **`bestand`** | Head (Noun) | Dutch (Germanic) | "File" or "Stock." This is the core noun that carries the main grammatical weight. |

*   **Compound Type:** This is an **endocentric compound**. The meaning of the whole word is a specific type of the head noun (`bestand`). An `audiobestand` is a type of `bestand`.
*   **Orthography:** In Dutch, compounds are typically written as one closed word (no space or hyphen), which is why it is `audiobestand` rather than `audio bestand`.

*   **Semantic Shift:** Originally, it meant something "standing" or "established." Over time, in the context of administration and later computing, it shifted to mean a "collection of data" or a "file" (something that exists on a storage medium).

### 3. Linguistic Summary

*   **Word Class:** Noun (Substantive).
*   **Morphological Process:** **Compounding** (Noun + Noun).
