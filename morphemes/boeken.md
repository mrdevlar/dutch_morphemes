From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`boeken`** is a fascinating example of **homography** (same spelling) and **morphological ambiguity**. Depending on the context, it can function as either a plural noun or a verb (infinitive or present tense).

In both cases, the word consists of **two morphemes**.

Here is the breakdown:

### 1. The Morphemes
Regardless of the meaning, the word is segmented identically:

| Morpheme | Type | Form | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **boek** | Free Morpheme (Root) | `boek` | Lexical meaning ("book" or "to book") |
| **-en** | Bound Morpheme (Suffix) | `-en` | Grammatical marker (Plural or Infinitive) |

---

### 2. Contextual Analysis

Because the surface form is identical, the linguistic analysis depends on the syntactic category (Part of Speech).

#### Case A: As a Noun (Plural)
*   **Meaning:** "Books"
*   **Structure:** `[boek]` + `[-en]`
*   **Analysis:**
    *   **`boek`**: The lexical root. It is a neuter noun meaning "book".
    *   **`-en`**: An **inflectional suffix**. In Dutch, this is one of the two standard plural markers (the other being `-s`). It indicates that the noun is plural.
    *   **Morphological Process:** Inflection (changing the number of the noun without changing its core meaning or part of speech).

#### Case B: As a Verb (Infinitive or Present Tense)
*   **Meaning:** "To book" / "To reserve" (or "we book", "you book", "they book")
*   **Structure:** `[boek]` + `[-en]`
*   **Analysis:**
    *   **`boek`**: The lexical root. In this context, it is a denominal verb (a verb derived from a noun).
    *   **`-en`**: An **inflectional suffix**.
        *   **Infinitive:** In Dutch, the standard ending for the dictionary form of a verb is `-en` (e.g., *lopen*, *werken*, *boeken*).
        *   **Present Tense Plural:** This suffix also marks the 1st, 2nd, and 3rd person plural in the present tense (e.g., *wij boeken* = "we book").
    *   **Morphological Process:** Inflection (marking the verb form for tense/mood/person).

---

### 3. Linguistic Nuances

#### Syncretism
The word `boeken` exhibits **syncretism**. This occurs when two different grammatical forms share the same morphological shape.
*   The suffix `-en` serves two distinct grammatical functions here: marking **Plural Nouns** and marking **Verb Infinitives/Plurals**.
*   The root `boek` serves two distinct lexical functions: **Noun** and **Verb**.

#### Derivation vs. Inflection
While the word `boeken` itself is formed via **inflection** (adding a suffix to a root), the relationship between the noun *boek* and the verb *boeken* involves **derivation**.
*   Historically, the verb was created from the noun (a process called **denominalization**).
*   However, synchronically (in modern Dutch), speakers treat the verb root `boek` as a distinct lexical entry from the noun root `boek`, even though they are etymologically identical.

#### Phonology (Pronunciation)
*   **`boek`**: Pronounced /buk/ (with a long 'oo' sound and a voiceless velar plosive /k/).
*   **`-en`**: Pronounced as a schwa /ə/ followed by a nasal /n/ (/ə(n)/). In casual speech, the final /n/ is often dropped (apocope), sounding like /bukə/.

### Summary
To summarize the morphology of **`boeken`**:

1.  **Root:** `boek` (Free morpheme)
2.  **Suffix:** `-en` (Bound morpheme)
3.  **Total Morphemes:** 2
4.  **Ambiguity:** The suffix `-en` is **polyfunctional** (it marks both plural nouns and verb infinitives), and the root `boek` is **polysemous** (it relates to both the physical object and the action of reserving).
