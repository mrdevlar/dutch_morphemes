From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`verzameling`** (meaning "collection," "gathering," or "assembly") is a complex word formed through **derivational morphology**. It is built from three distinct morphemes.

Here is the breakdown and explanation of each component.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented as follows:

> **ver-** + **zamel-** + **-ing**

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. `ver-` (Prefix)
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix (Bound Morpheme).
*   **Function:** This prefix modifies the meaning of the root verb. In Dutch, *ver-* often indicates:
    *   A change of state or condition.
    *   Completion or intensification of an action.
    *   A shift from intransitive to transitive (making the action affect an object).
*   **Semantic Contribution:** In the context of *verzamelen*, *ver-* adds the nuance of "bringing together" or "accumulating" rather than just the general act of gathering. It implies a process that results in a specific outcome.
*   **Etymology:** Cognate with the English prefix *for-* (as in *forbid* or *forget*) and the German *ver-*.

#### B. `zamel-` (Root / Stem)
*   **Type:** Lexical Root (Bound Root in this context).
*   **Base Form:** The verb *zamelen* (to gather).
*   **Function:** This carries the core lexical meaning of the word.
*   **Semantic Contribution:** It denotes the action of bringing items or people together.
*   **Etymology:** This root is historically related to the Dutch word *samen* (together). It stems from the Proto-Germanic *\*sam-* (same/together).
    *   *Linguistic Note:* In modern Dutch, *zamel* cannot stand alone as a word; it is a bound root that requires the infinitive ending *-en* to form the verb *zamelen*.

#### C. `-ing` (Suffix)
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix (Nominalizer).
*   **Function:** This suffix converts a verb into a noun (specifically, a deverbal noun).
*   **Semantic Contribution:** It indicates either:
    1.  **The Process:** The act of collecting.
    2.  **The Result:** The physical collection itself (e.g., a stamp collection).
*   **Cognates:** This is directly cognate with the English suffix *-ing* (as in *building* or *gathering*) and the German *-ung* (as in *Sammlung*).

### 3. The Derivational Process
The word formation follows a specific hierarchy:

1.  **Base Verb:** *zamelen* (to gather).
2.  **Derived Verb:** *ver-zamelen* (to collect/gather together). The prefix *ver-* is added to the stem *zamel-*.
3.  **Derived Noun:** *ver-zamel-ing*. The suffix *-ing* is added to the verb stem.
    *   *Phonological Note:* When adding *-ing* to the verb *verzamelen*, the infinitive ending *-en* is dropped.

### 4. Etymological Connections (Cognates)
To understand the word fully, it helps to look at its Germanic family tree:

*   **Dutch:** *Verzameling* (Collection)
*   **English:** *Gathering* (via the root *sam-* meaning "together") or *Collection* (via Latin *colligere*).
*   **German:** *Sammlung* (Collection). Note the similarity in the suffix *-ung* vs. Dutch *-ing*.
*   **Proto-Germanic:** *\*sam-* (Together). This is the ancestor of the English word **same** and the Dutch word **samen**.
