From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`veerkracht`** is a **compound noun** consisting of two free morphemes.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Segmentation
The word can be segmented into two distinct morphemes:
> **veer** + **kracht**

There is no linking element (such as an *-s-* or *-en-*) between the two parts, which is common in Dutch compounding.

---

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### **Morpheme 1: `veer`**
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun (used as the modifier in the compound).
*   **Literal Meaning:** "Spring" (mechanical) or "Feather" (bird).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *fēra*. It is cognate with the English word **feather** and the German word *Feder*.
*   **Semantic Shift:** In modern Dutch, *veer* has two primary meanings:
    1.  **Feather:** (e.g., *een veer van een vogel*).
    2.  **Spring:** (e.g., *een veer in een matras*).
    *   **Context in `veerkracht`:** Here, it refers to the **mechanical spring**. The semantic concept is **elasticity**—the physical property of a material to return to its original shape after being compressed or stretched.

#### **Morpheme 2: `kracht`**
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun (the head of the compound).
*   **Meaning:** "Force," "power," "strength," or "energy."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *kraft*. It is cognate with the German word *Kraft* and the English word **craft** (in its original sense of strength or skill, though English *craft* has shifted semantically).
*   **Function:** As the **head** of the compound, it determines the grammatical category (noun) and the general semantic category (a type of force/power).

---

### 3. Compound Structure & Semantics

#### **Type of Compound**
This is an **endocentric compound**.
*   **Head:** *kracht* (The word is a type of *kracht*).
*   **Modifier:** *veer* (It specifies *what kind* of *kracht*).

#### **Semantic Composition**
The meaning of the compound is not merely the sum of its parts ("spring force"), but a metaphorical abstraction:
1.  **Physical Origin:** Literally, it refers to the tension or restoring force of a mechanical spring.
2.  **Metaphorical Extension:** In modern usage, it describes **resilience**. Just as a spring bounces back after being pressed down, a person or system with *veerkracht* can recover from adversity, stress, or shock.
