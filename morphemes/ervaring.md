From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`ervaring`** (meaning "experience") is a complex noun derived from a verb. It can be segmented into three distinct morphemes: a prefix, a root, and a suffix.

Here is the breakdown:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
**`er-`** + **`var`** + **`-ing`**

---

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Prefix: `er-`
*   **Type:** Inseparable verbal prefix.
*   **Function:** In Dutch morphology, the prefix *er-* (cognate with German *er-*) often indicates a change of state, the completion of an action, or the result of a process. It turns the base action into something that is "achieved" or "undergone."
*   **Context:** It attaches to the root to form the verb **`ervaren`** (to experience).
*   **Note:** Unlike separable prefixes (like *op-* in *opbellen*), *er-* is inseparable. You cannot say "ik ervaar het" (I experience it) in the sense of separating the prefix in the past tense; the past participle remains *ervaren*, not *er-ervaren*.

#### B. The Root: `var`
*   **Type:** Lexical Root (Stem).
*   **Etymology:** This root comes from the Old Dutch verb **`varen`** (to go, to travel, to fare).
*   **Semantic Shift:** This is the most interesting linguistic aspect of the word.
    *   **Original Meaning:** Physical movement or travel (cognate with English *fare* as in "how do you fare?" or German *fahren* "to drive").
    *   **Shift:** Over time, the meaning shifted from "physically traveling" to "undergoing a journey through life" or "passing through events."
    *   **Current Meaning:** To undergo something mentally or emotionally; to experience.
*   **Phonology:** In the infinitive verb *ervaren*, the vowel is long (**aa**). In the derived noun *ervaring*, the vowel is short (**a**). This is a common morphophonemic alternation in Dutch (vowel shortening before certain suffixes or in closed syllables).

#### C. The Suffix: `-ing`
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix (Nominalizer).
*   **Function:** This suffix converts a verb into a noun (specifically, a feminine noun in traditional grammar, though modern Dutch treats it as a *de*-word).
*   **Meaning:** It denotes the action, process, or result of the verb.
*   **Cognates:** It is directly cognate with the English suffix **-ing** (as in *building*, *feeling*) and the German suffix **-ung** (as in *Erziehung*).

---

### 3. Etymological & Historical Context

To understand the word fully, we must look at its Proto-Germanic roots:

1.  **Proto-Germanic:** *\*faraną* (to go, travel).
2.  **Old Dutch:** *ervaringhe*.
3.  **Middle Dutch:** *ervaringhe*.
4.  **Modern Dutch:** *ervaring*.

The semantic evolution mirrors the English word **experience**.
*   **English:** *Experience* comes from Latin *experientia* (trial, proof), from *experiri* (to try, test).
*   **Dutch:** *Ervaring* comes from Germanic *varen* (to travel).

Both languages metaphorically equate "gaining knowledge" with "going through a journey." In Dutch, you literally "travel through" (*ervaren*) events to gain "experience" (*ervaring*).

### 5. Grammatical Note
*   **Gender:** *De* (common gender).
*   **Plural:** *Ervaringen* (adds *-en* to the singular form).
*   **Verb Form:** The base verb is *ervaren* (to experience).
    *   *Ik ervaar* (I experience)
    *   *Ik ervoer* (I experienced)
    *   *Ik heb ervaren* (I have experienced)
