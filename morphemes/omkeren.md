From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`omkeren`** is a classic example of a **separable compound verb** (*scheidbaar werkwoord*). It consists of two distinct morphemes that combine to create a specific semantic meaning.

Here is the morphological breakdown and linguistic analysis.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation

| Morpheme | Segment | Type | Meaning | Origin |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Prefix** | `om-` | Separable Prefix | Around, reversal, change of direction | Proto-Germanic *\*umbi* |
| **Root** | `-keren` | Verb Stem | To turn, to change direction | Proto-Germanic *\*kairaną* |

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Prefix: `om-`
*   **Grammatical Category:** In this context, `om` functions as a **verbal prefix**. While it is historically related to the preposition *om* (meaning "around" or "about"), when attached to a verb, it alters the argument structure and directionality of the action.
*   **Semantic Function:** It indicates **reversal** or **rotation**. It implies that the action of the root verb is being applied to change the orientation or state of the object.
*   **Phonology:** In the infinitive form (`omkeren`), the primary stress falls on the prefix: **`óm`keren**.
*   **Separability:** Crucially, `om-` is a **separable prefix**. In a main clause, the prefix detaches from the verb stem and moves to the end of the sentence.
    *   *Infinitive:* `Ik wil de auto **omkeren**.` (I want to turn the car around.)
    *   *Main Clause:* `Ik **keer** de auto **om**.` (I turn the car around.)

#### B. The Root: `-keren`
*   **Grammatical Category:** This is the **lexical root** (or base) of the verb. It carries the core semantic weight of the action.
*   **Meaning:** To turn, to face, or to direct.
*   **Etymology:** It derives from the Old Dutch *keren*, which comes from the Proto-Germanic root **\*kairaną**.
*   **Cognates:**
    *   **German:** *kehren* (to sweep/turn) or *umkehren* (to turn back).
    *   **English:** There is no direct cognate in modern English for *keren* in the sense of "turn." The English word *turn* comes from Latin (*tornus*). However, the Germanic root is related to the English word *churn* (to agitate/turn) and *quern* (a hand mill, which turns).
*   **Inflection:** As a weak verb (in modern usage), it takes the standard *-te* or *-de* endings for the past tense, though historically it had strong verb characteristics.
    *   Present: *ik keer*
    *   Past: *ik keerde*
    *   Participle: *gekeerd* (Note: In the compound `omkeren`, the *ge-* is inserted between the prefix and the stem).

---

### 3. Morphosyntactic Behavior

The interaction between these two morphemes creates specific grammatical rules in Dutch:

**1. The "Ge-" Rule (Past Participle)**
In Dutch, the past participle of a regular verb usually adds the prefix `ge-` to the stem (e.g., *maken* $\rightarrow$ *gemaakt*). However, when a verb has a separable prefix like `om-`, the `ge-` is inserted **between** the prefix and the stem.
*   *Structure:* `om` + `ge` + `keer` + `d`
*   *Result:* **`omgekeerd`** (reversed/turned around).
*   *Linguistic Significance:* This insertion proves that `om` is treated as a distinct morphological unit that is separable from the root `keer`.

**2. Stress Patterns**
*   **Infinitive:** Stress is on the prefix (**`óm`**keren). This distinguishes it from non-separable verbs where stress is on the stem (e.g., *be`keren* - to convert).
*   **Finite Verb:** In a sentence where the verb is conjugated, the stress shifts to the stem (*Ik **keer**...*), and the prefix moves to the end.

---

### 4. Semantic Composition

The meaning of `omkeren` is not merely the sum of its parts, but a specific **semantic composition**:

1.  **`Keren`** provides the action: *Turning*.
2.  **`Om`** provides the vector: *Around / Inversion*.

**Resulting Meaning:** To turn something so that the opposite side faces forward, or to reverse a process.
*   *Literal:* Turning a physical object (e.g., *omkeren van een kledingstuk* - turning a garment inside out).
*   *Figurative:* Reversing a situation (e.g., *de situatie omkeren* - to turn the situation around).

### Summary
Linguistically, **`omkeren`** is a **separable compound verb** formed by the prefix **`om-`** (indicating reversal) and the root **`-keren`** (indicating turning). Its behavior in syntax (separation in main clauses) and morphology (insertion of *ge-* in the participle) serves as a textbook example of Germanic verbal compounding in the Dutch language.
