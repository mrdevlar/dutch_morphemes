From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`voedingsdeskundige`** (meaning *nutritionist* or *dietitian*) is a complex compound noun. It is formed through a process of derivation and compounding.

Here is the morphological breakdown, analysis, and explanation of each morpheme.

### 1. Visual Segmentation
To understand the structure, we can segment the word as follows:

> **voed** + **ing** + **s** + **deskund** + **ig** + **e**

### 2. Morpheme-by-Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The First Component: `voedings`
This part functions as the modifier (specifying the field of expertise).

1.  **`voed-` (Root)**
    *   **Category:** Verbal Root.
    *   **Source:** Derived from the verb **`voeden`** (to feed, to nourish).
    *   **Meaning:** Relates to feeding or providing sustenance.
2.  **`-ing` (Suffix)**
    *   **Category:** Derivational Suffix (Deverbal Nominalizer).
    *   **Function:** Turns the verb `voeden` into a noun.
    *   **Result:** **`voeding`** (food, nutrition, diet).
3.  **`-s-` (Interfix / Linking Element)**
    *   **Category:** Interfix (often called a *voegklank* in Dutch linguistics).
    *   **Function:** This is a linking element used in Dutch compound nouns. Historically, it represents a genitive marker (possessive "of"), though in modern Dutch it is largely a morphological glue. It connects the first noun (`voeding`) to the second noun (`deskundige`).
    *   **Result:** **`voedings-`** (nutrition-related / of nutrition).

#### B. The Second Component: `deskundige`
This part functions as the head of the compound (the person/agent).

4.  **`deskund-` (Root)**
    *   **Category:** Bound Noun Root.
    *   **Source:** Historically related to **`kunde`** (skill/knowledge) and **`kennis`** (knowledge). Etymologically, it is often analyzed as *des* (of the) + *kund* (knowledge), but in modern morphology, `deskund` is treated as a single bound root meaning "expertise."
    *   **Meaning:** Knowledge, expertise, skill.
5.  **`-ig` (Suffix)**
    *   **Category:** Derivational Suffix (Adjectivalizer).
    *   **Function:** Turns the noun root `deskund` into an adjective.
    *   **Result:** **`deskundig`** (expert, knowledgeable, competent).
6.  **`-e` (Suffix)**
    *   **Category:** Derivational Suffix (Substantivizer).
    *   **Function:** Turns the adjective `deskundig` back into a noun (a person characterized by the adjective). This is a common pattern in Dutch for professions (e.g., *een rijke* = a rich person, *een arme* = a poor person).
    *   **Result:** **`deskundige`** (the expert).

### 4. Syntactic and Semantic Composition

*   **Compound Type:** Endocentric Compound.
    *   The word is **head-final**. The head of the compound is the last element (`deskundige`), which determines the grammatical category (noun) and the core meaning (a person).
    *   The first element (`voedings`) modifies the head, specifying *what kind* of expert.
*   **Literal Translation:** "Nutrition-expert-person."
*   **Semantic Shift:** While `voeding` means "food" or "nutrition," in this context, it refers to the *science* of nutrition. Therefore, the word does not mean "someone who feeds people" (like a cook), but "someone who possesses expertise regarding nutrition."

### 5. Grammatical Notes
*   **Pluralization:** If you were to pluralize this word, the `-s-` interfix remains, and the plural suffix `-en` is added to the very end: *voedingsdeskundigen*.
*   **Capitalization:** In Dutch, all nouns are capitalized. Since this is a compound noun, the whole word is capitalized: *De Voedingsdeskundige* (though usually written lowercase in the middle of a sentence: *de voedingsdeskundige*).
*   **Gender:** The word is a "de-word" (common gender), taking the article *de*.

### Conclusion
**`Voedingsdeskundige`** is a morphologically rich word that demonstrates the agglutinative tendencies of Dutch. It builds a specific professional title by nominalizing a verb (`voeden`), linking it to a root of knowledge (`deskund`), adjectivalizing it (`-ig`), and finally substantivizing it (`-e`) to denote a human agent.
