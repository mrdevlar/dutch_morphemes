From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`vergelijken`** (to compare) can be analyzed through **morphology** (the study of word structure), **semantics** (meaning), and **etymology** (historical origin).

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes.

### 1. Synchronic Segmentation (Modern Structure)
In modern Dutch, the word is segmented into three primary morphemes:

> **`ver-`** + **`gelijk`** + **`-en`**

| Morpheme | Type | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`ver-`** | Prefix | Derivational (changes part of speech/meaning) |
| **`gelijk`** | Root (Stem) | Lexical (carries the core meaning) |
| **`-en`** | Suffix | Inflectional (marks the infinitive form) |

---

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Prefix: `ver-`
*   **Type:** Inseparable Prefix (*onafscheidelijk voorvoegsel*).
*   **Stress:** In this word, the stress falls on the root (`ver-GELIJK-en`), not the prefix. This distinguishes it from separable prefixes (like *op-* in *opbellen*), where the stress would be on the prefix.
*   **Function:**
    *   **Derivational:** It converts the adjective `gelijk` into a verb.
    *   **Semantic:** The prefix `ver-` often indicates a change of state, a process, or a causative action (making something happen). Here, it implies "to bring into a state of..." or "to process."
    *   **Etymology:** Derived from Proto-Germanic *\*far-*, cognate with the German prefix *ver-* and the English prefix *for-* (as in *forbid* or *forget*).

#### B. The Root: `gelijk`
*   **Type:** Lexical Root (derived from an adjective).
*   **Meaning:** "Equal," "same," or "like."
*   **Function:** This is the semantic core of the word.
*   **Internal Structure (Diachronic):** While treated as a single root in modern Dutch, historically `gelijk` is itself complex:
    *   **`ge-`**: An old collective or state-forming prefix (cognate with English *ge-* in *geese* or German *ge-*).
    *   **`-lijk`**: A suffix meaning "body," "form," or "shape" (cognate with English *-like* and the word *lyke* in "lyke-wake").
    *   *Literal Historical Meaning:* "Having the same form/body."
*   **Derivation:** The verb is formed by taking the adjective `gelijk` and adding verbal morphology.

#### C. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix.
*   **Function:** Marks the verb as an **infinitive** (the base form found in dictionaries).
*   **Pronunciation:** In spoken Dutch, this is often reduced to a schwa sound /ə/ (e.g., *vergelijkən*).
*   **Grammar:** This is the standard infinitive marker for the vast majority of Dutch verbs (e.g., *lopen*, *werken*, *zien* -> *zien* is irregular, but *zien* + *en* is not applicable; *zien* is the infinitive itself, but *kijken* follows the rule).

---

### 3. Semantic Composition (How the meaning is built)

The meaning of the word is constructed through a logical progression of the morphemes:

1.  **`gelijk`** = Equal / Same.
2.  **`ver-` + `gelijk`** = To make equal / To bring into equality.
3.  **`vergelijken`** = To compare.

**The Semantic Shift:**
There is a subtle semantic shift here. If you literally "make equal," you are equalizing. However, in Dutch (and German *vergleichen*), the meaning shifted to **comparison**.
*   *Logic:* To compare two things is to check if they are "equal" or "alike" in specific aspects.
*   *Usage:* *Ik vergelijk deze auto met die auto.* (I compare this car with that car.)

### 4. Grammatical Context

*   **Part of Speech:** Transitive Verb (*overgankelijk werkwoord*). It requires a direct object.
*   **Verb Class:** Historically, `vergelijken` is a **strong verb** (irregular), though it has been regularized in many forms.
    *   *Infinitive:* vergelijken
    *   *Past Tense:* vergeleek (Strong) OR vergelijkt (Weak/Regularized)
    *   *Past Participle:* vergeleken (Strong pattern with vowel change *e* -> *ee*)
    *   *Note:* The vowel change in the participle (`vergeleken`) reveals the strong verb heritage hidden inside the regular-looking infinitive.
*   **Word Order:** Because `ver-` is an inseparable prefix, it does not detach in main clauses.
    *   *Correct:* Ik **vergelijk** de prijzen. (I compare the prices.)
    *   *Incorrect:* Ik **ver** de prijzen **gelijk**.

### Summary
The word **`vergelijken`** is a morphologically complex verb. It takes the concept of **equality** (`gelijk`), applies a **process/change** prefix (`ver-`), and marks it as a **verb** (`-en`). While it looks regular on the surface, its internal history and conjugation patterns reveal a deeper Germanic linguistic structure.
