From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`evenmin`** is a lexicalized compound adverb. It is formed by the combination of two distinct morphemes.

Here is the morphological and semantic breakdown:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into two free morphemes:
> **even** + **min**

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### A. `even`
*   **Part of Speech:** Adverb (in this context).
*   **Core Meaning:** "Equal," "same," "just," or "equally."
*   **Function in Compound:** It acts as a **comparative intensifier**. It establishes a relationship of equality between the current clause and a preceding clause.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *ēven*, from Old Dutch *ēven*, related to the Proto-Germanic *\*ebnaz*. Cognates include German *eben* and English *even*.
*   **Semantic Nuance:** While *even* can mean "briefly" (e.g., *wacht even* - wait a moment) or "smooth" (e.g., *een even oppervlak* - a smooth surface), in *evenmin*, it retains its meaning of "equally" or "just as."

#### B. `min`
*   **Part of Speech:** Adverb / Noun (historically a comparative adjective).
*   **Core Meaning:** "Less," "little," or "minus."
*   **Function in Compound:** It provides the **negative quantification**. It indicates a lack or a reduction.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *min*, from Old Dutch *min*, related to the Proto-Germanic *\*minnaz* (comparative of *\*mīnaz*). Cognates include German *min* (archaic/dialectal) and English *minus* (via Latin, though the Germanic root is distinct).
*   **Semantic Nuance:** In modern Dutch, *min* is rarely used as a standalone adverb for "less" (usually *minder* is used), but it survives in fixed compounds like *evenmin* and *minstens* (at least).

### 3. Semantic Composition (The "Math" of Meaning)
When combined, the morphemes create a specific logical meaning:

*   **Literal Translation:** "Equally little" or "Just as less."
*   **Functional Meaning:** "Neither" or "Nor."
*   **Logic:** If Statement A is negative (e.g., "I do not know"), Statement B is *evenmin* (equally little/not either).

It functions as the negative counterpart to **`evenzo`** ("likewise" or "just as much").
*   *Hij is rijk, en ik **evenzo**.* (He is rich, and I **likewise**.)
*   *Hij is niet rijk, en ik **evenmin**.* (He is not rich, and I **neither**.)

### 4. Syntactic and Pragmatic Function
*   **Category:** Negative Adverb.
*   **Usage:** It is used to negate a predicate in a second clause that corresponds to a negation in the first clause. It requires a negative context to be grammatical.
*   **Word Order:** It typically appears at the end of the clause or immediately after the subject/conjunction.
    *   *Example:* "Ik ga niet, en jij **evenmin**." (I am not going, and neither are you.)
*   **Negation:** Unlike English "neither," which often triggers subject-auxiliary inversion (*Neither do I*), Dutch *evenmin* does not trigger inversion on its own; it simply sits in the sentence, often replacing *ook niet* (also not).
    *   *Standard:* *Ik ook niet.*
    *   *Formal/Compound:* *Ik evenmin.*

### 5. Summary Table

| Morpheme | Type | Meaning | Role in Compound |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **even** | Adverb | Equally / Just | Comparator (sets up equality) |
| **min** | Adverb | Less / Little | Negator (sets up the lack) |
| **evenmin** | **Compound** | **Neither / Nor** | **Negative Adverb** |

### 6. Linguistic Note on Lexicalization
While *even* and *min* are both free morphemes that can exist independently, *evenmin* is **lexicalized**. This means the meaning of the whole is not simply the sum of the parts in a productive way; it is a fixed idiom. You cannot arbitrarily combine *even* with other words to create new negatives (e.g., *evenniet* is not a word). It belongs to a closed class of negative adverbs in Dutch.
