From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`extravagant`** is a fascinating case study because it is a **loanword** (leenwoord) of Latin origin that has been fully integrated into the Dutch lexicon.

While it functions as a single lexical unit in modern Dutch, its internal structure reveals three distinct morphemes derived from Latin.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation

| Morpheme | Type | Origin | Literal Meaning | Function in Dutch |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **extra-** | Prefix | Latin | Outside, beyond | Intensifier / Directional |
| **vag-** | Root (Bound) | Latin | To wander, roam | Semantic core |
| **-ant** | Suffix | Latin | -ing (Present Participle) | Adjectival marker |

---

### 2. Detailed Explanation of Morphemes

#### A. The Prefix: `extra-`
*   **Etymology:** From Latin *extra*, meaning "outside" or "beyond."
*   **Linguistic Status in Dutch:** This is a **productive** morpheme in modern Dutch. While it entered via Latin/French, Dutch speakers actively use it to modify other words (e.g., *extra groot* [extra large], *extra kosten* [extra costs]).
*   **Semantic Contribution:** In *extravagant*, it implies going "beyond" the normal or accepted boundaries.

#### B. The Root: `vag-`
*   **Etymology:** From the Latin verb *vagari*, meaning "to wander," "to roam," or "to stray."
*   **Linguistic Status in Dutch:** This is a **bound root**. Unlike *extra-*, the morpheme *vag-* does not exist independently in Dutch. You cannot say "ik vag" (I wander). It only appears in loanwords like *extravagant* or *extravagantie*.
*   **Semantic Contribution:** It provides the core imagery of "wandering." Historically, this referred to a mind or behavior that "wandered outside" the bounds of propriety or reason.

#### C. The Suffix: `-ant`
*   **Etymology:** From the Latin present participle ending *-ans, -antis*.
*   **Linguistic Status in Dutch:** This is a **non-productive** suffix in native Dutch, but it is common in international loanwords (e.g., *student*, *president*, *brilliant*). It functions to turn a verb stem into an adjective (or noun).
*   **Semantic Contribution:** It indicates an active quality. *Extravagant* literally means "wandering outside." In Dutch grammar, it functions as an adjective (e.g., *een extravagante stijl* [an extravagant style]).

---

### 3. Synchronic vs. Diachronic Analysis

To fully understand this word linguistically, we must distinguish between how it is analyzed historically versus how it is processed by a modern Dutch speaker.

**Diachronic (Historical) Analysis:**
As shown above, the word is transparently composed of three morphemes (*extra* + *vag* + *ant*). This explains the etymological meaning: "wandering beyond."

**Synchronic (Current) Analysis:**
For many modern Dutch speakers, the word is **partially opaque**.
1.  **`extra-`** is recognized as a prefix meaning "more" or "outside."
2.  **`-vagant`** is often treated as a single, unanalyzable chunk. Most speakers do not consciously associate *vagant* with "wandering."
3.  Therefore, in modern Dutch morphology, it is often treated as **two morphemes**: *extra-* + *vagant* (where *vagant* is a fossilized stem).

### 4. Semantic Shift (Metaphor)
The meaning of the word has shifted from the literal morphological meaning to a figurative one:
*   **Literal:** "Wandering outside" (of the path/road).
*   **Figurative:** "Wandering outside" (of social norms, budget, or taste).
*   **Current Dutch Meaning:** Excessive, luxurious, wild, or unconventional.

### 5. Dutch Grammatical Behavior
Although the internal structure is Latin, the word follows Dutch morphological rules for inflection:
*   **Adjective Inflection:** When used attributively before a noun, it takes the Dutch adjectival ending *-e*.
    *   *Dat is een **extravagant** plan.* (Neuter noun, no ending)
    *   *Dat is een **extravagante** vrouw.* (Common noun, adds *-e*)
*   **Derivation:** It can form a noun by adding the Dutch suffix *-ie* (from French *-ie*), creating **`extravagantie`** (extravagance).

### Summary
Linguistically, **`extravagant`** is a **Latinate loanword** in Dutch. It consists of the productive prefix **`extra-`**, the bound Latin root **`vag-`**, and the adjectival suffix **`-ant`**. While its etymological meaning is "wandering outside," in modern Dutch, it functions as a standard adjective meaning "excessive" or "unconventional," with the internal root *vag-* being largely opaque to speakers.
