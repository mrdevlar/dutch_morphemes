From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`lastig`** is a clear example of **derivational morphology**. It is formed by combining a lexical root with a derivational suffix to change the part of speech and refine the meaning.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word consists of two morphemes:

> **`last`** + **`-ig`**

*   **`last`**: Free morpheme (Root/Stem)
*   **`-ig`**: Bound morpheme (Derivational Suffix)

---

### 2. Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Root: `last`
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun
*   **Meaning:** Burden, load, tax, duty, or trouble.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Proto-Germanic **\*lastaz** (burden). It is cognate with the Old English *læst* (burden) and German *Last*.
*   **Semantic Note:** It is crucial to distinguish this from the English word "last" (meaning final). While they look identical in spelling, they are **false friends**. The Dutch *last* refers to weight or responsibility, not sequence.
*   **Function in this word:** It provides the core semantic content (the concept of "burden").

#### B. The Suffix: `-ig`
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix
*   **Function:** Adjectivizer. It turns a noun (or sometimes a verb) into an adjective.
*   **Meaning:** It conveys the meaning "having the quality of," "full of," or "characterized by."
*   **Cognates:** This is directly cognate with the English suffix **`-y`** (e.g., *cloud* + *y* = *cloudy*, *mess* + *y* = *messy*) and the German suffix **`-ig`** (e.g., *König* + *ig* = *königlich* - royal).
*   **Phonology:** In standard Dutch, this is pronounced as a voiceless velar fricative /x/ (similar to the 'ch' in Scottish *loch* or German *Bach*), though in some southern dialects or rapid speech, it may soften to /j/ (like English 'y').

---

### 3. Semantic Composition (The Whole)

When combined, the morphemes create a specific semantic shift:

1.  **Literal Composition:** "Having the quality of a burden."
2.  **Actual Meaning:** Difficult, troublesome, awkward, tricky, or inconvenient.
3.  **Usage:** It describes a situation or task that feels like a "burden" to the person performing it.
    *   *Example:* "Deze som is lastig." (This sum is difficult/troublesome.)

### 4. Linguistic Context & Related Forms

*   **Inflection:** If used attributively before a noun, the suffix `-ig` often takes an inflectional ending `-e` (e.g., *een lastig probleem* vs. *het probleem is lastig*). However, the lexical morphemes remain `last` + `ig`.
*   **Verb Derivation:** The root `last` can also be found in the verb **`lastigvallen`** (to pester/harass), literally "to burden-fall upon."
*   **Adverbial Use:** In Dutch, adjectives often function as adverbs without changing form. Therefore, *lastig* can mean "difficultly" or "troublesomely" depending on context.
