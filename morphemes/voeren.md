From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`voeren`** can be analyzed through both **synchronic morphology** (how the word is structured in modern Dutch) and **diachronic morphology** (its historical development and etymology).

Here is the breakdown and explanation of the morphemes.

---

### 1. Synchronic Morphological Analysis
In modern Dutch, speakers perceive the word as a single lexical unit (a verb) consisting of a root and an inflectional ending.

**Structure:** `voer` + `en`

| Morpheme | Type | Function | Explanation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`voer-`** | **Root / Stem** | Lexical | This carries the core semantic meaning. It is a **free morpheme** in the sense that it can stand alone as the imperative form (*"Voer de hond!"* / "Feed the dog!") and is identical to the noun *voer* (feed/fodder). |
| **`-en`** | **Inflectional Suffix** | Grammatical | This is the standard marker for the **infinitive** form of Dutch verbs. It indicates the action in its abstract, non-conjugated state. |

**Conjugation Note:**
*Voeren* is a **weak verb** (regular conjugation).
*   Present: *ik voer*
*   Past: *ik voerde*
*   Past Participle: *gevoerd*
The stem vowel remains stable (*oe* /uː/), distinguishing it from the strong verb *varen* (to sail), which changes vowel (*voer* -> *gevaren*).

---

### 2. Diachronic Morphological Analysis (Etymology)
To understand the deeper structure, we must look at the Proto-Germanic origins. Historically, the word was formed using a derivational process that is no longer productive in modern Dutch.

**Proto-Germanic Reconstruction:** *\*fōrijaną*

| Historical Morpheme | Function | Modern Equivalent |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`*fōr-`** | Root | Related to *\*faraną* (to go, travel). |
| **`*-ij-`** | Causative Suffix | A derivational suffix that turns a verb into a causative ("to make go"). |
| **`*-aną`** | Infinitive Suffix | Proto-Germanic infinitive marker. |

**The Causative Shift:**
The root *\*faraną* means "to go" (cognate with English *fare* and Dutch *varen*). The suffix *-ij-* added a causative meaning: "to make go" or "to lead."
*   **Original Meaning:** To lead, to guide, to conduct.
*   **Semantic Shift:** Over time, "leading an animal" evolved into "feeding an animal" (leading them to food or providing sustenance). This explains why *voeren* means both "to lead/steer" and "to feed."

---

### 3. Semantic Polysemy
Because of its historical roots, the single morpheme `voer-` carries a wide range of meanings in modern Dutch. Linguistically, this is **polysemy** (one word form with multiple related meanings).

1.  **To Feed:** *De kippen voeren* (To feed the chickens).
    *   *Connection:* Derived from the idea of leading animals to food or providing fodder (*voer*).
2.  **To Lead/Conduct:** *Een gesprek voeren* (To conduct a conversation); *Een beleid voeren* (To pursue a policy).
    *   *Connection:* Retains the original Proto-Germanic sense of "guiding" or "leading."
3.  **To Steer:** *Een schip voeren* (To steer a ship).
    *   *Connection:* Closely related to *varen* (to sail), sharing the root of movement.
4.  **To Line (Clothing):** *Een jas voeren* (To line a jacket).
    *   *Connection:* This is a specific semantic extension where the fabric "covers" or "guides" the inside of the garment. The noun *voering* (lining) is derived from this verb.

---

### 4. Cognates and Related Words
Analyzing `voeren` in the context of the Germanic language family highlights its morphological relatives:

*   **English:** *Fare* (as in "How do you fare?" = How do you get along/travel).
*   **English:** *Forage* (To search for food; related to the "feeding" aspect).
*   **German:** *Führen* (To lead/guide). This is the closest cognate to the "leading" meaning of *voeren*.
*   **Dutch:** *Varen* (To sail). Shares the root *\*faraną* but lacks the causative suffix.
*   **Dutch:** *Voer* (Noun). Meaning "feed" or "fodder." This is a **zero-derivation** (conversion) from the verb stem.

### Summary
*   **Word:** `voeren`
*   **Morphemes:** `voer` (Root) + `-en` (Infinitive Suffix).
*   **Type:** Weak Verb.
*   **Core Meaning:** To lead, to guide, or to provide sustenance.
*   **Linguistic Insight:** While it appears as a simple root verb today, it historically contained a causative suffix (*-ij-*) that transformed the concept of "going" into "making go" (leading/feeding).
