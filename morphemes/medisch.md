From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`medisch`** (meaning *medical*) can be analyzed through its morphological structure, etymology, and semantic composition.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word consists of two distinct morphemes:

> **`med-`** + **`-isch`**

*   **`med-`**: The lexical root (bound morpheme).
*   **`-isch`**: The derivational suffix (bound morpheme).

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Root: `med-`
*   **Type:** Bound Root (Lexical Morpheme).
*   **Status:** In modern Dutch, `med-` cannot stand alone as an independent word (you cannot say *"Ik ben med"*). It must be attached to a suffix or appear in a compound.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from the Latin word **`medicus`** (meaning *healer* or *doctor*) or the verb **`mederi`** (meaning *to heal*).
*   **Semantics:** It carries the core meaning related to **medicine, healing, or doctors**.
*   **Related Dutch Words:**
    *   *Medicijn* (medicine/drug)
    *   *Medicatie* (medication)
    *   *Medic* (archaic/academic term for a doctor)

#### B. The Suffix: `-isch`
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix.
*   **Function:** Adjectivalizer. It converts a noun or root into an adjective.
*   **Meaning:** It indicates "pertaining to," "characterized by," or "of the nature of."
*   **Etymology:** This suffix comes from the Latin **`-icus`** (via Greek **`-ikos`**), which entered the Germanic languages through Romance influence. It is cognate with the English suffix **`-ic`** (as in *medic**al*** or *poet**ic***) and the German suffix **`-isch`** (as in *medizin**isch***).
*   **Productivity:** It is a highly productive suffix in Dutch, used to form adjectives from nouns of foreign (mostly Latin/Greek) origin (e.g., *historisch*, *politiek*, *technisch*).

---

### 3. Orthographic and Phonological Notes
*   **Spelling:** The word is spelled with a single **`d`** (*medisch*).
    *   **Reasoning:** In Dutch orthography, a short vowel in a stressed syllable is followed by a single consonant. The stress falls on the first syllable (**`ME`**-disch), and the vowel `e` is short. Therefore, the consonant `d` is not doubled (unlike in a word like *bedden*, where the vowel is short but the syllable structure requires doubling for the plural).
*   **Pronunciation:** /ˈmeː.dɪs/ (IPA). The `e` is a mid-front unrounded vowel, and the `sch` is pronounced as /s/ (not /ʃ/ as in English "sh").

---

### 4. Semantic Composition
When the morphemes are combined, the meaning is compositional:

*   **`med-`** (healing/medicine) + **`-isch`** (pertaining to)
*   **Result:** "Pertaining to medicine/healing."

**Usage Context:**
It is typically used as an attributive adjective before a noun:
*   *Een **medisch** dossier* (A medical file)
*   *Een **medisch** centrum* (A medical center)

---

### 5. Important Linguistic Distinction
It is crucial not to confuse the root **`med-`** (from *medicus*) with the Dutch prefix **`mede-`**.

*   **`med-`** (in *medisch*): Relates to **medicine/healing**.
*   **`mede-`** (prefix): Means **"also," "together,"** or **"co-"** (e.g., *medewerker* = co-worker, *medestrijder* = fellow fighter). This prefix comes from the Old Dutch *mede*, related to the English *with* or *mid*.
