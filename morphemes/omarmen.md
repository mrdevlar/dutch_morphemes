From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`omarmen`** (meaning "to embrace" or "to hug") can be analyzed as a **derived verb** formed through compounding and affixation.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation of its components.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **om** + **arm** + **en**

| Morpheme | Type | Category | Meaning/Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **om-** | Prefix | Preposition / Adverb | "around", "about" |
| **-arm-** | Root (Stem) | Noun | "arm" (limb) |
| **-en** | Suffix | Inflectional / Derivational | Verbalizer / Infinitive marker |

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Prefix: `om-`
*   **Origin:** This is a preposition/adverb meaning "around" or "about" (cognate with English *on* or *around*, and German *um*).
*   **Function:** In this context, it acts as a **prepositional prefix**. It modifies the spatial meaning of the root.
*   **Grammatical Behavior:** In modern Dutch, `om-` in this specific word functions as an **inseparable prefix** (*onafscheidelijk voorvoegsel*).
    *   **Stress:** The stress falls on the root, not the prefix: *om-**AR**-men*.
    *   **Past Participle:** It does not take the *ge-* prefix in the perfect tense.
        *   *Correct:* *Ik heb hem **omarmd**.*
        *   *Incorrect:* *Ik heb hem **ge-omarmd**.*
    *   This distinguishes it from separable verbs (like *opbellen* -> *ik bel op*), where the prefix moves to the end of the clause.

#### B. The Root: `-arm-`
*   **Origin:** This is the lexical root derived from the noun **`de arm`** (the arm).
*   **Function:** It provides the core semantic content.
*   **Semantic Shift:** While the root is a body part, in this verbal construction, it functions metonymically. It does not mean "to arm" (as in weapons), but rather "to use the arms." The image evoked is wrapping one's arms around someone.

#### C. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Origin:** This is the standard Dutch infinitive ending.
*   **Function:**
    1.  **Verbalizer:** Historically, this suffix converted the noun phrase (*om* + *arm*) into a verb.
    2.  **Inflection:** In the current form, it marks the word as the **infinitive** (the dictionary form).
*   **Note:** In spoken Dutch, the final *-n* is often dropped (apocope), resulting in *omarme* (/ɔmˈɑrmə/), though the spelling retains the *-n*.

---

### 3. Linguistic Classification

*   **Word Formation Process:** This is a case of **Conversion** (or Zero Derivation) combined with **Prefixation**. The noun phrase *om de arm* (around the arm) was lexicalized into a verb.
*   **Verb Class:** It is a **weak verb** (regular verb).
    *   Present: *ik omarm*
    *   Past: *ik omarmde*
    *   Participle: *omarmd*
*   **Etymology:** The word dates back to Middle Dutch (*omarmen*). It is a calque or cognate of the German *umarmen* and the English *embrace* (which comes from Old French *embracier*, literally "to put arms around").

### 4. Summary of Meaning Construction
The meaning is constructed compositionally:
1.  **`om`** (around) + **`arm`** (arm) = The action of putting arms around something.
2.  **+ `en`** = To perform that action (verb).

**Literal Translation:** "To around-arm."
**Actual Meaning:** "To embrace."
