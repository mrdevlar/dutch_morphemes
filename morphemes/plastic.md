From a linguistic perspective, analyzing the Dutch word **"plastic"** requires distinguishing between its **synchronic** structure (how it is analyzed by speakers today) and its **diachronic** structure (its historical etymology).

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes in the Dutch word *plastic*.

### 1. Synchronic Analysis (Modern Dutch)
In contemporary Dutch morphology, the word is generally considered **monomorphemic**.

*   **Morpheme Count:** 1
*   **Type:** Free Root Morpheme
*   **Analysis:** `[plastic]`
*   **Explanation:** To a modern Dutch speaker, *plastic* is an unanalyzable unit. It is a **loanword** (specifically an *internationalism*) borrowed from French/English. Speakers do not intuitively segment the word into smaller meaningful parts (e.g., they do not perceive a Dutch suffix *-ic* or a root *plas-*).
*   **Function:** It functions as both a noun (*het plastic* — the material) and an adjective (*een plastic tas* — a plastic bag).

**Why is it not split?**
While English speakers might recognize *-ic* as a suffix (e.g., *poetic*, *musical*), in Dutch, the productive adjectival suffix is typically *-isch* (e.g., *artistiek*, *praktisch*). Because *plastic* ends in *-ic* (an English/French spelling convention) rather than *-isch*, it is treated as a single, fused lexical item in Dutch.
