From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`noemen`** (meaning "to name," "to call," or "to mention") can be analyzed as a **monomorphemic root** combined with an **inflectional suffix**.

Here is the morphological breakdown:

### 1. Segmentation
The word consists of two morphemes:
> **`noem`** + **`-en`**

---

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Root/Stem: `noem-`
*   **Type:** Lexical Morpheme (Free/Bound Root)
*   **Function:** This is the **verbal stem**. It carries the core semantic meaning of the word.
*   **Meaning:** To designate, to call, to name.
*   **Linguistic Note:** In Dutch morphology, this is the base form to which inflectional endings are attached. While it cannot stand alone as a complete grammatical word in modern Dutch (you cannot say "Ik noem" without a subject, nor can you use the bare stem as a noun), it is the lexical core.
*   **Phonology:** The vowel `oe` represents a long /uː/ sound. In Dutch orthography, this long vowel is preserved in the stem.

#### B. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Type:** Grammatical Morpheme (Bound Morpheme)
*   **Category:** Inflectional Suffix
*   **Function:** **Infinitive Marker**.
*   **Explanation:** In Dutch, the suffix *-en* is the standard marker used to form the **infinitive** of verbs (the citation form found in dictionaries). It indicates that the verb is in its non-finite, unconjugated state.
*   **Comparison:** This is distinct from the plural marker *-en* used on nouns (e.g., *hond* → *hond**en***). While they look identical, they are homophonous morphemes serving different grammatical categories (verbal infinitive vs. nominal plural).

### 3. Grammatical Context & Conjugation
To understand the morphemes fully, it helps to see how they interact during conjugation. The stem `noem-` remains constant, while the suffix changes based on person, number, and tense.

*   **Infinitive:** `noem` + `en` = **noemen** (to name)
*   **Present (1st/3rd Person Singular):** `noem` + `Ø` (zero suffix) = **noem** (I name / he names)
    *   *Note:* In the singular present, the infinitive suffix is dropped.
*   **Present (Plural):** `noem` + `en` = **noemen** (we name)
*   **Past Tense (Weak Verb):** `noem` + `d` + `e` = **noemde** (named)
    *   *Note:* The `-en` is replaced by the past tense markers `-de-` (for weak verbs).
*   **Past Participle:** `ge` + `noem` + `d` = **genoemd** (named)
    *   *Note:* The prefix `ge-` and suffix `-d` are added to the stem.
