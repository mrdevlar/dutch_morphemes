From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`stevig`** (meaning *sturdy, firm, solid, robust*) can be analyzed as a bimorphemic word. It consists of a bound root and a derivational suffix.

Here is the breakdown and explanation of the morphemes.

### 1. Morphemic Segmentation

| Word | Morpheme 1 | Morpheme 2 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **stevig** | **stev-** | **-ig** |

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### **A. The Root: `stev-`**
*   **Type:** **Bound Root** (or Lexical Root).
*   **Status:** This morpheme cannot stand alone as an independent word in Modern Dutch. You cannot say *"Dat is een stev"* (That is a stave/firm).
*   **Meaning:** It carries the core semantic content regarding **stability, firmness, or strength**.
*   **Phonology:** The vowel is /ɛ/ (as in *bed*).
*   **Linguistic Note:** Because the root is not a free morpheme, it is considered **opaque** to the modern speaker. While a speaker knows the word means "sturdy," they likely do not actively associate it with a noun or verb base in their daily mental lexicon.

#### **B. The Suffix: `-ig`**
*   **Type:** **Derivational Suffix**.
*   **Function:** Adjectivizer. It attaches to a noun, verb, or root to create an adjective.
*   **Meaning:** It conveys the meaning of **"having the quality of," "full of," or "characterized by."**
*   **Productivity:** This is a highly productive suffix in Dutch. It is cognate with the English suffix *-y* (e.g., *sandy*, *windy*) and the German suffix *-ig* (e.g., *königlich*).
*   **Examples:**
    *   *zand* (sand) + *-ig* → *zandig* (sandy)
    *   *kracht* (strength) + *-ig* → *krachtig* (powerful)
    *   *vriend* (friend) + *-ig* → *vriendelijk* (friendly) *[Note: -lijk is a variant, but -ig is the core pattern]*
    *   *stev-* + *-ig* → *stevig* (sturdy)

### 3. Etymological Context (Diachronic Analysis)

To fully understand the root `stev-`, we must look at the history of the word (diachronic morphology).

*   **Origin:** The word stems from Middle Dutch *stevich*, which evolved from Old Dutch *stēvig*.
*   **Proto-Germanic Root:** It traces back to the reconstructed Proto-Germanic root **\*stēb-** or **\*stōb-**, which meant "to stand firm" or "to be stiff."
*   **Cognates:**
    *   **English:** It is etymologically related to the English word **`stiff`**. Both share the Germanic root regarding rigidity.
    *   **Dutch:** It is related to the Dutch verb **`stijven`** (to stiffen/strengthen) and the adjective **`stijf`** (stiff).
*   **Semantic Shift:** Historically, the word meant "stiff." Over time, the meaning softened and shifted from "rigid/stiff" to "solid/sturdy." While *stijf* retains the meaning of "stiff" (often with a negative connotation of lack of flexibility), *stevig* retains the positive connotation of structural integrity.

### 4. Synchronic vs. Diachronic Perspective

*   **Diachronic (Historical):** The word was clearly formed by adding *-ig* to a root meaning "stiff/firm."
*   **Synchronic (Modern):** In Modern Dutch, the root `stev-` is **fossilized**. There is no active verb *steven* or noun *stev* that speakers use to generate the word. Therefore, while linguists analyze it as two morphemes (`stev` + `ig`), a native speaker often processes it as a single, unanalyzed lexical unit (a monomorphemic word in terms of psychological processing).

### Summary
*   **`stev-`**: A bound root meaning "firmness/stability" (related to *stijf/stiff*).
*   **`-ig`**: A derivational suffix meaning "characterized by."
*   **Combined Meaning:** "Characterized by firmness/stability" → **Sturdy**.
