From a linguistic perspective, **"opzien tegen"** is not a single word, but rather a **phrasal verb construction** (specifically a *separable verb* combined with a *preposition*). In Dutch grammar, this is known as a *scheidbaar werkwoord* (separable verb) that requires a prepositional object.

Here is the morphological and syntactic breakdown of the components.

### 1. Morphological Breakdown

The phrase consists of three distinct morphological units:

| Morpheme | Part of Speech | Etymology | Literal Meaning | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **op** | Particle / Prefix | Old Dutch *up* (cf. English *up*, German *auf*) | Up / On | Separable prefix indicating direction or completion. |
| **zien** | Verb Root | Old Dutch *sēn* (cf. English *see*, German *sehen*) | To see / To look | The lexical core of the verb. |
| **tegen** | Preposition | Old Dutch *tegen* (cf. English *against*, German *gegen*) | Against | Governs the object of the action. |

### 2. Grammatical Analysis

#### A. The Verb: *Opzien* (Separable Verb)
In Dutch, **opzien** is a **separable verb**. This means that in the infinitive form (e.g., *om op te zien*), the prefix **op** is attached to the root **zien**. However, in a main clause conjugated verb, the prefix separates and moves to the end of the clause.

*   **Infinitive:** *opzien* (one lexical unit).
*   **Conjugated:** *Ik **zie** er tegen **op**.* (The verb splits: *zie* ... *op*).

**Linguistic Note on "Op":**
In the infinitive, *op* acts as a **bound morpheme** (prefix). In the conjugated sentence, it acts as a **free morpheme** (particle). This is a characteristic feature of Germanic phrasal verbs.

#### B. The Preposition: *Tegen*
**Tegen** is a preposition that creates a **prepositional verb** (*voorzetselwerkwoord*) when combined with *opzien*. It dictates the case and relationship of the object.
*   It requires the object to be in the prepositional phrase.
*   It carries the semantic weight of "opposition" or "resistance."

### 3. Semantic Analysis (Meaning Construction)

The meaning of the phrase is **idiomatic**; it cannot be derived purely from the sum of its literal parts.

*   **Literal Composition:** "To look up against."
*   **Semantic Shift:**
    1.  **Zien (See/Look):** Implies perception or mental visualization.
    2.  **Op (Up):** In this context, it implies facing something or looking toward a future event.
    3.  **Tegen (Against):** Implies resistance, opposition, or friction.
*   **Resulting Meaning:** To face a future event with a sense of resistance, fear, or reluctance.
    *   *English Equivalent:* To dread, to be apprehensive about.

### 4. Syntactic Behavior (The "Er" Insertion)

A crucial linguistic feature of this construction is the use of the **expletive pronoun "er"**.

Because the object of *tegen* is often abstract (e.g., "the future," "the task"), Dutch syntax prefers to replace the prepositional object with **er** and place it between the verb and the preposition.

*   **Structure:** Subject + Verb + **er** + Preposition + Separable Prefix.
*   **Example:**
    *   *Ik zie **tegen** de vergadering **op**.* (I dread the meeting.)
    *   *Ik zie **er tegen op**.* (I dread it.)

In the second example, **er** functions as a placeholder (pro-form) for the object, allowing the preposition **tegen** to remain adjacent to the separable prefix **op** at the end of the sentence. This creates a "bracketing" structure common in Dutch syntax.

### Summary
*   **Morphemes:** `op` (particle/prefix) + `zien` (root) + `tegen` (preposition).
*   **Type:** Separable phrasal verb with a prepositional object.
*   **Meaning:** To dread or fear an upcoming event.
*   **Syntax:** Requires the separable prefix *op* to move to the end of the clause and often utilizes the expletive *er*.
