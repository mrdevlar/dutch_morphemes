From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`aanvankelijk`** is a complex word formed through derivation. It is an adverb (and sometimes an adjective) meaning "initially," "at first," or "originally."

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation of its components.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **aan** + **vang** + **elijk**

Alternatively, it can be viewed as a two-step derivation:
1.  **Base:** *aanvang* (noun: "beginning")
2.  **Suffix:** *-elijk* (adjectival/adverbial suffix)

However, to understand the full etymology and semantics, the three-part breakdown is more revealing.

---

### 2. Morpheme-by-Morpheme Analysis

#### A. Prefix: `aan-`
*   **Type:** Separable verbal prefix (in the original verb) / Prepositional prefix.
*   **Meaning:** "On," "at," "to," or indicating direction/completion.
*   **Function:** In this context, it adds a sense of "coming into contact with" or "starting upon."
*   **Etymology:** Cognate with the English preposition **"on"** and the German prefix **"an-"**.
*   **Usage:** It appears in many Dutch verbs indicating the start of an action (e.g., *aanvangen* - to begin, *aansteken* - to light/start).

#### B. Root: `vang-`
*   **Type:** Bound root (derived from a verb).
*   **Source Verb:** *vangen* (to catch).
*   **Meaning:** "Catch," "grasp," or "seize."
*   **Semantic Shift:** This is the most interesting part of the word. The literal meaning is "to catch." However, in the compound *aanvangen*, the meaning shifted metaphorically:
    *   *Literal:* To catch hold of something.
    *   *Metaphorical:* To "catch hold" of a task or time -> **To begin / To start.**
*   **Note on Spelling:** The infinitive ending *-en* of *vangen* is dropped to form the stem *vang*.
*   **Distinction:** It is crucial not to confuse this with *val-* (from *vallen*, "to fall"). *Aanval* means "attack" (falling upon), whereas *aanvang* means "beginning" (catching hold of).

#### C. Suffix: `-elijk`
*   **Type:** Derivational suffix.
*   **Function:** Turns a noun or verb stem into an adjective or adverb.
*   **Meaning:** "Pertaining to," "characterized by," or "-ly."
*   **Etymology:** Cognate with the English suffix **"-ly"** (as in *friendly*) and the German suffix **"-lich"** (as in *freundlich*).
*   **Grammar:**
    *   As an **adjective**: *de aanvankelijke plannen* (the initial plans).
    *   As an **adverb**: *aanvankelijk dacht ik...* (initially I thought...).

---

### 3. Semantic Composition (How the meaning is built)

1.  **`aan` + `vangen`** = *aanvangen* (Verb: to begin/start).
    *   *Concept:* To "catch on" to something.
2.  **`aanvangen`** $\rightarrow$ **`aanvang`** (Noun: the beginning).
    *   *Process:* Nominalization (turning the action into a concept).
3.  **`aanvang`** + **`-elijk`** = *aanvankelijk* (Adjective/Adverb).
    *   *Concept:* "Pertaining to the beginning."

**Literal Translation:** "Catch-on-ly" $\rightarrow$ "Pertaining to the start."

### Conclusion
The word **`aanvankelijk`** is a classic example of Dutch compounding and derivation. It encapsulates a metaphorical semantic shift where "catching" (`vangen`) evolves into "starting" (`aanvang`), which is then modified by the suffix `-elijk` to describe the state of being at the start.
