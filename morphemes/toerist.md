From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`toerist`** (meaning "tourist") can be analyzed through **morphology** (the study of word structure) and **etymology** (word origin).

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes, followed by a detailed explanation of their function and history.

### 1. Morphemic Segmentation

The word consists of two distinct morphemes:

> **`toer`** + **`-ist`**

*   **`toer`**: Root / Stem (Lexical Morpheme)
*   **`-ist`**: Derivational Suffix (Grammatical Morpheme)

---

### 2. Detailed Explanation of Morphemes

#### A. The Root: `toer-`
*   **Type:** Free Lexical Morpheme.
*   **Meaning:** In modern Dutch, *toer* refers to a trip, a tour, a round, or a turn (e.g., *een toer maken* = to take a trip).
*   **Status:** While the specific word *toerist* is a loanword, the root *toer* exists independently in the Dutch lexicon. It is a noun.
*   **Phonology:** It is a monosyllabic root ending in a liquid consonant (/r/).

#### B. The Suffix: `-ist`
*   **Type:** Bound Derivational Morpheme.
*   **Function:** **Agentive.** This suffix is used to create nouns that denote a person who performs a specific action, practices a specific art, or is associated with a specific field.
*   **Category Change:** It attaches to a noun (or sometimes a verb stem) to create a new noun denoting a person.
    *   *toer* (noun: trip) + *-ist* → *toerist* (noun: person who takes trips).
*   **Productivity:** The suffix *-ist* is highly productive in Dutch. It appears in many other words, such as:
    *   *artiest* (artist)
    *   *journalist* (journalist)
    *   *pianist* (pianist)
    *   *socialist* (socialist)

---

### 3. Etymological Context (Diachronic Analysis)

While the synchronic (current) analysis treats it as *toer* + *-ist*, the historical origin is important for a complete linguistic picture.

1.  **Origin:** The word was borrowed into Dutch in the 19th century from the French word **`touriste`**.
2.  **French Structure:** The French word was also derived from *tour* (turn/journey) + *-iste*.
3.  **Latin Root:** Both the French *tour* and the Dutch *toer* ultimately trace back to the Latin **`tornus`** (lathe, circle) and the Greek **`tornos`** (circle, compass).
4.  **Nativization:** Although *toerist* is a loanword, Dutch speakers have fully **nativized** it. It follows Dutch phonological rules (stress falls on the first syllable: **TOE**-rist) and Dutch morphological rules (pluralization: *toerist**en***).

### 4. Morphological Process

The process used to create this word is **Derivation**.

*   **Base:** *toer*
*   **Affix:** *-ist*
*   **Result:** *toerist*
*   **Semantic Shift:** The meaning shifts from the abstract concept of a "journey" to the specific "agent" who undertakes that journey.

### 5. Orthographic and Phonological Notes

*   **Spelling:** In Dutch, the root is spelled **`toer`** (with an 'oe' digraph representing the /u/ sound), whereas English uses *tour*. The suffix is spelled **`-ist`**, identical to English.
*   **Pluralization:** When pluralized, the suffix *-ist* remains part of the stem, and the Dutch plural marker *-en* is added:
    *   *toerist* + *-en* → **toeristen**.
    *   This confirms that *-ist* is not an inflectional ending (like a plural marker) but part of the lexical stem.
