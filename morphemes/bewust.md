From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`bewust`** (meaning *conscious*, *aware*, or *deliberate*) is a morphologically complex word derived from a verb. It consists of two primary morphemes: a prefix and a root.

Here is the breakdown and explanation of the morphemes in **`bewust`**.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented as follows:
> **be-** + **wust**

### 2. Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Prefix: `be-`
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix.
*   **Origin:** Proto-Germanic *\*bi-*, cognate with English *be-* (as in *beseech*, *befriend*) and German *be-*.
*   **Function:** In Dutch, the prefix *be-* is highly productive. It generally serves to:
    1.  **Transitivize:** Turn an intransitive verb into a transitive one (e.g., *slapen* "to sleep" $\rightarrow$ *beslapen* "to sleep on/over").
    2.  **Perfectivize:** Indicate the completion or thoroughness of an action.
    3.  **Adjectivalize:** In this specific case, combined with a participle stem, it creates an adjective indicating a state of being "endowed with" or "affected by" the root concept.
*   **Semantic Contribution:** Here, *be-* intensifies the root. It suggests that the state of "knowing" is fully realized or pervasive.

#### B. The Root: `wust`
*   **Type:** Bound Root (fossilized Past Participle Stem).
*   **Origin:** This is the tricky part. *Wust* is not a standalone verb in modern Dutch. It is the past participle stem of the strong verb **`weten`** (to know).
*   **Etymology:**
    *   **Modern Dutch:** *weten* (to know).
    *   **Old Dutch:** *witan*.
    *   **Proto-Germanic:** *\*witaną*.
    *   **Proto-Indo-European:** *\*weyd-* (to see, to know).
*   **Grammatical Form:** In Old and Middle Dutch, the past participle of *weten* was *gewust* (similar to how *weten* is related to the English *wit*). Over time, the *ge-* prefix was dropped in this specific compound, leaving *wust*.
*   **Semantic Contribution:** It carries the core meaning of "knowledge," "cognition," or "awareness."

### 3. Historical Development (Diachronic View)
To understand why *wust* looks the way it does, we must look at the conjugation of the verb *weten*:
*   **Infinitive:** *weten*
*   **Past Tense (Singular):** *wist*
*   **Past Participle:** *geweten* (modern) / *gewust* (archaic/dialectal)

The word **`bewust`** is essentially a fossilized past participle. Historically, it meant "having been made known" or "having knowledge."
*   **Old Dutch:** *biwust*
*   **Middle Dutch:** *bewust*
*   **Modern Dutch:** *bewust*

### 4. Semantic Shift
The meaning evolved from a literal state of knowledge to a psychological state:
1.  **Literal:** "Known" or "Made known."
2.  **Psychological:** "In the mind," "Perceived."
3.  **Modern:** "Conscious" or "Aware."

When you say *Ik ben me bewust van...* ("I am aware of..."), you are literally saying "I am in a state of having knowledge of..."

### 5. Related Morphological Families
Understanding *bewust* helps explain other Dutch words:

*   **`weten`**: The base verb (to know).
*   **`wist`**: The past tense of *weten* (knew).
*   **`onbewust`**: *on-* (negation prefix) + *bewust* = Unconscious / Unintentional.
*   **`bewusteloos`**: *bewust* + *-eloos* (suffix meaning "without") = Unconscious (physically).
*   **`bewustwording`**: *bewust* + *-wording* (suffix meaning "becoming") = Awareness / Awakening.

### 6. English Cognates
While English does not have the word *bewust*, the roots are shared:
*   **Root:** The *wust* root is cognate with the English word **`wit`** (as in "wits" or "I wot" in archaic English). Both come from *\*weyd-* (to see/know).
*   **Prefix:** The *be-* prefix is cognate with English **`be-`**.
*   **German:** The German word **`bewusst`** is identical in form and meaning.
