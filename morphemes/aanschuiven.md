From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`aanschuiven`** is a complex verb formed through derivation and inflection. It is classified as a **separable verb** (*scheidbaar werkwoord*).

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation of its components.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **`aan`** + **`schuif`** + **`-en`**

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Prefix: `aan-`
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix (Separable).
*   **Origin:** Preposition/Adverb.
*   **Function:** In this context, `aan` functions as a directional or aspectual prefix. It modifies the meaning of the root verb to indicate movement **towards** something, contact, or initiation.
*   **Semantic Contribution:** It adds the nuance of "up to," "onto," or "joining." Without it, the verb would simply mean "to slide." With it, the action is directed toward a specific point or group.
*   **Syntactic Behavior:** Because it is a separable prefix, in a main clause (V2 word order), this morpheme detaches from the root and moves to the end of the sentence (e.g., *Ik **schuif** aan* vs. *Ik wil **aanschuiven***).

#### B. The Root: `schuif-`
*   **Type:** Verbal Stem (Lexical Root).
*   **Origin:** Old Dutch *scooven* / Middle Dutch *schuiven*.
*   **Function:** This carries the core lexical meaning of the word.
*   **Semantic Contribution:** It means "to slide," "to push," or "to move along a surface."
*   **Phonology:** The stem contains the diphthong *ui* (/œy/), which is characteristic of Dutch.
*   **Cognates:** It is etymologically related to the English verb **"shove"** (though English *shove* implies more force, while Dutch *schuiven* implies sliding) and the German verb **"schieben"**.

#### C. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix.
*   **Function:** This is the standard marker for the **infinitive** form of Dutch verbs.
*   **Grammar:** It indicates that the verb is in its base, unconjugated form (used after modal verbs or as a noun).
*   **Note:** In the past participle form (*aangeschoven*), this suffix is replaced by the participial suffix `-en` (which looks the same but is part of the participle construction) and the prefix `ge-` is inserted between `aan` and `schuif`.

### 3. Semantic Composition (Meaning Construction)
The meaning of the compound is compositional but has developed idiomatic nuances:

1.  **Literal:** To slide something up to something else (e.g., *De stoel aanschuiven* – "To slide the chair up [to the table]").
2.  **Figurative/Idiomatic:** To join a queue or a group.
    *   *In de rij aanschuiven:* To join the line (literally: to slide up into the line).
    *   *Aanschuiven aan tafel:* To join the table for a meal.

### 4. Morphosyntactic Feature: Separability
From a morphological standpoint, `aanschuiven` is a classic example of a **separable compound verb**. This is evidenced by how the morphemes behave in different tenses:

*   **Infinitive:** `aan` + `schuif` + `en` (e.g., *Ik ga **aanschuiven***)
*   **Present Tense:** `schuif` + `aan` (e.g., *Ik **schuif** **aan***)
*   **Past Participle:** `aan` + `ge` + `schuif` + `en` (e.g., *Ik heb **aangeschoven***)

The insertion of `ge-` between the prefix and the stem in the past participle is the definitive morphological proof that `aan` is a separable prefix rather than an inseparable one (like `be-` or `ver-`, where `ge-` is not inserted, e.g., *bepalen*, *vergeten*).
