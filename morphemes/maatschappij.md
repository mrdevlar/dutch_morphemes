From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`maatschappij`** (meaning "society") is a complex noun formed through compounding and derivation. It consists of three distinct morphemes.

Here is the breakdown:

### 1. The Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented as follows:
> **`maat`** + **`schap`** + **`ij`**

*(Note: The double **`p`** in the middle is an orthographic feature to preserve vowel length, not a separate morpheme.)*

---

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. Root: `maat`
*   **Type:** Free Morpheme (Noun)
*   **Meaning:** Companion, partner, mate.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *mate*, Old High German *gimazo*.
*   **Cognates:** This is cognate with the English word **"mate"** (as in roommate or teammate).
*   **Linguistic Note:** In Dutch, *maat* is a homonym. It also means "measure" (e.g., *maatwerk* = custom work). However, in *maatschappij*, it strictly refers to the "companion/partner" meaning.
*   **Function:** It provides the core semantic concept of human association.

#### B. Suffix 1: `-schap`
*   **Type:** Bound Morpheme (Derivational Suffix)
*   **Meaning:** State, condition, quality, or collective group.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *-scap*.
*   **Cognates:** This is cognate with the English suffix **"-ship"** (e.g., friend**ship**, partner**ship**, leader**ship**).
*   **Function:** It turns the noun *maat* (companion) into an abstract noun *maatschap* (partnership/companionship). It indicates the *state* of being companions.

#### C. Suffix 2: `-ij`
*   **Type:** Bound Morpheme (Derivational Suffix)
*   **Meaning:** Place, institution, collective activity, or state of being.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *-ij*, related to Old High German *-ī*.
*   **Cognates:** This is cognate with the English suffixes **"-y"** or **"-ery"** (e.g., baker**y**, factory, compan**y**).
*   **Function:** It nominalizes the abstract concept further. While *maatschap* implies the relationship, *maatschappij* implies the **collective body** or the **organization** resulting from that relationship. It shifts the meaning from "partnership" to "the Society" (the collective of all partners).

---

### 3. Orthographic Note: The Double "P"
You may notice the spelling is `maatschap**p**ij` with a double **p**, but the morphemes are `schap` + `ij`.

*   **The Rule:** In Dutch orthography, when a suffix starting with a vowel (like `-ij`) is added to a stem ending in a single consonant preceded by a **short vowel**, the consonant is doubled to keep the vowel short.
*   **Application:** The stem is *maatschap*. The vowel **`a`** in *schap* is short. Therefore, when adding *-ij*, the **`p`** is doubled to *schappij*.
*   **Linguistic Significance:** This is a spelling convention, not a morphological addition. The morphological boundary remains between `schap` and `ij`.

---

### 4. Semantic Evolution
The word demonstrates a classic semantic broadening:

1.  **`maat`**: A specific individual companion.
2.  **`maatschap`**: The relationship between companions (partnership).
3.  **`maatschappij`**: The entire collective of people living together under shared rules (Society).

### Conclusion
Linguistically, *maatschappij* is a **derived compound**. It literally translates to "the institution/state of companions." It highlights the Germanic linguistic tendency to build complex social concepts by stacking suffixes onto a root word denoting human connection.
