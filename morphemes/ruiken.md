### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Root/Stem: `ruik-`
*   **Type:** Lexical Root (Free Morpheme base).
*   **Category:** Verbal Stem.
*   **Meaning:** This morpheme carries the core semantic content of the word. It refers to the sensory perception of odor or the act of emitting an odor.
*   **Phonology:** In standard Dutch, this is pronounced /rœy̯k/. The grapheme `ui` represents the diphthong /œy̯/, and `k` represents the voiceless velar plosive /k/.
*   **Etymology:**
    *   **Old Dutch:** *rūken*
    *   **Old High German:** *rūchen*
    *   **Proto-Germanic:** *\*rūkaną*
    *   **Proto-Indo-European (PIE):** Likely derived from the root *\*reuk-*, meaning "to smoke" or "to smell."
*   **Cognates:**
    *   **German:** *riechen* (to smell)
    *   **English:** *reek* (historically meant "to smell" generally, though in Modern English it has shifted to mean "to smell strongly/badly").
    *   **Swedish:** *ryka* (to smoke)

#### B. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix (Bound Morpheme).
*   **Category:** Infinitive Marker.
*   **Function:** In Dutch morphology, the suffix *-en* is the standard marker for the **infinitive** form of a verb. It indicates that the verb is in its base, unconjugated form (the form found in a dictionary).
*   **Grammar:** Unlike in Romance languages (like French or Spanish), the Dutch infinitive does not change based on person or number. It serves as the citation form.
    *   *Example:* *Ik ruik* (I smell) vs. *Ik wil ruiken* (I want **to smell**).
*   **Variation:** In some Dutch dialects or older forms of the language, this suffix might appear as *-e* (e.g., *ruike*), but *-en* is the standard in Modern Standard Dutch.

### 4. Semantic Nuance
From a semantic perspective, `ruiken` is a **neutral** verb regarding the quality of the smell.
*   It can mean "to perceive an odor" (e.g., *Ik ruik bloemen* - I smell flowers).
*   It can mean "to emit an odor" (e.g., *De kaas ruikt sterk* - The cheese smells strongly).
*   Unlike English, where "smell" can be ambiguous, or "reek" which implies a bad smell, Dutch uses `ruiken` for both pleasant and unpleasant odors unless modified by an adjective (e.g., *goed ruiken* vs. *slecht ruiken*).

### 5. Derivational Potential
The root `ruik-` can also serve as the base for other derived words, demonstrating its productivity in the lexicon:
*   **`geur`** (noun): Scent/odor (related etymologically but distinct morphologically).
*   **`reuk`** (noun): Sense of smell (e.g., *de reuk* - the smell).
*   **`ontruiken`**: To smell out / detect (prefix *ont-* + root *ruik* + suffix *-en*).
*   **`verruiken`**: To smell out / to detect (prefix *ver-* + root *ruik* + suffix *-en*).
