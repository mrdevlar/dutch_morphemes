From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`regenbui`** is a **compound noun** (*samengesteld zelfstandig naamwoord*). It consists of two free morphemes (roots) that are joined together to create a new lexical item.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Morpheme Segmentation
The word can be segmented into two distinct morphemes:
> **regen** + **bui**

There are no inflectional affixes (like plural markers or case endings) within the singular form of the word itself.

---

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### **Morpheme 1: `regen`**
*   **Form:** *regen*
*   **Lexical Category:** Noun (Zelfstandig naamwoord).
*   **Meaning:** "Rain."
*   **Grammatical Function:** **Modifier** (Determiner).
    *   In Dutch compounds, the first element modifies the second. It specifies the *type* of the head noun.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *regen*, from Proto-Germanic *\*reganaz*, and ultimately Proto-Indo-European *\*h₃reǵ-* (to rain). It is cognate with English *rain* and German *Regen*.
*   **Note on Number:** Although *regen* ends in *-en* (which often signals a plural in Dutch), it is grammatically **singular** and uncountable in this context (e.g., *de regen*, not *de regens*).

#### **Morpheme 2: `bui`**
*   **Form:** *bui*
*   **Lexical Category:** Noun (Zelfstandig naamwoord).
*   **Meaning:** "Shower," "squall," or "burst" (specifically regarding weather).
*   **Grammatical Function:** **Head** (The semantic center).
    *   The entire compound is a hyponym of this word. A *regenbui* is a specific kind of *bui*.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *boe* or *bui*. The origin is likely onomatopoeic or related to the verb *buien* (to blow/storm). It is related to Low German *Bui*.
*   **Inflection:** This morpheme carries the plural inflection for the whole word.
    *   Singular: *bui*
    *   Plural: *buien*

---

### 3. Compound Morphology Rules

#### **Endocentric Structure**
This is an **endocentric compound**. The meaning of the whole word is contained within the head (*bui*).
*   *Regenbui* = A type of *bui* (specifically one involving *regen*).

#### **Linking Elements (Fugue)**
In Dutch, compounds sometimes require a linking element (like *-s-*, *-en-*, or *-e-*).
*   **`regenbui`** uses **zero-linking**. The two nouns are juxtaposed directly without an interfix.
*   *Contrast:* *Slaap**s**kamer* (bedroom) requires an *-s-*. *Regenbui* does not.

#### **Pluralization**
In Dutch compounds, the plural marker attaches to the **head** (the last element), not the modifier.
*   Singular: *de regenbui*
*   Plural: *de regenbui**en***
*   *Note:* The first part (*regen*) remains unchanged.

#### **Stress Pattern**
Dutch compounds typically carry primary stress on the **first syllable** of the first element.
*   Pronunciation: **RE**-gen-bui (IPA: /ˈreːɣə(n)ˌbœy̯/)
*   This distinguishes it from a phrase where stress might be distributed differently.

---

### 4. Semantic Composition
The meaning is compositional but specific:
*   **Literal:** Rain + Shower.
*   **Semantic Shift:** While *regen* implies continuous precipitation and *bui* implies a sudden burst, the compound *regenbui* specifically refers to a **short, heavy period of rain** (a rain shower), distinguishing it from steady, light rain (*motregen*) or continuous rain (*buienregen* is also used, but *regenbui* is the standard term for a shower).
