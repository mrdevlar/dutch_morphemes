From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`computerscherm`** is a classic example of a **compound noun** (*samengesteld zelfstandig naamwoord*).

Here is the breakdown of its morphemes, including their etymological origins, grammatical function, and semantic contribution.

### 1. Visual Segmentation
To analyze the morphology, we first segment the word into its constituent parts:

> **`computer`** + **`s`** + **`scherm`**

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### A. `computer` (Free Morpheme / Loanword)
*   **Type:** Free morpheme (in the context of modern Dutch).
*   **Status:** Loanword (from English, originally Latin).
*   **Function:** Modifier (Dependent).
*   **Meaning:** Refers to the electronic device.
*   **Internal Etymology:** While synchronically (in current usage) treated as a single unit by Dutch speakers, diachronically (historically) it contains two morphemes:
    *   **`comput-`**: Root from Latin *computare* (to calculate/count).
    *   **`-er`**: Agentive suffix (one who/that which does).
    *   *Literal meaning:* "That which calculates."
*   **Note:** In modern Dutch morphology, `comput-` is not a productive root. A speaker cannot create new words using `comput-` (e.g., you cannot say `computen` for "to calculate"). Therefore, for the purpose of Dutch morphological analysis, `computer` is treated as **one indivisible morpheme**.

#### B. `s` (Interfix / Linking Element)
*   **Type:** Bound morpheme (specifically an **interfix** or *voegklank*).
*   **Function:** Phonological and morphological linker.
*   **Meaning:** None (semantically empty).
*   **Explanation:** In Dutch compounding, an `-s-` is often inserted between two nouns to facilitate pronunciation or to mark a genitive relationship (fossilized from the old genitive case).
    *   It does **not** mark the plural here (compare singular `computerscherm` vs. plural `computerschermen`; the `s` remains).
    *   It is a standard linking element for many loanwords ending in a vowel or specific consonants (e.g., *auto`s* + *s` + *schade* → *autoschade*).

#### C. `scherm` (Free Morpheme / Native Root)
*   **Type:** Free morpheme.
*   **Status:** Native Dutch word.
*   **Function:** Head of the compound.
*   **Meaning:** Screen, shield, or cover.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *scherm*, related to the verb *schermen* (to shield or protect).
*   **Semantic Role:** This is the **semantic head**. The compound refers to a type of *scherm* (screen), not a type of *computer*.

### 3. Syntactic and Semantic Structure

#### Endocentric Compound
`Computerscherm` is an **endocentric compound**. This means the meaning of the whole word is a hyponym (a specific type) of the head element.
*   **Head:** `scherm` (Screen)
*   **Modifier:** `computer` (Specifying the type of screen)
*   **Logic:** A `computerscherm` is a `scherm` (specifically one for a computer).

#### Word Order (Head-Final)
Dutch is a **head-final** language regarding compounds. The most important part of the meaning (the category) comes at the **end** of the word.
*   English: *Computer* (Modifier) + *Screen* (Head)
*   Dutch: *Computer* (Modifier) + *Scherm* (Head)
*   *Note:* In this specific case, the word order is identical to English, but the grammatical logic remains Dutch.

### 4. Phonological Features (Stress)
In Dutch compound nouns, the primary lexical stress typically falls on the **first element** of the compound.
*   **Stress Pattern:** **`COM`**puter-scherm
*   **Secondary Stress:** `scherm`
*   **Contrast:** If you were to say the words separately ("a computer and a screen"), the stress would be balanced. In the compound, the first element dominates.

Linguistically, **`computerscherm`** is a **head-final compound noun** consisting of a loanword modifier (`computer`), a linking interfix (`s`), and a native Dutch head (`scherm`). While `computer` has internal etymological structure (`comput` + `er`), it functions as a single atomic morpheme in the Dutch morphological system.
