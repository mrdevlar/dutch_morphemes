From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`toevoegen`** is a complex verb formed through derivation and inflection. It is classified as a **separable verb** (*scheidbaar werkwoord*).

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation of its constituent morphemes.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **toe** + **voeg** + **-en**

---

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. `toe` (Prefix / Particle)
*   **Type:** Separable Verbal Prefix (Partikel).
*   **Category:** Derivational Morpheme.
*   **Original Meaning:** Historically, *toe* functions as a preposition or adverb meaning "towards," "closed," "shut," or "on."
*   **Function:** In this context, it modifies the meaning of the root verb. While the root *voegen* implies joining or fitting, the prefix *toe* adds the directional or completive sense of "adding something **to** an existing whole."
*   **Syntactic Behavior:** This is the defining feature of the word. In Dutch main clauses, this prefix separates from the verb stem and moves to the end of the clause (e.g., *Ik voeg het bestand **toe***).

#### B. `voeg` (Root / Stem)
*   **Type:** Lexical Root (Lexeme).
*   **Category:** Free Morpheme (when standing alone as a stem).
*   **Meaning:** To join, to fit, to unite, or to join together.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *fōgian*, related to the German *fügen* and English *fudge* (in the sense of fitting together) or *join*.
*   **Function:** This carries the core semantic content of the action. Without the prefix, *voegen* implies the act of joining two things (e.g., *voeg de tegels* - join the tiles).

#### C. `-en` (Suffix)
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix.
*   **Category:** Inflectional Morpheme.
*   **Function:** Marks the **infinitive** form of the verb.
*   **Grammar:** In Dutch, the standard ending for the infinitive of regular and many irregular verbs is *-en*. It indicates that the verb is in its base, unconjugated form (e.g., *om te toevoegen* - to add).
*   **Note:** In some dialects or specific verbs, this may reduce to *-n*, but in standard Dutch, *toevoegen* retains the full *-en*.

---

### 3. Linguistic Summary

| Morpheme | Type | Function | Meaning Contribution |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **toe-** | Prefix (Particle) | Derivational | Adds direction/completion ("to" or "on") |
| **-voeg-** | Root | Lexical | Core action ("join" or "fit") |
| **-en** | Suffix | Inflectional | Marks Infinitive form |

### 4. Syntactic Implications (Separability)
From a morphosyntactic perspective, the presence of the prefix **`toe`** dictates how the word behaves in a sentence structure.

*   **Infinitive:** *Ik wil het **toevoegen**.* (I want to add it.)
*   **Present Tense (Main Clause):** *Ik **voeg** het **toe**.* (I add it.)
    *   *Note:* The morphemes split. The conjugated root (*voeg*) stays in the second position, while the prefix (*toe*) moves to the end.
*   **Subordinate Clause:** *...dat ik het **toevoeg**.* (...that I add it.)
    *   *Note:* The morphemes recombine at the end of the clause.

### 5. Semantic Shift
The combination of these morphemes creates a specific semantic shift known as **idiomatization**:
*   *Voegen* = To join/fit (physical connection).
*   *Toevoegen* = To add (abstract or physical inclusion into a set/list).

By analyzing the morphemes, we see that the word literally translates to "to join-towards," which linguistically evolved into the modern meaning of "to add."
