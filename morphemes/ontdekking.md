From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`ontdekking`** (meaning "discovery") is a complex word formed through **derivational morphology**. It is built from a root, a prefix, and a suffix.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes, followed by a detailed explanation of their function and history.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation

The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **ont-** + **dek** + **-ing**

*(Note: The double 'k' in the middle is an orthographic feature resulting from Dutch phonological rules, discussed below.)*

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Prefix: `ont-`
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix.
*   **Function:** Reversative / Privative.
*   **Meaning:** This prefix indicates the removal of something, the undoing of an action, or a change of state. It is cognate with the German prefix *ent-* and conceptually similar to the English *un-* (as in *uncover*) or Latin *ex-*.
*   **In this context:** It negates the action of the root. If the root means "to cover," adding *ont-* changes the meaning to "to uncover."

#### B. The Root: `dek`
*   **Type:** Lexical Root (Free Morpheme).
*   **Meaning:** "Cover," "lid," or "deck."
*   **Etymology:** This comes from the Proto-Germanic **\*thakam* (covering).
*   **Cognates:**
    *   English: *deck*, *thatch*.
    *   German: *Decke* (ceiling/blanket), *decken* (to cover).
*   **Phonology:** In Dutch, this root contains a short vowel (/ɛ/). In Dutch orthography, a short vowel in a stressed, closed syllable must be followed by a double consonant to prevent the vowel from becoming long. This is why the verb form is *dekken* (to cover) rather than *deken*.

#### C. The Suffix: `-ing`
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix.
*   **Function:** Nominalizer.
*   **Meaning:** It turns a verb into a noun (specifically, an abstract noun denoting the action or the result of the action).
*   **Cognates:**
    *   English: *-ing* (as in *building*, *finding*).
    *   German: *-ung* (as in *Entdeckung*).
*   **In this context:** It transforms the verb *ontdekken* (to discover) into the noun *ontdekking* (the discovery).

---

### 3. Morphophonology: The "Double K"
You may notice the spelling is **`ontdekking`** (with two 'k's), not *`ontdeking`*. This is a crucial morphophonological detail.

1.  **Verb Formation:** The base verb is *dekken* (to cover). Because the vowel *e* is short, the consonant *k* is doubled to close the syllable (*dek-ken*).
2.  **Prefix Addition:** When the prefix *ont-* is added, the verb becomes *ontdekken*.
3.  **Noun Derivation:** When the suffix *-ing* is added to the verb stem, the stem consonant is retained.
    *   Stem: *ontdekk-*
    *   Suffix: *-ing*
    *   Result: *ontdekking*

Therefore, the second 'k' is not a separate morpheme; it is part of the orthographic representation of the root `dek` within the verb stem, preserved when the noun suffix is attached.

---

### 4. Semantic Evolution
The meaning of the word has undergone a metaphorical shift, which is common in linguistics:

1.  **Literal Meaning:** "Un-covering" (removing a physical cover).
2.  **Figurative Meaning:** "Finding something previously hidden."
3.  **Modern Meaning:** "Discovery" (finding a new place, fact, or invention).

This mirrors the English word **discovery**, which comes from the Latin *dis-* (apart/un-) + *cooperire* (to cover). Both languages use the concept of "removing a cover" to describe the act of finding something new.
