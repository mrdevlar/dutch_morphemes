From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`vliegen`** is a morphologically simple word consisting of two morphemes. However, its analysis depends slightly on whether it is functioning as a **verb** (infinitive) or a **noun** (plural).

Here is the breakdown and explanation of the morphemes.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented as follows:

> **`vlieg`** + **`-en`**

*   **`vlieg`**: Root / Stem (Lexical Morpheme)
*   **`-en`**: Suffix (Inflectional Morpheme)

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Root: `vlieg-`
*   **Type:** Free Lexical Morpheme (Root).
*   **Function:** This carries the core semantic meaning of the word.
*   **Meaning:** "Fly" (the action of moving through the air) or "Fly" (the insect).
*   **Phonology:** In Standard Dutch, this is pronounced /vliɣ/.
    *   *Note on the 'g':* The letter **g** represents a voiced velar fricative [ɣ] (in the south) or a glottal fricative [h] (in the north/Netherlands).
*   **Etymology:** It stems from the Proto-Germanic **\*fleuganą**, which is cognate with the English verb *to fly* and the German verb *fliegen*.
*   **Grammatical Status:**
    *   As a **verb stem**, it is the base used for conjugation (e.g., *ik vlieg*, *jij vliegt*).
    *   As a **noun stem**, it is the singular form (e.g., *de vlieg* = the fly).

#### B. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Type:** Bound Inflectional Morpheme.
*   **Function:** This suffix changes the grammatical category or number of the root, but does not change the core meaning.
*   **Phonology:** Pronounced as a schwa followed by a nasal /ə(n)/. In rapid speech, the schwa is often elided, leaving just /n/.
*   **Grammatical Function (Context Dependent):**
    1.  **As a Verb (Infinitive Marker):** In Dutch, the dictionary form of a verb (the infinitive) almost always ends in `-en`. It marks the verb as non-finite (not tied to a specific subject or tense).
        *   *Example:* "Ik wil **vliegen**." (I want **to fly**.)
    2.  **As a Noun (Plural Marker):** In Dutch, `-en` is one of the most common suffixes used to form the plural of nouns.
        *   *Example:* "Er vliegen **vliegen**." (There are **flies** flying.)

---

### 3. Linguistic Ambiguity (Homography)
A key linguistic feature of `vliegen` is that it is **homographic** (spelled the same) but potentially **polysemous** (different meanings) depending on context.

| Context | Morphological Structure | Grammatical Category | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Verb** | `vlieg` (stem) + `-en` (infinitive) | Verb (Infinitive) | To fly |
| **Noun** | `vlieg` (singular stem) + `-en` (plural) | Noun (Plural) | Flies (insects) |

*Note: In the noun context, the root `vlieg` is a free morpheme (singular), whereas in the verb context, `vlieg` is technically a bound stem when used in the infinitive (though it can stand alone in the imperative: "Vlieg!").*

### 4. Verb Classification (Strong Verb)
From a morphological perspective, `vliegen` is classified as a **Strong Verb** (Class 3) in Dutch. This means its past tense forms are not created by adding a dental suffix (like `-te` or `-de`), but by changing the vowel of the root (Ablaut).

*   **Infinitive:** `vlieg` + `en` (vliegen)
*   **Past Singular:** `v` + `oo` + `g` (vloog) — *Vowel change: ie → oo*
*   **Past Participle:** `ge` + `v` + `oo` + `g` + `en` (gevlogen) — *Vowel change + Prefix + Suffix*
