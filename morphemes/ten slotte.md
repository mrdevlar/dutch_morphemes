From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch phrase **`ten slotte`** is not a single compound word, but rather a **fossilized prepositional phrase** that functions grammatically as a single adverb.

To understand its morphology, we must look at its historical composition, as it preserves grammatical features (specifically the **dative case**) that have largely disappeared from modern Dutch.

Here is the morphological breakdown:

### 1. The Phrase as a Whole
*   **Form:** `ten slotte`
*   **Part of Speech:** Adverbial phrase (functioning as an adverb).
*   **Meaning:** "Finally," "in the end," "lastly," or "in conclusion."
*   **Literal Translation:** "To the end" or "at the conclusion."

---

### 2. Morpheme Breakdown

#### A. `ten`
This is a **contraction** (portmanteau) of two distinct morphemes from older stages of the Dutch language.

*   **Morpheme 1:** `te`
    *   **Type:** Preposition.
    *   **Meaning:** "to," "at," or "in."
    *   **Function:** Indicates direction or location.
*   **Morpheme 2:** `den`
    *   **Type:** Definite Article.
    *   **Meaning:** "the."
    *   **Grammatical Feature:** This is the **dative masculine/neuter singular** form of the article.
    *   **Modern Status:** In modern Dutch, the dative case is extinct. The standard article is `de` or `het`. However, `ten` survives as a fixed contraction of `te` + `den`.

> **Linguistic Note:** You can see this same contraction in other Dutch phrases like *`ten minste`* (at least) or *`ten dele`* (partly).

#### B. `slotte`
This is a noun that has been inflected to match the preposition's case requirement.

*   **Morpheme 1:** `slot`
    *   **Type:** Noun Root (Lexeme).
    *   **Etymology:** From Middle Dutch *slot*, from Old Dutch *slot*, from Proto-Germanic **`*slutą`*.
    *   **Original Meaning:** "Lock" (as in a door lock).
    *   **Semantic Shift:** Over time, the meaning shifted from the physical object ("lock") to the action of locking ("closing") and finally to the abstract concept of a "conclusion" or "end" (e.g., the closing of a speech or a book).
*   **Morpheme 2:** `-e`
    *   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix.
    *   **Function:** This marks the **dative case**.
    *   **Modern Status:** In modern Dutch, nouns generally do not take case endings. The standard nominative form is `slot`. The `-e` here is a **fossilized ending** that remains only in this specific idiomatic expression.

---

### 3. Syntactic and Semantic Analysis

#### Grammaticalization
The phrase `ten slotte` is an example of **grammaticalization**.
1.  **Stage 1 (Literal):** A prepositional phrase indicating location or time relative to a physical closing (e.g., "at the lock/end").
2.  **Stage 2 (Metaphorical):** Used to indicate the end of a sequence of events.
3.  **Stage 3 (Idiomatic):** It has become a fixed adverb. You cannot modify it easily. For example, you cannot say *`ten grote slotte`* (to the big end) to mean "finally." It must remain `ten slotte`.

#### Comparison to English
To understand the morphology, compare it to English equivalents that also preserve archaic grammar:
*   **Dutch:** `ten slotte` (Preposition + Article + Noun + Dative Ending)
*   **English:** `at last` (Preposition + Adverb) or `in the end` (Preposition + Article + Noun).
*   **English Parallel:** The English word **`unto`** is similar to `ten`. It is a contraction of `to` + `unto` (archaic `to` + `the`), preserving a fused prepositional structure.
