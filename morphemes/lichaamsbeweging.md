From a morphological perspective, the Dutch word **`lichaamsbeweging`** is a **compound noun** (*samengesteld zelfstandig naamwoord*) that contains internal derivational morphology.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes, moving from the largest structural units down to the smallest.

### 1. Macro-Segmentation (The Compound)
The word is primarily composed of two main nouns joined together, with a linking element in between.

> **`lichaam`** + **`-s-`** + **`beweging`**

| Morpheme | Type | Part of Speech | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`lichaam`** | Free Root | Noun | Body |
| **`-s-`** | Bound (Linking) | Genitive/Linker | 's / of |
| **`beweging`** | Complex Root | Noun | Movement / Exercise |

### 2. Micro-Segmentation (Internal Structure)

To fully understand the word linguistically, we must also analyze the internal structure of the second component, **`beweging`**.

#### A. `lichaam` (Body)
*   **Morpheme Type:** Free Root Morpheme.
*   **Function:** The semantic head of the first part of the compound. It specifies *what* is moving.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *lichaem*, Old Dutch *likhamo*. Cognate with the German *Leib* (though *lichaam* is the standard modern Dutch term, while *lijf* is a cognate of *Leib*).

#### B. `-s-` (The Linking Element)
*   **Morpheme Type:** Bound Morpheme (specifically a **Linking Morpheme** or *voegklank*).
*   **Function:**
    1.  **Historical:** It functions as a genitive marker (possessive 's), implying "movement **of the** body."
    2.  **Synchronic:** In modern Dutch morphology, it is a phonological linker used to facilitate pronunciation and mark the boundary between the two nouns in a compound.
*   **Rule:** In Dutch, an *-s-* is frequently inserted between two nouns in a compound if the first noun is singular and ends in certain sounds, or simply due to lexicalization (fixed usage).

#### C. `beweging` (Movement)
This is a deverbal noun (a noun derived from a verb). It can be further broken down:

> **`bewegen`** + **`-ing`**

1.  **`bewegen`** (To move)
    *   **Morpheme Type:** Verb Stem.
    *   **Internal Structure:** Historically *be-* (prefix) + *wegen* (root). The root *wegen* is related to motion or swaying (cognate with English *wag* or *way* in some contexts, though etymologically complex).
    *   **Function:** Provides the action core of the word.

2.  **`-ing`**
    *   **Morpheme Type:** Derivational Suffix.
    *   **Function:** **Nominalization**. It converts the verb *bewegen* (to move) into a noun *beweging* (the act of moving / movement).
    *   **Productivity:** This is a highly productive suffix in Dutch (e.g., *lezen* → *lezing*, *werken* → *werking*).

### 3. Semantic Composition (How meaning is built)

The meaning of the whole word is constructed through **endocentric compounding**. This means the compound is a hyponym of its head.

1.  **Head:** The head of the compound is the final element, **`beweging`** (movement). The word is a type of movement.
2.  **Modifier:** The first element, **`lichaam`** (body), modifies the head. It restricts the type of movement to that which involves the body.
3.  **Linker:** The **`-s-`** binds them, creating a possessive or associative relationship ("Body's movement").

**Literal Translation:** "Body's-movement"
**Functional Translation:** "Physical exercise" or "Physical activity."

### 5. Linguistic Notes
*   **Orthography:** In Dutch, compound words are written as one single word (unlike English, which might write "body movement" as two words).
*   **Pluralization:** If this word were pluralized, the suffix would attach to the very end: *lichaamsbewegingen* (movements/exercises).
*   **Register:** This is a standard, neutral-to-formal term. In casual speech, Dutch speakers might use *sport* (sport) or *bewegen* (to move/exercise) as a verb, but *lichaamsbeweging* is the precise term for "physical activity" in medical or health contexts.
