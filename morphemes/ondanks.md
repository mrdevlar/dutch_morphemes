From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`ondanks`** (meaning "despite" or "in spite of") is a fascinating example of **lexicalization** and **grammaticalization**. While modern speakers treat it as a single, unanalyzable preposition, its history reveals a complex morphological structure.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes, moving from the ultimate historical components to the modern grammatical function.

### 1. Segmentation
To understand the morphology, we must look at the **diachronic** (historical) structure rather than just the **synchronic** (current) structure.

*   **Ultimate Segmentation:** `on-` + `dank` + `-s`
*   **Immediate Segmentation:** `ondank` + `-s`

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Prefix: `on-`
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix (Negation).
*   **Origin:** Proto-Germanic *\*un-*.
*   **Function:** Negates the meaning of the root. It is cognate with the English prefix **un-** (as in *unhappy*) and the German **un-**.
*   **Meaning:** "Not," "without," or "opposite of."

#### B. The Root: `dank`
*   **Type:** Free Morpheme (Noun).
*   **Origin:** Proto-Germanic *\*thankaz*.
*   **Function:** The lexical core of the word.
*   **Meaning:** Historically, this meant "thought," "gratitude," or "favor." In Middle Dutch, *dank* could also imply "will" or "pleasure" (e.g., *naar mijn dank* meant "to my liking").
*   **Cognates:** English **thank**, German **Dank**, Swedish **tack**.

#### C. The Suffix: `-s`
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix (Genitive Marker).
*   **Origin:** Old Dutch / Middle Dutch Genitive Case ending.
*   **Function:** This is the crucial element that turned a noun phrase into a preposition. In older stages of Dutch (and German), the genitive case was frequently used to form prepositions.
*   **Meaning:** It indicates possession or relation. In this context, it creates an adverbial/prepositional form meaning "of the..." or "in the state of...".
*   **Comparison:** This is the same `-s` found in words like **`tijdens`** (during, from *tijd* + *s*) or **`langs`** (along, from *lang* + *s*).

### 3. Semantic Evolution (How the meaning changed)

The literal translation of the morphemes provides the key to the semantic shift:

1.  **Literal Construction:** `on-` (not) + `dank` (gratitude/will) + `-s` (genitive).
    *   *Literal Meaning:* "Of the non-gratitude" or "Against the will."
2.  **Middle Dutch Usage:** The phrase *tegen ondenk* or *ondanks* was used to describe doing something contrary to someone's wishes or gratitude.
3.  **Modern Meaning:** Over time, the specific reference to "gratitude" faded, and the phrase generalized to mean "in opposition to" or "regardless of."
    *   *Example:* *Ondanks de regen* (Despite the rain) $\rightarrow$ "In opposition to the rain."

### 4. Synchronic vs. Diachronic Status

It is important to distinguish how a linguist analyzes this word today versus how it was formed:

*   **Diachronic (Historical):** As shown above, it is a compound of three morphemes (`on-` + `dank` + `-s`).
*   **Synchronic (Modern):** In contemporary Dutch grammar, **`ondanks`** is treated as a **monomorphemic preposition**.
    *   A modern speaker cannot productively add `on-` to other nouns to create prepositions (you cannot say *`onhuis`* to mean "despite the house").
    *   The root `dank` is no longer semantically active in this context; if you say *`ondanks`*, you are not thinking about "thanks" or "gratitude."
    *   The `-s` is no longer a productive genitive marker for prepositions (most modern prepositions do not end in `-s`).

### Summary Table

| Morpheme | Type | Meaning | Cognate |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **on-** | Prefix | Not / Without | English *un-*, German *un-* |
| **dank** | Root | Gratitude / Will | English *thank*, German *Dank* |

| **Total** | **Preposition** | **Despite / In spite of** | **English *despite*** |

### Conclusion
Linguistically, **`ondanks`** is a **fossilized preposition**. It represents a noun phrase (*on-dank*) that was grammaticalized into a preposition through the addition of a genitive suffix (*-s*). While it looks like a single unit to a modern speaker, its morphology reveals a history of negation, gratitude, and case marking.
