From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`antropologe`** (meaning "anthropologist," specifically female) can be broken down into three distinct morphemes.

Here is the morphological analysis:

### 1. The Morpheme Breakdown

| Morpheme | Form | Type | Origin | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | `antropo-` | Bound Root / Combining Form | Greek (*anthropos*) | "Human" or "Man" |
| **2** | `-loog` | Derivational Suffix | Greek (*logos*) | "Study of" / "Specialist in" |
| **3** | `-e` | Inflectional Suffix | Dutch (Germanic) | Feminine Gender Marker |

**Surface Form:** `antropo` + `loog` + `e` → **`antropologe`**

---

### 2. Detailed Explanation

#### A. `antropo-` (The Root)
*   **Etymology:** Derived from the Ancient Greek word *anthropos* (ἄνθρωπος), meaning "human being."
*   **Linguistic Note:** In English, this root is typically spelled *anthropo-* (retaining the 'h'). However, in Dutch orthography, the 'h' is often dropped in loanwords of Greek origin when it is not pronounced. Therefore, the standard Dutch spelling is **`antropo-`** rather than *anthropo-*.
*   **Function:** It acts as a **combining form**. It cannot stand alone as a word in Dutch; it must be attached to a suffix to create a noun.

#### B. `-loog` (The Agentive Suffix)
*   **Etymology:** Derived from the Ancient Greek word *logos* (λόγος), meaning "word," "reason," or "study/discourse."
*   **Function:** This is a **derivational suffix**. It changes the meaning of the root to denote a person who specializes in the study of that root.
    *   *Example:* *Biologie* (Biology) → *Bioloog* (Biologist).
*   **Gender:** In its base form, `-loog` is masculine or generic (common gender). It corresponds to the English suffix *-logist*.

#### C. `-e` (The Feminine Inflection)
*   **Etymology:** This is a native Dutch (Germanic) grammatical ending.
*   **Function:** This is an **inflectional suffix**. It does not change the core meaning of the profession but alters the grammatical gender of the noun to specify a female practitioner.
*   **Phonological/Orthographic Adjustment:** When the feminine suffix `-e` is added to the stem `antropoloog`, a spelling adjustment occurs. The long vowel `oo` is shortened to `o`, and the final `g` is followed by the `e`.
    *   *Masculine:* `antropoloog`
    *   *Feminine:* `antropologe`
    *   *Note:* In modern Dutch, this distinction is becoming less rigid in professional contexts (where *antropoloog* is often used for all genders), but *antropologe* remains the morphologically correct feminine form.

---

### 3. Summary of Morphological Process

The word is formed through a process of **compounding and derivation**:

1.  **Derivation:** The root `antropo-` is combined with the agentive suffix `-loog` to create the noun *antropoloog* (Anthropologist).
2.  **Inflection:** The feminine suffix `-e` is added to specify gender, resulting in *antropologe*.

**Comparison with English:**
*   **Dutch:** `antropo` + `loog` + `e`
*   **English:** `anthropo` + `log` + `ist` (Note: English uses *-ist* for the specialist, whereas Dutch uses *-loog*).
