From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`uitwisseling`** (meaning "exchange" or "interchange") is a **derived noun**. It is formed through a process of derivation from a separable verb.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation of its components.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **`uit`** + **`wissel`** + **`-ing`**

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Prefix: `uit-`
*   **Type:** Separable verbal prefix (in the source verb) / Inseparable prefix (in the derived noun).
*   **Origin:** Old Dutch *ūt*, Proto-Germanic *\*ūta*.
*   **Cognates:** English *out*, German *aus*.
*   **Meaning:** Literally "out" or "forth."
*   **Function:** In the context of this specific word, it modifies the root verb to indicate a **reciprocal** or **mutual** action. While *wisselen* means "to change," *uitwisselen* implies changing things *with* someone else (sending something out and receiving something back).
*   **Phonology:** In the noun *uitwisseling*, this syllable is **unstressed**. The primary stress falls on the root.

#### B. The Root: `wissel`
*   **Type:** Verbal Stem.
*   **Source:** Derived from the verb *wisselen* (to change, to swap).
*   **Origin:** Old Dutch *wizzelen*.
*   **Cognates:** Related to the concept of "alternating." While there isn't a direct single-word English cognate, it shares the Germanic root regarding change/alternation.
*   **Meaning:** To change, to swap, to alternate.
*   **Orthography:** The double consonant **`ss`** is crucial. In Dutch orthography, a short vowel (the /ɪ/ sound in *wissel*) in a closed syllable must be followed by a double consonant to maintain its shortness. If it were spelled *wiesel*, the 'i' would be pronounced long.
*   **Phonology:** This syllable carries the **primary stress** of the word (*uit-WIS-seling*).

#### C. The Suffix: `-ing`
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix (Nominalizer).
*   **Origin:** Old Dutch *-ing*, Proto-Germanic *\*-ingaz*.
*   **Cognates:** English *-ing* (as in *building* or *meeting*), German *-ung*.
*   **Function:** This suffix converts the verb (*uitwisselen*) into a noun (*uitwisseling*). It indicates the **action, process, or result** of the verb.
*   **Phonology:** This syllable is unstressed.

### 3. Syntactic and Semantic Context

To fully understand the morphology, one must look at the source verb: **`uitwisselen`**.

1.  **The Verb:** *uitwisselen* is a **separable verb** (scheidbaar werkwoord).
    *   *Example:* "Ik **wissel** mijn geld **uit**." (I exchange my money.)
    *   In a main clause, the prefix *uit-* separates and moves to the end of the sentence.
2.  **The Noun:** When this verb is nominalized into *uitwisseling*, the prefix becomes **inseparable**.
    *   The word is treated as a single lexical unit.
    *   The stress pattern shifts to the root (*wissel*), which is typical for Dutch nouns derived from separable verbs (compare: *de **op**name* vs. *de **op**nemen*).

### 5. Linguistic Classification
*   **Word Class:** Noun (Substantive).
*   **Gender:** Common (*de* uitwisseling).
*   **Plural:** *uitwisselingen* (adds the plural suffix *-en* to the existing noun).
*   **Morphological Process:** **Derivation** (specifically, nominalization of a phrasal verb).

In summary, **`uitwisseling`** is a morphologically transparent word where the meaning is the sum of its parts: "The process (`-ing`) of changing (`wissel`) out/with (`uit`) one another."
