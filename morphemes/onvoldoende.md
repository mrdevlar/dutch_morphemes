From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`onvoldoende`** is a complex word formed through derivational morphology. It is built upon a verb root, modified by prefixes and suffixes to create an adjective (which can also function as a noun).

Here is the hierarchical breakdown and explanation of its morphemes.

### 1. Hierarchical Morphological Structure

To understand the word fully, we must analyze it in layers, moving from the outermost affixes to the innermost root.

*   **Level 1 (Outermost):** `on-` + `voldoende`
*   **Level 2 (The Base):** `voldoend` + `-e`
*   **Level 3 (The Verb Stem):** `vol-` + `doen` + `-end`

**Full Decomposition:**
`on-` + `vol-` + `doen` + `-end` + `-e`

### 2. Morpheme-by-Morpheme Analysis

#### A. `on-` (Derivational Prefix)
*   **Type:** Negation Prefix.
*   **Function:** It negates the meaning of the base word it attaches to.
*   **Equivalent:** Similar to the English prefix *un-* (as in *unhappy*) or *in-* (as in *incorrect*).
*   **Semantics:** "Not" or "Lack of."
*   **Example:** *onmogelijk* (impossible), *onwaar* (untrue).

#### B. `voldoende` (The Base Adjective)
This is the core lexical unit to which the negation is applied. Historically, this is a present participle that has been lexicalized (frozen) into an adjective. It breaks down further:

#### C. `vol-` (Inseparable Prefix)
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix.
*   **Origin:** Related to the Dutch adjective *vol* (full).
*   **Function:** Indicates completeness or fulfillment.
*   **Semantics:** "Fully" or "Completely."
*   **Note:** In the verb *voldoen*, this prefix is inseparable (unlike *vollopen*, where it is separable). It modifies the meaning of the root verb.

#### D. `doen` (Lexical Root)
*   **Type:** Verb Root.
*   **Meaning:** "To do."
*   **Etymology:** From Proto-Germanic *\*dōną*.
*   **Semantics:** The action of performing or executing.
*   **Combination:** *vol-* + *doen* = *voldoen* (literally "to do fully," meaning "to fulfill" or "to satisfy").

#### E. `-end` (Derivational Suffix)
*   **Type:** Present Participle Suffix.
*   **Function:** Turns the verb (*voldoen*) into a participle/adjective.
*   **Equivalent:** Similar to the English *-ing* (as in *satisfying*).
*   **Result:** *voldoend* (satisfying / sufficient).

#### F. `-e` (Inflectional/Lexical Suffix)
*   **Type:** Adjectival Ending.
*   **Function:** In standard Dutch grammar, adjectives usually take an *-e* ending when modifying a noun (e.g., *een goede man*). However, in predicative position (after "to be"), adjectives usually drop the *-e* (e.g., *De man is goed*).
*   **Exception:** *Voldoende* is a **lexicalized participle**. It retains the *-e* even in predicative positions (e.g., *Het is voldoende* / "It is sufficient"). Therefore, this *-e* is now considered part of the fixed adjective form rather than a variable inflection.

### 3. Semantic Composition (Meaning Construction)

The meaning of the word is constructed logically from the bottom up:

1.  **`doen`** (to do) + **`vol-`** (full) $\rightarrow$ **`voldoen`** (to fulfill / to satisfy).
2.  **`voldoen`** + **`-end`** $\rightarrow$ **`voldoend`** (fulfilling / satisfying).
3.  **`voldoend`** + **`-e`** $\rightarrow$ **`voldoende`** (sufficient / adequate).
4.  **`on-`** + **`voldoende`** $\rightarrow$ **`onvoldoende`** (insufficient / inadequate).

### 4. Grammatical Function

While primarily an adjective, *onvoldoende* functions in three ways in modern Dutch:

1.  **Adjective:** *Dit is een **onvoldoende** antwoord.* (This is an insufficient answer.)
2.  **Predicative Adjective:** *De prestatie is **onvoldoende**.* (The performance is insufficient.)
3.  **Noun (Substantivized):** *Hij heeft een **onvoldoende** gehaald.* (He got a fail / a failing grade.)
    *   *Note:* In this context, it is often preceded by the indefinite article *een* and capitalized in some formal contexts (*een Onvoldoende*), though standard orthography keeps it lowercase unless it starts a sentence.
