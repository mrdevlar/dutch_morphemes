From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`hebben`** (meaning "to have") is a **bimorphemic** word in its infinitive form. It consists of a lexical root and an inflectional suffix.

Here is the breakdown and explanation of its morphological structure.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation

| Word | Morpheme 1 | Morpheme 2 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **hebben** | **heb-** | **-en** |

*   **heb-**: Lexical Root (Stem)
*   **-en**: Inflectional Suffix (Infinitive Marker)

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Root: `heb-`
*   **Type:** Free Lexical Morpheme (Root/Stem).
*   **Meaning:** This morpheme carries the core semantic meaning of the word: possession, holding, or experiencing.
*   **Phonology:** In standard Dutch, this is pronounced /hɛb/. The vowel is short (/ɛ/).
*   **Allomorphy:** This root is irregular. While `heb-` is the base form, it undergoes vowel changes (ablaut) and consonant changes depending on the tense and person.
    *   *Present:* Ik **heb** (I have), Jij **hebt** (You have), Hij **heeft** (He has).
    *   *Past:* Ik **had** (I had).
    *   *Participle:* Ge**had** (Had).
    *   *Note:* The shift from *heb* to *had* indicates that historically, this verb belonged to a class of verbs that used vowel gradation (similar to English *sing/sang*), though in modern Dutch it is conjugated largely like a weak verb.

#### B. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Type:** Inflectional Morpheme.
*   **Function:** It marks the verb as the **infinitive** (the non-finite, dictionary form). In Dutch grammar, this suffix is required to create the citation form of the verb.
*   **Phonology:** It is typically pronounced as a schwa /ə/ followed by a nasal /n/, often reduced to just /n/ or /ə/ in rapid speech depending on the dialect.
*   **Comparison:** This is the standard infinitive marker for the vast majority of Dutch verbs (e.g., *werk-en* [to work], *loop-en* [to walk]).

---

### 3. Orthographic Note: The Double `b`
A common point of confusion is why the root is spelled **`heb`** but the word is spelled **`hebben`**.

*   **The Rule:** Dutch orthography follows a rule regarding vowel length. A short vowel (like the /ɛ/ in *heb*) must be followed by a double consonant if the syllable is closed by a vowel suffix.
*   **The Process:**
    1.  Root: *heb* (Short vowel)
    2.  Suffix: *-en* (Starts with a vowel)
    3.  Combination: *heb* + *en*
    4.  Spelling Adjustment: To prevent the vowel from becoming long (which would happen if it were written *heben*), the consonant is doubled.
    5.  Result: **hebben**.
*   **Linguistic Reality:** Morphologically, there is only one /b/ in the root. The second *b* is an orthographic device to preserve the phonological quality of the root vowel, not a separate morpheme.

### 4. Etymological Perspective (Diachronic Morphology)
Looking at the history of the word helps explain its current structure:

*   **Proto-Indo-European (PIE):** *\*kap-* (meaning "to seize, grasp").
*   **Proto-Germanic:** *\*habjaną*.
    *   The *-jan-* suffix in Proto-Germanic was a causative/factitive suffix that eventually evolved into the modern Dutch *-en*.
*   **Old Dutch:** *hebban*.
*   **Middle Dutch:** *hebben*.
*   **Cognates:**
    *   English: *have*
    *   German: *haben*
    *   Swedish: *ha* (from *hava*)
    *   Gothic: *haban*

The word has retained its Germanic structure remarkably well, preserving the root *hab/hav/heb* and the infinitive ending derived from the Germanic *-jan*.

### 5. Grammatical Function
In a sentence, `hebben` serves two primary roles:
1.  **Main Verb:** Expressing possession (*Ik heb een auto* / "I have a car").
2.  **Auxiliary Verb:** Used to form the perfect tenses (*Ik heb gegeten* / "I have eaten").

### Summary
Linguistically, **`hebben`** is composed of the root **`heb-`** (carrying the meaning of possession) and the infinitive suffix **`-en`**. The double **`bb`** is an orthographic convention required by Dutch spelling rules to maintain the short vowel sound of the root when the vowel-initial suffix is added.
