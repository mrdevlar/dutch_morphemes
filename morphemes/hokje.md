From a morphological perspective, the Dutch word **`hokje`** is a compound of two distinct morphemes. It is a classic example of **derivational morphology**, where a suffix is added to a root word to change its meaning and grammatical properties.

Here is the breakdown:

### 1. Segmentation
The word can be segmented as follows:
> **`hok`** + **`-je`**

---

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Root Morpheme: `hok`
*   **Type:** Free Morpheme (Lexical Root)
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun (Substantive)
*   **Meaning:** A box, cage, stall, or enclosure. It implies a confined space.
*   **Grammatical Gender:** Common gender (*de* hok).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *hocke* or Low German *Hok*. It is related to the concept of "hocking" or crouching (enclosing oneself), though in modern Dutch, it refers strictly to the physical enclosure.
*   **Usage Note:** While *hok* exists as a standalone word, it is somewhat archaic or specific in modern usage (often referring to a dog kennel or a specific type of stall). It is much more frequently encountered in the diminutive form.

#### B. The Suffix Morpheme: `-je`
*   **Type:** Bound Morpheme (Derivational Suffix)
*   **Function:** Diminutive
*   **Meaning:** Indicates smallness, youth, or triviality.
*   **Grammatical Function:**
    1.  **Diminution:** It reduces the semantic size of the noun (a "small box").
    2.  **Gender Shift:** In Dutch, **all** diminutive nouns are grammatically neuter (*het*), regardless of the gender of the root word. Therefore, while *de hok* is common gender, *het hokje* is neuter.
    3.  **Phonology:** The spelling *-je* is used here because the root ends in a velar stop (/k/). (Note: If the root ended in a vowel, it would often become *-tje*, e.g., *auto* → *autotje*).

---

### 3. Morphophonology (Sound Interaction)
When combining `hok` and `-je`, no phonological changes (epenthesis) are required.
*   **Root:** /hɔk/
*   **Suffix:** /jə/
*   **Result:** /ˈhɔk.jə/
*   *Contrast:* Unlike the word *boom* (tree), which becomes *boompje* (inserting a /p/ to break up the consonant cluster), *hok* ends in a hard /k/ which flows directly into the /j/ glide of the suffix.

---

### 4. Semantic Shift and Idioms
The combination of these morphemes creates a specific semantic nuance:

1.  **Literal Meaning:** A small enclosure.
    *   *Example:* *Een hokje in de douche* (A shower stall).
    *   *Example:* *Een hokje in een hokkenhuis* (A stall in a pigeon house).
2.  **Metaphorical Meaning:** Because a *hok* is a confined space, *hokje* is frequently used metaphorically to describe mental categories or social labels.
    *   *Idiom:* **"Iemand in een hokje plaatsen."**
    *   *Translation:* "To pigeonhole someone."
    *   *Linguistic nuance:* The diminutive *-je* here suggests a small, restrictive mental box that limits how a person is perceived.
