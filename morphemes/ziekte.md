From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`ziekte`** (meaning "illness," "sickness," or "disease") is a compound of two distinct morphemes. It is an example of **derivational morphology**, where a suffix is added to a root to change its grammatical category (part of speech).

Here is the breakdown:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
**`ziek`** + **`-te`**

---

### 2. Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Root: `ziek`
*   **Type:** Free Morpheme (Lexical Root)
*   **Part of Speech:** Adjective
*   **Meaning:** "Sick" or "ill."
*   **Function:** This carries the core semantic meaning of the word. It describes the state of being unwell.
*   **Etymology:**
    *   Derived from Middle Dutch *ziek* or *seck*.
    *   Traces back to Old High German *sieh* and Proto-Germanic **\*seukaz*.
    *   **Cognates:** It is directly related to the English word **sick** and the German word **siech** (archaic/dialectal for sick).

#### B. The Suffix: `-te`
*   **Type:** Bound Morpheme (Derivational Suffix)
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun-forming suffix
*   **Meaning:** Indicates a **state**, **condition**, or **quality**.
*   **Function:** It performs **nominalization**. It transforms the adjective *ziek* (sick) into the abstract noun *ziekte* (sickness).
*   **Etymology:**
    *   Derived from Old Dutch *-te* or *-t*.
    *   Traces back to Proto-Germanic **\*-tō*.
    *   **Linguistic Comparison:** This suffix is cognate with the English suffix **-th** (as in *warm* $\rightarrow$ *warmth*, *deep* $\rightarrow$ *depth*, *sick* $\rightarrow$ *sickness* [though English uses *-ness* here, *-th* is the older Germanic equivalent]). It is also related to the German suffix *-te* (e.g., *Kraft* from *kräftig* is not a direct match, but *Lange* from *lang* shows similar nominalization patterns).

---

### 3. Phonological Note
*   **Spelling:** The root ends in a hard **k** (*ziek*). In Dutch, when adding a vowel-initial suffix, the spelling of the root often remains stable.
*   **Pronunciation:** /ˈzik.tə/
    *   *ziek*: /zik/ (The 'ie' is a long vowel /i:/).
    *   *-te*: /tə/ (The 'e' is a schwa, unstressed).

### 4. Related Morphological Families
To understand the productivity of these morphemes, it helps to look at related words:

*   **`ziek`** (Adj): "He is sick" (*Hij is ziek*).
*   **`ziekte`** (Noun): "The illness" (*De ziekte*).
*   **`ziekenhuis`** (Noun): "Hospital" (Literally: *sick* + *people* + *house*). Here, *ziek* takes the plural/collective suffix *-en* before combining with *huis*.
*   **`ziek zijn`** (Verb phrase): "To be sick."
