From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`degene`** is a **lexicalized compound**. While it functions as a single pronoun in modern syntax, it is morphologically transparent, meaning its internal structure is still visible.

Here is the breakdown and explanation of its morphemes.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into two distinct morphemes:

> **`de`** + **`gene`**

### 2. Analysis of Morphemes

#### Morpheme 1: `de`
*   **Category:** Definite Article (Determiner).
*   **Function:** It marks the noun/pronoun as **definite** (specific and known to the listener).
*   **Grammatical Features:**
    *   **Number:** Singular.
    *   **Gender:** Common gender (*de-woord*).
*   **Linguistic Note:** In this specific compound, the article has fused with the following element to create a specific pronoun. In standard Dutch, `de` usually precedes a noun (e.g., *de man*), but here it precedes a nominalized demonstrative.

#### Morpheme 2: `gene`
*   **Category:** Demonstrative Pronoun (Nominalized).
*   **Function:** It points to a specific entity ("that one").
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *ghene*, Old Dutch *gēn*. It is cognate with the English word **"yon"** (as in "yon hill") or the archaic "that."
*   **Status:** In modern Dutch, `gene` is generally a **bound morpheme** when used in this context. You rarely encounter `gene` standing alone as a free word in contemporary speech (unlike `die` or `dat`). It relies on the article `de` to form the complete pronoun.
*   **Meaning:** "One" or "that person."

### 3. Syntactic and Semantic Function
Together, **`degene`** functions as a **third-person singular demonstrative pronoun**.

*   **Meaning:** "The one," "that person," or "he/she who."
*   **Usage:** It is used to refer to a specific person previously mentioned or implied, often followed by a relative clause.
    *   *Example:* "**Degene** die belt, is mijn vader." (*The one who is calling is my father.*)
*   **Register:** It is slightly more formal or emphatic than simply using *die* (that) or *hij/zij* (he/she).

### 4. Morphological Paradigm (Plural Form)
A crucial linguistic observation regarding `degene` is how it behaves in the plural. This reveals that the first morpheme is not static but varies based on number.

*   **Singular:** `de` + `gene` = **`degene`** (The one)
*   **Plural:** `die` + `gene` + `n` = **`diegenen`** (Those ones)

**Why the change?**
In the plural, the definite article `de` is replaced by the demonstrative determiner `die`. This is a case of **suppletion** or paradigmatic variation within the compound.
*   Additionally, the plural form adds the suffix **`-n`** to `gene` (making it `genen`), which is the standard plural marker for many Dutch pronouns and nouns ending in a vowel.

### Conclusion
Linguistically, **`degene`** is a **definite nominalized demonstrative**. It is formed by the fusion of the definite article **`de`** and the demonstrative stem **`gene`**. While it is written as a single word in modern orthography, its morphological transparency allows speakers to intuitively understand its relationship to the plural form **`diegenen`**.
