From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`uitleg`** (meaning "explanation" or "instruction") is a fascinating example of **derivational morphology** and **nominalization**.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes, their functions, and their etymological history.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into two distinct morphemes:

> **`uit`** + **`leg`**

*   **`uit`**: Prefix / Separable Particle
*   **`leg`**: Root / Verb Stem

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Prefix: `uit`
*   **Category:** Preposition / Separable Verb Particle.
*   **Literal Meaning:** "Out," "out of," or "from within."
*   **Function:** In the context of the verb *uitleggen* (from which the noun is derived), `uit` acts as a **separable prefix**. It adds a directional or completive aspect to the action.
*   **Semantic Contribution:** It implies bringing something from a hidden or internal state to an external, visible state. Just as you might "lay out" a map on a table to show it to someone, you "lay out" information in words to make it clear.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *ūt*, Proto-Germanic *\*ūta*, and ultimately Proto-Indo-European *\*h₁ewdʰ-* (out). It is cognate with the English preposition **"out."**

#### B. The Root: `leg`
*   **Category:** Verb Stem (Bound Root in this context).
*   **Source Verb:** *Leggen* (to lay, to put, to place).
*   **Literal Meaning:** To place something down, to arrange, or to set.
*   **Function:** This is the core semantic carrier of the word. It denotes the physical action of placing.
*   **Morphological Note:** In the infinitive verb *uitleggen*, the root is *leg-* with the infinitive suffix *-en*. In the noun *uitleg*, the suffix *-en* is dropped. This is a process called **zero-derivation** (or conversion), where a verb stem is converted into a noun without adding an overt nominal suffix (like *-ing* in English).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *leggen*, Proto-Germanic *\*lagjaną*, and Proto-Indo-European *\*leǵ-* (to gather, collect, or lay). It is cognate with the English verb **"lay."**

---

### 3. Morphological Process: From Verb to Noun

To fully understand *uitleg*, one must look at its verbal counterpart: **`uitleggen`**.

1.  **Verb Construction:** *uit* (prefix) + *leggen* (verb).
    *   *Example:* "Ik leg het uit." (I explain it / I lay it out).
    *   In Dutch grammar, this is a **separable verb**. In a main clause, the prefix *uit* moves to the end of the sentence.
2.  **Nominalization:** To create the noun *uitleg*, the language takes the **stem** of the verb (*leg*) and combines it with the **prefix** (*uit*).
    *   The infinitive ending *-en* is removed.
    *   The resulting compound is treated as a single lexical unit (a noun).
    *   **Gender:** It is a *de*-word (*de uitleg*).

### 4. Semantic Composition (The Logic of Meaning)

Linguistically, the meaning of *uitleg* is compositional, meaning the meaning of the whole is derived from the sum of its parts, though with a metaphorical shift.

*   **Literal:** "Out" + "Lay" = To lay something out.
*   **Metaphorical Shift:** In the physical world, you lay out objects to display them. In the abstract world of communication, you "lay out" facts, ideas, or instructions to display them to the listener's mind.
*   **Result:** "Explanation."

### 5. Cross-Linguistic Comparison

*   **English:** The closest equivalent is the phrasal verb **"to lay out."**
    *   *Dutch:* "Kun je dat uitleggen?"
    *   *English:* "Can you lay that out?" (or "explain that").
*   **German:** The cognate is **`Erklärung`** (from *erklären*), which uses a different root (*klären* = to clear), but the Dutch *uitleg* is structurally closer to the English "lay out."
*   **French:** *Explication* (from Latin *ex-* "out" + *planare* "to make flat/plain"). Interestingly, both Dutch (*uit-leg*) and French (*ex-plan*) use a spatial metaphor of "making something flat/visible" to mean "explaining."

**Conclusion:**
The word **`uitleg`** is a nominalized compound derived from the separable verb *uitleggen*. It literally translates to "out-lay," metaphorically meaning to set forth information clearly for others to see or understand.
