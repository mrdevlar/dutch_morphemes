From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`onlangs`** (meaning "recently" or "lately") is a fascinating example of **lexicalization** and **derivational morphology**. While it functions as a single adverb in modern usage, it is historically composed of three distinct morphemes.

Here is the breakdown and explanation of the morphemes in **`onlangs`**.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented as follows:
> **`on-`** + **`lang`** + **`-s`**

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Prefix: `on-`
*   **Type:** Bound derivational morpheme (Prefix).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Proto-Germanic *\*un-*, cognate with English *un-* and German *un-*.
*   **Function:** **Negation.**
*   **Explanation:** In modern Dutch, `on-` is typically a negative prefix (e.g., *onmogelijk* = impossible, *onwaar* = untrue). In `onlangs`, it retains this negative function, but it is **fossilized**. It negates the concept of time duration.
    *   *Literal meaning:* "Not."

#### B. The Root: `lang`
*   **Type:** Free morpheme (Root/Stem).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Proto-Germanic *\*langaz*, cognate with English *long* and German *lang*.
*   **Category:** Adjective (originally).
*   **Function:** Denotes **duration** or **length of time**.
*   **Explanation:** This is the semantic core of the word. It refers to a span of time.
    *   *Literal meaning:* "Long."

#### C. The Suffix: `-s`
*   **Type:** Bound inflectional/derivational morpheme (Suffix).
*   **Etymology:** Historically the **genitive case ending** in Old and Middle Dutch.
*   **Function:** **Adverbial marker.**
*   **Explanation:** In older stages of Dutch (and still visible in some constructions), the genitive case was used to indicate time. The `-s` turns the adjective phrase into an adverb of time.
    *   *Parallel examples:* *daags* (daily, from *dag*), *soms* (sometimes, from *som*), *anders* (otherwise).
    *   *Literal meaning:* "In a manner of" or "of the time."

### 3. Semantic Composition (The "Math" of the Word)
When you combine these morphemes, the literal semantic composition is:

> **`on-`** (Not) + **`lang`** (Long) + **`-s`** (Adverbial marker)
> $\rightarrow$ **"Not-long-ly"** (in terms of time)

Over centuries, this phrase underwent **semantic bleaching** and **lexicalization**. The phrase "not long (ago)" condensed into a single lexical unit meaning "recently."

*   **Diachronic (Historical) View:** "Not long ago."
*   **Synchronic (Modern) View:** "Recently."

### 4. Linguistic Nuances and Common Confusion

#### The "Langs" Trap
A common point of confusion for learners (and sometimes linguists) is the similarity between `onlangs` and the word **`langs`** (meaning "along" or "past").
*   **`langs`**: Preposition/Adverb (Spatial).
*   **`onlangs`**: Adverb (Temporal).

While they look similar, they have different roots. `Langs` comes from the same root as `lang` but developed a spatial meaning ("along the length of"), whereas `onlangs` kept the temporal meaning ("length of time"). The `on-` prefix in `onlangs` is **not** related to the preposition `langs`.

#### Productivity
The prefix `on-` is **productive** in modern Dutch (you can add it to new adjectives to negate them, e.g., *onduidelijk*). However, in the specific word `onlangs`, it is **non-productive**. You cannot create new words by adding `on-` to time adjectives to mean "recently" (e.g., there is no word *onkort* meaning "recently"). The word is a closed lexical item.

### 5. Summary Table

| Morpheme | Form | Category | Function | Literal Contribution |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Prefix** | `on-` | Bound | Negation | "Not" |
| **Root** | `lang` | Free | Adjective | "Long" (duration) |
| **Suffix** | `-s` | Bound | Adverbializer | "In the manner of" / Genitive of time |
| **Total** | **`onlangs`** | **Adverb** | **Temporal** | **"Recently"** |

### Conclusion
From a morphological standpoint, **`onlangs`** is a **complex adverb** formed through the negation of a temporal adjective (`on` + `lang`) followed by an adverbial genitive suffix (`-s`). While modern speakers treat it as an atomic unit, its internal structure reveals a literal meaning of "not long," which perfectly encapsulates its definition of "recently."
