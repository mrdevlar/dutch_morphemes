From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`eindelijk`** (meaning "finally" or "at last") can be broken down into its constituent morphemes. This analysis involves looking at the word's structure (morphology), its history (etymology), and how its meaning has evolved (semantics).

### 1. Morphological Segmentation

The word consists of **two primary morphemes** in modern synchronic analysis, though a deeper historical analysis reveals three layers.

**Standard Analysis:**
> **`eind`** + **`elijk`**

**Historical/Deep Analysis:**
> **`eind`** + **`ig`** + **`lijk`**

---

### 2. Detailed Breakdown of Morphemes

#### A. The Root: `eind-`
*   **Form:** This is the stem of the noun **`einde`** (end).
*   **Type:** Free morpheme (it can stand alone as a word, e.g., *het eind* or *einde*).
*   **Meaning:** "End," "conclusion," or "termination."
*   **Phonology:** In this derivation, the final vowel *-e* of *einde* is dropped to create the stem *eind-*. This is a common process in Dutch derivation (e.g., *hond* from *honden*, *dag* from *dagen*).
*   **Stress:** The primary stress in the word falls on this root syllable (**`eind`**elijk).

#### B. The Suffix: `-elijk`
*   **Form:** A derivational suffix.
*   **Type:** Bound morpheme (cannot stand alone).
*   **Function:** It converts the noun stem into an **adverb** (or an adjective with adverbial force). It indicates manner, quality, or relation.
*   **Meaning:** Roughly translates to "-ly" in English (e.g., *friend* → *friend**ly***).
*   **The "g" Nuance:**
    *   In modern Dutch morphology, **`-elijk`** is often treated as a single suffix unit.
    *   However, etymologically, this suffix is a combination of **`-ig`** (an adjectival suffix meaning "having the quality of") and **`-lijk`** (an adverbial suffix meaning "like" or "in the manner of").
    *   *Why the 'g'?* Historically, the word was formed as *eind* + *ig* + *lijk*. While the adjective *eindig* exists today (meaning "finite"), the word *eindelijk* preserves the older semantic connection to "ending" rather than "finiteness."
    *   In words like *vriendelijk* (friendly), there is no root *vriendig*, so *-elijk* is clearly one unit. In *eindelijk*, the presence of the 'g' suggests the historical layering of *-ig-* + *-lijk-*, even if synchronically it functions as one suffix.

---

### 3. Semantic Evolution (Meaning Shift)

The meaning of *eindelijk* has undergone a specific semantic shift from literal to temporal.

1.  **Literal Meaning:** "End-like" or "Characterized by an end."
    *   If you look at the components literally: *End* + *ly*.
2.  **Intermediate Meaning:** "At the end."
    *   In Middle Dutch, it referred to the conclusion of a process.
3.  **Modern Meaning:** "Finally" / "At last."
    *   It now functions as a **temporal adverb**. It implies that an event has occurred after a long wait or a long process.
    *   *Example:* "Hij is **eindelijk** thuis." (He is **finally** home.)

---

### 4. Etymology and Cognates

*   **Old Dutch:** *endilīk*
*   **Middle Dutch:** *eindelīc*
*   **German Cognate:** **`endlich`**
    *   In German, *endlich* retains a dual meaning: "finally" (temporal) and "finite" (mathematical/philosophical). Dutch has split these meanings: *eindelijk* (finally) and *eindig* (finite).
*   **English Cognate:** **`End`** + **`-ly`**
    *   English has the word *endly*, but it is archaic. English uses *finally* (from Latin *finis*) instead. However, the structure is identical to the Dutch construction.

### Conclusion
Linguistically, **`eindelijk`** is a derived adverb formed by attaching the suffix **`-elijk`** to the noun stem **`eind-`**. While the suffix contains a historical 'g' (from *-ig-*), it functions in modern Dutch as a single unit that transforms the concept of "end" into the temporal marker "finally."
