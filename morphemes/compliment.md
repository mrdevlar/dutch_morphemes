From a linguistic perspective, analyzing the Dutch word **"compliment"** requires distinguishing between **synchronic morphology** (how the word is structured in modern Dutch) and **diachronic morphology** (its historical etymological structure).

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes.

### 1. Synchronic Analysis (Modern Dutch)
In contemporary Dutch, the word is treated as a **loanword** (leenwoord). To a native Dutch speaker, the internal structure is largely opaque.

*   **Morpheme Count:** 1
*   **Type:** Free Root (Noun)
*   **Analysis:** The word functions as a single, unanalyzable unit. A Dutch speaker does not intuitively break "compliment" into smaller meaningful parts when using it today. It is a **monomorphemic** word in the current language system.

However, because it ends in **-ment**, it belongs to a class of loanwords (like *moment*, *element*, *instrument*) where the suffix *-ment* is recognizable as a noun-forming element, even if the root (*complie-*) is not a standalone Dutch word.

### 2. Diachronic Analysis (Etymological Structure)
To understand the internal "break up" of the word, we must look at its Latin and French origins. The Dutch word was borrowed from French (*compliment*), which came from Latin.

Historically, the word consists of **three morphemes**:

| Morpheme | Origin | Type | Literal Meaning | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **com-** | Latin *com-* | Prefix | "Together" / "With" | Intensifier or association |
| **plere** | Latin *plēre* | Root | "To fill" | Core semantic meaning |
| **-ment** | Latin *-mentum* | Suffix | "Result of action" | Nominalizer (turns verb to noun) |

**Etymological Construction:**
1.  **Root:** *plere* (to fill).
2.  **Verb:** *complere* (to fill up, to complete).
3.  **Noun:** *complimentum* (that which completes or fulfills).

### 3. Semantic Shift (Meaning Evolution)
The morphological breakdown explains the drastic shift in meaning from Latin to Dutch:

1.  **Latin:** *Complimentum* originally referred to the act of "completing" a social duty or ceremony.
2.  **French:** The meaning narrowed to refer to the polite words used to fulfill that social duty.
3.  **Dutch:** The meaning narrowed further to specifically mean "an expression of praise or admiration."
