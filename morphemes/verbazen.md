### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Prefix: `ver-`
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix.
*   **Function:** In Dutch, `ver-` is a highly productive prefix with several functions. In the context of `verbazen`, it serves two main purposes:
    1.  **Change of State:** It indicates entering a new state.
    2.  **Reflexivity/Causativity:** Many `ver-` verbs in Dutch are inherently reflexive (e.g., *zich verbazen* - to surprise oneself / to be surprised). It can also imply causing the state in someone else (transitive: *het verbaast mij* - it surprises me).
*   **Phonology:** It is unstressed and pronounced as /vər/.

#### B. The Root: `baz-`
*   **Type:** Bound Root (Lexical Morpheme).
*   **Status:** In Modern Dutch, `baz-` is a **bound morpheme**. This means it cannot stand alone as an independent word. You cannot say *"Ik baze"* in modern Dutch.
*   **Historical Status:** Historically, this root was a free verb. In Middle Dutch, the verb was **`bāzen`** (to wonder, to be amazed). Over time, the standalone verb fell out of use, leaving `baz-` fossilized within derived words like `verbazen` and `verbazing`.
*   **Etymology:** The origin is somewhat obscure. Some etymologists suggest a link to the word **`baas`** (master), implying a state of being "overcome" or "at a loss" (similar to the English idiom "at a loss"). Others suggest a connection to the concept of "wondering" or "marveling."

#### C. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix.
*   **Function:** This is the standard marker for the **infinitive** form of Dutch verbs (e.g., *lopen*, *werken*, *verbazen*).
*   **Phonology:** In standard pronunciation, the final `-n` is often dropped in casual speech (e.g., /vərˈbaːzə/), but it remains in the orthography and formal grammar.

### 3. Semantic Composition

How do these parts combine to create meaning?

1.  **Core Meaning (`baz-`):** The concept of "wonder" or "astonishment."
2.  **Modification (`ver-`):** The prefix transforms the static concept of wonder into an active process or a change of state.
    *   **Intransitive/Reflexive:** *Zich verbazen* (To be surprised / To wonder). Here, `ver-` indicates the subject enters the state of amazement.
    *   **Transitive:** *Iets verbazen* (To surprise something/someone). Here, `ver-` acts causatively (to cause the state of amazement).

### 4. Derivational Family

To understand the root `baz-` better, it helps to look at its derivatives, which confirm the stem is `verbaz-`:

*   **`verbazing`** (Noun): *verbaz-* + *-ing* (Suffix for action/state). Meaning: "Amazement" or "Surprise."
*   **`verbazend`** (Adjective/Participle): *verbaz-* + *-end* (Present participle suffix). Meaning: "Astonishing" or "Amazing."
*   **`verbazelijk`** (Adjective): *verbazend* + *-lijk* (Suffix for quality). Meaning: "Astonishingly" or "Remarkable."

### 5. Syntactic Note

From a syntactic perspective, the prefix `ver-` dictates the argument structure of the verb:
*   **Reflexive:** *Ik verbaas **mij** over de prijs.* (I am surprised by the price.)
*   **Transitive:** *De prijs **verbaast** mij.* (The price surprises me.)

This flexibility is characteristic of the `ver-` prefix in Germanic languages, which often allows a verb to shift between transitive and intransitive/reflexive meanings without changing the root.

**`Verbazen`** is a **prefixed verb** built on a **fossilized root**.
*   **`ver-`**: Changes the state (to become surprised).
*   **`baz-`**: The core meaning (wonder/amaze), no longer used independently.
*   **`-en`**: Marks it as an infinitive verb.
