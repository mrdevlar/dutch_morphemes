From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`beroepen`** is a fascinating example of **homography** (identical spelling for different meanings) and **layered morphology**. Depending on the context, it can function as a verb (infinitive or past participle) or a noun (plural).

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
At the deepest level of analysis, the word consists of three morphemes:

> **be** + **roep** + **en**

However, the grammatical function of the suffix **-en** changes depending on whether the word is being used as a verb or a noun.

---

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Prefix: `be-`
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix.
*   **Function:** This is one of the most common prefixes in Dutch (and Germanic languages). It typically serves to **transitivize** a verb (make it take a direct object) or to indicate that an action is applied to an object or covers a surface.
*   **Meaning:** In this context, it adds a sense of "calling upon" or "directing a call toward."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Proto-Germanic *\*bi-*, related to the English prefix *be-* (as in *befriend* or *beset*).

#### B. The Root: `roep`
*   **Type:** Lexical Root (Stem).
*   **Meaning:** "Call," "cry," or "shout."
*   **Cognates:**
    *   German: *rufen* (to call).
    *   English: *rope* (etymologically distinct, but related to the concept of binding/calling in older Germanic roots), though the direct cognate for "call" is *cry* (from Old English *crican*, different root) or *shout*. The closest semantic relative is the German *Ruf* (reputation/call).
*   **Phonology:** The root contains a long vowel /u:/ (spelled *oe*), which is characteristic of this Germanic root.

#### C. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix (Variable).
*   **Function:** This is the source of the ambiguity. It serves two distinct grammatical roles:
    1.  **Verb Infinitive Marker:** In Dutch, the standard ending for the infinitive form of a verb is *-en* (e.g., *lopen*, *werken*, *beroepen*).
    2.  **Noun Plural Marker:** For many Dutch nouns ending in a schwa or specific consonants, *-en* is the standard plural suffix (e.g., *tafel* → *tafels*, but *beroep* → *beroepen*).

---

### 3. Contextual Breakdown (The Two Meanings)

Because the morphemes combine in the same order, the word is analyzed differently based on its part of speech.

#### Case 1: The Verb (To Appeal / To Invoke)
*   **Form:** Infinitive or Past Participle.
*   **Usage:** *Ik wil mij beroepen op de wet.* (I want to appeal to the law.)
*   **Morphological Structure:** `[be-]` (prefix) + `[roep]` (root) + `[-en]` (infinitive ending).
*   **Reflexive Note:** This verb is almost always used reflexively (*zich beroepen*). The prefix *be-* implies the action is directed back at the subject or an authority.
*   **Conjugation:** It is a weak verb in modern usage (*beroepde*, *beroepen*), though historically related to the strong verb *roepen* (*riep*, *geroepen*).

#### Case 2: The Noun (Professions / Occupations)
*   **Form:** Plural Noun.
*   **Usage:** *Er zijn veel beroepen in deze stad.* (There are many professions in this city.)
*   **Morphological Structure:** `[beroep]` (singular noun stem) + `[-en]` (plural suffix).
*   **Derivation History:** The singular noun *beroep* (profession) is derived from the verb *beroepen*. Historically, a "profession" was a "calling" (a call from God or society).
    *   *Roep* (Call) → *Beroep* (Calling/Vocation) → *Beroepen* (Professions).

---

### 4. Semantic Shift (Etymology & Meaning)
The evolution of meaning in this word highlights a common linguistic phenomenon: **Metaphorical Extension**.

1.  **Literal:** *Roepen* = To make a sound with the voice (to call).
2.  **Figurative (Verb):** *Beroepen* = To "call upon" an authority or a right (to appeal).
3.  **Metonymy (Noun):** *Beroep* = A "calling." In medieval times, one's job was often viewed as a divine calling. Over time, the religious connotation faded, leaving the secular meaning of "occupation" or "profession."

**Conclusion:**
Linguistically, **`beroepen`** is a compound of the prefix **`be-`**, the root **`roep`**, and the suffix **`-en`**. Its ambiguity arises because the suffix **-en** serves as both the infinitive marker for the verb "to appeal" and the plural marker for the noun "professions," both of which share the same etymological root of "calling."
