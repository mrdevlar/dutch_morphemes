While `blik` is one morpheme, it exhibits **polysemy** (one form, multiple related meanings). In modern Dutch, it functions primarily as a masculine noun (*de blik*) with two distinct but historically connected meanings:

1.  **The Glance/Look:** Refers to a look, gaze, or glance (e.g., *een blik werpen* — to cast a glance).
2.  **The Tin/Can:** Refers to a metal container (e.g., *een blik bier* — a can of beer) or sheet metal/tinplate.

To understand why one morpheme covers both "glance" and "tin," we must look at the history of the word (diachronic morphology).

*   **Proto-Germanic:** Reconstructed as **\*blekką** or **\*blīkaną**.
    *   Meaning: "To flash," "to shine," or "to gleam."
*   **Proto-Indo-European (PIE):** Reconstructed as **\*bʰleg-**.
    *   Meaning: "To burn," "to shine," or "to flash."
    *   *Note:* This is the same root that gives us English words like **blink**, **blaze**, **black** (originally "burnt/shiny"), and **bleach**.

#### The Semantic Shift
The connection between the two modern meanings lies in the concept of **shininess**:

1.  **Shine $\rightarrow$ Glance:** A "glance" is metaphorically a flash of light or a quick flash of vision. (Compare English "flash" or "glance" which also relate to light).
2.  **Shine $\rightarrow$ Tin:** Sheet metal (tinplate) is shiny. In Dutch, the material itself is called *blik* (tinplate). Consequently, a container made of this material is also called a *blik* (can).
