From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`ijskoud`** is a **compound adjective**. It is formed by combining two distinct free morphemes.

Here is the morphological breakdown and analysis:

### 1. Segmentation
The word can be segmented into two morphemes:
> **ijs** + **koud**

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### Morpheme 1: `ijs`
*   **Lexical Category:** Noun (Substantive).
*   **Meaning:** "Ice."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *īs*, from Proto-Germanic *\*īsą*, and ultimately from Proto-Indo-European *\*ei-* (to freeze).
*   **Cognates:** English *ice*, German *Eis*, Swedish *is*.
*   **Function in Compound:** In this context, `ijs` functions as the **modifier** (or determinant). It does not refer to the physical substance of ice itself, but rather acts as an intensifier. It specifies the *degree* or *quality* of the coldness (i.e., "cold *like* ice").

#### Morpheme 2: `koud`
*   **Lexical Category:** Adjective.
*   **Meaning:** "Cold."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *koud*, from Proto-Germanic *\*kaldaz*, and ultimately from Proto-Indo-European *\*gel-* (to be cold).
*   **Cognates:** English *cold*, German *kalt*, Old Norse *kaldr*.
*   **Function in Compound:** `koud` is the **head** of the compound. It carries the core semantic meaning of the word. The entire compound describes a temperature state.

### 3. Morphological Structure & Semantics

*   **Compound Type:** This is an **endocentric compound**. The meaning of the whole word falls under the category of the head (`koud`). An "ijskoud" object is a type of "cold" object.
*   **Semantic Relationship:** The relationship between the two morphemes is **comparative** or **similative**. It implies "as cold as ice."
*   **Orthography:** In Dutch, compound words are typically written as a single closed unit (unlike English, which often uses a hyphen for this specific construction, e.g., *ice-cold*). There is no **fugenelement** (linking sound like *-s-* or *-en-*) inserted between the two morphemes.
*   **Inflection:** As an adjective, `ijskoud` can be inflected. When placed before a noun with a definite article or in the plural, it typically takes the inflectional suffix *-e*.
    *   *Base form:* `ijskoud` (e.g., *Het water is ijskoud* / The water is ice-cold).
    *   *Attributive form:* `ijskoude` (e.g., *Een ijskoude dag* / An ice-cold day).

### 5. Cross-Linguistic Comparison
This structure is nearly identical to the English compound **ice-cold**. Both languages utilize a noun-adjective compound structure where the noun serves to intensify the adjective. However, Dutch morphology prefers the closed compound form (`ijskoud`), whereas English often retains the hyphen (`ice-cold`) or treats it as two words depending on the dictionary style.
