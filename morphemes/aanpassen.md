From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`aanpassen`** is a complex verb composed of three distinct morphemes. It is a classic example of a **separable verb** (*scheidbaar werkwoord*) in Dutch morphology.

Here is the breakdown and explanation of each component.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented as follows:
> **`aan`** + **`pass`** + **`en`**

---

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Prefix: `aan-`
*   **Type:** Separable Prefix (*Afscheidbaar voorvoegsel*).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *an*, cognate with German *an* and English *on*.
*   **Function:**
    *   **Directional/Aspectual:** In this context, it adds a sense of direction ("towards") or completion. It implies an action directed *onto* something or a change of state resulting in a fit.
    *   **Syntactic Behavior:** This is the defining feature of the word. In a main clause, this prefix separates from the root and moves to the end of the sentence.
        *   *Example:* "Ik **pas** de kleding **aan**." (I adjust the clothing.)
    *   **Phonology:** In Dutch separable verbs, the primary stress falls on the prefix. Therefore, the pronunciation is **`AAN`-pas-sen**, not *aan-PAS-sen*.

#### B. The Root: `pass-`
*   **Type:** Verbal Stem (*Wortel/Stam*).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *passen*, which traces back to Old French *passer* (to pass) and Latin *passus* (step). However, in Dutch, the meaning specialized from "to pass" to "to fit" or "to match" (similar to how a key fits a lock).
*   **Meaning:** To fit, to match, to suit.
*   **Inflection:** This is a **weak verb** (regular).
    *   Past tense: *paste*
    *   Past participle: *gepast*
*   **Cognates:** It is related to the English word *pass* (in the sense of "passing a test" or "fitting a description"), though the Dutch meaning is closer to the English concept of "fit."

#### C. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Type:** Infinitive Suffix (*Infinitiefuitgang*).
*   **Function:** This is the standard grammatical marker that identifies the word as a verb in its base (dictionary) form.
*   **Grammar:** Almost all Dutch verbs in the infinitive end in *-en* (e.g., *lopen*, *werken*, *zien*).
*   **Note:** In the present tense conjugation for *ik/jij/hij*, this suffix is dropped (e.g., *ik pas*, *jij past*).

---

### 3. Semantic Composition (How the meaning is built)
Linguistically, the meaning of the compound is derived compositionally:

1.  **`pass`** = To fit / To match.
2.  **`aan`** = Towards / On / To make happen.
3.  **`aanpassen`** = To make something fit *onto* something else.

**Resulting Meaning:** To adapt, to adjust, to modify, or to tailor.
*   *Example:* "Ik moet mijn gedrag **aanpassen**." (I must adapt my behavior.)
*   *Example:* "De schoenen moeten **aangepast** worden." (The shoes need to be adjusted/fitted.)

### 4. Grammatical Classification
*   **Word Class:** Verb (*Werkwoord*).
*   **Verb Type:** **Separable Verb** (*Scheidbaar Werkwoord*).
    *   This is crucial for syntax. You cannot treat `aanpassen` as a single unit in a main clause.
    *   *Correct:* "Ik **pas** het **aan**."
    *   *Incorrect:* "Ik **aanpas** het."
*   **Verb Type:** Transitive (*Overgankelijk*). It requires a direct object (you adapt *something*).

### Summary Table

| Morpheme | Type | Meaning/Function | Stress |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **aan-** | Prefix | Directional/Aspectual ("onto") | **Stressed** |
| **pass-** | Root | To fit / match | Unstressed |
| **-en** | Suffix | Infinitive marker | Unstressed |

This structure is highly productive in Dutch; many verbs follow this pattern (e.g., *aankomen* (to arrive/gain weight), *aankleden* (to dress)). Understanding the separability of `aan-` is the key to mastering the syntax of this word.
