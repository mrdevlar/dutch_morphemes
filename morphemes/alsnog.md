From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`alsnog`** is a **lexicalized compound adverb**. It consists of two distinct free morphemes that have fused over time to create a specific idiomatic meaning.

Here is the breakdown and explanation of the morphemes:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into two morphemes:
> **`als`** + **`nog`**

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### Morpheme 1: `als`
*   **Original Category:** Conjunction / Preposition / Adverb.
*   **Core Meaning:** "If," "when," "like," "as."
*   **Function in Compound:** In this specific compound, the conditional meaning of "if" is largely bleached (weakened). Instead, it functions to establish a **comparative or temporal frame**. It suggests "in the manner of" or "in the event that."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *als*, originating from Proto-Germanic *\*alaz* (all) + *\*hwa* (what/which).

#### Morpheme 2: `nog`
*   **Category:** Adverb.
*   **Core Meaning:** "Still," "yet," "more."
*   **Function in Compound:** This morpheme provides the aspect of **continuation** or **persistence**. It indicates that an action is occurring despite time passing or contrary to a previous state of affairs.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *nog*, originating from Proto-Germanic *\*nawh* (not yet/now).

### 3. Semantic Composition (The "Whole")
While the literal sum of the parts might suggest "if still" or "as still," the compound has undergone **semantic shift** (idiomatization).

*   **Literal Interpretation:** "In the case that it still happens."
*   **Actual Meaning:** "After all," "eventually," "still," or "yet."
*   **Nuance:** It implies that an action was not originally planned, expected, or completed on time, but it happened **despite** those expectations or delays. It often carries a tone of concession or surprise.

**Example:**
*   *Ik dacht dat ik niet zou gaan, maar ik ging **alsnog**.*
*   (I thought I wouldn't go, but I went **after all**.)

### 4. Syntactic and Orthographic Properties
*   **Word Class:** Adverb (*Bijwoord*).
*   **Orthography:** It is written as a **closed compound** (one word). In older texts, you might occasionally see it written as two words (*als nog*), but modern standard Dutch (based on the *Groene Boekje* spelling rules) mandates it as one word.
*   **Stress Pattern:** In Dutch compounds, stress typically falls on the first element (e.g., *HUIS*dier). However, in adverbial compounds like *alsnog*, the stress often falls on the second element (**alsnóg**) to emphasize the persistence ("still"), though this can vary by dialect and speaker.

### 5. Linguistic Classification
*   **Type:** Endocentric Compound (specifically an adverbial compound).
*   **Status:** **Lexicalized.** This means the combination is stored in the mental lexicon as a single unit with a meaning that is not entirely predictable from the individual words alone.
*   **Morphological Productivity:** Low. You cannot freely combine *als* and *nog* with other words to create new meanings (e.g., you cannot say *alsmorgen* or *alsvandaag* with the same logic).
