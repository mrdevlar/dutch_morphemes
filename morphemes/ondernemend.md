From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`ondernemend`** is a complex word formed through derivational morphology. It is the present participle of the verb *ondernemen*, functioning primarily as an adjective.

Here is the morphemic breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Morphemic Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **`onder`** + **`nem`** + **`end`**

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Prefix: `onder-`
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix (Inseparable).
*   **Literal Meaning:** "Under" or "down" (cognate with English *under* and German *unter*).
*   **Function:** In this specific compound verb, it does not retain the spatial meaning of "underneath." Instead, it functions as an intensifier or a marker of initiative. It implies taking something "upon oneself" or assuming a burden/responsibility.
*   **Phonological Note:** In the verb *ondernemen*, the stress falls on the root (**on-der-NE-men**), not the prefix. This classifies *onder-* as an **inseparable prefix** in this context. (Contrast with separable verbs like *ondergaan*, where stress is on the prefix).
*   **Etymology:** From Old Dutch *under*, Proto-Germanic *\*under*.

#### B. The Root: `nem-`
*   **Type:** Lexical Root (Verb Stem).
*   **Base Form:** *nemen* (to take).
*   **Meaning:** "To take," "to seize," or "to grasp."
*   **Morphological Adjustment:** The infinitive is *nemen*. To form the stem for the participle, the infinitive ending *-en* is removed, leaving the stem *nem-*.
*   **Etymology:** From Old Dutch *neman*, Proto-Germanic *\*namjaną*, and ultimately Proto-Indo-European *\*nem-* (to distribute, allot, or take).
*   **Cognates:** English *name* (originally "to allot"), *nimble* (from *niman*), and German *nehmen*.

#### C. The Suffix: `-end`
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix (Present Participle).
*   **Function:**
    1.  **Verbal:** Creates the present participle form of the verb (equivalent to English *-ing*).
    2.  **Adjectival:** In Dutch, present participles are frequently used as adjectives. Here, it transforms the action of "undertaking" into a quality or characteristic.
*   **Meaning:** "Characterized by doing [root action]."
*   **Cognates:** English *-ing* (e.g., *undertaking*), German *-end*.

---

### 3. Semantic Composition (How the meaning is built)

The meaning of the word is constructed hierarchically:

1.  **`nemen`** = To take.
2.  **`onder-` + `nemen`** = To take upon oneself $\rightarrow$ **`ondernemen`** (To undertake, to start a business, to embark on).
3.  **`ondernemen`** + **`-end`** = Characterized by undertaking $\rightarrow$ **`ondernemend`**.

**Semantic Shift:**
While the literal translation is "undertaking," the modern semantic usage of the adjective **`ondernemend`** has shifted toward **"enterprising," "dynamic,"** or **"adventurous."** It describes a person or entity that is willing to take risks and initiate projects.
