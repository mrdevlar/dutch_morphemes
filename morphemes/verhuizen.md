From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch verb **`verhuizen`** (to move house / to relocate) can be analyzed through **morphology**, the study of the internal structure of words.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes:

### 1. Morphemic Segmentation
The word consists of three distinct morphemes:

> **ver-** + **huiz-** + **-en**

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Prefix: `ver-`
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix.
*   **Function:** This prefix changes the lexical category or meaning of the root. In this specific context, it creates a **denominal verb** (a verb derived from a noun).
*   **Semantic Value:** The prefix *ver-* is highly productive in Dutch. It often indicates a change of state, a process of completion, or a movement away from a starting point.
    *   In *verhuizen*, it adds the meaning of "changing location" or "undergoing a transition regarding the house."
    *   Without the prefix, the root *huizen* (plural of *huis*) would just mean "houses." The prefix transforms the concept of "house" into the *action* of moving it.

#### B. The Root: `huiz-`
*   **Base Form:** *huis* (noun, meaning "house").
*   **Type:** Lexical Root.
*   **Phonological Note (Voicing):** You will notice the root is spelled with a **`z`** here, but the singular noun is spelled with an **`s`** (*huis*).
    *   This is due to **intervocalic voicing**. In Dutch, when a voiceless fricative (like *s*) is placed between two vowels (the *i* in *huis* and the *e* in *-en*), it becomes voiced (*z*).
    *   Underlying form: */huɪs/*
    *   Surface form in this context: */huɪz/*
*   **Semantic Value:** Refers to the physical dwelling or residence.

#### C. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix.
*   **Function:** This marks the **infinitive** form of the verb.
*   **Grammar:** In Dutch, the standard ending for the infinitive of a verb is *-en* (e.g., *lopen*, *werken*, *zien*).
*   **Distinction:** It is important not to confuse this with the plural suffix *-en* found on nouns (e.g., *huizen* = houses). While they look identical, in *verhuizen*, the *-en* is purely verbal (marking the verb form), whereas in the noun *huizen*, it marks plurality.

---

### 3. Morphological Process Summary

| Morpheme | Form | Type | Function | Meaning Contribution |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Prefix** | `ver-` | Derivational | Creates verb from noun | Indicates change of state/location |
| **Root** | `huiz-` | Lexical | Semantic core | House / Home |
| **Suffix** | `-en` | Inflectional | Marks Infinitive | Non-finite verb form |

### 4. Linguistic Context
*   **Word Class:** Denominal Verb (Verb derived from a Noun).
*   **Transitivity:** It is generally used intransitively (e.g., *Ik verhuis* - I move), though it can be used transitively in specific contexts (e.g., *Ik verhuis de kast* - I move the cabinet).
*   **Cognates:**
    *   **German:** *verziehen* (to move away / to distort).
    *   **English:** There is no direct cognate for the prefix *ver-*, but the root relates to *house*. The construction is similar to the English "to house-move," though English typically uses the phrasal verb "to move house."

### Conclusion
Linguistically, **`verhuizen`** is a complex word formed by taking the noun root **`huis`**, applying a derivational prefix **`ver-`** to indicate a change of location, and adding an inflectional suffix **`-en`** to mark it as an infinitive verb, all while undergoing a phonological voicing change from *s* to *z*.
