From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`doodstil`** is a **compound adjective** formed through the process of **compounding** (in Dutch: *samenstelling*).

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation of the word.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word consists of two free morphemes (meaningful units that can stand alone):

> **dood** + **stil**

### 2. Analysis of Morphemes

#### Morpheme 1: `dood`
*   **Part of Speech:** Adjective (derived from the noun *de dood* / death).
*   **Literal Meaning:** "Dead."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Proto-Germanic *\*dauþaz*, related to the English word *dead* and the German word *tot*.
*   **Function in this Compound:** **Intensifier.**
    *   In this specific context, `dood` does not carry its literal semantic meaning of "no longer alive." Instead, it functions as a **degree modifier** or intensifier.
    *   It amplifies the quality of the second morpheme to an absolute extreme. It is semantically equivalent to "completely," "utterly," or "absolutely."
    *   *Linguistic Note:* This is a common pattern in Germanic languages where words related to death are used to emphasize intensity (e.g., English "dead tired," "dead serious").

#### Morpheme 2: `stil`
*   **Part of Speech:** Adjective.
*   **Meaning:** "Quiet," "silent," "still."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *stil*, related to the German *still*.
*   **Function in this Compound:** **Head.**
    *   In a compound word, the "head" determines the grammatical category and the core meaning of the new word.
    *   Since `stil` is an adjective, `doodstil` is also an adjective.
    *   The core semantic content is "quietness."

### 3. Syntactic and Semantic Combination

*   **Compound Type:** Endocentric Adjective Compound.
    *   The meaning of the whole word is a subset of the meaning of the head (`stil`), modified by the first element (`dood`).
*   **Semantic Shift:** The combination creates a **hyperbolic meaning**.
    *   *Stil* = Quiet.
    *   *Doodstil* = So quiet it feels like death / Absolute silence.
*   **Orthography:** In modern Dutch, this is written as a **closed compound** (one word). Historically, some intensifying compounds were written with a hyphen, but `doodstil` is standardly closed.

### 4. Grammatical Behavior (Inflection)
Because `doodstil` functions as an adjective, it follows Dutch adjective inflection rules when placed before a noun.

*   **Predicative (after the verb):** No ending.
    *   *Het is **doodstil**.* (It is dead quiet.)
*   **Attributive (before a noun):** Takes an **-e** ending.
    *   *Een **doodstille** kamer.* (A dead quiet room.)
    *   *De **doodstille** nacht.* (The dead quiet night.)

This construction is highly productive in Germanic languages. The use of "death" as an intensifier is a shared semantic feature:
