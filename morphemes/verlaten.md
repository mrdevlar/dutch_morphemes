From a morphological perspective, the Dutch verb **`verlaten`** can be segmented into two distinct morphemes.

Here is the breakdown:

### 1. Segmentation
**`ver-`** + **`laten`**

---

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Prefix: `ver-`
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix (Bound Morpheme).
*   **Classification:** Inseparable Prefix (*onafscheidelijk voorvoegsel*).
*   **Function:** It modifies the meaning of the root verb. In Dutch, the prefix *ver-* often indicates:
    *   A change of state or condition.
    *   Completion or thoroughness.
    *   Movement away or loss.
*   **Phonology/Stress:** In Dutch, inseparable prefixes are **unstressed**. The primary stress falls on the root syllable.
    *   Pronunciation: *ver-**LA**-ten* (not **VER**-laten).
*   **Morphosyntax:** Because it is an inseparable prefix, it prevents the addition of the standard past participle marker *ge-*.
    *   *Incorrect:* ~~ge-~~*verlaten*
    *   *Correct:* *verlaten* (e.g., *Hij heeft het huis verlaten* / He has left the house).

#### B. The Root: `laten`
*   **Type:** Lexical Root / Base Verb (Free Morpheme).
*   **Meaning:** To let, to allow, or to leave (in the sense of leaving something behind).
*   **Etymology:** Cognate with the English verb **"to let"** and the German verb **"lassen"**. It stems from the Proto-Germanic *\*laisan*.
*   **Verb Class:** It is a **strong verb** (irregular).
    *   Infinitive: *laten*
    *   Past Singular: *liet*
    *   Past Plural: *lieten*
    *   Past Participle: *gelaten* (Note: When used *without* the prefix *ver-*, it takes the standard *ge-* prefix).

---

### 3. Semantic Composition (How the meaning is built)
The meaning of the compound is not simply the sum of its parts, but a specific idiomatic combination:

1.  **`laten`** implies "to let go" or "to leave behind."
2.  **`ver-`** intensifies this action, implying a permanent separation, a movement away, or a state of abandonment.

**Result:** To leave someone or something behind completely; to abandon or desert.

| Morpheme | Type | Status | Meaning/Function | Stress |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **ver-** | Prefix | Bound | Away / Change of state / Completion | Unstressed |
| **laten** | Root | Free | To let / To leave | **Stressed** |

### 5. Linguistic Note on Homography
It is important to note that **`verlaten`** is homographic (spelled the same) for two different grammatical forms:
1.  **Infinitive:** *Ik wil het huis **verlaten**.* (I want to leave the house.)
2.  **Past Participle:** *Ik heb het huis **verlaten**.* (I have left the house.)

This is a specific characteristic of Dutch verbs with inseparable prefixes: the infinitive and the past participle share the exact same form, unlike separable verbs (e.g., *opbellen* vs. *opgebeld*) or regular verbs (e.g., *werken* vs. *gewerkt*).
