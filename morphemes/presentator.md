From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`presentator`** is a classic example of a **loanword** (leenwoord) derived from Latin, adapted into Dutch morphology. It is a **derivational** formation, meaning a new word (a noun) is created from a verbal root.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
In Dutch morphology, the word is typically analyzed as consisting of **two** primary morphemes:

> **`present-`** + **`-ator`**

However, to understand the etymology fully, we must look at the internal Latin structure, which reveals **three** morphemes:

> **`present-`** + **`-a-`** + **`-tor`**

Below is the detailed explanation of each component.

---

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Root: `present-`
*   **Type:** Lexical Root (Stem)
*   **Origin:** Latin *praesent-* (stem of *praesens*, the present participle of *praeesse*, meaning "to be before" or "to show").
*   **Meaning:** In this context, it relates to "showing," "displaying," or "being present before an audience."
*   **Dutch Context:** This root is productive in Dutch. It appears in related words like:
    *   *presentatie* (presentation)
    *   *presenteren* (to present)
    *   *present* (gift/present, though this is a semantic shift from the Latin *praesens* meaning "gift" in the sense of something brought before someone).
*   **Phonology:** In Dutch, the Latin diphthong *ae* (from *praesens*) was simplified to *e*.

#### B. The Suffix: `-ator` (or `-a-` + `-tor`)
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix (Agentive)
*   **Origin:** Latin *-ator* (masculine agent noun suffix).
*   **Function:** It indicates the **doer** or **agent** of the action defined by the root. It turns a verb/root into a noun referring to a person.
*   **Internal Breakdown:**
    *   **`-a-`**: A thematic vowel connecting the stem to the suffix.
    *   **`-tor`**: The core agentive marker (compare English *-ter* as in *actor* or *writer*).
*   **Dutch Context:** In modern Dutch, `-ator` is often treated as a single, unanalyzable suffix block borrowed from international scientific and professional terminology. It is almost exclusively used for professional roles involving communication or technology.
    *   *Examples:* *narrator*, *creator*, *regulator*, *ventilator*.
*   **Gender:** In Latin, *-tor* was masculine. In Dutch, nouns ending in *-ator* are **common gender** (de-words), though they historically referred to men. Today, they apply to any gender (e.g., *de presentator* can be a man or a woman).

---

### 3. Grammatical Properties

*   **Word Class:** Noun (*Zelfstandig naamwoord*).
*   **Gender:** Common (*De*).
*   **Plural:** *presentatoren*.
    *   *Note:* The plural suffix *-en* is added to the stem. The *-or* changes to *-oren* (a common pattern for Latin loanwords in Dutch, e.g., *dokter* → *doktoren*, *directeur* → *directeuren*).
*   **Stress Pattern:** The stress falls on the **antepenultimate** syllable (third from the end).
    *   *pre-sen-**ta**-tor*
    *   This is typical for Dutch words ending in *-ator*.

### 4. Etymological Path
1.  **Latin:** *praesentare* (verb: to place before, to show) → *praesentator* (noun: one who shows).
2.  **French:** *présentateur* (Dutch borrowed heavily from French during the 17th–19th centuries).
3.  **English:** *presenter* (Dutch also borrowed from English in the 20th century, but *presentator* remained the standard term for TV/Radio hosts).
4.  **Dutch:** *presentator*.

### 5. Summary Table

| Morpheme | Segment | Origin | Function | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Root** | `present-` | Latin *praesent-* | Lexical Base | To show / to be before |
| **Suffix** | `-ator` | Latin *-ator* | Agentive | One who performs the action |
| **Total** | **`presentator`** | **Latin via French/English** | **Noun** | **One who presents (TV, radio, event)** |

### 6. Linguistic Note on Usage
While *presentator* is the standard term for a TV or radio host, Dutch also uses the verb *presenteren*. Interestingly, there is a slight semantic distinction in Dutch compared to English. In English, a "presenter" is the person. In Dutch, *presentator* is the person, but *presentatie* is the act.

There is also a synonym **`omroeper`** (broadcaster), which is a native Germanic formation (*om* + *roepen* = to call around), but *presentator* is preferred for the specific role of hosting a show, whereas *omroeper* sounds more archaic or technical.
