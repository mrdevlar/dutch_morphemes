Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation of the word.

*   **Structure:** `[hitte]` + `[golf]`

*   **Compound Type:** Endocentric Compound (The meaning is a specific type of the head noun).
*   **Head:** `golf` (The word is a type of *wave*).
*   **Modifier:** `hitte` (Specifies the *type* of wave).
*   **Stress Pattern:** In Dutch compounds, primary stress typically falls on the **first** element.

*   **Suffix:** `-te` (Derivational Suffix)

    *   **Process:** *heet* (hot) $\rightarrow$ *hitte* (heat).
    *   **Note on Phonology:** There is a vowel change (ablaut) from *ee* to *i* during this derivation (*heet* $\rightarrow$ *hitte*). This is a historical remnant of Germanic vowel gradation patterns found in Dutch (similar to *breed* $\rightarrow$ *breedte*, though the vowel change is not consistent across all adjectives).

#### Component B: `golf` (Wave)
This is a **root noun**. It does not have internal derivational morphology in this context.

*   **Root:** `golf`
    *   **Meaning:** Wave (physical movement of water, or metaphorical surge).

### 3. Syntactic and Semantic Analysis

#### The Linking Element
In Dutch compounding, a linking element (often an *-s-*, known as a *tussenvoegsel*) is sometimes inserted between two nouns (e.g., *liefdes*verklaring).
*   **In `hittegolf`:** There is **no linking *-s-***.
*   **Reason:** The first noun `hitte` ends in a vowel (*-e*). While linking *-s-* is common after nouns ending in *-e* (like *liefde*), it is not mandatory. In this specific lexicalized compound, the two nouns are fused directly.

#### Semantic Relation
*   **Metaphor:** The word relies on a metaphorical mapping. A physical wave (`golf`) has a crest, a trough, and a duration. A `hittegolf` is a meteorological phenomenon that "surges" through a region, peaks, and then recedes, mimicking the behavior of a water wave.
*   **Translation:** It corresponds directly to the English compound "heatwave" (Heat + Wave).
