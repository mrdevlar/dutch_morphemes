From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`handschoenen`** (meaning "gloves") is a complex word formed through two distinct morphological processes: **compounding** and **inflection**.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation.

### 1. Morpheme Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **`hand`** + **`schoen`** + **`en`**

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. `hand` (Root / Free Morpheme)
*   **Type:** Lexical Root (Free Morpheme).
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun.
*   **Meaning:** "Hand."
*   **Function:** This acts as the **modifier** (or dependent) in the compound. It specifies *which* part of the body the object is designed for.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Proto-Germanic *\*handuz*, cognate with the English word "hand."

#### B. `schoen` (Root / Free Morpheme)
*   **Type:** Lexical Root (Free Morpheme).
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun.
*   **Meaning:** "Shoe."
*   **Function:** This acts as the **head** (or nucleus) of the compound. In Dutch (and Germanic) compounding, the rightmost element usually determines the grammatical category and the core meaning of the new word. Therefore, a *handschoen* is semantically a type of *schoen* (shoe/covering).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Proto-Germanic *\*skōnaz*, cognate with the English word "shoe."
*   **Compound Formation:** When combined with *hand*, it creates the singular noun **`handschoen`** (glove). Literally, it translates to "hand-shoe."

#### C. `en` (Suffix / Bound Morpheme)
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix (Bound Morpheme).
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun Inflection.
*   **Meaning:** Plural marker.
*   **Function:** This indicates that the noun refers to more than one item.
*   **Application:** It attaches to the end of the entire compound word (*handschoen* + *en*), not just the root *schoen*.
*   **Phonology:** In this specific case, the suffix is pronounced as a schwa followed by a nasal sound (/ən/).

### 4. Linguistic Notes

*   **Endocentric Compound:** This is an endocentric compound because the meaning of the whole word is a hyponym of the head. A *handschoen* is a specific kind of *schoen*.
*   **Lack of Linking Element:** In Dutch compounding, a linking element (like *-s-* or *-e-*) is sometimes inserted between roots (e.g., *liefdes*verklaring). In *handschoen*, there is **no linking element**; the roots are juxtaposed directly.
*   **Stress Pattern:** In Dutch compounds, the primary stress typically falls on the **first** element. Therefore, the stress is on **`HÁND`**schoen, not *hand`SCHOEN`*.
*   **Cognate Comparison:** This structure is very similar to German.
    *   Dutch: *Hand* + *schoen* = *Handschoen*
    *   German: *Hand* + *schuh* = *Handschuh*
    *   English: *Glove* (Old English *glōf*, unrelated to "hand" or "shoe").
    *   *Note:* This highlights how Dutch and German retain the transparent "hand-shoe" metaphor, whereas English uses a distinct lexical item.

### Summary
The word **`handschoenen`** is a pluralized compound noun. It consists of the modifier **`hand`**, the head noun **`schoen`**, and the plural inflectional suffix **`en`**.
