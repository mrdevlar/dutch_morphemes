From a morphological perspective, the Dutch word **`vallen`** (meaning "to fall") can be segmented into two distinct morphemes.

Here is the breakdown and linguistic explanation:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
**`val`** + **`-en`**

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Root/Stem: `val-`
*   **Type:** Lexical Root (Free/Bound depending on context).
*   **Function:** This morpheme carries the core **lexical meaning** of the word. It denotes the action of dropping, descending, or collapsing.
*   **Phonology:** Phonetically, this is pronounced /vɑl/.
*   **Orthography:** In the infinitive form `vallen`, the consonant `l` is doubled. This is a Dutch spelling convention (orthography) used to indicate that the preceding vowel (`a`) is short (/ɑ/). If it were spelled `valen`, the `a` would be pronounced as a long vowel (/aː/). Therefore, the double `l` is not a separate morpheme but a spelling marker for the vowel quality of the root.
*   **Etymology/Cognates:** This root is of Proto-Germanic origin (*fallaną*). It is cognate with:
    *   English: *fall*
    *   German: *fallen*
    *   Old Norse: *falla*
*   **Verb Class:** `val-` belongs to the class of **strong verbs** (sterke werkwoorden). This means its past tense forms are created through vowel gradation (ablaut) rather than adding a dental suffix (like *-de* or *-te*).
    *   *Example:* Present *val-* vs. Past *viel-* (I fell).

#### B. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix.
*   **Function:** This morpheme marks the **grammatical form** of the verb. Specifically, it marks the **infinitive** (the citation form found in dictionaries).
*   **Paradigm:** In Dutch, the infinitive ending is almost always `-en` (or `-n` after a vowel). This distinguishes it from other conjugated forms:
    *   *Ik val* (I fall) — Stem only (1st person singular present).
    *   *Jij valt* (You fall) — Stem + `-t` (2nd person singular present).
    *   *Wij vallen* (We fall) — Stem + `-en` (1st person plural present).
    *   *Vallen* (To fall) — Stem + `-en` (Infinitive).
*   **Historical Context:** This suffix derives from the Proto-Germanic infinitive ending *-aną*, which evolved into *-an* in Old Dutch and eventually *-en* in Modern Dutch.

### 4. Linguistic Classification
*   **Morphological Structure:** **Bimorphemic** (composed of two morphemes).
*   **Word Formation:** This is not a compound word (like *slaapkamer*), nor is it a derived word in the sense of adding a new part of speech (like *schoon* $\to$ *schoonmaken*). It is a **flexed** (inflected) form of the verb lexeme.
*   **Language Typology:** Dutch is generally considered a **fusional** language (where one affix can carry multiple grammatical meanings), but in this specific instance, the segmentation is relatively transparent (agglutinative-like), where the suffix clearly marks the infinitive mood.

### Conclusion
The word **`vallen`** consists of the semantic root **`val-`** ("fall") and the inflectional infinitive suffix **`-en`**. Together, they form the dictionary citation form of the verb.
