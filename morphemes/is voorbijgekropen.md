From a linguistic perspective, it is important to clarify first that **`is voorbijgekropen`** is not a single lexical word, but rather a **verb phrase** (specifically, a conjugated verb form in the perfect tense). It consists of two orthographic words: the auxiliary verb **`is`** and the past participle **`voorbijgekropen`**.

Below is the morphological breakdown and linguistic explanation of the components.

### 1. Overview of Structure
*   **Tense:** Perfect (Voltooid Tegenwoordige Tijd).
*   **Verb Type:** Separable Verb (*scheidbaar werkwoord*).
*   **Verb Class:** Strong Verb (*sterk werkwoord* / Class 2).
*   **Auxiliary:** *Zijn* (used for verbs indicating motion or change of state).

### 3. Detailed Linguistic Explanation

#### A. The Auxiliary: `is`
*   **Lexeme:** *zijn* (to be).
*   **Morphology:** This is a suppletive form. The root of *zijn* is irregular.
*   **Syntax:** In Dutch, the perfect tense is formed with either *hebben* (to have) or *zijn* (to be). Verbs indicating **motion** or **change of location** typically take *zijn*. Since *kruipen* (to crawl) combined with *voorbij* (past) implies movement from point A to point B, the auxiliary *is* is required.

#### B. The Separable Prefix: `voorbij`
*   **Composition:** This is a compound particle consisting of *voor* (front/for) + *bij* (near/by).
*   **Function:** In the infinitive form, the verb is **`voorbijkruipen`** (to crawl past).
*   **Separability Rule:** In Dutch, separable verbs split in main clauses (e.g., *Hij **kruip**t **voorbij***). However, in the perfect tense (participle form), the prefix remains attached to the participle, but the participle marker *ge-* is inserted **between** the prefix and the stem.
    *   *Structure:* [Prefix] + [ge-] + [Stem]
    *   *Result:* `voor` + `bij` + `ge` + `kropen` → **`voorbijgekropen`**.

#### C. The Participle Marker: `ge-`
*   **Function:** This is the standard prefix used to form the past participle in Dutch.
*   **Placement:** For separable verbs, *ge-* cannot go at the very beginning (unlike inseparable verbs like *bezoeken* → *bezoekt*). It must "sandwich" the prefix.
    *   *Incorrect:* `gevoorbijkropen`
    *   *Correct:* `voorbijgekropen`

#### D. The Verb Stem & Ablaut: `kropen`
*   **Lexeme:** *kruipen* (to crawl).
*   **Verb Class:** This is a **Strong Verb** (Class 2). Strong verbs form their past tense and participle through **ablaut** (vowel gradation) rather than adding a *-d* or *-t* suffix (which weak verbs do).
*   **The Vowel Change:**
    *   Infinitive: *kruip-* (diphthong *ui*)
    *   Past Participle Stem: *krop-* (monophthong *o*)
*   **The Suffix:** The *-en* at the end is the standard ending for the past participle of strong verbs (and weak verbs).
    *   *Root:* `kruip`
    *   *Ablauted Stem:* `krop`
    *   *Participle Suffix:* `-en`
    *   *Combined:* `kropen`

---

### 4. Summary of the Construction

To reconstruct the logic of the word formation:

1.  **Start with the Infinitive:** *voorbijkruipen* (to crawl past).
2.  **Apply Perfect Tense:** Requires auxiliary *zijn* + Past Participle.
3.  **Form the Participle:**
    *   Take the prefix: *voorbij-*
    *   Insert the participle marker: *-ge-*
    *   Apply strong verb ablaut to the root (*kruip* → *krop*): *-krop-*
    *   Add participle ending: *-en*
    *   **Result:** *voorbijgekropen*
4.  **Conjugate Auxiliary:** 3rd person singular of *zijn* → *is*.
5.  **Final Phrase:** **`is voorbijgekropen`** (has crawled past / is crawled past).

### Example Sentence
> "De tijd **is voorbijgekropen**."
> *(Time has crawled past.)*
> *Note: This is a metaphorical use, as time does not literally crawl.*
