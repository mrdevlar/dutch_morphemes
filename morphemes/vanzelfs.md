From a linguistic perspective, the word **`vanzelfs`** is a fascinating example of Dutch historical morphology, specifically regarding the **adverbial genitive**.

However, a crucial preliminary note is required: In modern Standard Dutch, the word is almost exclusively spelled and used as **`vanzelf`**. The form **`vanzelfs`** is considered archaic, dialectal, or a hypercorrection in contemporary usage. Nevertheless, analyzing `vanzelfs` reveals the historical grammatical structure that underpins the modern word.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation.

### 1. Morpheme Breakdown

The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

| Morpheme | Form | Category | Meaning/Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | `van` | Preposition | "of", "from" |
| **2** | `zelf` | Pronoun | "self" |
| **3** | `s` | Suffix | Genitive marker (adverbial) |

---

### 2. Detailed Linguistic Analysis

#### A. `van` (Preposition)
*   **Origin:** Old Dutch *fan*, related to German *von* and English *of/from*.
*   **Function:** It indicates origin, source, or separation.
*   **In this context:** It establishes a relationship of origin. The action originates *from* the subject itself, rather than from an external force.

#### B. `zelf` (Pronoun)
*   **Origin:** Old Dutch *selb*, related to German *selbst* and English *self*.
*   **Function:** It is a reflexive pronoun used for emphasis or to indicate identity.
*   **In this context:** It refers to the subject of the sentence (the agent). It implies that the action is performed by the entity itself, without outside help.

#### C. `s` (The Adverbial Genitive Suffix)
*   **Origin:** This is the most linguistically significant morpheme here. It is a fossilized remnant of the **genitive case** in Germanic languages.
*   **Function:** Historically, adding an *-s* to a noun or pronoun phrase turned it into an adverbial phrase indicating time, manner, or place.
*   **Comparison:**
    *   *s' morgens* (of the morning) $\rightarrow$ *s'morgens*
    *   *s' avonds* (of the evening) $\rightarrow$ *s'avonds*
    *   *ergens* (some + where + s)
    *   *nergens* (no + where + s)
    *   *anders* (other + s)
*   **In this context:** The `s` turns the prepositional phrase "van zelf" (of self) into a fixed adverb meaning "of itself." It signifies possession or relation: "from the self's side."

---

### 3. Semantic Composition (How the meaning is built)

When combined, the morphemes create a literal meaning that evolved into an idiomatic one:

1.  **Literal:** "Of self" or "From self."
2.  **Conceptual:** Something that happens without external intervention; it originates from the nature of the thing itself.
3.  **Idiomatic Translation:** "Automatically," "naturally," "by itself," or "of course."

**Example Sentence:**
> *Het lost zich **vanzelfs** op.* (Archaic/Dialectal)
> *Het lost zich **vanzelf** op.* (Modern Standard)
> **Translation:** "It resolves itself automatically."

---

### 4. Why is the 's' usually dropped in Modern Dutch?

From a diachronic (historical) linguistics perspective, the shift from `vanzelfs` to `vanzelf` represents **morphological leveling** and the loss of the case system.

1.  **Loss of Genitive:** Dutch has largely lost its case system (nominative, accusative, dative, genitive). The adverbial genitive (the *-s* ending on adverbs) is disappearing.
2.  **Lexicalization:** The phrase `van zelf` fused into a single lexical unit (`vanzelf`). Once fused, the grammatical marker (`s`) became redundant.
3.  **Analogy:** Most modern Dutch adverbs do not end in *-s* unless they are specifically time/place markers (like *nergens*). Because `vanzelf` functions more like a manner adverb, the *-s* was dropped to align with the modern system.

### 5. Related Form: `vanzelfsprekend`

It is important not to confuse `vanzelfs` with the common adjective **`vanzelfsprekend`** ("self-evident" or "obvious").
*   **Breakdown:** `van` + `zelf` + `sprekend` (speaking).
*   **Literal:** "Speaking of itself."
*   **Note:** In this word, the `s` is **not** present because `sprekend` is a participle, not a genitive adverb.

### Summary
While **`vanzelf`** is the correct modern spelling, **`vanzelfs`** is a valid morphological construction consisting of a preposition (`van`), a reflexive pronoun (`zelf`), and a historical adverbial genitive suffix (`s`). It literally means "of itself," serving as a fossilized reminder of the Dutch case system.
