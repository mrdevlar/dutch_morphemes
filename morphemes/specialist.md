From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`specialist`** can be analyzed through **morphology** (the study of word structure). While it is a loanword, it has been fully integrated into Dutch morphology.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes, followed by a detailed explanation of each component.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word is composed of two primary morphemes:

> **`special`** + **`-ist`**

*   **`special`**: The lexical stem (root).
*   **`-ist`**: The derivational suffix.

*(Note: A deeper etymological analysis reveals three morphemes: `speci` + `-al` + `-ist`, but in synchronic Dutch morphology—how the word functions today—speakers perceive `special` as the base.)*

---

### 2. Detailed Explanation of Morphemes

#### A. The Stem: `special-`
*   **Type:** Bound Root / Lexical Stem.
*   **Origin:** Latin *specialis* (meaning "particular" or "of a kind").
*   **Function:** This morpheme carries the core semantic meaning of the word. It relates to the concept of "specificity" or "particular expertise."
*   **Dutch Context:**
    *   In Dutch, the native adjective form is **`speciaal`** (meaning "special").
    *   However, in the word *specialist*, the stem appears as **`special-`**. This is a common phenomenon in international loanwords where the Latin stem is preserved rather than the Dutch adjective form.
    *   It is related to the Dutch verb **`specialiseren`** (to specialize).
*   **Morphophonology:** Note the spelling and pronunciation shift. In *speciaal*, the 'c' is pronounced as /s/. In *specialist*, the 'c' is often pronounced as /ʃ/ (the "sj" sound in Dutch), influenced by the following 'i'. This is a morphophonemic variation common in loanwords.

#### B. The Suffix: `-ist`
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix (Agentive).
*   **Origin:** Latin *-ista* / Greek *-istes*.
*   **Function:** This suffix converts the stem into an **agent noun**. It denotes "a person who practices, is concerned with, or is an expert in" the field defined by the stem.
*   **Productivity:** This is a highly **productive suffix** in Dutch. It is used to create nouns for professions and enthusiasts from various stems.
    *   *Examples:* `pianist` (piano + ist), `journalist` (journal + ist), `terrorist` (terror + ist), `bioloog` (biologist - note the variation).
*   **Gender:** In Dutch, nouns ending in *-ist* are common gender (**`de`** words).
    *   *Example:* **De** specialist.

### 3. Deeper Etymological Breakdown (Diachronic View)
If we look at the historical construction of the word (before it entered Dutch), the stem `special-` itself is composed of two older morphemes:

1.  **`speci-`**: The root from Latin *species* (meaning "kind," "sort," or "appearance").
2.  **`-al-`**: An adjectival suffix (turning the noun into an adjective, *specialis* = "of a kind").
3.  **`-ist`**: The agentive suffix added later.

So, historically: **`speci`** (kind) + **`-al`** (adjective) + **`-ist`** (person).
However, for a modern Dutch speaker, the word is processed as **`special`** + **`-ist`**.

---

### 4. Inflectional Morphology (Pluralization)
To fully understand the morphemes, we must look at how the word changes in the plural. This confirms where the stem ends and the suffixes begin.

*   **Singular:** `specialist` (`special` + `-ist`)
*   **Plural:** `specialisten` (`special` + `-ist` + `-en`)

The addition of the plural suffix **`-en`** attaches to the end of the entire word *specialist*, confirming that `-ist` is part of the lexical stem in the plural form, not a separate inflectional marker.
