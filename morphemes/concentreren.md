From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`concentreren`** (to concentrate) is a complex verb formed through **derivational morphology** (creating a new word) and **inflectional morphology** (marking grammatical form). It is a loanword adapted from Latin via French.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation of the morphemes.

### 1. Visual Segmentation
We can segment the word into four distinct morphemes:

> **con-** + **centr-** + **-er-** + **-en**

### 3. Etymological Path
To understand the morphology fully, one must trace the word's history:

1.  **Latin:** *concentrare*
    *   *con-* (together) + *centrum* (center) + *-are* (verb suffix).
2.  **French:** *concentrer*
    *   The Latin *-are* evolved into the French infinitive *-er*.
3.  **Dutch:** *concentreren*
    *   Dutch adopted the French form but adapted the spelling and pronunciation to fit Dutch phonology.
    *   The French *-er* became the Dutch verbal suffix *-eren*.
    *   The Dutch infinitive ending *-en* was added to the stem.

### 4. Semantic Composition (How meaning is built)
The meaning of the word is compositional, built from the sum of its parts:

1.  **`centr-`** establishes the concept of a **center**.
2.  **`con-`** adds the concept of **gathering** or **intensifying** toward that center.
3.  **`-er-`** turns this concept into an **action** (to do something with the center).
4.  **`-en`** makes it a **verb** in its base form.

**Literal Translation:** "To bring together into a center."
**Modern Usage:** To focus attention, to make something denser, or to gather resources.

### 5. Grammatical Note on the Stem
In Dutch morphology, the **stem** of the verb is **`concentreer-`**.
*   When conjugating, the `-en` is dropped (e.g., *ik concentreer*, *hij concentreert*).
*   The spelling of the stem includes a double `e` (`ee`) to indicate the long vowel sound /eː/, which is a result of the interaction between the root `centr-` and the suffix `-er-`.

### Summary
*   **Word Class:** Verb (Infinitive).
*   **Morphological Type:** Agglutinative (morphemes are strung together clearly).
*   **Key Feature:** It is a **loanword verb** utilizing the Dutch *-eren* suffix class, which distinguishes it from native Germanic verbs (which typically use *-en* directly on the root, like *werken*).
