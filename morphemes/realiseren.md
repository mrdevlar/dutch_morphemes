From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`realiseren`** is a complex verb formed through derivation and inflection. It is a loanword that has been fully integrated into Dutch morphology.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes, followed by an explanation of their etymology and function.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation

The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **`real`** + **`iseer`** + **`n`**

Alternatively, from a derivational perspective (before inflection), it is often analyzed as:

> **`real`** + **`iseren`**

### 2. Detailed Explanation of Morphemes

#### A. The Root: `real`
*   **Type:** Free morpheme (Lexeme).
*   **Origin:** Latin *realis* (from *res*, meaning "thing" or "matter").
*   **Function:** This is the semantic core of the word. In Dutch, *real* exists as an adjective (e.g., *een realistische verwachting* / a realistic expectation), though it is often used in specific contexts (finance, philosophy) where *werkelijk* is the more common native equivalent.
*   **Meaning:** Pertaining to reality; actual.

#### B. The Derivational Suffix: `-iseer` (or `-iseren`)
*   **Type:** Bound morpheme (Derivational suffix).
*   **Origin:** French *-iser* / Latin *-izare*.
*   **Function:** This is a **verbalizer**. It converts the adjective root (`real`) into a verb.
*   **Semantic Value:** It carries a **causative** or **factitive** meaning. It implies "to make X" or "to cause to become X."
    *   *Real* + *-iseer* = "To make real."
*   **Dutch Orthography Note:** In Dutch spelling rules, verbs borrowed with the suffix *-iseren* typically take a double *e* in the stem to indicate vowel length.
    *   Infinitive: `realiseren`
    *   Stem (used for conjugation): `realiseer` (e.g., *ik realiseer*, *jij realiseert*).
    *   The *n* is dropped for the stem, leaving the long vowel sound /eː/ represented by `ee`.

#### C. The Inflectional Suffix: `-n`
*   **Type:** Bound morpheme (Inflectional suffix).
*   **Function:** This marks the **infinitive** form of the verb.
*   **Context:** In Dutch, the infinitive is formed by adding *-en* to the stem. However, because the stem ends in *-eer*, the suffix is phonologically realized as *-n* attached to the stem `realiseer`.
    *   *Stem:* `realiseer`
    *   *Infinitive:* `realiseer` + `n` = `realiseren` (Note the spelling adjustment: the *ee* remains, but the *r* is part of the stem, so it becomes *realiseer-en* -> *realiseren*).

### 3. Etymological Path
The word traveled through several languages before reaching modern Dutch:
1.  **Latin:** *realis* (adjective) → *realizare* (verb, rare in classical Latin, common in Medieval Latin).
2.  **French:** *réaliser* (18th century).
3.  **Dutch:** *realiseren* (19th century).

### 4. Semantic Shift (Polysemy)
Linguistically, it is interesting to note the semantic evolution of this word in Dutch compared to its English cognate (*realize*):

1.  **Literal Meaning (Causative):** "To make real" or "to achieve."
    *   *Example:* "Ik wil mijn droom realiseren." (I want to realize/achieve my dream.)
    *   *Note:* In English, "realize a dream" is common, but "realize a profit" is more technical. In Dutch, *realiseren* is the standard verb for "to accomplish" or "to execute."
2.  **Figurative Meaning (Cognitive):** "To become aware of" or "to understand."
    *   *Example:* "Ik realiseer me dat ik te laat ben." (I realize that I am too late.)
    *   *Note:* This meaning is identical to English. However, in Dutch, the reflexive form (*zich realiseren*) is mandatory for this meaning. You cannot say "Ik realiseer dat..." without the reflexive pronoun *me*.
