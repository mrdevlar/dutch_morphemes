From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`onbeleefd`** (meaning "rude" or "impolite") can be analyzed through **morphology**, the study of the internal structure of words.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes, moving from the surface structure to the deeper etymological structure.

### 1. Surface Segmentation (Synchronic Analysis)
In modern Dutch, speakers generally perceive the word as consisting of two main parts: a negative prefix and a base adjective.

**`on-`** + **`beleefd`**

| Morpheme | Type | Function | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`on-`** | Derivational Prefix | Negation | "Not" / "Un-" |
| **`beleefd`** | Free Morpheme (Adjective) | Base | "Polite" / "Well-mannered" |

---

### 2. Deep Structure (Diachronic/Internal Analysis)
While `beleefd` functions as a single adjective in modern usage, it is historically and morphologically complex. It is derived from a verb form. If we break down `beleefd` further, we get a three-part structure:

**`be-`** + **`leef`** + **`-d`**

#### A. The Prefix: `on-`
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix.
*   **Function:** It negates the meaning of the adjective it attaches to.
*   **Linguistic Equivalent:** It is cognate with the English prefix **`un-`** (e.g., *un-polite*).
*   **Usage:** In Dutch, `on-` is the standard negative prefix for adjectives (e.g., *onmogelijk* = impossible, *onwaar* = untrue).

#### B. The Base: `beleefd`
*   **Type:** Adjective (derived from a past participle).
*   **Meaning:** Polite, well-bred.
*   **Internal Breakdown:**
    1.  **`be-`**: A verbal prefix. In Dutch, `be-` often turns a noun or intransitive verb into a transitive verb, or creates a participle that describes a state.
    2.  **`leef`**: The root/stem. This comes from the verb **`leven`** (to live).
    3.  **`-d`**: The suffix marking the **past participle**. In Dutch, past participles often function as adjectives (e.g., *gemaakt* = made).

#### C. The Etymological Logic
The word has an interesting history that explains the meaning:
1.  **Root:** *Leven* (to live).
2.  **Participle:** *Geleefd* (lived/experienced).
3.  **Prefixation:** *Be-* replaces the standard participle prefix *ge-* in this specific construction.
4.  **Semantic Shift:** Originally, *beleefd* meant "well-lived" or "experienced in life." Over time, this evolved to mean "well-bred" or "having good manners" (someone who knows how to "live" in society).
5.  **Negation:** Adding `on-` negates this, resulting in "not well-bred" $\rightarrow$ **rude**.

### 4. Comparison with English
To understand the linguistic logic, compare it to English:

*   **Dutch:** `on-` + `be-` + `leef` + `-d`
*   **English:** `un-` + `polite`

While English uses a Latinate root (*polite* from *politus*), Dutch uses a Germanic root (*leven* / *live*). However, the **derivational strategy** is identical:
1.  Take a base concept (Polite / Well-lived).
2.  Add a negative prefix (Un- / On-).

### Summary
*   **`on-`**: Negates the word (Not).
*   **`beleefd`**: The state of being polite.
*   **`leef`**: The core concept of living/existing.
*   **`-d`**: Turns the verb into a descriptive state (Adjective).

Therefore, **`onbeleefd`** literally translates to "not well-lived" or "not well-bred," which semantically equates to **rude**.
