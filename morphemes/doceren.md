From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch verb **`doceren`** can be analyzed through two lenses: **synchronic morphology** (how the word is structured in modern Dutch) and **diachronic morphology** (its historical/etymological structure).

Here is the breakdown and explanation of the morphemes.

### 1. Synchronic Analysis (Modern Dutch)
In contemporary Dutch grammar, the word is segmented into a **verb stem** and an **infinitive suffix**.

| Morpheme | Form | Type | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`docer-`** | Stem | Bound Root | Carries the core lexical meaning ("to teach/lecture"). |
| **`-en`** | Suffix | Inflectional | Marks the verb as an **infinitive** (the base form). |

*   **Pronunciation:** /doːˈseːrə(n)/
*   **Conjugation:** It is a **weak verb** (regular).
    *   *Present:* ik docer**e**, jij docer**t**
    *   *Past:* ik docer**de**
    *   *Past Participle:* ge**docer**d

### 2. Diachronic Analysis (Etymology)
To understand *why* the stem is `docer-` and not just `doc-`, we must look at the word's origin. `Doceren` is a **loanword** (internationalism) derived from Latin.

| Morpheme | Origin | Form | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`doc-`** | Latin Root | `doc-` | The semantic core meaning "to teach" or "to show." |
| **`-er-`** | Latin Stem | `-ēre` | Originally the Latin infinitive ending. In Dutch, this `r` was absorbed into the stem to fit Dutch phonotactics. |
| **`-en`** | Dutch | `-en` | The native Dutch infinitive suffix added to the loan stem. |

**Historical Path:**
1.  **Latin:** *docēre* (to teach).
2.  **French/Academic Latin:** The stem *doc-* was used in academic contexts.
3.  **Dutch:** The Latin infinitive ending *-re* was adapted to the Dutch infinitive ending *-en*, but the *r* was retained in the stem to preserve the sound of the original word.

### 3. Semantic Nuance
While the root `doc-` simply means "teach," the specific morphological construction `doceren` carries specific semantic weight in Dutch:

*   **`leren`:** General term for "to learn" or "to teach" (often primary/secondary education).
*   **`onderwijzen`:** Formal "to teach/instruct."
*   **`doceren`:** Specifically refers to **lecturing at a higher education level** (university or college). It implies a formal presentation of knowledge to a group of students.

### 4. Related Morphological Family
The root `doc-` appears in several other Dutch words, showing how the same morpheme generates different parts of speech:

*   **`docent`** (Noun): A lecturer/teacher.
    *   Breakdown: `doc-` (root) + `-ent` (agent noun suffix, from Latin *-ent-*).
*   **`docentuur`** (Noun): The position of a lecturer.
    *   Breakdown: `docent` + `-uur` (suffix denoting office/position).
*   **`docent`** vs **`doceren`**:
    *   `Docent` = The person (Agent).
    *   `Doceren` = The action (Verb).

### Summary
*   **Word:** `doceren`
*   **Stem:** `docer-` (derived from Latin *docēre*)
*   **Suffix:** `-en` (Dutch infinitive marker)
*   **Meaning:** To lecture (specifically in higher education).

In short, `doceren` is a **Latin-rooted verb** that has been fully integrated into Dutch morphology by adding the standard Dutch infinitive ending **`-en`** to the adapted Latin stem **`docer-`**.
