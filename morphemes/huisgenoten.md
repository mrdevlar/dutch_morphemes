From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`huisgenoten`** is a **compound noun** that includes an **inflectional suffix**.

Here is the morphological breakdown, followed by a detailed explanation of each component.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **`huis`** + **`genoot`** + **`-en`**

### 2. Detailed Breakdown

#### A. `huis` (Root / Free Morpheme)
*   **Category:** Noun (Lexical Morpheme).
*   **Meaning:** "House" or "home."
*   **Function:** In this compound, it acts as the **modifier** (or dependent). It specifies the location or context of the head noun.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *hūs*, from Old Dutch *hūs*, ultimately from Proto-Germanic **\*hūsą**. It is cognate with the English word *house* and the German word *Haus*.
*   **Status:** It is a **free morpheme**, meaning it can stand alone as an independent word.

#### B. `genoot` (Root / Free Morpheme)
*   **Category:** Noun (Lexical Morpheme).
*   **Meaning:** "Companion," "associate," "partner," or "member."
*   **Function:** This is the **head** of the compound. The entire word refers to a type of *genoot*.
*   **Derivation:** This noun is derived from the verb *genieten* (to enjoy). Historically, the root implies "one who shares" or "one who partakes." In a social context, a *genoot* is someone who shares a specific bond or situation with another.
    *   *Note:* While related to *genieten* (to enjoy), in this context, the semantic meaning has shifted toward "sharing a space or life."
*   **Status:** It is a **free morpheme** (e.g., *een genoot* = a companion), though it is rarely used in isolation in modern speech compared to its use in compounds.

#### C. `-en` (Suffix / Bound Morpheme)
*   **Category:** Inflectional Suffix.
*   **Function:** **Plural marker**.
*   **Rule:** In Dutch morphology, when a compound word is pluralized, the plural suffix is attached to the **head** of the compound (the last element), not the modifier.
    *   Singular: *huisgenoot* (housemate)
    *   Plural: *huisgenoot* + *-en* = *huisgenoten* (housemates)
*   **Status:** It is a **bound morpheme**, meaning it cannot stand alone and must attach to a root.

### 3. Syntactic and Semantic Structure

**Type of Word:** Noun-Noun Compound (*Zelfstandig naamwoord + Zelfstandig naamwoord*).

**Structure:**
1.  **Modifier:** `huis` (House)
2.  **Head:** `genoot` (Companion)
3.  **Inflection:** `-en` (Plural)

**Semantic Composition:**
The meaning is compositional, meaning the meaning of the whole is derived from the sum of its parts.
*   *Huis* (House) + *Genoot* (Companion) = A companion who shares a house.
*   In English, this translates to **"housemates"** or **"roommates."**

### 4. Linguistic Notes on Usage

*   **Stress Pattern:** In Dutch compounds, the primary stress typically falls on the **first element**.
    *   Pronunciation: **HUIS**-genoten (not huis-GE-no-ten).
*   **Linking Elements:** Dutch compounds sometimes use linking sounds (like *-s-*, *-e-*, or *-en-*) between the two nouns (e.g., *liefdes*verklaring). However, *huisgenoot* is a "direct compound" and does not require a linking element.
*   **Related Compounds:** The root *genoot* is productive in Dutch and appears in other compounds with similar meanings:
    *   *Gezinsgenoot* (Family member/living with family)
    *   *Medegenoot* (Fellow companion)
    *   *Liefdesgenoot* (Love partner)
