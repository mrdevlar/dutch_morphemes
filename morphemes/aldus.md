From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`aldus`** is a **lexicalized compound adverb**. While it functions as a single unit in modern Dutch, it is historically composed of two distinct morphemes.

Here is the breakdown and explanation of the morphemes, etymology, and semantic evolution.

### 1. Morphological Breakdown

| Morpheme | Form | Part of Speech (Historical) | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **`al-`** | Adverb / Quantifier | "All", "entirely", "completely" |
| **2** | **`-dus`** | Adverb / Demonstrative | "Thus", "so", "in this manner" |
| **Total** | **`aldus`** | Adverb | "Thus", "accordingly", "in this way" |

---

### 2. Detailed Etymological Analysis

#### Morpheme 1: `al-`
*   **Origin:** Proto-Germanic **\*allaz**.
*   **Cognates:** English *all*, German *all*, Old Norse *allr*.
*   **Function in Compound:** In this specific context, `al-` functions as an **intensifier**. It does not carry the temporal meaning of the modern Dutch word *al* (which means "already"). Instead, it retains its original quantifier meaning of "entirely" or "wholly."
*   **Semantic Contribution:** It emphasizes the completeness of the manner described by the second morpheme.

#### Morpheme 2: `-dus`
*   **Origin:** Old Dutch **`thius`** / **`thus`**. This traces back to Proto-Germanic **\*þus** (related to demonstrative pronouns).
*   **Cognates:** English *thus* (archaic/formal), German *dus* (dialectal/obsolete, related to *so*).
*   **Function in Compound:** This is a **demonstrative adverb**. It points to a specific manner or state of affairs previously mentioned or implied.
*   **Modern Status:** The morpheme survives as a free morpheme in modern Dutch as the word **`dus`** (meaning "so" or "therefore").

---

### 3. Semantic Evolution (Grammaticalization)

The formation of *aldus* represents a process of **lexicalization** and **semantic bleaching**.

1.  **Literal Composition:** Originally, the phrase meant "all thus" or "entirely in this way." It was a combination of an intensifier and a demonstrative.
2.  **Fusion:** Over time (Middle Dutch period), the two words fused into a single lexical item. The boundary between the morphemes became opaque to modern speakers.
3.  **Semantic Narrowing:** While *dus* in modern Dutch is a general connector meaning "so/therefore," *aldus* has become restricted to a more formal register. It implies "as follows" or "according to the preceding statement."
    *   *Example:* "Hij deed het **aldus**." (He did it **thus/in this manner**.)
    *   *Example:* "**Aldus** getekend." (Signed **thus** / Signed **as follows**.)

### 4. Syntactic and Register Notes

*   **Register:** *Aldus* is considered **formal**, **administrative**, or **legalistic**. In colloquial speech, a Dutch speaker would typically use *zo* (so) or *op deze manier* (in this way).
*   **Productivity:** The compound is **non-productive**. You cannot use the pattern `al-` + `dus` to create new words (e.g., you cannot say *`alzo`* to mean "all so" in the same way, though *alzo* exists as an archaic synonym).
*   **Comparison to English:** The English word **`thus`** is a direct cognate of the second morpheme (`-dus`). English does not have the `al-` intensifier attached to it, making *aldus* a distinctively Dutch (and Low German) development.

### Summary
Linguistically, **`aldus`** is a fossilized compound where the quantifier **`al`** ("all") intensifies the demonstrative adverb **`dus`** ("thus"). Together, they form a formal adverb meaning "in this specific manner" or "accordingly."
