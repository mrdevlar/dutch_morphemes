### 2. Morpheme-by-Morpheme Analysis

#### 1. `on-` (Negative Prefix)
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix.
*   **Function:** Negation.
*   **Explanation:** This is the Dutch equivalent of the English prefix **un-** (as in *unannounced*) or the German *un-*. It attaches to adjectives or participles to reverse their meaning.
*   **Phonology:** It is unstressed.

#### 2. `aan-` (Separable Verbal Prefix)
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix (Part of the verb stem).
*   **Function:** Aspectual/Directional.
*   **Explanation:** This is part of the separable verb **`aankondigen`** (to announce). In Dutch, many verbs consist of a prefix and a root. When conjugated in the past participle, the prefix `aan-` remains attached to the front, and the participle marker `ge-` is inserted between the prefix and the root.
*   **Semantic Role:** It adds a sense of "towards" or "completion" to the root action.

#### 3. `ge-` (Participle Prefix)
*   **Type:** Inflectional Prefix.
*   **Function:** Past Participle Marker.
*   **Explanation:** This is the standard marker for the past participle of weak verbs in Dutch.
*   **Rule:** In inseparable verbs (like *beloven*), `ge-` is omitted. In separable verbs (like *aankondigen*), `ge-` is placed **between** the prefix and the stem (`aan-` + `ge-` + `kondig`).

*   **Type:** Lexical Root.

*   **Etymology:** Historically, this stem is derived from the Old Dutch adjective **`kond`** (meaning "known" or "familiar," related to the modern word *kennis* for "knowledge") + the adjectival suffix **`-ig`**.
    *   *kond* (known) + *-ig* (suffix) $\rightarrow$ *kondig* (making known).
    *   *Note:* In modern synchronic morphology, `kondig` is often treated as a single atomic stem, but its internal structure is visible etymologically.

#### 5. `-d` (Participle Suffix)
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix.
*   **Function:** Past Participle Ending.
*   **Explanation:** This marks the verb as a weak (regular) past participle.

The word **`onaangekondigd`** is a **participial adjective**.
