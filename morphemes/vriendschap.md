From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`vriendschap`** (meaning "friendship") is a **derived noun**. It consists of two distinct morphemes: a free root morpheme and a bound derivational suffix.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented as follows:

> **vriend** + **-schap**

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### A. Root Morpheme: `vriend`
*   **Type:** Free Morpheme (can stand alone as a word).
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun.
*   **Meaning:** "Friend."
*   **Function:** This is the semantic core of the word. It provides the primary lexical meaning.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *vrient*, which comes from the Proto-Germanic *\*frijōndz* (the present participle of the verb *\*frijōną*, meaning "to love").
*   **Cognates:** English *friend*, German *Freund*, Swedish *vän* (via a different root path but related concept), Old English *frēond*.

#### B. Suffix Morpheme: `-schap`
*   **Type:** Bound Morpheme (cannot stand alone; must attach to a root).
*   **Part of Speech:** Derivational Suffix.
*   **Meaning:** Indicates a "state," "condition," "quality," or "collective body."
*   **Function:** It changes the part of speech or the semantic category of the root. Here, it transforms the concrete noun *vriend* (a person) into an abstract noun *vriendschap* (the relationship or state between friends).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *-scap*, from Proto-Germanic *\*-skapiz*.
*   **Cognates:** English *-ship* (as in *friendship*, *hardship*), German *-schaft* (as in *Freundschaft*, *Gesellschaft*).

### 3. Morphological Process
The formation of this word is an example of **Derivation**.
*   **Process:** Affixation (specifically, suffixation).
*   **Input:** Noun (*vriend*).
*   **Output:** Abstract Noun (*vriendschap*).
*   **Productivity:** The suffix *-schap* is highly productive in Dutch. It can be attached to many other nouns to create abstract concepts (e.g., *lid* "member" → *lidmaatschap* "membership"; *leerling* "pupil" → *leerlingenschap* "pupilage").

### 4. Phonological and Orthographic Notes
*   **Linking Element:** In Dutch morphology, an *-s-* often appears between roots and suffixes (known as a *tussenvoegsel* or linking *s*). However, in the case of *-schap*, the *-s-* is etymologically **part of the suffix itself**, not a separate linking element inserted between the root and the suffix.
    *   *Contrast:* In the word *liefdesverklaring* ("declaration of love"), the *-s-* is a linking element between *liefde* and *verklaring*.
    *   *In `vriendschap`:* The structure is strictly Root + Suffix.
*   **Pluralization:** If this word were pluralized, the suffix *-en* would be added to the end of the compound: *vriendschap* + *en* → **`vriendschappen`**. This confirms that *-schap* is part of the stem, not the inflectional ending.

### Conclusion
Linguistically, **`vriendschap`** is a transparent compound of a Germanic root and a Germanic suffix. It follows the standard Germanic pattern of creating abstract nouns denoting relationships or statuses (compare English *friendship* and German *Freundschaft*). The meaning is compositional: the state (*-schap*) of being a friend (*vriend*).
