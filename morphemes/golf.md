Here is the breakdown and explanation of the morphemes in **`golf`**.

### 1. Morphological Structure
**Word:** `golf`

**Structure:** Monomorphemic (one single morpheme)

*   **Analysis:** The word contains no prefixes, suffixes, or infixes. It is a **bound root** in the sense that it cannot be broken down further into smaller meaningful units (unlike *`golf-club`* which is a compound). It stands alone as a **free morpheme**.

### 2. Etymological Polysemy (Homonymy)
From a linguistic perspective, it is crucial to note that **`golf`** is not just one word with one history. In modern Dutch, it functions as a **homonym** (words that are spelled and pronounced the same but have different origins and meanings).

There are three distinct lexical items spelled `golf`:

#### A. `golf` (Wave) — *Native Germanic*
*   **Meaning:** A wave (of water), undulation.
*   **Origin:** Middle Dutch *golfe*, derived from Middle Low German. It is a native Germanic word.
*   **Cognates:** Related to the verb *`golven`* (to wave/undulate). It is distantly related to the English word *`gulf`* (via the concept of a "swallowing" or "bending" shape), though English *gulf* entered via Romance languages (Italian *golfo*), whereas Dutch *golf* (wave) is native.
*   **Linguistic Status:** This is the primary, native meaning.

#### B. `golf` (The Sport) — *Loanword*
*   **Meaning:** The game of golf.
*   **Origin:** Direct loan from English *`golf`* (19th century).
*   **Linguistic Status:** A **loanword** (leenwoord). It was adopted without phonological adaptation (it is pronounced with the Dutch /ɣ/ sound, but the spelling remains identical to English).

#### C. `golf` (Bay/Inlet) — *Loanword*
*   **Meaning:** A large bay or inlet (e.g., *De Golf van Mexico*).
*   **Origin:** From Italian *`golfo`* or French *`golfe`*.
*   **Linguistic Status:** A **loanword**. In Dutch, this meaning often overlaps with the native "wave" spelling, creating a homograph.

### 3. Derivational Potential
While `golf` itself is a single morpheme, it serves as a **base** (stem) for various derivational processes in Dutch. This demonstrates its productivity in the language:

*   **Diminutivation:**

*   **Verbalization:**

*   **Compounding:**

    *   `golf` + `baan` $\rightarrow$ **`golfbaan`** (golf course).

### Summary
Linguistically, **`golf`** is a **monomorphemic free root**. However, its simplicity is deceptive. It represents a **homonymic convergence** where a native Germanic word (wave) shares its form with Romance and English loanwords (bay, sport).
