From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`uniek`** (meaning "unique") is best analyzed as a **monomorphemic** word in its base form. However, to fully understand its structure, we must look at it through two lenses: **synchronic** (how it functions in modern Dutch) and **diachronic** (its historical etymology).

Here is the breakdown:

### 1. Synchronic Analysis (Modern Dutch)
In contemporary Dutch morphology, `uniek` is treated as a single, indivisible unit.

*   **Morpheme Count:** 1
*   **Type:** **Free Morpheme** (it can stand alone as a word).
*   **Category:** **Lexical Morpheme** (it carries the core semantic meaning).
*   **Status:** **Loanword** (it was borrowed from another language, not constructed from native Dutch roots).

**Crucial Note on the "un-" prefix:**
For English speakers, it is tempting to analyze `un-` as a negative prefix (like in *unhappy* or *unusual*). **This is incorrect in Dutch.**
*   In English, *un-* is a derivational morpheme meaning "not."
*   In Dutch, the negative prefix is **`on-`** (e.g., *onmogelijk* = impossible).
*   Therefore, in `uniek`, the `un-` is **not** a separate morpheme; it is part of the root.

### 2. Diachronic Analysis (Etymology)
While a modern Dutch speaker perceives `uniek` as one piece, historically it is composed of two morphemes borrowed from Latin.

*   **Origin:** Latin *unicus* (meaning "single," "sole," or "unique").
*   **Path:** Latin $\rightarrow$ French (*unique*) $\rightarrow$ Dutch (*uniek*).

**Historical Morphological Breakdown:**
1.  **`un-` (Root):** Derived from Latin *unus* (meaning "one").
2.  **`-iek` (Suffix):** Derived from Latin *-icus* (an adjective-forming suffix indicating belonging or relation).

**Semantic Evolution:**
*   *Unus* (one) + *-icus* (pertaining to) $\rightarrow$ "Pertaining to one" $\rightarrow$ "Single/Sole" $\rightarrow$ "Unique."

### 3. Inflectional Morphology
While `uniek` itself is one morpheme, it is an adjective and therefore subject to Dutch inflection rules. When used in a sentence, it may take a **bound morpheme** (suffix).

*   **Stem:** `uniek`
*   **Inflectional Suffix:** `-e` (attributive ending)

**Examples:**
*   *Een **uniek** huis* (A unique house) $\rightarrow$ No suffix (indefinite singular).
*   *Het **unieke** huis* (The unique house) $\rightarrow$ Suffix `-e` added.

In this context, the word consists of:
1.  **`uniek`** (Free lexical morpheme)
2.  **`-e`** (Bound inflectional morpheme)

### Conclusion
Linguistically, **`uniek`** is a **monomorphemic loanword**. It functions as a single lexical unit in modern Dutch, despite having a compound history in Latin (*unus* + *-icus*). It is vital not to parse the `un-` as a negative prefix, as is common in English.
