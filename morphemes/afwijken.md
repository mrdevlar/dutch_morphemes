From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`afwijken`** is a compound verb consisting of two distinct morphemes. It is a classic example of a **separable verb** (scheidbaar werkwoord) in Dutch morphology.

Here is the breakdown and explanation of the morphemes:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented as follows:
> **`af`** + **`wijken`**

---

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Prefix: `af`
*   **Type:** Separable Prefix (Particle).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *af*, related to Old High German *ab*, and cognate with English **`off`** and German **`ab`**.
*   **Core Meaning:** It denotes separation, direction away from a source, or completion.
*   **Function:** In this context, it acts as a **derivational morpheme**. It modifies the meaning of the base verb `wijken`. While `wijken` implies moving or yielding, adding `af` changes the directionality to imply moving *away* from a specific point, standard, or path.
*   **Grammatical Behavior:** In Dutch main clauses, this prefix separates from the verb stem and moves to the end of the sentence (e.g., *"De route wijkt **af**"*).

#### B. The Verb Stem: `wijken`
*   **Type:** Lexical Verb (Infinitive).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *wīken*, Old Dutch *wīkan*. It stems from the Proto-Germanic root **`*wīkaną`**, meaning "to yield, to give way, to move aside."
*   **Cognates:**
    *   German: *weichen* (to yield/soften).
    *   English: *wreak* (historically related to moving/turning, though semantically distant now) or *wick* (in the sense of a wick moving/flaring, though this is debated). The closest semantic English equivalent is **`yield`** or **`give way`**.
*   **Core Meaning:** To retreat, to yield, to move aside, or to change position.
*   **Inflection:**
    *   Present: *wijkt*
    *   Past: *week*
    *   Past Participle: *geweken* (Note: In the full verb, the participle becomes *afgeweken*).

---

### 3. Semantic Composition (The Whole Word)

When combined, the morphemes create a specific semantic meaning through **composition**:

*   **Literal:** "To yield away" or "to move aside from."
*   **Figurative/Idiomatic:** "To deviate," "to differ," or "to depart from."

**Semantic Shift:**
While `wijken` implies a physical movement (like a soldier yielding ground), the addition of `af` abstracts the meaning. It is most commonly used to describe abstract deviations, such as:
*   Deviating from a plan (*afwijken van het plan*).
*   Differing from a norm (*afwijken van de norm*).
*   Departing from a rule (*afwijken van de regels*).

### 4. Grammatical Context (Dutch Syntax)
Linguistically, `afwijken` is significant because it demonstrates the **V2 word order** and **separable verb** rules of Dutch:

1.  **Infinitive:** *Ik wil **afwijken**.* (I want to deviate.)
2.  **Main Clause:** *Het wijkt **af**.* (It deviates.) -> The prefix `af` is displaced.
3.  **Past Participle:** *Het is **afgeweken**.* (It has deviated.) -> The prefix `ge-` is inserted between the prefix and the stem (a rule specific to separable verbs).
