From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`neuropsychologie`** is a complex compound word derived from Greek roots via Latin and French scientific terminology. It is classified as a **loanword** (*leenwoord*) or **internationalism** in Dutch, meaning it is shared across many European languages with little modification.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation of the morphemes.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented hierarchically into three primary morphemes:

> **`neuro-`** + **`psych-`** + **`-logie`**

Alternatively, it can be viewed as a compound of two larger units:
> **`neuro-`** + **`psychologie`**

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. `neuro-` (Combining Form / Prefix)
*   **Origin:** Ancient Greek *neuron* (νεῦρον), meaning "nerve" or "sinew."
*   **Function:** In this context, it acts as a **combining form** functioning as a prefix. It modifies the meaning of the head noun to specify the domain of the nervous system.
*   **Meaning:** Relating to nerves, the brain, or the nervous system.
*   **Linguistic Note:** The final *-o* is a **linking vowel** (fugue) typical of Greek-derived scientific compounds in Dutch (e.g., *biologie*, *geologie*). It connects the root to the following element.

#### B. `psych-` (Root)
*   **Origin:** Ancient Greek *psyche* (ψυχή), meaning "soul," "mind," or "breath."
*   **Function:** This is the lexical **root** of the word.
*   **Meaning:** Relating to the mind, mental processes, or behavior.
*   **Linguistic Note:** In the standalone Dutch word *psychologie*, the *o* following this root is often analyzed as part of the suffix complex, but etymologically, it links the root *psych* to *logie*.

#### C. `-logie` (Derivational Suffix)
*   **Origin:** Ancient Greek *-logia* (-λογία), from *logos* (λόγος), meaning "word," "reason," or "study/discourse."
*   **Function:** This is a **derivational suffix**. It turns the preceding roots into a noun denoting a field of study or science.
*   **Internal Structure:** It can be further broken down into the stem **`-log-`** (study/discourse) + the nominalizing suffix **`-ie`** (common in Dutch for abstract nouns of Greek origin, e.g., *democratie*, *biologie*).
*   **Meaning:** The study of; the science of.

### 3. Morphological Process
The formation of this word involves two distinct morphological processes:

1.  **Derivation:** The word *psychologie* is formed by adding the suffix *-logie* to the root *psych-*.
2.  **Compounding:** The combining form *neuro-* is compounded with the noun *psychologie*.
    *   **Head:** The head of the compound is *psychologie* (the study of the mind).
    *   **Modifier:** *neuro-* specifies *which* aspect of the mind (the biological/nervous aspect).

### 4. Linguistic Context in Dutch
*   **Orthography:** In Dutch, unlike English (which sometimes hyphenates *neuro-psychology*), this is written as a single closed compound word (*samenstelling*).
*   **Stress Pattern:** Dutch stress rules for words ending in *-logie* typically place the primary stress on the syllable immediately preceding the suffix.
    *   *neu-ro-psy-cho-**lo**-gie*
*   **Pluralization:** As a noun, it can be pluralized using the standard Dutch plural suffix *-en*, though it is often used as a mass noun (uncountable).
    *   *De neuropsychologieën* (The neuropsychologies).
*   **Register:** This is a high-register, academic term (*vaktaal*). It is not part of the basic Germanic vocabulary of Dutch (unlike words like *hond* or *huis*).
