From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`uitbreiden`** (to expand, to extend) is a complex verb formed through derivation and inflection. It is a classic example of a **separable compound verb** (*scheidbaar werkwoord*).

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation of its components.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **uit** + **breid** + **en**

1.  **`uit-`** (Prefix)
2.  **`breid-`** (Verbal Root/Stem)
3.  **`-en`** (Infinitive Suffix)

---

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Prefix: `uit-`
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix (specifically, a separable verbal prefix).
*   **Literal Meaning:** "Out," "outside," or "forth."
*   **Function:** In this context, it acts as a directional modifier. It indicates that the action of the root verb is moving outward or beyond a current boundary.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *ūt*, from Proto-Germanic *\*ūt*.
*   **Cognates:** English **out**, German **aus**.
*   **Linguistic Note:** While *uit* can function as a free morpheme (e.g., as a preposition in *uit het huis* / "out of the house"), in *uitbreiden*, it is bound to the verb. However, unlike inseparable prefixes (like *be-* or *ver-*), *uit-* separates from the verb in main clause syntax (e.g., *Ik breid het **uit***).

#### B. The Root: `breid-`
*   **Type:** Verbal Root (Stem).
*   **Base Verb:** *breiden*.
*   **Meaning:** In modern isolation, *breiden* primarily means "to knit." However, in the context of *uitbreiden*, it retains an older, broader meaning of "to spread," "to widen," or "to extend."
*   **Function:** This carries the core lexical meaning of the word.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *breiden*, from Proto-Germanic *\*bredjaną*.
*   **Cognates:**
    *   English **broad** (semantic shift from "spread out" to "wide").
    *   German **breiten** (to spread).
    *   English **breadth** (width).
*   **Semantic Shift:** It is important to note the semantic shift. While a Dutch person today thinks of knitting when they hear *breiden*, the compound *uitbreiden* relies on the archaic sense of "spreading out" (making something wider/larger), similar to how English "broaden" works.

#### C. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix.
*   **Function:** Marks the verb as an **infinitive** (the dictionary form).
*   **Grammar:** This is the standard infinitive ending for the vast majority of Dutch verbs (weak and strong). It does not change the core meaning of the word but indicates its grammatical mood (non-finite).
*   **Linguistic Note:** In conjugated forms, this suffix changes or disappears (e.g., *ik breid uit* / *wij breiden uit*).

---

### 3. Syntactic Behavior: Separable Verbs
From a syntactic-linguistic perspective, *uitbreiden* is classified as a **separable verb** (*scheidbaar werkwoord*).

*   **In the Infinitive:** The morphemes are joined: *uitbreiden*.
*   **In a Main Clause:** The prefix `uit-` moves to the end of the clause, separating from the root `breid-`.
    *   *Example:* "Wij willen ons bedrijf **uitbreiden**." (We want to expand our company.)
    *   *Example:* "Wij **breiden** ons bedrijf **uit**." (We expand our company.)
*   **In the Past Participle:** The prefix `uit-` is reattached, and the ge- prefix is inserted between the prefix and the root.
    *   *Example:* "uitgebreid" (expanded).

### 4. Semantic Composition (Meaning Construction)
The meaning of the word is compositional, derived from the sum of its parts:

*   **`breid-`** (Spread/Widen) + **`uit-``** (Out) = **To spread out** $\rightarrow$ **To expand.**

This is semantically parallel to the English construction **"out" + "broaden"** (though English does not use this specific compound). A closer English equivalent in structure is **"extend"** (Latin *ex-* "out" + *tendere* "stretch"), where the prefix adds the directional "outward" force to the root action.
