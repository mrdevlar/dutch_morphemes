From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`haalbaar`** is a classic example of **derivational morphology**. It is formed by combining a verb stem with a suffix to create a new part of speech (an adjective).

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes:

### 1. The Segmentation
The word can be segmented into two distinct morphemes:
> **`haal`** + **`-baar`**

---

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Root/Stem: `haal-`
*   **Type:** Bound Root / Verb Stem.
*   **Source:** This is the stem of the Dutch verb **`halen`** (to fetch, to get, to reach, to haul).
*   **Etymology:**
    *   **Old Dutch:** *halen*
    *   **Middle Dutch:** *halen*
    *   **Proto-Germanic:** *\*haljaną* (to fetch, to draw).
    *   **Cognates:** It is related to the English word **`haul`** and the German word **`holen`**.
*   **Phonology:** The double vowel **`aa`** indicates a long vowel sound (/aː/). In Dutch orthography, a long vowel in a closed syllable (or before a consonant cluster) is typically written with double letters.
*   **Semantic Role:** It provides the core action or concept: the act of obtaining or reaching something.

#### B. The Suffix: `-baar`
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix.
*   **Function:** Adjectivalizer. It turns a verb into an adjective.
*   **Meaning:** It conveys **passive potential**. It means "capable of being [verb]ed" or "worthy of being [verb]ed."
*   **Etymology:**
    *   **Proto-Germanic:** *\*-bazaz* (related to the verb *beran*, to bear/carry).
    *   **Cognates:** This is the direct equivalent of the English suffix **`-able`** (e.g., *readable*, *breakable*) and the German suffix **`-bar`** (e.g., *tragbar* - bearable).
*   **Semantic Role:** It modifies the root to indicate feasibility or possibility.

---

### 3. Morphological Synthesis (How they combine)

When you combine the stem and the suffix, the meaning shifts from an action to a quality of that action:

1.  **Literal Construction:** "Able to be fetched" or "Capable of being reached."
2.  **Semantic Shift:** Over time, the meaning generalized from physical fetching to abstract achievement.
3.  **Modern Meaning:** **Feasible, achievable, attainable, doable.**

**Example Usage:**
*   *Is dit plan haalbaar?* (Is this plan feasible?)
*   *Een haalbare doelstelling.* (An achievable goal.)

### 4. Linguistic Classification
*   **Word Class:** Adjective.
*   **Morphological Process:** **Suffixation** (specifically, verbal-to-adjectival derivation).
*   **Voice:** Passive. The suffix *-baar* inherently implies a passive voice construction (something *is being* fetched), as opposed to active capability (like *-ig* in *krachtig* - powerful).
