From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`uitverkopen`** is a complex verb formed through compounding and derivation. It is a **separable verb** (*scheidbaar werkwoord*) that contains an **inseparable prefix** within its stem.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into four distinct morphemes:

> **`uit`** + **`ver`** + **`koop`** + **`en`**

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

| Morpheme | Type | Function | Meaning / Contribution |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`uit`** | **Separable Prefix**<br>*(Scheidbaar voorvoegsel)* | Derivational / Aspectual | Indicates direction ("out") or completion ("completely"). In this context, it implies selling until the stock is empty. |
| **`ver`** | **Inseparable Prefix**<br>*(Onscheidbaar voorvoegsel)* | Derivational | Changes the valency and direction of the root. It transforms "buying" into "selling." |
| **`koop`** | **Root / Stem**<br>*(Stam)* | Lexical | The core meaning related to "purchase" or "trade." Derived from the verb *kopen* (to buy). |
| **`en`** | **Inflectional Suffix**<br>*(Infinitiefuitgang)* | Inflectional | Marks the word as a verb in the infinitive form. |

### 3. Hierarchical Structure
Linguistically, the construction happens in two layers:

1.  **Layer 1 (Base Verb):** `ver` + `koop` + `en` = **`verkopen`** (to sell).
    *   Here, the prefix `ver-` is **inseparable**. It attaches permanently to the stem.
2.  **Layer 2 (Compound Verb):** `uit` + `verkopen` = **`uitverkopen`** (to sell out).
    *   Here, the prefix `uit-` is **separable**. It attaches to the already derived verb `verkopen`.

### 4. Syntactic and Morphological Behavior
The interaction between the separable `uit` and the inseparable `ver` creates specific grammatical rules in Dutch:

#### A. Word Order (Main Clauses)
Because `uit` is a separable prefix, it moves to the end of the clause in the present tense, while `ver` stays attached to the root.
*   *Infinitive:* Ik wil de voorraad **uitverkopen**.
*   *Main Clause:* Ik **verkoop** de voorraad **uit**.
    *(Note: `ver` remains with `koop`, but `uit` separates.)*

#### B. Past Participle Formation
This is a crucial linguistic feature.
*   **Rule:** Verbs with an **inseparable prefix** (like `ver-`, `be-`, `ont-`) do **not** take the participle prefix `ge-`.
*   **Application:** Even though `uit` is separable, the presence of the inseparable `ver-` inside the stem prevents the insertion of `ge-`.
*   **Result:** *uitverkopen* $\rightarrow$ **`uitverkocht`** (not *uitgeverkocht*).
    *   Structure: `uit` + `ver` + `koop` + `t` (past participle suffix).

#### C. Noun Formation
The verb can be nominalized by removing the infinitive suffix `en` and adding the definite article `de`.
*   *Verb:* uitverkopen
*   *Noun:* **de uitverkoop** (the sale / the clearance).
    *   Here, the separable `uit` fuses with the stem to form a compound noun.

### 5. Semantic Composition
The meaning is compositional, built from the bottom up:

1.  **`kopen`**: To acquire goods (Buy).
2.  **`ver-kopen`**: To transfer goods to someone else in exchange for money (Sell). The `ver-` reverses the perspective of the transaction.
3.  **`uit-ver-kopen`**: To sell until the goods are "out" (gone). The `uit-` adds a completive aspect (telic aspect), meaning the action continues until the object (stock) is exhausted.

### Summary
*   **Word:** `uitverkopen`
*   **Gloss:** OUT-SELL-BUY-INF
*   **Translation:** To sell out / To sell off.
*   **Key Linguistic Feature:** A separable verb (`uit-`) built upon a verb with an inseparable prefix (`ver-`), resulting in a past participle without `ge-` (*uitverkocht*).
