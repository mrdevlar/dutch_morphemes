From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`steunen`** can be analyzed through **morphology** (the study of word structure) and **etymology** (word origins).

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes:

### 1. Segmentation
The word is composed of two distinct morphemes:

> **steun** + **-en**

---

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Root/Stem: `steun`
*   **Type:** Free Morpheme / Lexical Root
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun (base) / Verb Stem
*   **Meaning:** "Support," "prop," "buttress," or "backing."
*   **Linguistic Function:** This is the core semantic unit of the word. It carries the primary meaning.
    *   As a **noun** (*de steun*), it refers to the physical object or abstract concept of support (e.g., *De steun van de muur* — The support of the wall).
    *   As a **verb stem**, it provides the action of supporting.
*   **Etymology & Cognates:**
    *   **Origin:** Derived from Middle Dutch *stōnen*.
    *   **Root Connection:** It is historically linked to the verb **`staan`** (to stand). The semantic logic is "to make something stand" or "to keep something upright."
    *   **False Friend Warning:** Do not confuse this with the Dutch word **`steen`** (stone). While a stone can be used as a support, *steunen* is not derived from *steen*.
    *   **Cognates:** It shares a Germanic root with the concept of standing firm. In English, the closest conceptual cognate is not "stone," but rather the stability implied in "stand" or "stay."

#### B. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix
*   **Function:** Marks the **Infinitive** form of the verb.
*   **Grammar:** In Dutch, the vast majority of verbs form their infinitive (the "to" form in English) by adding *-en* to the stem.
    *   *Example:* *werk* (work) + *-en* = *werken* (to work).
    *   *Example:* *loop* (walk) + *-en* = *lopen* (to walk).
*   **Pronunciation:** In standard Dutch, this is pronounced as a schwa sound /ə/ (often sounding like a short 'u' or 'uh'), making the pronunciation roughly /ˈstøː.nə(n)/. In rapid speech, the final 'n' is often dropped or nasalized.

---

### 3. Morphological Classification
*   **Word Class:** Verb (Infinitive).
*   **Verb Type:** Regular (Weak) Verb.
    *   Because it is a regular verb, the past tense is formed by adding *-de* or *-te* to the stem (*steunde*), and the past participle adds the prefix *ge-* and the suffix *-d* (*gesteund*).
*   **Derivation vs. Inflection:**
    *   The addition of *-en* is **inflectional**. It does not change the part of speech (the stem *steun* is already verbal in this context) or the core meaning; it simply marks the grammatical mood (infinitive).
    *   However, since *steun* exists independently as a noun, there is a **zero-derivation** (conversion) relationship between the noun *steun* and the verb *steunen*.

### 5. Semantic Nuance
Linguistically, *steunen* covers a range of meanings depending on context:
1.  **Physical:** To lean against or prop up (*Hij steunt tegen de muur* — He leans against the wall).
2.  **Abstract:** To support a cause or person (*Ik steun dit project* — I support this project).
3.  **Financial:** To subsidize or fund (*De overheid steunt de kunst* — The government subsidizes the arts).

In all cases, the core morpheme **`steun`** retains the fundamental concept of **stability** derived from the idea of "standing."
