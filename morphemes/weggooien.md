From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`weggooien`** is a classic example of a **separable compound verb** (*scheidbaar werkwoord*).

Here is the morphological breakdown and linguistic analysis of the word.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
We can segment the word into three distinct morphemes:

> **`weg`** + **`gooi`** + **`-en`**

### 2. Morpheme-by-Morpheme Analysis

#### A. `weg` (Particle / Adverb)
*   **Category:** Adverbial Particle (in this context).
*   **Literal Meaning:** Away, off, gone.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *weg*, related to the noun *weg* (way/road) and the English word *way*. However, in this verbal construction, it functions as a directional particle indicating movement away from a reference point.
*   **Function:** It acts as a **preverb**. It modifies the semantic meaning of the verb stem. While `gooien` means "to throw," adding `weg` changes the meaning to "to throw away" or "to discard."
*   **Polysemy:** Note that `weg` is also a standalone noun meaning "road" or "way." In `weggooien`, it is not a noun; it is a grammatical particle.

#### B. `gooi` (Verb Stem)
*   **Category:** Lexical Root (Verb Stem).
*   **Meaning:** Throw.
*   **Etymology:** From Middle Dutch *goyen*.
*   **Function:** This carries the core lexical meaning of the action.
*   **Morphology:** This is the **stem** of the verb. In Dutch morphology, the infinitive is formed by adding the suffix `-en` to the stem.

#### C. `-en` (Inflectional Suffix)
*   **Category:** Inflectional Suffix.
*   **Function:** This is the **infinitive marker**. It marks the verb as being in its base, unconjugated form (the dictionary form).
*   **Grammar:** In Dutch, almost all verbs in the infinitive end in `-en` (e.g., *lopen*, *eten*, *slapen*). It does not carry tense or person information in this form.

### 3. Linguistic Features & Behavior

To fully understand `weggooien`, one must look beyond simple segmentation into syntax and phonology.

#### A. Syntax: Separability (*Scheidbaarheid*)
This is the most defining linguistic feature of this word. Although written as one word in the infinitive, it behaves as two separate units in a sentence structure.
*   **Infinitive:** *Ik wil het **weggooien**.* (I want to throw it away.)
*   **Main Clause:** *Ik **gooi** het afval **weg**.* (I throw the trash away.)
*   **Subordinate Clause:** *...dat ik het afval **weggooi**.* (...that I throw the trash away.)

In the main clause, the particle `weg` is "kicked" to the end of the sentence, while the conjugated stem `gooi` stays in the second position. This is known as the **V2 rule** combined with **verb separation**.

#### B. Phonology: Stress Pattern
In Dutch, stress placement distinguishes between separable and inseparable compound verbs.
*   **`WEGgooien`**: The primary stress falls on the particle **`weg`**.
*   **Comparison:** Compare this to an inseparable verb like *beloven* (to promise), where the stress is on the stem (*beLOven*).
*   **Significance:** The stress on `weg` signals to the listener that this is a separable verb, which predicts the syntactic behavior described above.

#### C. Semantics: Compositionality
The meaning of `weggooien` is largely **compositional**, meaning the meaning of the whole is derived from the sum of its parts:
*   `gooien` (Throw) + `weg` (Away) = Throw Away.
*   However, it has developed a specific idiomatic nuance: it implies **disposal** or **discarding** permanently, rather than just physically throwing something in a different direction.

**Conclusion:**
Linguistically, `weggooien` is a **particle verb** formed derivationally by combining an adverbial particle (`weg`) with a verb stem (`gooi`), inflected with an infinitive suffix (`-en`). Its behavior is governed by the rules of Dutch separable verbs, affecting word order, stress, and conjugation.
