From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`vergelijking`** (meaning "comparison" or "equation") can be analyzed through **derivational morphology**. It is a complex word formed by combining a root with a prefix and a suffix.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **ver-** + **gelijk** + **-ing**

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Root: `gelijk`
*   **Type:** Free Morpheme (Root/Base).
*   **Original Part of Speech:** Adjective.
*   **Meaning:** "Equal," "same," or "alike."
*   **Function:** This is the semantic core of the word. It provides the fundamental concept of equality or similarity.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Proto-Germanic **\*galīkaz*.
*   **Cognates:**
    *   English: *like* (as in "alike"), *equal* (conceptually related).
    *   German: *gleich* (equal/same).
    *   Note: In modern Dutch, *gelijk* still exists as an independent adjective (e.g., *gelijk hebben* = to be right/equal).

#### B. The Prefix: `ver-`
*   **Type:** Bound Morpheme (Derivational Prefix).
*   **Function:** **Verbalizer.** It attaches to the adjective *gelijk* to create a verb (*vergelijken*).
*   **Semantic Role:** In Dutch, the prefix *ver-* often indicates a change of state, a process, or a transformation. When attached to *gelijk*, it shifts the meaning from a static state ("being equal") to an action ("to make equal" or "to bring into alignment").
*   **Resulting Word:** *vergelijken* (to compare).
*   **Etymology:** From Old Dutch *far-*, related to German *ver-*.

#### C. The Suffix: `-ing`
*   **Type:** Bound Morpheme (Derivational Suffix).
*   **Function:** **Nominalizer.** It attaches to the verb stem (*vergelijk-*) to create a noun.
*   **Semantic Role:** It turns the action of the verb into a noun representing either the **process** (the act of comparing) or the **result** (the comparison itself).
*   **Resulting Word:** *vergelijking* (comparison).
*   **Etymology:** From Old Dutch *-unga*, related to English *-ing*.
*   **Cognates:** English *-ing* (e.g., *building*, *painting*), German *-ung* (e.g., *Vergleichung* is rare, but *Bildung* exists).

### 3. Derivational Pathway
Linguistically, this word is formed through a two-step derivation process:

1.  **Adjective $\rightarrow$ Verb:**
    *   *gelijk* (equal) + *ver-* $\rightarrow$ *vergelijken* (to compare / to make equal).
2.  **Verb $\rightarrow$ Noun:**
    *   *vergelijk-* (verb stem) + *-ing* $\rightarrow$ *vergelijking* (comparison).

### 4. Semantic Composition
When combined, the morphemes create a specific meaning:
*   **Literal construction:** "The act of making equal."
*   **Actual usage:** "Comparison."
*   **Nuance:** In mathematics, a *vergelijking* is an "equation" (where two sides are made equal). In general language, it is a "comparison" (looking for similarities/equality between two things).
