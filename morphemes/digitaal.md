From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`digitaal`** (meaning "digital") is a **derived word** formed through the combination of a bound root and a derivational suffix. It is a loanword that has been fully integrated into Dutch morphology.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into two distinct morphemes:

> **digit** + **aal**

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Root: `digit-`
*   **Type:** Bound Root (Lexical Morpheme)
*   **Origin:** Latin *digitus* (meaning "finger" or "toe").
*   **Status in Dutch:** This is a **bound root**, meaning it does not stand alone as an independent word in modern Dutch. While the English word "digit" exists as a noun, the Dutch equivalent for the noun "digit/number" is usually *cijfer*. However, the root *digit-* survives in technical and scientific vocabulary (e.g., *digitale*, *digit*).
*   **Semantic Meaning:** Originally referred to the fingers used for counting. Over time, the meaning shifted from the physical finger to the numbers represented by fingers (0–9), and finally to the concept of discrete numerical data.

#### B. The Suffix: `-aal`
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix
*   **Origin:** Latin *-alis* (via French *-al* and International Scientific Vocabulary).
*   **Function:** It changes the part of speech from a noun (or noun stem) to an **adjective**.
*   **Meaning:** It conveys the meaning "pertaining to," "characterized by," or "of the nature of."
*   **Orthography:** In Dutch, this suffix is spelled with a double **`aa`** to indicate a long vowel sound (/aː/). This is a standard orthographic rule in Dutch (e.g., *nationaal*, *fysicaal*, *lokaal*).

### 3. Synchronic vs. Diachronic Perspective

*   **Diachronic (Historical):** Historically, the word entered Dutch from the Latin adjective *digitalis* or the French *digital*. The Dutch spelling adapted to fit Dutch phonology and orthography (changing the French *-al* to the Dutch *-aal*).
*   **Synchronic (Current):** For a modern Dutch speaker, the word is analyzed as the stem *digit-* combined with the productive adjective suffix *-aal*. This is the same pattern used in words like *nationaal* (*natie* + *-aal*) or *sociaal* (*socie* + *-aal*).

### 4. Semantic Shift (Etymology of Meaning)
The meaning of the morphemes has undergone a specific evolution:
1.  **Literal:** Pertaining to fingers (*digitus*).
2.  **Mathematical:** Pertaining to numbers/digits (counting on fingers).
3.  **Technological:** Pertaining to data represented by discrete numbers (0s and 1s), as opposed to analog signals.

### 5. Phonological Note
*   **Stress:** The stress in Dutch falls on the final syllable: *di-gi-**TAAL***. This is typical for Dutch adjectives ending in *-aal*.
*   **Vowel Length:** The double `aa` represents a long open /aː/ sound.
