From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`ongemakkelijk`** (meaning *uncomfortable*, *awkward*, or *inconvenient*) is a complex word formed through **derivational morphology**. It consists of three distinct morphemes layered upon one another.

Here is the breakdown and explanation of each component.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
Visually, the word can be segmented as follows:

> **`on-`** + **`gemak`** + **`-elijk`**

However, to understand the spelling and phonology, it is helpful to view the suffix as **`-lijk`** with an inserted vowel. A more precise structural analysis is:

> **`on-`** + **`gemak`** + **`-lijk`** (orthographically realized as `-kelijk`)

---

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Prefix: `on-`
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix.
*   **Function:** Negation (Privative).
*   **Meaning:** "Not," "lack of," or "opposite of."
*   **Linguistic Context:** This is a Germanic prefix cognate with the English **`un-`** (e.g., *uncomfortable*) and the German **`un-`** (e.g., *unbequem*). It attaches to adjectives (and sometimes nouns) to reverse their meaning.
*   **Attachment:** In this specific word, it attaches to the adjective *gemakkelijk* to create its antonym.

#### B. The Root: `gemak`
*   **Type:** Free Morpheme (Noun Root).
*   **Meaning:** "Comfort," "ease," "convenience," or "facility."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *gemak*, related to the adjective *mak* (ready, fit, or easy).
*   **Phonology:** The vowel **`a`** in *mak* is a **short vowel** (/a/). This is crucial for the spelling of the word (see Section 3).

#### C. The Suffix: `-elijk` (or `-lijk`)
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix.
*   **Function:** Adjectivalizer (turns a noun into an adjective).
*   **Meaning:** "Having the quality of," "like," or "characterized by."
*   **Linguistic Context:** This is cognate with the English suffix **`-ly`** (e.g., *friendly*, *manly*) and the German **`-lich`** (e.g., *männlich*).
*   **Note on Form:** The underlying suffix is **`-lijk`**. However, when attached to a noun ending in a consonant (like *gemak*), an epenthetic vowel **`e`** is often inserted for ease of pronunciation, resulting in the spelling **`-elijk`**.

---

### 3. Orthographic and Phonological Note: The Double "K"
You may notice the word is spelled with a double **`k`** (*gemakkelijk*), even though the root *gemak* ends in one `k` and the suffix *lijk* begins with an `l`.

*   **The Rule:** In Dutch orthography, a short vowel in a stressed syllable must be followed by a double consonant if the syllable is closed by the next morpheme.
*   **The Process:**
    1.  Root: *gemak* (Short /a/ sound).
    2.  Suffix: *-lijk*.
    3.  If written as *gemaklijk*, the first syllable (*ge-mak*) would be open, potentially implying a long vowel sound (like in *maken*).
    4.  To preserve the short /a/ sound, the final consonant of the root is doubled: *gemak* + *lijk* → **`gemakkelijk`**.
*   **Result:** The first `k` belongs to the root *gemak*, and the second `k` is orthographic doubling to maintain the vowel quality before the suffix begins.

---

### 4. Semantic Composition (Meaning Construction)
The meaning of the word is built hierarchically:

1.  **`gemak`** (Noun): Comfort / Ease.
2.  **`gemak` + `-elijk`** → **`gemakkelijk`** (Adjective): Having the quality of comfort/ease (*comfortable* or *easy*).
3.  **`on-` + `gemakkelijk`** → **`ongemakkelijk`** (Adjective): Not comfortable/easy (*uncomfortable* or *awkward*).

### Conclusion
**`Ongemakkelijk`** is a classic example of Dutch **agglutinative** tendencies within a Germanic framework. It layers a negative prefix onto a noun-derived adjective. The spelling reflects strict phonological rules regarding vowel length (the double `k`), ensuring the word is pronounced with a short, stressed vowel in the root syllable.
