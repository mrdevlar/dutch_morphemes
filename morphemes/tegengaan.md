From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`tegengaan`** is a **compound verb** consisting of two distinct morphemes. It is a classic example of a **separable verb** (*scheidbaar werkwoord*) in Dutch morphology.

Here is the breakdown and explanation of its morphemes:

### 1. Morphological Breakdown

| Morpheme | Form | Type | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | `tegen-` | Separable Prefix / Particle | Against, opposite, counter |
| **2** | `-gaan` | Verb Root (Infinitive) | To go |
| **Total** | **`tegengaan`** | **Compound Verb** | **To oppose / to counteract** |

---

### 2. Detailed Linguistic Analysis

#### A. The Root: `-gaan`
*   **Category:** Verb (Infinitive).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *gān*, related to the English *go* and German *gehen*.
*   **Function:** This is the semantic core of the word. It provides the action. In this compound, the literal meaning of "movement" is often metaphorical, implying a direction of action rather than physical locomotion.

#### B. The Prefix: `tegen-`
*   **Category:** Preposition / Adverb functioning as a **Separable Prefix**.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *tegan*, related to German *gegen* and English *again* (historically related to "against").
*   **Function:** In this context, `tegen` modifies the direction or nature of the verb `gaan`. It indicates opposition.
*   **Linguistic Note:** Unlike inseparable prefixes in Dutch (like *be-*, *ge-*, *ver-*, *ont-*), which are stressed on the root (e.g., *be**kom**en*), separable prefixes carry the primary stress (e.g., **te**gen**gaan**).

#### C. Syntactic Behavior (Separability)
The most distinct linguistic feature of `tegengaan` is its **separability**. In Dutch syntax, the prefix and the root separate when the verb is conjugated in a main clause.

*   **Infinitive:** `tegengaan` (One word)
*   **Present Tense:** `Ik ga ... tegen` (Two words)
    *   *Example:* "Ik ga de regels **tegen**." (I go against the rules / I oppose the rules.)
*   **Past Participle:** `tegengegaan`
    *   *Rule:* The participle marker `-ge-` is inserted **between** the prefix and the root.
    *   *Structure:* `tegen` + `ge` + `gaan` = `tegengegaan`.

#### D. Semantic Composition
The meaning of the compound is **idiomatic** but transparent (compositional).
*   **Literal:** "To go against."
*   **Figurative:** "To oppose," "to resist," "to counteract," or "to prevent."
*   **Comparison:** This functions very similarly to the English phrasal verb "to go against."

### 3. Etymological Context
*   **Proto-Germanic:**
    *   *tegen* traces back to *\*tugan-* (turning, direction).
    *   *gaan* traces back to *\*gāną* (to go).
*   **Development:** Over time, the combination shifted from a literal physical movement in an opposite direction to an abstract action of resistance or opposition.

### Example in Context
> "De overheid probeert de verspreiding van het virus **tegen te gaan**."
> *(The government tries to **counteract** the spread of the virus.)*

In this sentence, you can see the morphemes split (`tegen` moves to the end of the clause), demonstrating the separable nature of the compound.
