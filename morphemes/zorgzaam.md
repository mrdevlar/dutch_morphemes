From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`zorgzaam`** is a classic example of **derivational morphology**. It is formed by combining a lexical root with a derivational suffix to create a new word with a specific grammatical category (an adjective) and a nuanced meaning.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word consists of two distinct morphemes:

> **zorg** + **-zaam**

*   **zorg**: The root (base) morpheme.
*   **-zaam**: The derivational suffix.

---

### 2. Analysis of the Root: `zorg`

*   **Part of Speech:** Noun (can also function as a verb stem in *zorgen*).
*   **Meaning:** "Care," "concern," "worry," or "attention."
*   **Etymology:**
    *   Derived from Old Dutch *sorga*.
    *   Traces back to Proto-Germanic **\*surgō**.
    *   Ultimately from Proto-Indo-European (PIE) **\*serg-**, meaning "to watch over" or "to guard."
*   **Cognates:**
    *   **English:** *Sorrow* (historically related to care/worry), *Surgery* (via Latin *chirurgia*, from Greek *cheirourgia* "hand work," but the Germanic root shares the concept of "watching over").
    *   **German:** *Sorge* (worry/care).
*   **Semantic Role:** In this specific compound, *zorg* carries the positive connotation of "care" or "attentiveness" rather than the negative connotation of "anxiety" or "worry."

### 3. Analysis of the Suffix: `-zaam`

*   **Part of Speech:** Derivational Suffix (forms adjectives).
*   **Meaning:** "Full of," "characterized by," "inclined to," or "prone to."
*   **Etymology:**
    *   Derived from the Old Dutch *sām* (related to gathering or collecting).
    *   Traces back to Proto-Germanic **\*-samaz**.
*   **Cognates:**
    *   **English:** **-some** (e.g., *handsome*, *widesome*, *lonesome*). While *-some* is less productive in modern English than it used to be, it shares the exact same Germanic root.
    *   **German:** **-sam** (e.g., *langsam* [slow], *same* [quiet/peaceful]).
*   **Function:** It transforms the noun *zorg* into an adjective describing a quality or disposition.

---

### 4. Semantic Composition (The Whole)

When combined, the morphemes create the following meaning:

*   **Literal Construction:** "Care" + "Full of/Characterized by"
*   **Resulting Meaning:** **Caring, attentive, solicitous, conscientious.**

**Linguistic Nuance:**
While the English equivalent *careful* is structurally similar (*care* + *-ful*), *zorgzaam* is often more positive.
*   *Careful* in English can imply "cautious" (avoiding mistakes).
*   *Zorgzaam* implies "attentive to the needs of others" or "conscientious."

### 5. Related Word Family
To understand the morphology better, it helps to look at the word family surrounding the root *zorg*:

1.  **Zorgen** (Verb): To care for / to worry.
2.  **Zorg** (Noun): Care / Worry.
3.  **Zorgvuldig** (Adjective): Careful / Diligent (*zorg* + *vuldig* [full of]).
4.  **Voorzichtig** (Adjective): Cautious (*voor* [before] + *zorg* + *ig* [suffix]).
5.  **Zorgzaam** (Adjective): Caring / Attentive (*zorg* + *zaam*).
