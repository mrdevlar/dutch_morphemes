From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`duisternis`** (meaning "darkness") is a derived noun formed through a process of **derivation**. It consists of two distinct morphemes: a lexical root and a derivational suffix.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented as follows:

> **`duister`** + **`-nis`**

---

### 2. Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Root: `duister`
*   **Morphological Category:** Free Morpheme (Adjective)
*   **Meaning:** Dark, dim, gloomy, obscure.
*   **Function:** This is the lexical base that provides the core semantic content of the word.
*   **Etymology:**
    *   **Middle Dutch:** *duuster*
    *   **Old Dutch:** *dūstari*
    *   **Proto-Germanic:** *\*dūstraz*
    *   **Proto-Indo-European (PIE):** The root is likely **\*dʰews-**, meaning "to breathe," "smoke," or "mist."
    *   **Semantic Shift:** The connection between "breath/smoke" and "darkness" is metaphorical; mist or smoke obscures light, creating darkness.
*   **Cognates:**
    *   **English:** *Dusky* (shares the same Germanic root regarding dim light).
    *   **German:** *düstere* (gloomy/dark).
    *   **Old English:** *dūster*.
    *   *Note:* While English uses the word "darkness," the English word "dark" comes from a different Germanic root (*\*derk-*), whereas Dutch *duister* aligns more closely with the English word "dusk."

#### B. The Suffix: `-nis`
*   **Morphological Category:** Bound Morpheme (Derivational Suffix)
*   **Meaning:** State, condition, quality, or result.
*   **Function:** This is a **nominalizer**. It converts the adjective *duister* (dark) into an abstract noun *duisternis* (the state of being dark).
*   **Etymology:**
    *   **Middle Dutch:** *-nisse*
    *   **Old Dutch:** *-nissa*
    *   **Proto-Germanic:** *\*-nassuz*
*   **Cognates:**
    *   **English:** *-ness* (e.g., *darkness*, *kindness*, *happiness*).
    *   **German:** *-nis* (e.g., *Erkenntnis* - knowledge/insight, *Fremdnis* - alienation).
    *   **Old English:** *-nes*.
*   **Phonology/Orthography:** In Dutch, this suffix is spelled *-nis* in the singular. When pluralized, it becomes *-nissen* (e.g., *duisternissen*), confirming that the *n* belongs to the suffix, not the root.

---

### 3. Semantic Composition
The meaning of the whole word is compositional, meaning it is derived directly from the sum of its parts:

1.  **`duister`** (Dark) + **`-nis`** (State/Condition of)
2.  **Literal Translation:** "The state of being dark."
3.  **Actual Meaning:** Darkness.

### 5. Comparative Note
While the Dutch word *duisternis* is structurally identical to the English word **darkness** (Adjective + *-ness*), the roots differ slightly.
*   **English:** *Dark* + *ness*
*   **Dutch:** *Duister* + *nis*

This highlights a common feature in Germanic languages: while the grammatical structure for forming abstract nouns is shared (the *-ness/-nis* suffix), the specific lexical roots for "dark" diverged between the West Germanic dialects that became English and Dutch.
