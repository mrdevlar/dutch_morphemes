From a linguistic perspective, the analysis of the Dutch word **`horloge`** requires a distinction between **synchronic morphology** (how the word functions in modern Dutch) and **diachronic morphology** (the historical etymological breakdown).

Here is the breakdown and explanation.

### 1. Synchronic Analysis (Modern Dutch)
In contemporary Dutch, **`horloge`** is considered **monomorphemic**.

*   **Status:** It is a **loanword** (specifically from French).
*   **Morphological Structure:** `[horloge]`
*   **Explanation:** To a native Dutch speaker, the word cannot be segmented into smaller meaningful units. The parts *hor-* and *-loge* do not carry independent meaning in the Dutch language system. You cannot combine *hor-* with other Dutch words to create new meanings, nor does *-loge* function as a productive suffix in Dutch.
*   **Inflection:** It behaves like a standard noun.
    *   **Plural:** *horloges* (adding the suffix *-s*)
    *   **Diminutive:** *horlogeje* (adding the suffix *-je*)
    *   **Compounding:** It can serve as the base for compounds, e.g., *horlogeband* (watch strap) or *horlogemaker* (watchmaker). In these cases, the entire word *horloge* acts as a single root morpheme.

### 2. Diachronic Analysis (Etymological Breakdown)
While modern Dutch treats it as one unit, the word has a rich history of morphological segmentation in its source languages (Greek $\rightarrow$ Latin $\rightarrow$ French $\rightarrow$ Dutch).

**Origin Path:**
Ancient Greek $\rightarrow$ Medieval Latin $\rightarrow$ Old French $\rightarrow$ Middle Dutch $\rightarrow$ Modern Dutch.

**Etymological Morphemes:**
If we trace the word back to its Ancient Greek root **ὡρολόγιον** (*hōrológion*), we can identify three distinct morphemes:

1.  **`hōra-`** (Greek: *ὥρα*)
    *   **Meaning:** "Hour," "time," or "season."
    *   **Function:** The semantic root indicating the measurement of time.
2.  **`-log-`** (Greek: *λόγος* / *logos*)
    *   **Meaning:** "Word," "reason," "account," or "reckoning."
    *   **Function:** In this context, it implies a "reckoning" or "calculation."
    *   *Note:* This is the same root found in English words like *logic*, *dialogue*, or *biology*.
3.  **`-ion`** (Greek: *-ιον*)
    *   **Meaning:** A neuter diminutive suffix.
    *   **Function:** Turns the concept into a concrete object (a "little hour-reckoner").

**Semantic Evolution:**
*   **Greek:** *hōrológion* = "A device for reckoning hours."
*   **Latin:** *horologium* (adopted directly from Greek).
*   **French:** *horloge* (The Latin *-ium* ending was dropped, and the spelling shifted to French orthography).
*   **Dutch:** *horloge* (Borrowed from French in the 17th/18th century, retaining the French spelling and pronunciation).
