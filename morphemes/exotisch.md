From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`exotisch`** (meaning *exotic*) can be analyzed through **morphology** (the study of word structure) and **etymology** (the history of the word).

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word consists of two morphemes:

> **`exot-`** + **`-isch`**

*   **`exot-`**: The lexical root (bound root).
*   **`-isch`**: The derivational suffix.

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Root: `exot-`
*   **Type:** Bound Root (Lexical Morpheme).
*   **Status:** In modern Dutch, this is not a free morpheme. You cannot use "exot" as a standalone word (e.g., you cannot say *"Dat is exot"*). It only exists attached to a suffix.
*   **Meaning:** It carries the core semantic content of "foreign," "outside," or "from abroad."
*   **Etymology:**
    *   **Ultimate Origin:** Ancient Greek **`ἔξω` (exō)**, meaning "outside" or "from without."
    *   **Intermediate:** The Greek adjective **`ἐξωτικός` (exōtikos)** meant "foreign" or "external."
    *   **Transmission:** This passed into Latin as **`exoticus`**, then into French as **`exotique`**, before entering Dutch.
    *   **Note on the 't':** Etymologically, in Greek, the *t* was part of the adjectival suffix (*-tikos*). However, in the history of the word as it entered Western European languages (Latin/French/Dutch), the *t* has been reanalyzed as part of the stem. Therefore, in Dutch morphology, we treat **`exot-`** as the base.

#### B. The Suffix: `-isch`
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix (Bound Morpheme).
*   **Function:** It changes the part of speech of the root. It turns the abstract concept of the root into an **adjective**.
*   **Meaning:** It signifies "relating to," "characterized by," or "of the nature of."
*   **Etymology:**
    *   **Origin:** Greek **`-ικός` (-ikos)** $\rightarrow$ Latin **`-icus`** $\rightarrow$ Old High German **`-isc`** $\rightarrow$ Modern Dutch **`-isch`**.
    *   **Productivity:** This is a highly productive suffix in Dutch. It is used to form adjectives from nouns or other roots, often of foreign (Latin/Greek) origin.
    *   **Examples:** *historisch* (historical), *logisch* (logical), *musical* (musical), *politiek* (political - note the spelling variation).
*   **Phonology:** In this specific context, the spelling **`ch`** represents the voiceless alveolar fricative **/s/** sound (as in *s*), not the voiceless velar fricative **/x/** (the guttural 'ch' sound in *lachen*). This is standard for Greek-derived words ending in *-isch*.

---

### 3. Grammatical and Semantic Synthesis

*   **Word Class:** Adjective (*bijvoeglijk naamwoord*).
*   **Derivational Process:**
    1.  Start with the concept of "outside/foreign" (`exot-`).
    2.  Apply the adjectival suffix (`-isch`).
    3.  Result: "Relating to what is outside/foreign" $\rightarrow$ **Exotic**.
*   **Inflection:** As a Dutch adjective, it can be inflected for gender/number when used attributively (before a noun).
    *   *Een **exotisch** dier* (An exotic animal - common gender/singular).
    *   *Exotische **dieren*** (Exotic animals - plural).
    *   *Het **exotische** fruit* (The exotic fruit - neuter gender).

### 4. Related Morphological Families
To understand the morphemes better, it helps to look at cognates and derivatives within Dutch:

*   **`exotisme`** (Noun): `exot-` + `-isme` (suffix denoting a doctrine, practice, or state). Meaning: *Exoticism*.
*   **`exotica`** (Noun): Plural form often used to refer to exotic goods or plants.
*   **English Cognate:** *Exotic*. The morphological structure is identical (`exot-` + `-ic`), reflecting the shared Latin/Greek heritage.
