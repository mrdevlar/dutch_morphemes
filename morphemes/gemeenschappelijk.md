From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`gemeenschappelijk`** is a complex adjective formed through derivational morphology. It is built by stacking suffixes onto a lexical root.

Here is the breakdown and explanation of its morphemes.

### 1. Visual Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphological units:

> **gemeen** + **schap** + **elijk**

*(Note: Orthographically, the 'p' is doubled in the final form, and an 'e' is inserted before the final suffix.)*

---

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### A. Root: `gemeen`
*   **Type:** Free morpheme (Lexical Root).
*   **Part of Speech:** Adjective (historically).
*   **Meaning:** "Common," "shared," "public," or "mutual."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *gemeen*, Old Dutch *gimēn*, and Proto-Germanic **\*ga-mēniz* (where *ga-* implies "together" and *mēniz* implies "mind" or "measure").
*   **Linguistic Note:** In modern Dutch, the word *gemeen* has undergone semantic shift and can also mean "mean" or "nasty." However, in this compound, it retains its original etymological meaning of "shared" or "common."
*   **Cognates:** English *common* (via the same Proto-Germanic root), German *gemein*.

#### B. Suffix 1: `-schap`
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix.
*   **Function:** Nominalizer (turns an adjective into a noun).
*   **Meaning:** Indicates a state, condition, or collective body.
*   **Intermediate Word:** *gemeen* + *schap* = **`gemeenschap`** (Community / Society).
*   **Linguistic Note:** This suffix is cognate with the English suffix *-ship* (e.g., *friendship*, *hardship*).
*   **Stress:** In the noun *gemeenschap*, the primary stress usually falls on the root (*ge-MEEN-schap*).

#### C. Suffix 2: `-lijk` (appearing as `-elijk`)
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix.
*   **Function:** Adjectivizer (turns a noun into an adjective).
*   **Meaning:** "Having the quality of," "pertaining to," or "characterized by."
*   **Intermediate Word:** *gemeenschap* + *lijk* = **`gemeenschappelijk`**.
*   **Allomorphy:** The underlying suffix is *-lijk*. However, when attached to nouns ending in *-schap*, an epenthetic vowel *-e-* is inserted to break up the consonant cluster and facilitate pronunciation. Thus, it appears as the allomorph **-elijk**.
*   **Cognates:** English *-ly* (e.g., *friendship* → *friendshiply* is archaic, but *kingly*, *manly* follow the same Germanic pattern).

---

### 3. Orthographic Adjustments (Spelling Rules)
While the morphemes are `gemeen` + `schap` + `lijk`, the spelling changes to **`gemeenschappelijk`**. This is governed by Dutch orthographic rules:

1.  **Gemination (Doubling of 'p'):** When the suffix *-lijk* is added to a noun ending in *-schap*, the final consonant of the noun (*p*) is doubled.
    *   *Gemeenschap* (1 'p') $\rightarrow$ *Gemeenschappelijk* (2 'p's).
    *   This doubling preserves the short vowel sound of the preceding syllable in the phonological structure of the word.
2.  **Epenthesis (Insertion of 'e'):** As mentioned above, the *-e-* is inserted before *-lijk* to create the allomorph *-elijk*.

### 4. Semantic Composition
The meaning of the word is compositional, built layer by layer:

1.  **`gemeen`**: Shared / Common.
2.  **`gemeen` + `schap`**: The state or body of being shared $\rightarrow$ **Community**.
3.  **`gemeenschap` + `elijk`**: Characterized by the state of community $\rightarrow$ **Communal / Common / Shared**.
