From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`willen`** is a verb in its **infinitive** form. It can be segmented into two distinct morphemes: a lexical root and an inflectional suffix.

Here is the morphological breakdown:

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### **Morpheme 1: `will-` (Lexical Root / Stem)**
*   **Type:** Free/Bound Root (Lexical Morpheme).
*   **Meaning:** "to want," "to wish," or "to will." It carries the core semantic content of the word.
*   **Grammatical Category:** Verb (specifically, a **modal verb**).
*   **Morphophonological Note:**
    *   While the infinitive is spelled **`willen`**, the underlying grammatical stem used for conjugation is actually **`wil`**.
    *   In Dutch orthography, when a stem ends in a short vowel followed by a single consonant (like `wil` /wɪl/), the consonant is doubled before a vowel-initial suffix (like `-en`) to preserve the short vowel sound.
    *   Therefore, morphologically, the root is **`wil-`**, but it appears as **`will-`** in this specific surface form to maintain phonological integrity.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Proto-Germanic **\*willjaną**. It is cognate with the English verb **"will"** (as in "I will go") and the German verb **"wollen."**

#### **Morpheme 2: `-en` (Inflectional Suffix)**
*   **Type:** Bound Morpheme (Inflectional).
*   **Function:** **Infinitive Marker.**
*   **Explanation:** In Dutch, the suffix `-en` (sometimes reduced to `-n` in speech) marks the verb as an infinitive. This is the "dictionary form" of the verb.
*   **Additional Functions:** This same suffix is also used for:
    *   The plural present tense (e.g., *wij willen* "we want").
    *   The imperative plural (e.g., *Willen jullie...?* "Do you want...?").
*   **Pronunciation:** In standard Dutch, this is often pronounced as a schwa /ə/ or a syllabic nasal /n/, and in rapid speech, the `-n` is frequently elided (dropped).
