From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`weleens`** is best analyzed as a **lexicalized compound adverb**. While modern speakers often perceive it as a single unit, its structure reveals a historical combination of two distinct adverbs.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into two primary morphemes:

> **`wel`** + **`eens`**

*(Note: In contemporary standard Dutch spelling, this is often written as two words, **`wel eens`**, but **`weleens`** is a widely accepted lexicalized variant.)*

---

### 2. Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The First Morpheme: `wel`
*   **Part of Speech:** Adverb.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *wella*, cognate with German *wohl* and English *well*.
*   **Core Meaning:** Originally denoted "good," "well," or "in a good manner."
*   **Function in Compound:** In this context, `wel` has undergone **semantic bleaching**. It no longer carries the full weight of "well." Instead, it functions as an **intensifier** or a **modal particle**. It adds a nuance of possibility, affirmation, or emphasis.
    *   *Example:* In "Dat is wel waar" (That is indeed true), `wel` emphasizes the truth.
    *   *In `weleens`:* It suggests "indeed" or "at some point."

#### B. The Second Morpheme: `eens`
*   **Part of Speech:** Adverb (Temporal).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *ēnēs*. This is the **fossilized genitive case** of the numeral *een* (one).
*   **Historical Structure:** `een` (one) + `-s` (genitive suffix).
*   **Linguistic Parallel:** This is structurally identical to the English word **`once`** (one + -s) or **`always`** (all + -s). In older Germanic languages, the genitive case was frequently used to indicate time (e.g., "of one time" $\rightarrow$ "once").
*   **Core Meaning:** "One time," "at one time," or "ever."
*   **Function in Compound:** It provides the temporal aspect of the word, indicating an indefinite point in time.

---

### 3. Semantic Composition (How the meaning is built)

When combined, the meanings of the two morphemes fuse to create a specific pragmatic function:

1.  **Literal Reconstruction:** "Well" + "Once" $\rightarrow$ "Indeed once."
2.  **Semantic Shift:** Over centuries, the phrase specialized. It moved from meaning a specific single instance ("once") to an **indefinite frequency**.
3.  **Current Meaning:**
    *   **Ever:** Used in questions to ask about past experience (*Heb je dat weleens gedaan?* / Have you ever done that?).
    *   **Sometimes/Occasionally:** Used to indicate something happens now and then (*Ik zie hem weleens* / I see him sometimes).
    *   **Politeness/Softening:** It is often used to soften a request or suggestion (*Zou je dat weleens kunnen?* / Could you perhaps do that?).

### 4. Linguistic Classification

*   **Type:** **Lexicalized Compound.**
    While it is composed of two words, it functions as a single lexical item in the mental lexicon of a Dutch speaker. The meaning is not simply the sum of its parts (you cannot translate it literally as "well once" in most contexts).
*   **Orthographic Variation:**
    *   **`wel eens` (Two words):** The prescriptive standard recommended by the *Groene Boekje* (official Dutch spelling guide) when used as an adverbial phrase.
    *   **`weleens` (One word):** A common variant that has become lexicalized, often preferred in informal writing or specific dialects.
*   **Morphophonology:** There is no significant sound change at the boundary between `wel` and `eens` (unlike some Dutch compounds where vowels drop or consonants assimilate), which supports the analysis that it is a relatively recent lexicalization compared to ancient compounds like *huiswerk* (house + work).

### Summary Table

| Morpheme | Origin | Original Meaning | Function in `weleens` |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`wel`** | Old Dutch *wella* | Well / Good | Modal particle (emphasis/possibility) |
| **`eens`** | Old Dutch *ēnēs* | Of one time (Genitive) | Temporal adverb (indefinite time) |
| **`weleens`** | Compound | "Indeed once" | Adverb meaning "ever," "sometimes," or "perhaps" |

### Conclusion
Linguistically, **`weleens`** is a fossilized temporal adverb formed by combining a modal adverb (`wel`) with a fossilized genitive numeral (`eens`). It serves as a classic example of how Germanic languages use case endings (the `-s` in `eens`) to create adverbs of time, similar to English *once* or *always*.
