From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`stereotypering`** is a complex noun formed through a process of derivation. It involves layers of morphology, combining ancient Greek roots with Germanic suffixes.

Here is the breakdown and explanation of the morphemes.

### 1. Visual Segmentation
To understand the structure, we can segment the word into its constituent morphemes (the smallest units of meaning):

> **`stereo`** + **`type`** + **`er`** + **`ing`**

*Note: In strict Dutch morphological analysis, the verb stem is often treated as a unit (`stereotyper-`), but etymologically and semantically, the Greek roots are distinct.*

---

### 2. Morpheme-by-Morpheme Analysis

#### A. `stereo-` (Greek Root / Combining Form)
*   **Origin:** Ancient Greek *stereos* (στερεός).
*   **Literal Meaning:** "Solid," "firm," or "three-dimensional."
*   **Function:** Prefix/Root.
*   **Context:** In this word, it implies something that is "fixed" or "rigid." It is a bound morpheme in Dutch (it rarely stands alone as a word, though it appears in words like *stereofoon*).

#### B. `-type` (Greek Root)
*   **Origin:** Ancient Greek *typos* (τύπος).
*   **Literal Meaning:** "Impression," "mold," "figure," or "model."
*   **Function:** Root.
*   **Context:** Combined with *stereo-*, it originally referred to a "solid mold" used in printing (where the type was cast in metal).
*   **Dutch Lexicalization:** In modern Dutch, `stereotiep` (noun) is often treated as a single lexical root, but linguistically it is a compound of these two Greek morphemes.

#### C. `-er` (Dutch Verbalizer Suffix)
*   **Origin:** Germanic/Dutch derivational suffix (part of the infinitive suffix *-eren*).
*   **Function:** Verbalizer (turns a noun into a verb).
*   **Context:** This morpheme transforms the noun *stereotiep* into the verb *stereotyperen* (to stereotype).
*   **Phonology/Orthography:** In the final word `stereotypering`, the `r` is visible because the suffix `-ing` attaches to the **verb stem**.
    *   Verb: *stereotyperen*
    *   Stem: *stereotyper-*
    *   Therefore, the `r` remains in the noun form.

#### D. `-ing` (Dutch Nominalizer Suffix)
*   **Origin:** Old Dutch *-ing* / *-ung* (cognate with English *-ing* and German *-ung*).
*   **Function:** Nominalizer (turns a verb into a noun).
*   **Meaning:** Indicates the **process**, **action**, or **result** of the verb.
*   **Context:** It changes the action of "stereotyping" (*stereotyperen*) into the concept of "stereotyping" (*stereotypering*).

---

### 3. Derivational History (The Chain of Formation)
To fully understand the morphology, it helps to see the step-by-step derivation chain:

1.  **Root (Greek):** *stereos* + *typos* $\rightarrow$ Concept of a "solid mold."
2.  **Dutch Noun:** `stereotiep` (The fixed image/model).
3.  **Dutch Verb:** `stereotiep` + `-eren` $\rightarrow$ `stereotyperen` (To apply the fixed image).
4.  **Dutch Noun (Process):** `stereotyper-` (Stem) + `-ing` $\rightarrow$ **`stereotypering`** (The act of stereotyping).

### 4. Semantic Shift
From a semantic perspective, the morphemes show a clear evolution of meaning:
*   **Physical:** Originally referred to a physical printing plate (solid mold).
*   **Psychological:** In the 20th century, the meaning shifted metaphorically. The "solid mold" became a "fixed mental image" applied to groups of people.
*   **Grammatical:** The suffixes `-er` and `-ing` shift the focus from the *object* (the stereotype) to the *action* (the act of stereotyping).
