From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`daardoor`** is a **compound word** formed through a specific grammatical process known as **R-pronominalization**.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Segmentation
The word consists of two distinct morphemes:
> **`daar`** + **`door`**

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### Morpheme 1: `daar`
*   **Type:** Free morpheme (can stand alone as a word).
*   **Category:** Demonstrative Adverb / Locative Adverb.
*   **Meaning:** "There."
*   **Linguistic Function:** In this specific construction, `daar` functions as an **R-word** (an adverb ending in *-r*). It acts as a **pro-form**. Instead of referring to a physical location, it refers back to a previously mentioned situation, fact, or abstract concept (similar to "that" in English).
*   **Etymology:** Cognate with English *there* and German *dort*.

#### Morpheme 2: `door`
*   **Type:** Free morpheme (can stand alone as a word).
*   **Category:** Preposition.
*   **Meaning:** "Through," "by," or "because of."
*   **Linguistic Function:** It indicates the means, agency, or cause of an action.
*   **Etymology:** Cognate with English *through* and German *durch*.

### 3. Syntactic and Semantic Combination
When these two morphemes combine, they form a **Pronominal Adverb** (*voornaamwoordelijk bijwoord*).

*   **The "R-Word" Rule:** In Dutch grammar, prepositions generally cannot be separated from their object when the object is a neuter pronoun (like *het* or *dat*).
    *   *Incorrect:* `door dat` (through that)
    *   *Correct:* `daardoor` (through that / because of that)
    *   *Mechanism:* The preposition (`door`) is moved to the front, and the neuter pronoun (`dat`) is replaced by the R-word (`daar`).
*   **Semantic Shift:** While `door` literally means "through," in the compound `daardoor`, the meaning shifts from spatial ("through that") to **causal** ("because of that" or "therefore").

### 4. Stress and Phonology
*   **Stress:** The primary stress falls on the first morpheme: **`DAAR`-door**.
*   **Phonology:** The two morphemes are fused into a single phonological word. There is no pause between them.

### Summary
Linguistically, **`daardoor`** is a **lexicalized compound** consisting of a **demonstrative R-adverb** (`daar`) and a **preposition** (`door`). It serves as a cohesive device in discourse, linking a consequence to a previously stated cause.
