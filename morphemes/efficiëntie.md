From a linguistic perspective, the analysis of the Dutch word **`efficiëntie`** (efficiency) involves looking at it through two lenses: **synchronic** (how the word is structured in modern Dutch) and **diachronic** (its historical etymological roots).

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation.

---

### 1. Synchronic Analysis (Modern Dutch Morphology)
In contemporary Dutch, native speakers generally perceive this word as a derivation from an existing adjective. The word is segmented into two morphemes:

| Morpheme | Form | Part of Speech | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Stem** | `efficiënt` | Adjective | The base lexical unit meaning "efficient." |
| **Suffix** | `-ie` | Derivational Suffix | Turns the adjective into an abstract noun. |

**Word Structure:** `efficiënt` + `-ie` → `efficiëntie`

*   **The Stem (`efficiënt`):** In Dutch, this is treated as an unanalyzable unit (a loanword). While it has internal history, a modern Dutch speaker does not actively parse it into smaller parts when using the language. It functions as the root.
*   **The Suffix (`-ie`):** This is a common derivational suffix in Dutch used to create abstract nouns from adjectives or verbs, particularly those of Latin or French origin (e.g., *competent* → *competentie*, *intensief* → *intensiteit*). It denotes a state, quality, or condition.

---

### 2. Diachronic Analysis (Etymology)
To understand the internal structure of the stem `efficiënt`, we must look at its Latin origins. The word entered Dutch via French (*efficacité*) and Latin (*efficientia*).

**Latin Breakdown:**
1.  **`ef-`** (Prefix): A variant of *ex-* meaning "out" or "thoroughly."
2.  **`fic-`** (Root): From the verb *facere*, meaning "to make" or "to do."
3.  **`-ent-`** (Suffix): A present participle suffix indicating an agent or quality (similar to English *-ing* or *-ent*).
4.  **`-ia`** (Suffix): An abstract noun suffix indicating a state or condition.

**Evolution to Dutch:**
*   Latin *efficientia* → French *efficacité* / Latinized *efficiency* → Dutch *efficiëntie*.
*   The Dutch adjective *efficiënt* captures the Latin participle *efficiens*.
*   The Dutch noun suffix *-ie* replaces the Latin *-ia*.

---

### 3. Orthographic Note: The Trema (¨)
A crucial linguistic feature of this word is the **trema** (diaeresis) on the **`ë`**.

*   **Spelling:** `efficiëntie`
*   **Function:** The trema indicates that the vowel **`e`** is pronounced separately from the preceding **`i`**.
*   **Phonology:** Without the trema, the sequence `ie` in Dutch is typically read as a long vowel sound /iː/ (as in *fiets*). The trema forces a syllable break:
    *   *Without trema:* `...ciëntie` (might be misread as one long sound).
    *   *With trema:* `...ci-ën-tie` (/iː/ + /ɛ/).
*   **Morphological Boundary:** The trema also visually reinforces the boundary between the stem and the suffix. It ensures the reader understands that the `ie` at the end is the suffix, while the `ë` belongs to the stem `efficiënt`.

### Summary
*   **Morphemes:** `efficiënt` (Adjective Stem) + `-ie` (Nominalizing Suffix).
*   **Process:** Derivation (Adjective → Abstract Noun).
*   **Origin:** Latin *efficientia* (via French).
*   **Key Feature:** The trema (`ë`) preserves the syllabic separation and pronunciation of the stem's final vowel.
