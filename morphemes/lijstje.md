From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`lijstje`** is a derived noun consisting of two distinct morphemes. Below is a detailed morphological breakdown, including phonological rules and grammatical implications.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented as follows:

> **`lijst`** + **`-je`**

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Root: `lijst`
*   **Type:** Free Morpheme (Lexical Morpheme)
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun
*   **Meaning:** "List" (a series of names, numbers, or items written or printed one below the other).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *lijst*, related to the English word *list* (in the sense of a border or edge, and by extension, a list of items).
*   **Grammatical Gender:** In its base form, *lijst* is a **common gender** noun (*de* lijst).
*   **Phonology:** /lɛist/ (The *ij* digraph represents a diphthong similar to the 'ei' in English 'eye').

#### B. The Suffix: `-je`
*   **Type:** Bound Morpheme (Derivational Suffix)
*   **Function:** Diminutive
*   **Meaning:** Indicates smallness, endearment, or triviality. It transforms the concept of a "list" into a "little list."
*   **Allomorphy:** In Dutch, the diminutive suffix has several allomorphs (variants) depending on the phonological ending of the stem. The standard form is *-je*, but it changes to *-tje*, *-pje*, *-etje*, or *-kje* based on specific phonotactic rules.
    *   **Rule Application:** The stem *lijst* ends in the voiceless consonant cluster /st/.
    *   **Constraint:** Because the stem already ends in a voiceless cluster, no additional consonant (like a *t*) is inserted for ease of pronunciation. Therefore, the allomorph remains the base form **-je**.
    *   *(Contrast: If the stem were a vowel, like `auto`, it would become `autotje`. If the stem ended in `m`, like `boom`, it would become `boompje`.)*
*   **Grammatical Effect:** The addition of a diminutive suffix in Dutch **always** changes the grammatical gender of the noun to **neuter** (*het*).

### 3. Phonological and Orthographic Interaction
The combination of these morphemes follows specific Dutch spelling rules regarding consonant clusters:

*   **Stem:** *lijst* (ends in /st/)
*   **Suffix:** *-je* (starts with /j/)
*   **Result:** *lijstje*
*   **Pronunciation:** /lɛistjə/
*   **Note on Spelling:** You might expect a spelling like *lijsttje* (doubling the *t*), but Dutch orthography generally avoids triple consonant clusters or redundant voiceless stops in this context. Since the /t/ sound is already present in the /st/ cluster, adding another /t/ is phonologically unnecessary and orthographically incorrect.

### 4. Semantic and Grammatical Shift
The derivation creates a shift in both meaning and grammar:

| Feature | Base Word (`lijst`) | Derived Word (`lijstje`) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Meaning** | A list (neutral size) | A little list / checklist |
| **Gender** | Common (*de*) | Neuter (*het*) |
| **Plural** | *lijsten* | *lijstjes* |
| **Connotation** | Formal / General | Often informal, specific, or affectionate |

### Summary
Linguistically, **`lijstje`** is a **monomorphemic stem** (*lijst*) combined with a **derivational diminutive suffix** (*-je*). This process is a classic example of **suffixation** in Dutch morphology, which triggers a mandatory **gender shift** (from *de* to *het*) and adheres to specific **phonotactic constraints** regarding the insertion of consonants.
