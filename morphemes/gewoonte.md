From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`gewoonte`** (meaning "habit" or "custom") is a **deverbal noun**. This means it is a noun derived from a verb.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation of its components.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation

The word can be segmented into three morphemes:

> **ge-** + **wont** + **-e**

*(Note: In modern synchronic analysis, some linguists may group the **t** with the suffix as **-te**, but etymologically the **t** belongs to the root stem. We will explain this distinction below.)*

---

### 2. Detailed Explanation of Morphemes

#### A. The Prefix: `ge-`
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix.
*   **Function:** In Dutch, `ge-` is highly productive. It serves several functions, but in this context, it acts as a **nominalizing prefix**. It helps transform a verb stem into a noun (specifically, an abstract noun or a resultative noun).
*   **Examples:**
    *   *gebruik* (use) from *bruiken*
    *   *geboorte* (birth) from *baren*
    *   *gezicht* (face/sight) from *zien*
*   **In `gewoonte`:** It marks the word as a noun derived from the action of the root verb.

#### B. The Root: `wont`
*   **Type:** Lexical Root (Stem).
*   **Origin:** This comes from the Old Dutch and Middle Dutch past participle stem of the verb **`wonen`** (to live, to dwell).
*   **The "T" Nuance:**
    *   In modern Dutch, the verb is *wonen* and the past participle is *gewoond*.
    *   However, historically (Middle Dutch), the past participle stem was **`wont`**.
    *   Therefore, the **`t`** in *gewoonte* is etymologically part of the root, not a suffix.
    *   *Synchronic View:* Modern speakers often perceive the pattern as *gewoon* + *-te* (by analogy with words like *geboorte*), but the historical root is *wont*.
*   **Semantic Meaning:** Originally, this root meant "dwelling" or "place of living."

#### C. The Suffix: `-e`
*   **Type:** Inflectional/Derivational Suffix.
*   **Function:** In older stages of Dutch (and still visible here), `-e` was a common marker for **feminine nouns**. It nominalizes the stem.
*   **Phonology:** In modern Dutch, this `-e` is pronounced as a schwa /ə/ (the "uh" sound at the end).
*   **Note on `-te`:** Because the root historically ended in `t` (*wont*), the combination of root `t` + suffix `e` creates the sound cluster `-te`. In modern Dutch morphology, this is often re-analyzed as a single abstract noun suffix **`-te`** (similar to *liefde*, *kennis*, *geboorte*), even though the `t` originated in the root.

---

### 3. Etymology and Semantic Shift

The most fascinating part of this word is how the meaning changed over time (Semantic Shift).

1.  **Original Meaning (Physical):** The root *wonen* means "to dwell" or "to live."
    *   *Literal sense:* "The state of dwelling" or "The place where one lives."
2.  **Intermediate Meaning (Mental):** Over time, the concept of "dwelling" shifted from a physical house to a mental state. Just as you live in a house, you "live" in a certain way of behaving.
3.  **Current Meaning (Abstract):** By the Middle Dutch period, the meaning had shifted to **"custom"** or **"habit."**
    *   *Logic:* A habit is a "mental dwelling place" or a state where one resides regularly.

### 4. Related Words (Word Family)

To understand the morphemes better, it helps to look at the word family:

*   **`wonen`** (Verb): To live / to dwell. (The base verb).
*   **`woning`** (Noun): Dwelling / House. (Uses the suffix *-ing* to denote the place).
*   **`gewoon`** (Adjective): Usual / Normal / Ordinary.
    *   *Connection:* This is the modern past participle form of *wonen* (historically *gewoont*). It means "accustomed to" or "lived in," which evolved to mean "standard."
*   **`gewoonlijk`** (Adverb): Usually.

### Conclusion
Linguistically, **`gewoonte`** is a fossilized form. It preserves an older past participle stem (`wont`) that has disappeared from the modern verb conjugation (*wonen* $\rightarrow$ *gewoond*), but survived in this specific noun. It is a perfect example of how a physical concept ("dwelling") can evolve into an abstract psychological concept ("habit") through morphological derivation.
