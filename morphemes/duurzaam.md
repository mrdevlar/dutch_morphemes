From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`duurzaam`** (meaning *sustainable* or *durable*) is a **derived adjective**. It consists of two distinct morphemes: a lexical root and a derivational suffix.

Here is the morphological breakdown and analysis:

### 1. Morphological Structure
**`duur`** + **`-zaam`** $\rightarrow$ **`duurzaam`**

*   **Morpheme 1 (Root):** `duur`
*   **Morpheme 2 (Suffix):** `-zaam`
*   **Word Class:** Adjective
*   **Phonology:** /ˈdyːr.zaːm/

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Root: `duur`
*   **Meaning:** In modern Dutch, the word *duur* primarily means "expensive." However, in the context of *duurzaam*, it retains an older, secondary meaning: **"lasting," "enduring," or "long-term."**
*   **Etymology:** This root stems from Old Dutch *dur* or *duer*. It is cognate with the German verb *dauern* (to last) and the English word *durable*.
*   **Semantic Shift (Polysemy):** This is a fascinating linguistic point. In Dutch, the concepts of "expensive" and "lasting" merged into the single word *duur*.
    *   *Expensive:* Implies high value/worth.
    *   *Lasting:* Implies high quality/durability.
    *   *German Split:* In German, these meanings diverged into *teuer* (expensive) and *dauern* (to last).
    *   *English Split:* In English, they diverged into *dear* (expensive/loved) and *durable* (lasting).
*   **Function in this word:** Here, it acts as the semantic base indicating the quality of endurance.

#### B. The Suffix: `-zaam`
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix (Adjectival).
*   **Function:** It transforms a root (usually a verb or adjective) into an adjective that indicates **capability, tendency, or fullness of a quality**. It is roughly equivalent to the English suffix *-some* (as in *troublesome*, *handsome*) or *-ful* (as in *helpful*).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Proto-Germanic *\*-samaz*. It is cognate with the German suffix *-sam* (e.g., *sinnlos* vs. *sinnvoll*... wait, better example: *langsam* / slow, literally "long-some") and the English *-some*.
*   **Productivity:** This is a highly productive suffix in Dutch. It can be attached to many roots to create new adjectives (e.g., *werkzaam* - active/working, *krachtzaam* - powerful, *zorgzaam* - caring).

---

### 3. Semantic Composition (How the meaning is built)

When combined, the morphemes create a specific semantic meaning:

1.  **Literal Construction:** "Lasting" + "-like/-ful" $\rightarrow$ **"Characterized by lasting."**
2.  **Modern Usage:** In contemporary Dutch (and global environmental discourse), *duurzaam* has been lexicalized to mean **"sustainable."** It implies a system or resource that can be maintained over a long period without depletion.
3.  **Linguistic Note on "False Friends":** An English speaker might look at *duur* and think "expensive." Therefore, *duurzaam* might be misinterpreted as "expensive-able." Linguistically, this is incorrect; the word relies on the archaic "lasting" definition of the root.

### Conclusion
Linguistically, **`duurzaam`** is a classic example of **derivational morphology**. It takes a root with a polysemous history (*duur*) and applies a productive adjectival suffix (*-zaam*) to specify the quality of endurance, resulting in the modern concept of sustainability.
