From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`opsturen`** is a **separable compound verb** (in Dutch: *scheidbaar werkwoord*).

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation of its components.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **`op`** + **`stuur`** + **`-en`**

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### A. `op` (The Particle / Prefix)
*   **Type:** Free morpheme functioning as a **verbal particle** (partikel).
*   **Origin:** Preposition/Adverb.
*   **Function:** It acts as a **derivational** element in this context. It modifies the meaning of the base verb.
*   **Meaning:** Literally "up," "on," or "upon."
*   **Linguistic Note:** In Dutch, this is not an inseparable prefix (like *be-* or *ver-*). It retains its stress and can separate from the verb during conjugation in main clauses.

#### B. `stuur` (The Verb Stem)
*   **Type:** Bound morpheme (Root).
*   **Origin:** Old Dutch *stōren* (to push/drive). Cognate with English *steer* and German *steuern*.
*   **Function:** The lexical core of the word. It carries the primary semantic weight.
*   **Meaning:** "To send," "to direct," or "to steer."
*   **Phonology:** The stem vowel is a long /yː/ (written as *uu*).

#### C. `-en` (The Infinitive Suffix)
*   **Type:** Bound morpheme (Inflectional suffix).
*   **Function:** Marks the verb as the **infinitive** form.
*   **Grammar:** In Dutch, the standard infinitive ending for verbs is *-en* (or *-n* after certain stems). It does not change the lexical meaning but indicates the grammatical mood (non-finite).

---

### 3. Syntactic Behavior (Separability)
The most defining linguistic feature of `opsturen` is its **separability**.

*   **In the Infinitive:** The morphemes are joined: *Ik wil het **opsturen**.* (I want to send it up/forward).
*   **In a Main Clause:** The particle `op` separates from the stem `stuur` and moves to the end of the clause (V2 word order rule).
    *   *Ik **stuur** het **op**.* (I send it up).
    *   *Morphological split:* `stuur` (conjugated verb) ... `op` (particle).
*   **In the Past Participle:** The prefix `ge-` is inserted **between** the particle and the stem.
    *   *op* + *ge* + *stuur* + *d* = **`opgestuurd`**.
    *   This confirms that `op` is a separable particle, not a true prefix (inseparable prefixes like *be-* do not take *ge-*: *bezorgd*, not *gebezorgd*).

### 4. Semantic Composition
The meaning of `opsturen` is largely **compositional**, meaning it is derived from the sum of its parts, though with specific idiomatic nuances.

*   **Literal:** To send something in an upward direction.
*   **Figurative/Idiomatic:** To forward (an email/document), to mail something to a specific destination (often implying sending it *away* from the speaker or to a superior authority).
    *   *Example:* "Ik moet dit rapport **opsturen** naar de directeur." (I have to forward this report to the director).

### Conclusion
Linguistically, **`opsturen`** is a **particle verb** consisting of a directional particle (`op`) and a weak verb stem (`stuur`) marked for the infinitive (`-en`). Its morphology dictates specific syntactic rules regarding word order and participle formation, distinguishing it from inseparable verbs in the Dutch language.
