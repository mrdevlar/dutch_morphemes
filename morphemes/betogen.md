From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch verb **`betogen`** can be analyzed by breaking it down into its constituent morphemes (the smallest units of meaning).

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word is segmented as follows:
**`be-`** + **`tog`** + **`-en`**

*   **`be-`**: Derivational Prefix
*   **`tog`**: Bound Root (Stem)
*   **`-en`**: Inflectional Suffix

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Prefix: `be-`
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix (Inseparable).
*   **Function:** In Dutch, the prefix *be-* is highly productive. It typically transforms a noun or an intransitive verb into a transitive verb. It often adds a sense of "covering," "affecting," or "completing" an action upon an object.
*   **In `betogen`:** Here, it turns the root into a transitive verb. It implies acting *upon* something (e.g., arguing *a point*, demonstrating *a cause*).
*   **Note:** Verbs with the inseparable prefix *be-* never take the *ge-* prefix in the past participle (e.g., *betoogd*, not *gebetoogd*).

#### B. The Root: `tog` (Historically `toog`)
*   **Type:** Bound Root (Lexical Morpheme).
*   **Status:** This is a **bound morpheme**, meaning it cannot stand alone as an independent word in Modern Dutch. You cannot say *"Ik toog"* (I toog) in contemporary language.
*   **Etymology:** The root is historically related to the Middle Dutch verb *toogen* or *toog*. Etymologically, it is often linked to the concept of "aiming," "drawing," or "setting against" (related to *trekken* - to pull/draw, or *tegen* - against).
*   **Phonological Note (Vowel Lengthening):** This is the most interesting linguistic feature of this word.
    *   In the infinitive **`betogen`**, the root appears as **`tog`** (short *o*). This is because the syllable is **closed** by the consonant *g* before the suffix *-en* is added (*be-tog-en*).
    *   In the related noun **`betoog`** (an argument/demonstration) or the past tense **`betoogde`**, the root appears as **`toog`** (long *oo*). This is because the syllable is **open** (*be-toog*), triggering the Dutch rule of **open syllable lengthening**.
    *   Therefore, while the spelling changes, the underlying lexical root is **`toog`**.

#### C. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix.
*   **Function:** This is the standard marker for the **infinitive** form of Dutch verbs.
*   **Grammar:** It indicates that the verb is in its base form (e.g., *om te betogen* - to argue). It does not carry tense or person information.

---

### 3. Semantic Polysemy (Meaning)
Linguistically, `betogen` is a case of **polysemy** (one word with multiple related meanings). The morphological structure remains the same, but the semantic interpretation shifts based on context:

1.  **To Argue/Propound:** To present a line of reasoning (e.g., *Hij betoogde dat...* / He argued that...).
2.  **To Demonstrate/Protest:** To march or show public support/opposition (e.g., *Ze betogen voor klimaat* / They are demonstrating for the climate).
3.  **To Prove/Show:** To make something evident (archaic or formal usage).

| Morpheme | Form | Type | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Prefix** | `be-` | Derivational | Makes the verb transitive; inseparable. |
| **Root** | `tog` / `toog` | Bound Root | The core lexical meaning (aiming/acting). |
| **Suffix** | `-en` | Inflectional | Marks the infinitive verb form. |

### 5. Related Forms (Paradigm)
To fully understand the morphology, it helps to see how the root vowel changes across the paradigm due to the syllable structure mentioned above:

*   **Infinitive:** *betog**en*** (Short *o*, closed syllable)
*   **Present Stem:** *betog* (Short *o*)
*   **Past Tense:** *betoo**gde*** (Long *oo*, open syllable)
*   **Past Participle:** *betoo**gd*** (Long *oo*, open syllable)
*   **Noun:** *betoo**g*** (Long *oo*, open syllable)

This variation highlights the interaction between **morphology** (word structure) and **phonology** (sound rules) in the Dutch language.
