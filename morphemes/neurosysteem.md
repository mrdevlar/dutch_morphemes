From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`neurosysteem`** (meaning "nervous system") is a **compound noun** (*samenstelling*).

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation of its components.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word consists of two distinct morphemes:

> **`neuro-`** + **`systeem`**

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. `neuro-`
*   **Type:** Bound Morpheme (Combining Form / Prefix-like).
*   **Function:** Derivational. It modifies the meaning of the head noun to specify a relation to nerves or the nervous system.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from the Ancient Greek word *neuron* (νεῦρον), meaning "nerve," "sinew," or "string."
*   **Linguistic Status:** In modern Dutch, *neuro-* is not a standalone word. It is a **combining form** used specifically in scientific and medical terminology (e.g., *neuroloog*, *neurologie*, *neurochirurgie*). It attaches to a following noun to create a new concept.

#### B. `systeem`
*   **Type:** Free Morpheme (Noun).
*   **Function:** The **Head** of the compound. In Dutch compounding, the rightmost element is the head; it determines the grammatical category (noun) and the semantic core of the word.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from the Greek *systēma* (σύστημα), via Latin *systema* and French *système*.
*   **Linguistic Status:** This is a standalone lexical item in Dutch. While etymologically it contains the prefix *syn-* (together) and a root related to *histanai* (to set/stand), in **synchronic** (current) Dutch morphology, it is treated as a single, indivisible unit.

### 3. Morphological Process: Compounding (*Samenstelling*)
The formation of this word follows the standard Dutch rule for **endocentric compounding**:

1.  **Structure:** [Modifier] + [Head]
2.  **Meaning:** A *systeem* (system) that is *neuro* (related to nerves).
3.  **Orthography:** In Dutch, compound nouns are written as one solid word without spaces or hyphens (unlike English, which might write "neuro system" or "neuro-system").
4.  **Stress Pattern:** In Dutch compounds, the primary lexical stress typically falls on the **first element** of the compound.
    *   Pronunciation: **NEU**-ro-systeem (Primary stress on *neu*, secondary stress on *systeem*).

### 5. Linguistic Note: Synchronic vs. Diachronic
It is important to distinguish between the **synchronic** (current) and **diachronic** (historical) analysis:
*   **Synchronic:** For a modern Dutch speaker, `systeem` is one morpheme. You cannot break it down further to understand the word's current meaning or grammar.
*   **Diachronic:** Historically, `systeem` comes from *syn-* (together) + *tem* (standing). However, this internal structure is no longer productive in Dutch; speakers do not analyze `systeem` as two parts when using the word today. Therefore, for a morphological analysis of modern Dutch, `neurosysteem` is a **two-morpheme** word.
