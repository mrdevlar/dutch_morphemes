From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`dagelijks`** (meaning "daily" or "everyday") can be segmented into three distinct morphemes.

Here is the breakdown:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
**`dag`** + **`-elijk`** + **`-s`**

---

### 2. Detailed Explanation of Morphemes

#### **A. Root: `dag`**
*   **Type:** Free Morpheme (Lexical Root)
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun
*   **Meaning:** "Day"
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Proto-Germanic **`*dagaz`**, which is cognate with the English word *day* and the German word *Tag*.
*   **Function:** This is the semantic core of the word. It provides the base concept of time (a 24-hour period) upon which the rest of the word is built.

#### **B. Derivational Suffix: `-elijk`**
*   **Type:** Bound Morpheme (Derivational Suffix)
*   **Part of Speech:** Adjective-forming
*   **Meaning:** "-ly", "-like", "pertaining to"
*   **Function:** This suffix converts the noun *dag* into an adjective. It indicates a relationship or characteristic.
    *   *Dag* (Noun) + *-elijk* = *Dagelijk* (Adjective: "daily" or "pertaining to a day").
*   **Linguistic Note:** In modern Dutch synchronic analysis, `-elijk` is treated as a single unit. However, historically (diachronically), it is a compound of the suffixes `-el` (often forming nouns or adjectives) and `-ijk` (adjectival). For example, compare *vriendelijk* (friendly) or *koninklijk* (royal).
*   **Spelling:** The *-e-* is part of the suffix itself, not a linking vowel inserted between *dag* and *lijk*.

#### **C. Suffix: `-s`**
*   **Type:** Bound Morpheme (Inflectional / Adverbial)
*   **Part of Speech:** Adverbial Marker
*   **Meaning:** "In a ... manner" or "Every ..."
*   **Function:** This suffix performs two related functions in this context:
    1.  **Adverbialization:** It turns the adjective *dagelijk* into an adverb. In Dutch, adding *-s* to an adjective is a common way to form an adverb of time or manner (e.g., *soms* from *som*, *maandelijks* from *maandelijk*).
    2.  **Uninflected Form:** In Dutch grammar, adjectives ending in *-s* (like *dagelijks*) are often the "base" form used when the adjective is not inflected for gender/number (e.g., *een dagelijks probleem*).
*   **Etymology:** Historically, this *-s* is a remnant of the **Old Dutch genitive case** (*dages* -> *dagelijks*). In Old Germanic, time expressions were often formed using the genitive case (similar to English "day's work" or "yesterday's news"). Over time, the genitive ending fossilized into a standard adverbial suffix.

---

### 3. Grammatical Usage & Synthesis

When combined, the morphemes create a word that functions primarily as an **adverb** or an **uninflected adjective**.

*   **As an Adverb:**
    *   *Hij komt **dagelijks** naar school.*
    *   (He comes **daily** to school.)
*   **As an Adjective (Uninflected):**
    *   *Het is een **dagelijks** probleem.*
    *   (It is a **daily** problem.)
    *   *Note:* If the noun is definite or plural, the adjective usually takes an *-e* ending (*de **dagelijkse** routine*), but the root morphemes remain the same.

### 4. Cross-Linguistic Comparison

To understand the structure better, it helps to compare it with cognates in related languages:

| Language | Word | Morphological Breakdown |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Dutch** | **dagelijks** | `dag` + `-elijk` + `-s` |
| **English** | **daily** | `day` + `-ly` |
| **German** | **täglich** | `Tag` + `-lich` |
| **Old English** | **dæġlic** | `dæġ` + `-lic` |

**Key Difference:** While English and German use a single adjectival suffix (*-ly* / *-lich*), Dutch adds the extra adverbial *-s* to create the standard form *dagelijks*.

### Summary
*   **`dag`**: The concept of "day".
*   **`-elijk`**: Makes it an adjective ("day-like").
*   **`-s`**: Makes it an adverb or standard uninflected form (historically genitive).
