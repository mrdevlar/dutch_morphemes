From a linguistic perspective, analyzing the Dutch word **"project"** requires distinguishing between its **synchronic** structure (how it functions in modern Dutch) and its **diachronic** structure (its historical etymology).

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes.

### 1. Synchronic Analysis (Modern Dutch)
In contemporary Dutch morphology, the word **project** is considered **monomorphemic**.

*   **Status:** Free Root Morpheme.
*   **Explanation:** To a modern Dutch speaker, "project" is an unanalyzable unit of meaning. It cannot be broken down into smaller meaningful parts within the Dutch language system. You cannot remove "pro-" and keep "ject," nor can you add Dutch prefixes to it (e.g., you cannot say *her-project*).
*   **Word Class:** Noun (*het project*).
*   **Stress:** The stress falls on the second syllable: /prɔˈjɛkt/. (This differs from the English noun, which stresses the first syllable).

### 2. Diachronic Analysis (Etymology)

**Origin:** Latin *proiectum* (neuter past participle of *proicere*).

| Morpheme | Origin | Meaning | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |

| **-ject-** | Latin Root (*iacere*) | "To throw" | Verbal Root |
| **-t** | Latin Suffix (*-tum*) | Past Participle Marker | Nominalization / Adjectival |

**Literal Etymological Meaning:** "That which has been thrown forward."

### 3. The "Fossilized" Morphemes
In linguistics, when a loanword enters a language, its internal affixes often become **fossilized**. This means they lose their status as independent morphemes in the borrowing language.

*   **The Root *-ject-*:** While not a standalone word in Dutch, this root is recognizable in the Dutch lexicon through other loanwords. It appears in:

*   **The Prefix *pro-*:** This is a productive prefix in Dutch (e.g., *pro-actief*, *pro-americaans*), but in the word *project*, it is not acting as a separable prefix. It is locked into the lexical item.
