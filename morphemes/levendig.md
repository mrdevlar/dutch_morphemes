From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`levendig`** (meaning *lively*, *vibrant*, or *alive*) is a complex adjective formed through derivational morphology. It is built upon a Germanic root and modified by two distinct suffixes.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes, their functions, and their etymological history.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation

The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **`lev`** + **`end`** + **`ig`**

*   **`lev`**: Root (Lexeme)
*   **`end`**: Derivational Suffix (Present Participle)
*   **`ig`**: Derivational Suffix (Adjectival)

---

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Root: `lev-`
*   **Form:** `lev-`
*   **Type:** Free/Bound Root (Stem)
*   **Meaning:** "Life" or "to live."
*   **Etymology:** This root stems from the Proto-Germanic **\*lib-**. It is cognate with the English verb *live*, the German verb *leben*, and the Latin *liber* (in the sense of "free" or "alive," though the semantic shift differs).
*   **Function:** This is the core semantic unit. It provides the base meaning of existence or biological life.
*   **Note:** In the infinitive verb *leven* (to live), the *-en* is the standard Dutch infinitive ending. The stem used for derivation is *lev-*.

#### B. The Participle Suffix: `-end`
*   **Form:** `-end`
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix (Present Participle)
*   **Meaning:** "Doing the action" or "in the state of."
*   **Function:** This suffix turns the verb stem into a present participle (similar to the English *-ing*).
*   **Intermediate Word:** `lev` + `end` = **`levend`**.
    *   *Levend* means "living" (as opposed to dead). It describes the biological state of being alive.
*   **Phonology:** In Dutch, this suffix is pronounced /ənt/ (schwa + n + t).

#### C. The Adjectival Suffix: `-ig`
*   **Form:** `-ig`
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix (Adjectival)
*   **Meaning:** "Having the quality of," "full of," or "characterized by."
*   **Function:** This suffix turns the participle *levend* into a qualitative adjective. It adds a layer of intensity or characteristic behavior.
*   **Final Word:** `levend` + `ig` = **`levendig`**.
    *   While *levend* means "alive" (state of being), *levendig* means "lively" (state of activity/energy).
*   **Etymology:** This suffix comes from the Proto-Germanic **\*-agaz**. It is cognate with the English adjectival suffix *-y* (e.g., *sunny*, *happy*) and the German suffix *-ig* (e.g., *sonnig*, *lebendig*).

---

### 3. Semantic Composition (How the meaning builds)

The meaning of the word shifts as each morpheme is added:

1.  **`lev`** (Root): Concept of **Life**.
2.  **`lev` + `end`** (*Levend*): Concept of **Being Alive** (Static state).
    *   *Example:* "Een levend dier" (A living animal).
3.  **`levend` + `ig`** (*Levendig*): Concept of **Being Lively** (Dynamic quality).
    *   *Example:* "Een levendig gesprek" (A lively conversation).

The addition of **`-ig`** is crucial. Without it, the word describes a biological fact (alive/dead). With it, the word describes a personality trait, atmosphere, or level of energy (vibrant/animated).

### 4. Cross-Linguistic Comparison

To understand the structure better, it helps to compare it with English and German cognates:

| Language | Word | Morphological Structure | Literal Gloss |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Dutch** | **`levendig`** | `lev` + `end` + `ig` | `live` + `ing` + `y` |
| **English** | **`lively`** | `life` + `ly` | `life` + `y` |
| **German** | **`lebendig`** | `leb` + `end` + `ig` | `live` + `ing` + `y` |

*   **Note on English:** English forms this concept differently. It takes the noun *life* and adds *-ly* (which functions adjectivally here, though usually adverbially). Dutch and German take the participle *living* (*levend/lebend*) and add the adjectival suffix *-ig/-y*.

### 5. Summary

*   **Word:** *Levendig*
*   **POS:** Adjective
*   **Structure:** Root + Participle Suffix + Adjectival Suffix
*   **Linguistic Process:** Derivation (specifically, denominal/deverbal derivation).
*   **Key Insight:** The word is not simply "life" + "adjective." It is "living" + "adjective." The intermediate participle stage (*levend*) is essential to the modern Dutch meaning, distinguishing between simply being alive (*levend*) and being energetic or vivid (*levendig*).
