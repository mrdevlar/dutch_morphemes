From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`zullen`** is a complex case because it functions as a modal auxiliary verb with an irregular paradigm. While it appears to be a simple word, its morphological structure involves both synchronic (current) inflection and diachronic (historical) irregularities.

Here is the breakdown and explanation of the morphemes in **`zullen`**.

---

### 1. Synchronic Morphological Breakdown
In modern Dutch morphology, we can segment the word into two distinct morphemes:

| Morpheme | Form | Type | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Root / Stem** | `zull-` | Lexical Morpheme | Carries the core semantic meaning ("to will/shall"). |
| **Suffix** | `-en` | Inflectional Morpheme | Marks the **infinitive** form of the verb. |

**Explanation:**
*   **`zull-` (The Stem):** This is the base form used to generate the infinitive and the plural present tense (e.g., *wij zullen* - "we shall"). Note that in Dutch orthography, the double `l` indicates that the preceding vowel (`u`) is short.
*   **`-en` (The Infinitive Marker):** This is the standard suffix for Dutch verbs in their dictionary form (e.g., *lopen*, *maken*, *zien*). It has no semantic meaning of its own but signals that the verb is not conjugated for person or number.

---

### 2. Morphophonological Irregularity (The *Zal* vs. *Zull* Alternation)
A strictly linguistic analysis must address the fact that the root `zull-` is not stable across the entire conjugation paradigm. This is a phenomenon known as **ablaut** (vowel gradation) or **stem suppletion** within the verb's paradigm.

*   **Infinitive/Plural:** `zull-en` / `wij zull-en` (Stem: *zull-*)
*   **Singular Present:** `ik zal` / `hij zal` (Stem: *zal-*)

**Linguistic Note:**
While the word provided is `zullen`, the root morpheme is technically **suppletive** in the present tense. The vowel changes from `a` (in *zal*) to `u` (in *zullen*). This is a remnant of the Germanic **strong verb** system (specifically Class 7 strong verbs), where vowel changes indicated tense or grammatical number. In modern Dutch, this is considered an irregularity.

---

### 3. Diachronic Morphology (Etymology)
To fully understand the morphemes, we must look at the word's history (Proto-Germanic).

*   **Proto-Germanic Root:** **`*sal-`**
*   **Proto-Germanic Verb:** **`*saljaną`** (to owe, to be obliged to).
*   **Old Dutch:** *sullen*
*   **Cognates:**
    *   English: **`shall`**
    *   German: **`sollen`**
    *   Old Norse: *skulu*

**Semantic Shift:**
Originally, the root `*sal-` was related to money or debt (related to the English word *sale* or *salary*).
1.  **Stage 1:** "To owe" (obligation).
2.  **Stage 2:** "To be obliged to" (moral obligation).
3.  **Stage 3:** "To be going to" (future tense marker).

In modern Dutch, `zullen` has largely lost the sense of "obligation" (which is now covered by *moeten*) and functions primarily as a marker for the **future tense** or **volition**.

---

### 4. Grammatical Classification
From a syntactic and morphological perspective, `zullen` belongs to a specific class of verbs:

*   **Verb Class:** **Modal Auxiliary Verb** (Modaliteitshulpwerkwoord).
*   **Defectiveness:** It is a defective verb in the sense that it does not have a standard past participle used in perfect tenses (you do not say *ik heb gezullt*; instead, you use the infinitive construction *ik heb moeten/willen/zullen* + infinitive).
*   **Infinitive Construction:** It is one of the few verbs that can appear in the "double infinitive" construction (e.g., *Ik ga het doen* vs. *Ik ga het **zullen doen***).

### Summary
*   **Word:** `zullen`
*   **Morphemes:** `zull-` (Root) + `-en` (Infinitive Suffix).
*   **Key Feature:** The root exhibits **vowel alternation** (*zal* vs. *zull-*) depending on person/number, a relic of Germanic strong verb morphology.
*   **Origin:** Proto-Germanic `*saljaną` (to owe/obligate), cognate with English *shall*.
