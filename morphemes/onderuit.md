From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`onderuit`** is a **compound directional adverb** (or a separable verbal particle). It is formed through the process of **compounding**, where two independent lexical items are joined to create a new semantic unit.

Here is the morphological and semantic breakdown:

### 1. Morphological Structure
The word consists of two free morphemes (meaningful units that can stand alone):

*   **Morpheme 1:** `onder`
*   **Morpheme 2:** `uit`

**Structure:** `[onder] + [uit]` $\rightarrow$ **`onderuit`**

### 2. Analysis of Individual Morphemes

#### A. `onder`
*   **Lexical Category:** Preposition / Adverb.
*   **Core Meaning:** "Under," "below," "beneath."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *under*, Proto-Germanic *\*under*, and Proto-Indo-European *\*ndher-*. It is cognate with the English word *under* and German *unter*.
*   **Function in Compound:** It establishes a vertical vector pointing downward or a position relative to a lower point.

#### B. `uit`
*   **Lexical Category:** Preposition / Adverb / Particle.
*   **Core Meaning:** "Out," "out of."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *ut*, Proto-Germanic *\*ut*, and Proto-Indo-European *\*ud-* (meaning "out" or "up"). It is cognate with the English word *out* and German *aus*.
*   **Function in Compound:** It establishes a vector of exit, removal, or emergence from a contained space.

### 3. Semantic Composition (The Meaning of the Whole)
In linguistics, the meaning of a compound is not always the simple sum of its parts (non-compositionality). While `onder` + `uit` literally suggests "under" + "out," the lexicalized meaning of **`onderuit`** has evolved into specific idiomatic concepts:

1.  **Literal Spatial Meaning:** "Down and out." It describes a trajectory that goes downward and exits a space.
    *   *Example:* "De ladder gleed onderuit." (The ladder slid down and out.)
2.  **Figurative/Resultative Meaning:** "To fail," "to collapse," "to be ruined," or "to decline." This is the most common usage. It implies a downward spiral leading to a negative exit state.
    *   *Example:* "Zijn bedrijf ging onderuit." (His business went under / collapsed.)
3.  **Physical Exhaustion:** "To drop down" due to fatigue.
    *   *Example:* "Ik viel onderuit van vermoeidheid." (I collapsed from exhaustion.)

### 4. Syntactic Function
`Onderuit` functions primarily in two syntactic roles:

*   **As a Separable Verb Particle:** It is frequently attached to verbs to create separable verbs (*scheidbare werkwoorden*). In the infinitive, it is written as one word; in the main clause, it splits.
    *   *Infinitive:* `onderuitgaan` (to go down/fail).
    *   *Conjugated:* "Hij **gaat** onder **uit**." (He is going down/failing.)
*   **As a Directional Adverb:** It modifies a verb to indicate direction.
    *   *Example:* "Hij viel **onderuit**." (He fell down/out.)

### 5. Linguistic Comparison
*   **vs. `naar onder`:** `Naar onder` is purely spatial (downwards). `Onderuit` implies a result or a specific path (downwards *and* out/away).
*   **vs. English "Down and Out":** The English idiom "down and out" (meaning destitute or defeated) is a very close semantic equivalent to the figurative use of `onderuit`.
*   **vs. `naar buiten`:** `Naar buiten` means "to the outside." `Onderuit` is more specific, implying a downward exit.

In conclusion, `onderuit` is a **directional compound** where the combination of "under" and "out" creates a specific semantic vector representing **decline, collapse, or a downward exit**, functioning syntactically as an adverb or a separable verb particle.
