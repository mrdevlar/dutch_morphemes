From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`tegenwoordig`** is a complex compound word. To understand it fully, we must look at both its **synchronic structure** (how it is analyzed by speakers today) and its **diachronic history** (its etymological origin), as the modern spelling obscures the original meaning.

Here is the morphological breakdown:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **tegen** + **woord** + **-ig**

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. `tegen` (Prefix / Preposition)
*   **Type:** Free morpheme (Preposition).
*   **Literal Meaning:** "Against," "towards," or "opposite."
*   **Function:** In this compound, it acts as a directional prefix. Historically, it implies facing something or being situated opposite to something else.
*   **Modern Usage:** It retains its core sense of opposition or direction (e.g., *tegen* = against).

#### B. `woord` (Root / Stem)
*   **Type:** Bound/Free morpheme (Noun stem).
*   **The Linguistic Twist:** This is the most critical part of the analysis.
    *   **Surface Appearance:** In modern Dutch orthography, this looks exactly like the noun **`woord`** (meaning "word").
    *   **Etymological Reality:** Historically, this morpheme derives from the verb stem **`word-`** (from the verb **`worden`**, meaning "to become" or "to turn").
    *   **Phonetic Shift:** In Middle Dutch, the distinction between the noun *woord* (word) and the verb stem *word* (become) was less distinct in spelling. Over time, the spelling standardized to *woord*, leading to a "folk etymology" where speakers associate it with "word," even though the meaning has nothing to do with language.
*   **Original Meaning:** "Turning" or "Becoming."

#### C. `-ig` (Suffix)
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix.
*   **Function:** Adjectivalizer. It turns a noun or verb stem into an adjective (similar to the English suffixes *-y*, *-ish*, or *-ful*).
*   **Meaning:** "Having the quality of" or "characterized by."
*   **Result:** It changes the concept of "turning against" into an adjective describing a state of being.

### 3. Semantic Evolution (How the meaning changed)

To understand *why* these morphemes create the meaning "present," we must trace the semantic shift:

1.  **Original Construction:** *tegen* (against/towards) + *worden* (to turn/become).
2.  **Literal Sense:** "Turning towards" or "Facing."
3.  **Metaphorical Shift:** If you are "facing" someone or something, you are physically there with them.
4.  **Modern Meaning:** "Being here" $\rightarrow$ **"Present"** (in time or space).

**Example:**
*   *Hij is **tegenwoordig**.* (He is **present** / He is here now.)
*   *De **tegenwoordige** tijd.* (The **present** tense.)

Understanding the morphemes helps explain related words in the Dutch lexicon:
