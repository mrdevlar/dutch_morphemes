From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`leeftijd`** (meaning "age") is a **compound word** (samenstelling). It is formed by combining a verb stem with a noun, connected by a fossilized inflectional ending.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation of the morphemes.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphological units:

> **leef** + **t** + **tijd**

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. `leef` (Verb Stem)
*   **Source:** The verb **`leven`** (to live).
*   **Type:** Bound Root / Verb Stem.
*   **Function:** This is the lexical root of the word. In Dutch morphology, when a verb is used in a compound, it often appears in its stem form.
*   **Note:** The infinitive is *leven*, but the stem (used for conjugation) is *leef*.
*   **Meaning:** "To live" or "existence."

#### B. `t` (Fossilized Inflection / Linking Element)
*   **Source:** The 3rd person singular present tense ending of the verb *leven* (e.g., *hij **leeft*** = "he lives").
*   **Type:** Bound Morpheme (Inflectional Remnant).
*   **Function:** In modern Dutch, this *t* acts as a **linking consonant** (*koppelklank*). However, unlike standard linking elements like *-s-* (as in *liefdes*verklaring) or *-en-* (as in *kinder*wagen), this *t* is a "fossilized" grammatical ending.
*   **Linguistic Nuance:** Historically, this compound was formed from the phrase *de tijd dat men leeft* ("the time that one lives"). Over time, the finite verb form *leeft* was lexicalized into the compound. Therefore, the *t* is technically an inflectional morpheme that has lost its grammatical function and become part of the lexical word.

#### C. `tijd` (Noun)
*   **Source:** The noun **`tijd`**.
*   **Type:** Free Morpheme.
*   **Function:** The head of the compound (the main noun). In Dutch compounds, the last element usually determines the grammatical category and the core meaning.
*   **Meaning:** "Time."

### 3. Syntactic and Semantic Structure

*   **Compound Type:** Verb-Noun Compound (specifically, a finite verb form + noun).
*   **Head:** *tijd* (Time).
*   **Modifier:** *leeft* (Living).
*   **Semantic Composition:** "Living time" $\rightarrow$ "The duration of one's life" $\rightarrow$ **"Age."**

### 4. Comparison with Related Words
To understand the morphology better, it helps to compare *leeftijd* with similar constructions:

*   **`levensduur`** (Life span):
    *   *Leven* (stem) + *s* (linking genitive) + *duur* (duration).
    *   Here, the linking element is *-s-*, which is more common for noun-noun or verb-noun compounds in Dutch.
*   **`werktijd`** (Working hours):
    *   *Werk* (noun/verb stem) + *tijd*.
    *   No linking *t* is needed because *werk* is not conjugated in the same way in this context.
*   **`leeftijden`** (Plural):
    *   *leeftijd* + *en* (plural suffix).
    *   This confirms that *leeftijd* functions as a single lexical noun unit in the plural form.

### Conclusion
Linguistically, **`leeftijd`** is a lexicalized compound where a verb stem (*leef*) is fused with a noun (*tijd*) via a fossilized verb ending (*t*). It is a classic example of how Dutch morphology preserves historical grammatical forms within modern vocabulary.
