From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`hopeloos`** (meaning "hopeless") is a derived adjective. It is formed through a process of **derivation**, where a suffix is added to a noun root to change its part of speech and meaning.

Here is the detailed morphological breakdown and explanation.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into the following components:

> **hoop** + **e** + **loos**

*   **hoop**: The lexical root (Noun).
*   **e**: A linking vowel (schwa/epenthesis).
*   **loos**: The derivational suffix (Adjective).

*(Note: In some linguistic analyses, the suffix is treated as the variant **-eloos** when attached to specific stems, but the core semantic unit is **-loos**.)*

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Root: `hoop`
*   **Category:** Noun (Substantive).
*   **Meaning:** "Hope."
*   **Function:** This is the **lexical base** of the word. It carries the core semantic content.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *hope*, Old Dutch *hopa*. It is a Germanic cognate of the English word *hope* (from Proto-Germanic *\*hōpō*).
*   **Phonology:** In isolation, the noun is pronounced with a long /oː/ sound, spelled with a double vowel (**oo**) because it forms a closed syllable (*hoop*).

#### B. The Linking Vowel: `e`
*   **Category:** Interfix / Epenthetic Vowel.
*   **Meaning:** None (It is semantically empty).
*   **Function:** This is a **linking element** (in Dutch: *schakelklank*). It is inserted for phonological and orthographic reasons.
    *   **Historical Reason:** In Middle Dutch, the noun was often *hope*. The adjective *hopeloos* retains this older stem form (*hope-*) before the suffix, whereas the modern noun has shortened to *hoop*.
    *   **Phonotactic Reason:** It breaks up the consonant sequence to facilitate pronunciation, though /pl/ is a valid onset in Dutch.
    *   **Orthographic Reason:** Its presence changes the syllable structure (see below).

#### C. The Suffix: `-loos`
*   **Category:** Derivational Suffix.
*   **Meaning:** "Without," "lacking," or "devoid of."
*   **Function:** It is a **privative suffix**. It changes the part of speech from a **Noun** (*hoop*) to an **Adjective** (*hopeloos*). It negates the quality of the root.
*   **Etymology:** From Middle Dutch *loos* (free from, loose), related to Old High German *lōs*. It is the direct cognate of the English suffix **-less** (both derive from Proto-Germanic *\*lausaz*).
*   **Examples:** *werkloos* (jobless), *zinloos* (pointless), *krachteloos* (powerless).

### 3. Orthography and Phonology (The "Spelling Puzzle")
A key linguistic feature of *hopeloos* is the spelling of the vowel **o**.

*   **The Noun:** *De **hoop***.
    *   Here, the syllable is closed by the 'p'. In Dutch orthography, a long vowel in a **closed syllable** must be written with a double letter to indicate length. Hence: **oo**.
*   **The Adjective:** ***Hopeloos***.
    *   When the suffix is added, the syllabification changes. The word is divided as: **ho - pe - loos**.
    *   The 'p' becomes the onset of the second syllable (*pe*).
    *   This leaves the first syllable (*ho*) as an **open syllable**.
    *   In Dutch orthography, a long vowel in an **open syllable** is written with a **single letter**. Hence: **o**.

This is why the spelling shifts from *hoop* to *hopeloos*, even though the pronunciation of the vowel remains long (/oː/).

### 4. Semantic Composition
The meaning of the word is **compositional**, meaning it is derived directly from the sum of its parts:

1.  **hoop** (Hope)
2.  **-loos** (Without)
3.  **Result:** "Without hope" $\rightarrow$ **Hopeless**.
