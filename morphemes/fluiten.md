From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`fluiten`** can be analyzed through **morphology** (the study of word structure).

Depending on the context, `fluiten` functions as either the **infinitive form of a verb** or the **plural form of a noun**. In both cases, the morphological segmentation is identical, though the grammatical function of the suffix differs slightly.

Here is the breakdown:

### 1. Segmentation
The word consists of two morphemes:
> **`fluit`** + **`-en`**

---

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Root (Lexical Morpheme)
*   **Form:** `fluit`
*   **Type:** Free morpheme (can stand alone as a word).
*   **Meaning:** This is the **stem** or root. It carries the core lexical meaning.
    *   As a noun, it means "flute" (the musical instrument).
    *   As a verb stem, it relates to the action of "whistling" or "playing a flute."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *flote* or *fluit*, related to the sound produced by blowing air.

#### B. The Suffix (Grammatical Morpheme)
*   **Form:** `-en`
*   **Type:** Bound morpheme (cannot stand alone).
*   **Function:** This is an **inflectional suffix**. It does not change the part of speech or the core meaning, but rather modifies the grammatical category of the word.
    *   **Context 1 (Verb):** When attached to the verb stem, `-en` marks the **Infinitive** form (the dictionary form).
        *   *Example:* "Ik wil **fluiten**" (I want **to whistle**).
    *   **Context 2 (Noun):** When attached to the noun stem, `-en` marks the **Plural** number.
        *   *Example:* "De **fluiten** van de orkest" (The **flutes** of the orchestra).

---

### 3. Linguistic Classification

| Feature | Classification | Explanation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Morphology Type** | **Inflectional** | The suffix adds grammatical information (tense/number) without creating a new lexical word. |
| **Word Structure** | **Agglutinative** (in this instance) | The morphemes are strung together linearly (Stem + Suffix) without significant phonological fusion. |
| **Verb Class** | **Weak Verb** | If treated as a verb, `fluiten` is a regular (weak) verb. Past tense is formed by adding `-te` (*fluitte*), not by changing the vowel. |

### 4. Summary of Usage

To fully understand the morphology, one must look at how the word behaves in a sentence:

1.  **As a Verb (Infinitive):**
    *   *Stem:* `fluit`
    *   *Infinitive Marker:* `-en`
    *   *Meaning:* To whistle.
    *   *Conjugation:* The `-en` is dropped for the present tense singular (*ik fluit*, *hij fluit*).

2.  **As a Noun (Plural):**
    *   *Singular:* `fluit` (The flute)
    *   *Plural Suffix:* `-en`
    *   *Meaning:* Flutes.
    *   *Note:* In Dutch, many nouns ending in a vowel or specific consonants take `-en` for the plural (e.g., *boom* → *bomen*, *fluit* → *fluiten*).

### Conclusion
Linguistically, **`fluiten`** is a **bimorphemic** word composed of the lexical root **`fluit`** and the inflectional suffix **`-en`**. The specific meaning depends on whether the word is functioning syntactically as a verb (infinitive) or a noun (plural).
