From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch verb **`onthouden`** is a complex word formed through derivation and inflection. Below is a detailed morphological breakdown, followed by an explanation of the phonological and semantic properties.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **`ont-`** + **`houd`** + **`-en`**

### 2. Morpheme-by-Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Prefix: `ont-`
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix (Inseparable).
*   **Function:** This prefix modifies the meaning of the base verb. In Dutch morphology, `ont-` is classified as an **inseparable prefix** (onafscheidelijk voorvoegsel).
*   **Semantic Contribution:** The prefix `ont-` is polysemous (has multiple related meanings). Historically, it is related to the German *ent-* and English *un-* or *out-*. In the context of `onthouden`, it generally conveys one of two nuances depending on usage:
    1.  **Separation/Withholding:** To hold something *away* from someone (e.g., *geld onthouden* = to withhold money).
    2.  **Retention/State Change:** To hold something *in* a specific state (e.g., *iets onthouden* = to remember/retain something in the mind).
*   **Morphological Marker:** Because it is an inseparable prefix, verbs starting with `ont-` **never** take the `ge-` prefix in the past participle (e.g., *onthouden*, not *geonthouden*).

#### B. The Root: `houd`
*   **Type:** Lexical Root (Verb Stem).
*   **Base Verb:** *Houden* (to hold).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *holdan*, from Proto-Germanic *\*haldaną*. It is cognate with the English verb **hold**.
*   **Function:** This carries the core lexical meaning of the word (grasping, keeping, maintaining).
*   **Phonology:** In the infinitive form, the stem is followed by the vowel *u* (spelled *ou*), but morphologically, the root is considered *houd-*.

#### C. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix.
*   **Function:** This is the standard marker for the **infinitive** form of Dutch verbs.
*   **Grammar:** It indicates that the verb is in its base, unconjugated form (e.g., *ik wil onthouden* vs. *ik onthoud*).

### 3. Phonological and Orthographic Features

From a phonological and orthographic perspective, `onthouden` exhibits specific traits that confirm its morphological structure:

*   **Stress Pattern:** The stress falls on the **stem**, not the prefix.
    *   Pronunciation: /ɔnˈɦʌu̯.də(n)/
    *   Stress: on-**THOU**-den.
    *   *Linguistic Rule:* In Dutch, if the stress is on the stem, the prefix is inseparable. (Contrast with *aanhouden*, where stress is on *aan*, making it separable).
*   **Past Participle:** As noted above, the past participle is **`onthouden`**.
    *   It does not take the `ge-` circumfix (unlike regular verbs like *maken* $\rightarrow$ *gemaakt*).
    *   This is a definitive diagnostic feature of inseparable prefixes (`be-`, `ge-`, `her-`, `ver-`, `ont-`, `mis-`).

### 4. Semantic Analysis (Polysemy)

The word `onthouden` is a classic example of **polysemy**, where one morphological form carries two distinct but related meanings based on context.

1.  **To Remember / Retain:**
    *   *Context:* Cognitive process.
    *   *Logic:* You "hold" the information in your mind. The `ont-` here implies a state of keeping or retaining (similar to *ontvangen* - to receive/keep).
    *   *Example:* "Ik kan je naam niet **onthouden**." (I cannot remember your name.)

2.  **To Withhold / Deny:**
    *   *Context:* Physical or abstract possession.
    *   *Logic:* You "hold" something back from someone else. The `ont-` here implies separation or removal from the recipient.
    *   *Example:* "Ze **onthouden** hem het eten." (They are withholding food from him.)
