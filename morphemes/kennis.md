From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`kennis`** (meaning "knowledge" or "acquaintance") is a **deverbal noun**. This means it is derived from a verb.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation of the word.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into two morphemes:

> **`ken`** + **`nis`**

*   **`ken`**: The root (or stem).
*   **`-nis`**: The derivational suffix.

### 2. Detailed Explanation of Morphemes

#### A. The Root: `ken-`
*   **Source:** This root comes from the Dutch verb **`kennen`** (to know, to be acquainted with).
*   **Meaning:** The core semantic meaning is "to know" or "to recognize."
*   **Phonology:** The vowel in this root is short (/ɛ/). In Dutch orthography, a short vowel in a closed syllable must be followed by a double consonant.
*   **Cognates:** This root is cognate with the English verb **`ken`** (archaic or dialectal for "to know") and the German verb **`kennen`**.

#### B. The Suffix: `-nis`
*   **Type:** Derivational suffix (specifically, a nominalizer).
*   **Function:** It converts a verb (or sometimes an adjective) into an abstract noun. It indicates a state, condition, or result of the action.
*   **Meaning:** In this context, it turns the action of "knowing" into the abstract concept of "knowledge."
*   **Productivity:** While historically very common, the suffix `-nis` is **semi-productive** in modern Dutch. It is still found in words like *getuigenis* (testimony), *dichtenis* (poetry), and *verbinding* (connection - though this uses *-ing* often, *-nis* exists in older forms). However, it is less common than the suffix `-ing` for creating new nouns today.
*   **Cognates:** This corresponds to the German suffix **`-nis`** (e.g., *Kenntnis*, *Erkenntnis*) and the English suffix **`-ness`** (e.g., *darkness*, *kindness*), which share the same Proto-Germanic origin.

### 3. Orthographic Note: The Double 'n'
You might wonder why the word is spelled **`kennis`** with a double 'n' if the root is `ken-`.

*   **Syllabification:** When the suffix `-nis` is added to the root `ken-`, the syllable boundary falls between the two 'n's: **`ken-nis`**.
*   **Vowel Length Rule:** In Dutch spelling rules, a short vowel (like the /ɛ/ in *ken*) must be followed by a double consonant if the syllable is closed. Since the first syllable is *ken* (closed by the first *n* of the suffix), the spelling requires **`nn`** to indicate that the vowel remains short. If it were spelled *kenis*, a Dutch reader might pronounce the 'e' as a long vowel (/eː/).

### 4. Etymology and History
*   **Proto-Germanic:** The word traces back to the Proto-Germanic noun **`\*kunniz`**.
*   **Old Dutch:** It appears in Old Dutch as **`kennis`**.
*   **Semantic Shift:** Originally, the root implied the *ability* to know. Over time, the noun `kennis` shifted to refer to the *content* of what is known (information, facts).
*   **English Comparison:**
    *   Dutch: *kennen* (verb) + *-nis* (suffix) = *kennis* (noun).
    *   English: *ken* (verb) + *-ledge* (suffix, from Old French *lache* via Latin *legere*) = *knowledge* (noun).
    *   Note: English lost the direct cognate noun *ken* (which survives only as a verb), whereas Dutch retained the noun form *kennis*.

### Conclusion
Linguistically, **`kennis`** is a **bimorphemic** word consisting of the verbal root **`ken-`** and the abstract nominalizing suffix **`-nis`**. The spelling reflects Dutch orthographic rules regarding short vowels, and the word shares a deep Germanic heritage with English "ken" and German "Kenntnis."
