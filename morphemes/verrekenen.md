From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`verrekenen`** is a complex verb formed through derivation and inflection. Below is a detailed morphological breakdown and explanation.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **`ver-`** + **`reken-`** + **`-en`**

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Prefix: `ver-`
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix (Inseparable).
*   **Function:** In Dutch, the prefix *ver-* is highly productive. It generally indicates a change of state, a transformation, completion, or a movement "away" or "through."
*   **In this context:** When attached to *rekenen*, *ver-* shifts the meaning from the general act of calculating to the specific act of **settling** or **offsetting**. It implies bringing the calculation to a conclusion or balancing one thing against another.
*   **Grammatical Property:** This is an **inseparable** prefix.
    *   **Stress:** The stress falls on the root, not the prefix (*ver**RE**kenen*).
    *   **Past Participle:** It does not take the standard *ge-* prefix. The past participle is *verrekend* (not *geverrekend*).

#### B. The Root (Stem): `reken-`
*   **Type:** Lexical Root (Base).
*   **Meaning:** To count, calculate, compute, or reckon.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *rekenen*, which stems from the Proto-Germanic *\*raknōną*.
*   **Cognates:**
    *   English: *Reckon* (e.g., "I reckon that...")
    *   German: *Rechnen* (to calculate)
    *   Frisian: *Rekken*
*   **Semantic Role:** This provides the core semantic content of the word (the domain of mathematics or accounting).

#### C. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix.
*   **Function:** This is the standard marker for the **infinitive** form of a Dutch verb.
*   **Grammar:** It indicates that the verb is in its base form, not conjugated for person or number (e.g., *ik reken*, *jij rekent*, *wij rekenen*).

### 3. Semantic Composition (How the meaning is built)
The meaning of the whole word is not simply the sum of its parts, but a specific semantic shift caused by the prefix.

*   **`rekenen`**: To perform mathematical operations (e.g., *Ik reken de som uit* — I calculate the sum).
*   **`verrekenen`**: To calculate **against** something else to reach a balance.
    *   *Example:* "De kosten worden verrekend met de inkomsten." (The costs are **offset/settled** against the income.)
    *   *Nuance:* It implies a transactional or accounting context where debts, credits, or costs are balanced out rather than just computed.

### 4. Syntactic Behavior
Because `ver-` is an inseparable prefix in this specific word, the verb behaves differently than separable verbs (like *opbellen* or *aankomen*).

| Form | Construction | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Infinitive** | `ver-` + `rekenen` | Ik wil het **verrekenen**. |
| **Present** | `ver-` + `reken` + `t` | Hij **verrekent** de factuur. |
| **Past Participle** | `ver-` + `reken` + `d` | Ik heb de factuur **verrekend**. |
| **Imperative** | `ver-` + `reken` | **Verrek** dit bedrag! |

*Note: You will never see a `ge-` inserted between the prefix and the root (e.g., *geverrekend* is incorrect).*

### 5. Summary Table

| Morpheme | Type | Meaning/Function | Stress |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **ver-** | Prefix | Transformation / Completion / Offset | Unstressed |
| **reken-** | Root | To count / calculate | **Stressed** |
| **-en** | Suffix | Infinitive marker | Unstressed |

**Linguistic Takeaway:**
`Verrekenen` is a classic example of **prefixation** in Germanic languages where an inseparable prefix modifies the aspect of the verb from a process (*calculating*) to a result-oriented action (*settling accounts*).
