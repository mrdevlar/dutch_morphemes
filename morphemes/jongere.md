From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`jongere`** is a complex word formed through a combination of derivation and inflection. It consists of three distinct morphemes.

Here is the breakdown:

### 1. Morphemic Segmentation
**`jong`** + **`-er`** + **`-e`**

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### **A. Root: `jong`**
*   **Type:** Free morpheme (Lexical root).
*   **Part of Speech:** Adjective.
*   **Meaning:** "Young."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *jong*, which stems from Proto-Germanic **\*jungaz* (cognate with English *young* and German *jung*).
*   **Function:** This carries the core semantic meaning of the word.

#### **B. Derivational Suffix: `-er`**
*   **Type:** Bound morpheme (Derivational suffix).
*   **Function:** Comparative marker.
*   **Meaning:** "More" or "-er" (indicating a higher degree of the quality).
*   **Process:** This suffix attaches to the root to create the comparative form of the adjective.
    *   *jong* (young) $\rightarrow$ *jonger* (younger).
*   **Note:** Unlike German (*jung* $\rightarrow$ *jünger*), Dutch does not typically apply an umlaut (vowel change) to the root vowel when adding this suffix.

#### **C. Inflectional Suffix: `-e`**
*   **Type:** Bound morpheme (Inflectional suffix).
*   **Function:** Adjective inflection (Weak declension).
*   **Meaning:** Grammatical agreement marker.
*   **Context:** In Dutch, adjectives generally take an *-e* ending in specific syntactic environments:
    1.  **Attributive use with a definite article:** *De **jongere** man* (The younger man).
    2.  **Attributive use with a plural noun:** *De **jongere** mensen* (The younger people).
    3.  **Substantivization:** When the adjective is used as a noun. *De **jongere*** (The younger one).
*   **Phonology:** This final *-e* is pronounced as a schwa (/ə/).

---

### 3. Syntactic and Semantic Context

To understand why the word ends in `-e`, we must look at Dutch adjective inflection rules.

*   **Base Comparative:** *jonger* (e.g., *Hij is **jonger** dan zij* / He is younger than she is). Here, the adjective is predicative and takes no ending.
*   **Inflected Comparative:** *jongere* (e.g., *Hij is de **jongere** broer* / He is the younger brother). Here, the adjective precedes a noun and agrees with the definite article (*de*), requiring the inflectional *-e*.

**Substantivized Usage:**
The word *jongere* is also frequently used as a noun (a substantivized adjective).
*   *De **jongere** moet eerst leren.* (The younger one must learn first.)
In this case, the morphological structure remains the same (`jong` + `-er` + `-e`), but the syntactic category shifts from Adjective to Noun.

---

### 4. Important Distinction: `jongere` vs. `jongeren`

It is common for learners to confuse *jongere* with the plural noun **`jongeren`** (young people).

*   **`jongere`**: Comparative adjective or singular noun ("the younger one").
    *   Morphology: `jong` + `-er` (comparative) + `-e` (inflection).
*   **`jongeren`**: Plural noun ("young people").
    *   Morphology: `jong` + `-er` (agentive suffix, denoting a person) + `-en` (plural marker).
    *   *Note:* In *jongeren*, the *-er* does not mean "more young," but rather "one who is young."
