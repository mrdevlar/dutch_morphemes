From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`langskomen`** is a **compound verb** (specifically a verb-particle construction). It is formed by combining a prepositional/adverbial element with a verbal root.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation of the word.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
At the surface level, the word consists of two primary morphemes:
1.  **`langs`** (Particle/Preposition)
2.  **`komen`** (Verb)

However, if we analyze the internal structure of these components further, we can identify four distinct morphemes:

| Morpheme | Type | Function | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`lang`** | Free Root | Adjective | Long |
| **`-s`** | Bound Derivational | Adverbial Suffix | Along / Past (Locative) |
| **`kom`** | Free Root | Verb Stem | To come |
| **`-en`** | Bound Inflectional | Infinitive Suffix | (Marks the verb as infinitive) |

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Components

#### A. The Particle: `langs`
*   **Structure:** `lang` + `-s`
*   **`lang` (Root):** Originally an adjective meaning "long" (cognate with English *long* and German *lang*).
*   **`-s` (Suffix):** In Old and Middle Dutch, the genitive or dative case ending `-s` was often used to form adverbs of place or direction.
    *   *Example:* `huis` (house) $\rightarrow$ `huis-s` (at home).
    *   *Result:* `langs` literally evolved to mean "along" or "past."
*   **Current Status:** In modern Dutch, `langs` is lexicalized as a single preposition/adverb meaning "along," "by," or "past."

#### B. The Verb: `komen`
*   **Structure:** `kom` + `-en`
*   **`kom` (Stem):** The core semantic root indicating movement toward the speaker or a specific location. (Cognate with English *come* and German *kommen*).
*   **`-en` (Suffix):** The standard infinitive marker for Dutch verbs.

---

### 3. Syntactic Morphology: The Separable Verb
From a syntactic and morphological perspective, `langskomen` is classified as a **scheidbaar werkwoord** (separable verb). This is a crucial feature of Dutch morphology.

While the dictionary form is written as one word (`langskomen`), in a main clause, the morphemes separate. The particle `langs` moves to the end of the clause, while the verb stem `kom` is conjugated and placed in the second position.

*   **Infinitive:** `Ik wil **langskomen**.` (I want to drop by.)
*   **Present Tense:** `Ik **kom** morgen **langs**.` (I am dropping by tomorrow.)
*   **Past Tense:** `Ik **kwam** gisteren **langs**.` (I dropped by yesterday.)

**Linguistic Note:** This separation proves that `langs` and `komen` retain their individual morphological identities even when compounded. They are not fused into a single, unbreakable lexical unit like the English word "understand."

---

### 4. Semantic Composition (Meaning)
The meaning of the compound is not simply the sum of its parts, but rather a specific idiomatic extension:

1.  **Literal:** `komen` (to come) + `langs` (along/past) = To come along/past a location.
2.  **Idiomatic:** To visit someone briefly without a formal appointment.
    *   *Nuance:* It implies a casual, short visit ("dropping by").
    *   *Contrast:* `Bezoeken` (to visit) is more formal; `langskomen` is casual.

### 5. Etymological Context
*   **`Lang`:** Proto-Germanic **`*langaz`**.
*   **`-s`:** Proto-Germanic genitive/locative marker.
*   **`Komen`:** Proto-Germanic **`*kumaną`**.

### Summary
Linguistically, **`langskomen`** is a **separable compound verb**.
*   **Morphologically:** It combines the adverbial particle `langs` (derived from *long* + *adverbial suffix*) with the verb `komen`.
*   **Syntactically:** It behaves as a single unit in the infinitive but splits into two distinct morphemes in conjugated sentences.
*   **Semantically:** It denotes a casual, brief visit, derived from the physical action of coming past a location.
