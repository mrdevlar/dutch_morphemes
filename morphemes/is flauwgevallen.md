From a strict linguistic perspective, it is important to first clarify that **`is flauwgevallen`** is not a single word, but rather a **verb phrase** consisting of two distinct words functioning together as the predicate of a sentence.

It represents the **Present Perfect tense** (*Voltooid Tegenwoordige Tijd*) of the separable verb *flauwvallen* ("to faint").

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation of the components.

### 1. The Auxiliary Verb: `is`

| Morpheme | Form | Lexeme (Dictionary Form) | Grammatical Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`is`** | `is` | *zijn* (to be) | **Auxiliary Verb**<br>3rd person singular, present tense, indicative. |

*   **Linguistic Note:** In Dutch, the perfect tense of verbs indicating a change of state or motion (like *vallen* - to fall) uses the auxiliary *zijn* (to be) rather than *hebben* (to have).
*   **Morphology:** `is` is a **suppletive form**. It does not look like the root *zijn*; it is an irregular form specific to the 3rd person singular present.

### 2. The Past Participle: `flauwgevallen`

This is the core of your query. While written as one word in this position, it is morphologically complex. It is the past participle of the **separable compound verb** *flauwvallen*.

In Dutch morphology, when a separable verb is put into the perfect tense, the prefix moves to the end of the clause, and the participle marker **`ge-`** is inserted *between* the prefix and the verb stem.

Here is the breakdown of `flauwgevallen`:

| Morpheme | Segment | Lexeme/Root | Grammatical Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`flauw`** | Prefix | *flauw* (adverb/adjective) | **Separable Prefix**<br>Indicates the manner of falling (faintly/weakly). |
| **`ge`** | Infix | *ge-* | **Participle Marker**<br>Standard marker for the past participle in Dutch. |
| **`val`** | Stem | *val-* (from *vallen*) | **Verb Stem**<br>The root of the strong verb *vallen* (to fall). |
| **`en`** | Suffix | *-en* | **Inflectional Suffix**<br>Standard ending for the past participle of strong verbs. |

#### Detailed Morphological Process:
1.  **Base Verb:** *flauwvallen* (to faint).
    *   This is a **separable verb** (*scheidbaar werkwoord*). In the present tense, it splits: *Hij valt flauw* (He faints).
2.  **Participle Formation:**
    *   Take the stem of the main verb: *val-* (from *vallen*).
    *   Add the participle suffix: *val-en*.
    *   Add the participle prefix: *ge-val-en* (*gevallen*).
    *   **Crucial Rule:** Because the verb has a separable prefix (*flauw-*), the *ge-* cannot go at the very front. It must be inserted **between** the prefix and the stem.
    *   Result: *flauw* + *ge* + *val* + *en* = **`flauwgevallen`**.

### 3. Semantic Analysis (Meaning)

*   **`flauw`**: Literally means "faint," "weak," or "silly." In this compound, it modifies the action of falling to mean losing consciousness.
*   **`vallen`**: To fall.
*   **`is ... gevallen`**: Indicates the action is completed (perfect aspect) and relates to the subject's current state (has fainted).

### Summary
The phrase **`is flauwgevallen`** translates to **"has fainted"** (or "fainted" in narrative past).

*   **`is`**: Auxiliary (to be).
*   **`flauw`**: Separable prefix.
*   **`ge`**: Participle infix.
*   **`val`**: Verb stem.
*   **`en`**: Participle suffix.

This structure is a classic example of Dutch **morphophonology**, where the position of the morpheme `ge-` changes depending on whether the verb is separable or inseparable.
