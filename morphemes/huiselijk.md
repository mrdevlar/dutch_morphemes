From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`huiselijk`** is a derived adjective formed through a process of **derivational morphology**. It consists of two distinct morphemes: a lexical root and a derivational suffix.

Here is the breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented as follows:

> **huis** + **-elijk**

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### **Morpheme 1: `huis`**
*   **Type:** Free morpheme / Root / Lexeme.
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun.
*   **Meaning:** "House," "home," or "dwelling."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *hūs*, from Proto-Germanic **\*hūsą*. It is cognate with the English word **house** and the German word **Haus**.
*   **Function:** This provides the core semantic content (the concept of the physical structure or the concept of home).

#### **Morpheme 2: `-elijk`**
*   **Type:** Bound morpheme / Derivational Suffix.
*   **Part of Speech:** Adjectivalizer (turns a noun into an adjective).
*   **Meaning:** "Having the quality of," "related to," or "characteristic of."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *-elike*, from Old Dutch *-alīk*, from Proto-Germanic **\*-alīkaz*.
*   **Cognates:** This is the Dutch equivalent of the English suffix **-ly** (as in *friendly*) or **-al** (as in *domestic*), and the German suffix **-lich** (as in *häuslich*).
*   **Phonological Note:** In Dutch morphology, this suffix often appears as *-elijk* when attached to noun stems (like *huis*, *vriend*, *zak*), though it can appear as *-lijk* in other contexts (e.g., *mannelijk* from *man*). In this specific word, the *e-* is part of the suffixal form.

### 3. Morphological Process
*   **Process:** **Derivation**.
*   **Category Change:** The process changes the word class from a **Noun** (*huis*) to an **Adjective** (*huiselijk*).
*   **Productivity:** This is a highly productive pattern in Dutch. You can apply *-elijk* to many nouns to create adjectives (e.g., *zak* → *zakelijk* [business-related], *vriend* → *vriendelijk* [friendly]).

### 4. Semantic Composition
The meaning of the whole word is compositional, meaning it is derived from the sum of its parts, though it has developed specific nuances:

*   **Literal:** "House-related."
*   **Contextual Meaning 1 (Domestic):** Often used to describe things happening within the home or family unit.
    *   *Example:* **`Huiselijk geweld`** (Domestic violence).
*   **Contextual Meaning 2 (Homey/Cozy):** Describes an atmosphere that feels like a home.
    *   *Example:* **`Een huiselijke sfeer`** (A homey/cozy atmosphere).

### 5. Linguistic Comparison
To understand the morpheme better, it helps to compare it across Germanic languages:

| Language | Root (Noun) | Suffix | Resulting Adjective | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |

### Summary
In the word **`huiselijk`**, the root **`huis`** provides the semantic base ("house"), and the suffix **`-elijk`** functions as a grammatical operator that converts that noun into an adjective meaning "pertaining to the house." This creates a standard Dutch adjective used to describe domestic matters or a cozy atmosphere.
