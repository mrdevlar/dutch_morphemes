From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`zwemmen`** (meaning "to swim") can be analyzed through **morphology**, the study of the internal structure of words.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes, followed by an explanation of the orthographic (spelling) rules that affect its appearance.

### 1. Morphemic Segmentation
The word consists of two distinct morphemes:

> **`zwem`** + **`-en`**

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Root (Lexical Morpheme): `zwem-`
*   **Type:** Free/Bound Root (In isolation, this stem cannot function as a complete finite verb in Dutch without inflection, though it carries the core meaning).
*   **Function:** This is the **lexical morpheme**. It carries the primary semantic content of the word: the action of moving through water.
*   **Phonology:** The vowel in this stem is short (/ɛ/).
*   **Etymology:** This root is of Germanic origin. It is cognate with:
    *   Old High German: *swemman*
    *   Old English: *swimman* (Modern English: *swim*)
    *   German: *schwimmen*
    *   Proto-Germanic: **\*swimmaną*

#### B. The Suffix (Inflectional Morpheme): `-en`
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix.
*   **Function:** This is the **infinitive marker**. In Dutch grammar, adding *-en* to a verb stem creates the dictionary form (the "to" form in English).
*   **Grammar:** It indicates that the verb is in its base, non-conjugated state.
*   **Comparison:**
    *   *Ik zwem* (I swim) — Stem used with 1st person singular.
    *   *Jij zwemt* (You swim) — Stem + *-t*.
    *   *Om te zwemmen* (To swim) — Stem + *-en*.

### 3. Orthographic Note: The Double Consonant (`mm`)
While the morphological breakdown is `zwem` + `en`, you may notice the spelling is **`zwemmen`** (with a double *m*).

*   **Linguistic Phenomenon:** Consonant Gemination (Doubling).
*   **The Rule:** In Dutch orthography, if a verb stem ends in a **short vowel** followed by a single consonant, and you add a suffix that begins with a vowel (like *-en*), the final consonant of the stem is doubled.
*   **The Reason:** This preserves the pronunciation of the short vowel.
    *   If spelled *zwemen*, the first syllable would be open (*zwe-*), which typically signals a **long** vowel sound in Dutch.
    *   By spelling it *zwemmen*, the syllable remains closed (*zwem-*), ensuring the vowel remains **short**.
*   **Morphological Status:** The second *m* is **not** a separate morpheme. It is a graphemic device used to signal the phonological structure of the root morpheme `zwem-`.

### 4. Morphological Class (Strong Verb)
From a historical linguistic perspective, `zwemmen` is classified as a **strong verb** (*sterk werkwoord*). This means it forms its past tense through a vowel change (ablaut) rather than just adding a *-te* or *-de* suffix (which would make it a weak verb).

*   **Infinitive:** `zwemmen` (Stem: `zwem-`)
*   **Past Tense (Singular):** `zwom` (Vowel change: *e* → *o*)
*   **Past Tense (Plural):** `zwommen` (Vowel change + suffix)
*   **Past Participle:** `gezwommen` (Prefix *ge-* + stem + suffix *-en*)
