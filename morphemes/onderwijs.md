From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`onderwijs`** (meaning "education" or "teaching") is a **compound noun**. It is formed by combining two distinct morphemes.

Here is the breakdown and explanation of its morphology.

### 1. Segmentation
The word can be segmented as follows:

> **`onder`** + **`wijs`**

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### Morpheme 1: `onder`
*   **Type:** Preposition / Prefix
*   **Literal Meaning:** "Under," "below," or "among."
*   **Function in Compound:** In this specific context, `onder` functions as a **prepositional prefix**. While it retains its spatial meaning etymologically, in the compound *onderwijs*, it has undergone semantic bleaching and specialization.
*   **Semantic Role:** Historically, it implies the act of guiding or instructing someone from a position of authority or support (literally "leading from below" or "showing the way"). It modifies the second element to specify the *type* of manner or instruction.

#### Morpheme 2: `wijs`
*   **Type:** Noun (derived from a verb)
*   **Literal Meaning:** "Way," "manner," or "mode."
*   **Etymology:** This morpheme is cognate with the English word **"wise"** (in the archaic sense of "in a ... manner," as in *otherwise* or *likewise*) and the German noun **"Weise"** (way/manner).
*   **Root Connection:** It is derived from the verb **`wijzen`** (to point, to show, to teach).
    *   *Verb:* `wijzen` (to point/show)
    *   *Noun:* `wijs` (a way/manner of pointing/showing)
*   **Function in Compound:** This is the **head** of the compound (following the Right-Hand Head Rule in Dutch morphology). The word *onderwijs* is a type of *wijs* (a specific manner or way).

### 3. Morphological Process: Compounding
*   **Process:** **Noun-Noun Compounding** (or Preposition-Noun Compounding).
*   **Structure:** `[Modifier] + [Head]`
    *   Modifier: `onder`
    *   Head: `wijs`
*   **Explanation:** Dutch frequently creates new nouns by joining two existing words. Here, the preposition `onder` is fused with the noun `wijs`. There is no linking vowel (like *-s-* or *-en-*) between them, which is common in Dutch compounds (compare *schoolbus* vs. *schoolbus* vs. *onderwijs*).

### 4. Derivational Family
To fully understand the morphology, it helps to look at the related verb form:

*   **`onderwijzen`** (verb: to teach/instruct)
    *   Segmentation: `onder` + `wijzen`
    *   Here, `wijzen` is the infinitive verb.
*   **`onderwijs`** (noun: education)
    *   Segmentation: `onder` + `wijs`
    *   Here, the infinitive ending *-en* is dropped, and the stem is nominalized.

*Note: While `onderwijs` looks like it could be `onder` + `wijz` + `-s` (where -s is a nominalizer), synchronically (in modern Dutch), `wijs` is treated as a standalone morpheme because it exists as a word in other contexts (e.g., `op zijn eigen wijs` = "in his own way").*

### 5. Semantic Shift
The literal translation of the morphemes ("under-way") does not immediately yield the modern meaning ("education"). This is due to **semantic shift**:
1.  **Original:** "A way of being under" or "guidance."
2.  **Middle Dutch:** *onderwîs* referred to instruction or teaching.
3.  **Modern Dutch:** *onderwijs* specifically refers to the system or act of formal education.

### 6. Cognates and Comparisons
*   **English:** There is no direct single-word cognate, but the components exist: *under* + *wise* (archaic noun sense).
*   **German:** *Unterricht* (different root) or *Weise* (way/manner).
*   **Frisian:** *ûnderwiis* (very similar structure).
