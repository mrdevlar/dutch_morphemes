From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`volledig`** (meaning "complete," "full," or "entire") is a fascinating example of morphological derivation and semantic shift.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes, followed by a detailed linguistic explanation.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation

To analyze the word, we can break it down into its constituent morphemes (the smallest units of meaning). There are two ways to view this: the **synchronic** (modern) view and the **diachronic** (historical/etymological) view.

**The Granular Breakdown:**
> **`vol`** + **`led`** + **`ig`**

**The Compound Breakdown (Historical):**
> **`vol`** + **`ledig`**

---

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. Root: `vol`
*   **Type:** Free Morpheme (Root)
*   **Meaning:** "Full."
*   **Function:** This is the semantic core of the word. It carries the primary meaning of containing something to capacity.
*   **Related Words:** *vol* (adjective), *vullen* (verb: to fill), *volheid* (noun: fullness).

#### B. The Middle Element: `led` (or `ledig`)
*   **Type:** Bound Morpheme (in modern Dutch) / Free Morpheme (historically)
*   **Meaning:** Historically, this comes from the word **`ledig`**, which means "empty," "free," or "vacant."
*   **Function:** This is the most complex part of the word. In modern Dutch, `led` does not exist as an independent word. It is a fossilized stem.
*   **Etymology:** The word *ledig* comes from Middle Dutch *ledich*, related to the verb *laten* (to let/leave). Originally, it meant "left alone" or "loose."

#### C. Suffix: `ig`
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix
*   **Meaning:** "Having the quality of" or "characterized by."
*   **Function:** This turns the preceding stem into an adjective. It is cognate with the English suffix *-y* (e.g., *cloudy*, *happy*) or *-ish*.
*   **Note:** This suffix is already present inside the historical word *ledig*.

---

### 3. The Semantic Paradox: "Full" + "Empty"

The most interesting linguistic feature of *volledig* is the apparent contradiction in its etymology.

*   **`vol`** = Full
*   **`ledig`** = Empty

If you combine them literally, you get "Full-Empty." How did this become "Complete"?

**The Logic of the Compound:**
In Middle Dutch, the compound *volledich* was understood as **"filled up to the point where there is no *ledig* (empty space) left."**
It describes a state of **resultative completion**. The emptiness has been eliminated by the fullness. Over time, the negative meaning of *ledig* (empty) was bleached (lost) in this specific compound, and the word came to mean simply "thoroughly full" or "complete."

### 4. Synchronic vs. Diachronic Analysis

Linguists distinguish between how a word is analyzed today versus how it was formed.

*   **Diachronic (Historical):**
    *   Structure: **Compound** (*vol* + *ledig*).
    *   Logic: "Full [so that it is not] empty."
    *   This explains the meaning best.

*   **Synchronic (Modern):**
    *   Structure: **Derived Adjective** (*vol* + *-ig* with an infix/extension *-led-*).
    *   Logic: For a modern Dutch speaker, *volledig* is often treated as a single lexical unit. They do not actively think of *ledig* (empty) when they say *volledig*.
    *   The segment *-led-* is now an **unanalyzable stem extension**. You cannot create new words using this pattern (e.g., you cannot say *koudledig* for "coldly").

### 5. Grammatical Function

*   **Part of Speech:** Adjective (can function as an Adverb).
*   **Inflection:**
    *   *Predicative:* "De fles is **volledig**." (The bottle is full.)
    *   *Attributive:* "Een **volledige** fles." (A full bottle.) *Note the addition of the inflectional suffix `-e`.*
    *   *Adverbial:* "Ik ben **volledig** moe." (I am completely tired.)

### Conclusion
From a linguistic perspective, **`volledig`** is a **lexicalized compound**. While it is morphologically composed of the root *vol* and the suffix *-ig*, the intervening *-led-* element preserves the history of the word *ledig* (empty). The word serves as a classic example of how language resolves semantic contradictions through usage, transforming "full-empty" into "complete."
