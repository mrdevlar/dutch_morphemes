From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`uiteenzettingen`** is a complex noun derived from a separable verb. It can be segmented into five distinct morphemes.

Here is the breakdown:

### 1. Segmentation
**`uit`** + **`een`** + **`zet`** + **`ing`** + **`en`**

### 3. Detailed Explanation

#### **A. The Prefixes: `uit` + `een`**
*   **`uit`**: This is a directional prefix meaning "out." It indicates movement away from a center or a state of being enclosed.
*   **`een`**: Historically, this is the number "one." In the compound adverb **`uiteen`** (apart), it implies separation into distinct units ("out" + "one").
*   **Combined Function**: Together, **`uiteen-`** acts as a separable prefix complex. In the verb *`uiteenzetten`*, it modifies the action of "setting" to mean "setting out" or "spreading out." In the noun form, it retains this sense of "laying out" or "expounding."
    *   *Linguistic Note:* While `uiteen` is often treated as a single lexical unit in modern dictionaries, etymologically and morphologically, it is a compound of two morphemes.

#### **B. The Root: `zet`**
*   **Source**: This is the stem of the verb **`zetten`** (to put, to set, to place).
*   **Form**: The infinitive is *`zetten`* (with a geminate `tt`), but the stem used for derivation is **`zet`**.
*   **Meaning**: It provides the core action of the word.

#### **C. The Derivational Suffix: `-ing`**
*   **Function**: This is a nominalizing suffix. It converts the verbal action (*uiteenzetten* - to explain) into a concrete noun (*uiteenzetting* - an explanation/exposition).
*   **Process**: `zet` + `ing` = `zetting`.
*   **Phonology**: The `t` from the stem `zet` remains before the suffix. There is no epenthetic (inserted) `t` here because the stem already ends in `t`.

#### **D. The Inflectional Suffix: `-en`**
*   **Function**: This is the standard plural marker for many Dutch nouns (specifically those ending in `-ing`).
*   **Process**: `zetting` + `en` = `zettingen`.
*   **Grammar**: This indicates that there is more than one exposition or explanation.

---

### 4. Semantic Composition (How the meaning builds)

To understand the full meaning, we can reconstruct the logic from the inside out:

1.  **`zet`**: To put/place.
2.  **`uit` + `een`**: Out + Apart.
3.  **`uiteenzetten`**: To put things apart/out (to lay out an argument).
4.  **`uiteenzetting`**: The act or result of laying out an argument (an exposition/explanation).
5.  **`uiteenzettingen`**: Multiple expositions/explanations.

**Literal Translation:** "Out-one-put-ings"
**Actual Meaning:** "Expositions" or "Explanations."

### 5. Summary of Morphological Structure
The word follows a standard Dutch derivational pattern for verbs with separable prefixes:

1.  **Verb:** `uit` + `een` + `zet` + `en` (Infinitive)
2.  **Nominalization:** `uit` + `een` + `zet` + `ing` (Singular Noun)
3.  **Pluralization:** `uit` + `een` + `zet` + `ing` + `en` (Plural Noun)

**Stress Pattern:** In Dutch, nouns derived from separable verbs typically carry the primary stress on the first syllable of the prefix.
