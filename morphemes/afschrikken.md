From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`afschrikken`** is a complex verb formed through derivational morphology. It consists of three distinct morphemes that combine to create a specific meaning related to deterrence.

Here is the breakdown and explanation of the morphemes:

### 1. Morphological Breakdown

| Morpheme | Type | Function | Meaning/Contribution |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`af-`** | **Prefix** (Derivational) | Separable | "Off", "Away", "Down" |
| **`schrik`** | **Root** (Lexical) | Semantic Core | "Fright", "Startle", "Shock" |
| **`-en`** | **Suffix** (Inflectional) | Infinitive Marker | Marks the word as a verb in its base form |

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis

#### **A. The Prefix: `af-`**
*   **Classification:** This is a **separable prefix** (*scheidbaar voorvoegsel*). In Dutch grammar, verbs with this prefix behave differently in main clauses compared to subordinate clauses or infinitives.
*   **Etymology:** Cognate with the English preposition *off* and the German prefix *ab-*. It originates from Proto-Germanic *\*af*.
*   **Semantic Function:** In this context, `af-` adds a **directional** or **resultative** meaning. It implies movement away from a state or an action. When attached to a verb of fear or startling, it shifts the meaning from simply "being scared" to "scaring **away**" or "scaring **off**."
*   **Syntactic Behavior:**
    *   *Infinitive:* "Ik wil je **afschrikken**." (I want to deter you.)
    *   *Conjugated:* "Ik **schrik** je **af**." (I deter you.)
    *   *Note:* The prefix separates from the stem in the present tense main clause.

#### **B. The Root: `schrik`**
*   **Classification:** This is the **lexical root** (or stem).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *scric*, related to the noun *schrik* (fright). It is cognate with the German *Schreck* and the English *shriek* (though English *shriek* shifted to sound, while Dutch/German kept the sense of sudden fear).
*   **Semantic Function:** This carries the core semantic load of the word. It denotes a sudden, intense feeling of fear or the act of startling someone.
*   **Valency Change:** The root verb *schrikken* can be intransitive (*Ik schrik* = I am startled) or transitive (*Hij schrikt mij* = He startles me). However, when combined with `af-`, the resulting verb *afschrikken* is almost exclusively **transitive** and causative: it implies causing someone to be scared *away* from a specific action.

#### **C. The Suffix: `-en`**
*   **Classification:** This is an **inflectional suffix**.
*   **Function:** In Dutch, `-en` is the standard marker for the **infinitive** form of a verb (the dictionary form). It does not change the core meaning of the word but indicates its grammatical category (verb) and non-finite status.
*   **Phonology:** In rapid speech, the final `-n` is often dropped (apocope), sounding like *afschrikke*.

---

### 3. Semantic Composition (How they combine)

The meaning of `afschrikken` is not merely the sum of its parts, but a specific idiomatic combination:

1.  **Literal Construction:** "To frighten off."
2.  **Actual Meaning:** "To deter," "to discourage," or "to dissuade."
3.  **Nuance:** It implies using a threat, a negative consequence, or a frightening example to prevent someone from doing something.
    *   *Example:* "De hoge straffen moeten **afschrikken**." (The high penalties are meant to **deter**.)

### 4. Related Linguistic Forms

*   **Adjective:** `afschrikkend` (deterrent / frightening). Here, `-end` is a present participle suffix turning the verb into an adjective.
*   **Noun:** `de afschrikking` (deterrence). Here, `-ing` is a nominalization suffix.
*   **German Cognate:** `abschrecken`. The structure is identical (`ab-` + `schrecken` + `-en`), highlighting the shared Germanic morphology.
*   **English Equivalent:** There is no single-word cognate. The closest semantic equivalents are phrasal verbs like **"scare off"** or **"deter."**

### Summary
Linguistically, **`afschrikken`** is a **separable compound verb**. It utilizes the derivational prefix **`af-`** to modify the root **`schrik`** (fright), creating a causative meaning of "scaring away," and is marked as an infinitive by the suffix **`-en`**.
