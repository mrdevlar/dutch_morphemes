From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`zenuwachtig`** is a complex adjective formed through derivation. It can be segmented into its constituent morphemes to reveal its internal structure and meaning.

Here is the breakdown and explanation of the morphemes.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation

Visually, the word can be segmented in two ways depending on the level of analysis (synchronic vs. diachronic):

*   **Broad Segmentation (Modern Dutch):** `zenuw` + `achtig`
*   **Fine Segmentation (Historical/Deep Morphology):** `zenuw` + `acht` + `ig`

For a complete linguistic explanation, we will analyze the fine segmentation, as it reveals the etymological logic of the suffix.

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

| Morpheme | Type | Part of Speech | Meaning / Function | Etymology |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`zenuw`** | Free Morpheme (Root) | Noun | **Nerve** | From Middle Dutch *senewe*, Proto-Germanic *\*sinawō*. Cognate with English *sinew* (though English *sinew* refers to tendon, while Dutch *zenuw* refers to nerve). |
| **`acht`** | Bound Stem | Noun/Verb Stem | **Regard / Consideration** | From Middle Dutch *achte*, Old Dutch *ahta*. Related to the verb *achten* (to regard/consider). In this context, it implies "having the nature of." |
| **`-ig`** | Derivational Suffix | Adjective | **-y / -ish** | A common Germanic adjectival suffix indicating possession of a quality (cognate with English *-y* as in *sunny* or *-ish* as in *childish*). |

### 3. Detailed Explanation

#### The Root: `zenuw`
This is the semantic core of the word. It is a **free morpheme**, meaning it can stand alone as a word (*de zenuw* = the nerve). In this compound, it provides the specific domain of the adjective: the nervous system or nerves.

#### The Suffix Complex: `-achtig`
In modern Dutch morphology, **`-achtig`** is often treated as a single, complex derivational suffix. However, linguistically, it is composed of two parts:

1.  **`acht`**: Historically, this comes from the noun *acht* (regard/attention) or the verb *achten* (to value/consider). When combined with a noun, it creates a meaning similar to "worthy of" or "having the characteristics of."
2.  **`-ig`**: This is the standard adjectivalizer. It turns the preceding stem into an adjective.

**Combined Meaning:** When `zenuw` is combined with `-achtig`, the literal meaning is "nerve-like" or "having the characteristics of nerves."

### 4. Semantic Shift and Usage

*   **Literal Meaning:** Resembling a nerve.
*   **Idiomatic Meaning:** **Nervous, anxious, jittery, or high-strung.**
    *   *Example:* "Hij is erg zenuwachtig." (He is very nervous.)

This is a classic example of **metonymy** in morphology. The physical organ (*zenuw*) is used to describe the psychological state associated with the overstimulation of that organ.

### 5. Comparison with English

It is helpful to compare this to English to understand the morphological difference:

*   **Dutch:** `zenuw` (Germanic root) + `-achtig` (Germanic suffix) = **Zenuwachtig**
*   **English:** `nerve` (Latin root *nervus*) + `-ous` (Latin suffix *-osus*) = **Nervous**

While English uses a Latinate construction for this concept, Dutch uses a purely Germanic construction. A closer English equivalent in terms of morphology would be **"sinewy"** (from *sinew* + *-y*), though *sinewy* in English means "strong/tough" rather than "anxious."

### 6. Syntactic Properties

*   **Word Class:** Adjective (*bijvoeglijk naamwoord*).
*   **Inflection:** Like most Dutch adjectives, it can be inflected for definiteness and plurality (e.g., *een zenuwachtige man* vs. *de zenuwachtige mannen*).
*   **Derivational Productivity:** The suffix `-achtig` is highly productive in Dutch. It can be attached to almost any noun to create an adjective meaning "X-like."
    *   *Kind* (child) + *achtig* = *kinderachtig* (childish)
    *   *Vrouw* (woman) + *achtig* = *vrouwachtig* (womanly)
    *   *Dier* (animal) + *achtig* = *dierachtig* (animal-like)

### Summary
The word **`zenuwachtig`** is a **derived adjective**. It consists of the noun root **`zenuw`** ("nerve") and the complex adjectival suffix **`-achtig`** ("-like"). Together, they form a word describing a state of being characterized by nerves, i.e., anxiety or nervousness.
