From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`mezelf`** is a compound reflexive pronoun. It consists of two distinct morphemes that combine to create a specific grammatical function.

Here is the breakdown and explanation of the morphemes:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented as follows:
> **me** + **zelf**

### 2. Analysis of Morpheme 1: `me`
*   **Form:** *me*
*   **Category:** Personal Pronoun (Weak/Clitic form)
*   **Person/Number:** 1st Person Singular
*   **Case:** Objective (Accusative/Dative)
*   **Linguistic Function:**
    *   **Cliticization:** In Dutch, personal pronouns often have a "strong" (stressed) form and a "weak" (unstressed) form. The strong form is **`mij`**, while the weak form is **`me`**.
    *   **Usage:** In the compound *mezelf*, the weak form *me* is standard because the stress usually falls on the second element (*zelf*). If the speaker wants to emphasize the pronoun specifically (e.g., "I did it to *myself*, not someone else"), they would use the strong form: *mijzelf*.
    *   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *mī*, cognate with English *me* and German *mich*.

### 3. Analysis of Morpheme 2: `zelf`
*   **Form:** *zelf*
*   **Category:** Reflexive Marker / Intensifier (Historically a noun/adverb)
*   **Meaning:** "Self"
*   **Linguistic Function:**
    *   **Reflexivity:** This morpheme indicates that the action of the verb reflects back onto the subject. It turns the object pronoun into a reflexive pronoun.
    *   **Independence:** Unlike English, where *-self* is a bound suffix (e.g., *my-self*), in Dutch, *zelf* is technically a **free morpheme**. It can stand alone as an adverb or noun (e.g., *Ik ben zelf* — "I am myself"). However, in this context, it functions as part of a pronominal compound.
    *   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *selb*, cognate with English *self* and German *selbst*.

### 4. Syntactic and Morphological Context
*   **Compound Type:** This is a **pronominal compound**. Dutch creates reflexive pronouns by combining the object form of the personal pronoun with *zelf*.
    *   *Ik* (I) + *me* (me) + *zelf* (self) = *Ik zie mezelf* (I see myself).
    *   *Jij* (You) + *je* (you) + *zelf* (self) = *Je ziet jezelf* (You see yourself).
    *   *Hij* (He) + *zich* (him) + *zelf* (self) = *Hij ziet zichzelf* (He sees himself).
*   **Stress Pattern:** The compound follows a specific stress rule. The primary stress falls on **`zelf`**.
    *   *me-ZELF* (Standard)
    *   *MIJ-zelf* (Emphatic)

### Conclusion
Linguistically, **`mezelf`** is not a single root word but a **compound** formed by the **weak clitic form of the first-person object pronoun** (*me*) and the **reflexive intensifier** (*zelf*). It serves to mark the grammatical relationship where the subject and the object of the verb are identical.
