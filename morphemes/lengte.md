From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`lengte`** (meaning "length") is a classic example of **derivational morphology**. It is formed by taking an adjective and adding a suffix to create a noun (nominalization).

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes:

### 1. Segmentation
The word can be segmented into two distinct morphemes:
> **`leng-`** + **`-te`**

---

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### **Morpheme 1: `leng-` (The Stem/Root)**
*   **Type:** Bound Root / Allomorph.
*   **Source:** This stem is derived from the Dutch adjective **`lang`** (meaning "long").
*   **Phonological/Orthographic Change:** You will notice the vowel has changed from **`a`** (*lang*) to **`e`** (*leng*).
    *   In linguistics, this is a remnant of **i-mutation (umlaut)**. In Proto-Germanic, there was an *i* or *j* sound in the suffix that caused the preceding vowel to shift forward in the mouth (from *a* to *e*). Over time, the *i/j* sound disappeared, but the vowel change remained.
    *   This is why the stem is not *lang-* but *leng-*.
*   **Semantic Role:** It carries the core lexical meaning of the word ("longness" or the concept of being long).

#### **Morpheme 2: `-te` (The Suffix)**
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix.
*   **Function:** **Nominalization**. It converts the adjective (*lang*) into an abstract noun (*lengte*).
*   **Category:** It creates a feminine noun (in traditional grammar) or a common gender noun (in modern Dutch).
*   **Productivity:** While this suffix exists in other words (e.g., *hoogte* from *hoog*, *breedte* from *breed*), it is not fully productive in modern Dutch. You cannot simply add *-te* to any adjective to make a noun (e.g., *korte* is not the noun for "shortness"; that would be *korting* or *kortigheid*). It is mostly found in a specific set of lexicalized words.

---

### 3. Comparative Linguistics (Germanic Cognates)
To fully understand the morphology of `lengte`, it is helpful to look at its Germanic cousins. All three languages underwent the same historical vowel mutation process:

| Language | Adjective (Root) | Noun (Derived) | Vowel Change |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Dutch** | *lang* | **`lengte`** | *a* $\rightarrow$ *e* |
| **German** | *lang* | *Länge* | *a* $\rightarrow$ *ä* |
| **English** | *long* | *length* | *o* $\rightarrow$ *e* |

*   **Note on English:** English uses the suffix **-th** (from Proto-Germanic *-þō*), while Dutch and German use **-te** (from Proto-Germanic *-þi-*, which evolved differently in the continental languages). However, the vowel mutation (*a/o* to *e*) is the shared linguistic feature.

### 4. Summary
*   **Word:** `lengte`
*   **Structure:** `[Root: leng-]` + `[Suffix: -te]`
*   **Process:** Derivation (Adjective $\rightarrow$ Noun).
*   **Key Feature:** Historical **i-mutation** caused the root vowel to shift from *a* to *e*.

In short, `lengte` is the nominalized form of "long," marked by a historical vowel shift and a Germanic abstract noun suffix.
