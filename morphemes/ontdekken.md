From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch verb **`ontdekken`** (meaning "to discover") is a complex word formed through derivation. It consists of three distinct morphemes.

Here is the breakdown and explanation of each component:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented as follows:
> **ont-** + **dek** + **-en**

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Prefix: `ont-`
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix (Inseparable).
*   **Function:** This prefix modifies the meaning of the root verb. In Dutch, `ont-` (cognate with German `ent-` and English `un-`) generally indicates **removal**, **reversal**, or **initiation**.
*   **Phonology:** It is an *inseparable* prefix. This means the primary stress of the word falls on the root, not the prefix (pronounced *ont-DÉK-ken*). If it were separable, the stress would be on the prefix (e.g., *`op`-nemen*).
*   **Semantic Contribution:** In this specific context, it adds the meaning of "undoing" or "removing" the state of the root.

#### B. The Root: `dek`
*   **Type:** Lexical Root (Free Morpheme).
*   **Meaning:** "Cover," "lid," or "deck."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Proto-Germanic **\*thaką* (roof, cover). It is cognate with the English word **`deck`** and **`thatch`**.
*   **Semantic Contribution:** This provides the core semantic content of the action: the act of covering something.

#### C. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix.
*   **Function:** This is the standard marker for the **infinitive** form of Dutch verbs.
*   **Grammar:** It indicates that the word is a verb in its base form (equivalent to English "to" + verb).
*   **Note:** In the past participle (`ontdekt`), this suffix is replaced by `-t`.

### 3. Semantic Composition (Literal vs. Figurative)
When you combine these morphemes, the meaning evolves logically:

1.  **`dek`** = to cover.
2.  **`ont-`** = to remove/un-.
3.  **Literal Meaning:** "To un-cover."
4.  **Figurative Meaning:** "To discover."

This is a classic example of semantic shift. Just as you physically "uncover" an object to see it, you "uncover" information or a place to bring it to light.

### 4. Comparative Linguistics
The structure of `ontdekken` is remarkably similar to its cognates in other Germanic languages, highlighting the shared morphological history:

*   **German:** `entdecken` (Prefix `ent-` + Root `decken` + Suffix `-en`).
*   **English:** `discover` (Prefix `dis-` [Latin for apart/away] + Root `cover` [Latin *cooperire*]).
    *   *Note:* While English uses a Latin root, the morphological logic (Prefix + Cover) is identical to the Dutch/Germanic construction.

### 5. Grammatical Classification
*   **Word Class:** Verb (Transitive).
*   **Verb Type:** Weak verb (Regular).
    *   *Present:* ik ontdek (I discover)
    *   *Past:* ik ontdekte (I discovered)
    *   *Participle:* ik heb ontdekt (I have discovered)
*   **Prefix Behavior:** Because `ont-` is inseparable, the past participle does **not** take the `ge-` prefix.
    *   *Correct:* `ontdekt`
    *   *Incorrect:* `ge-ont-dekt`
