From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`voornemen`** is a compound word formed through derivational morphology. It functions primarily as a verb (to intend/plan) but can also be nominalized (the intention).

Here is the breakdown of its morphemes, their etymology, and their semantic contribution.

### 1. Morphological Breakdown

The word consists of two distinct morphemes:

1.  **`voor-`** (Prefix)
2.  **`nemen`** (Root/Base Verb)

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Prefix: `voor-`
*   **Type:** Separable Prefix (*scheidbaar voorvoegsel*).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *fora*, related to the Proto-Germanic **\*fur*. It is cognate with the English prefix **fore-** and the German **vor-**.
*   **Literal Meaning:** "Before," "in front of," or "forward."
*   **Semantic Function:** In spatial contexts, it indicates position (e.g., *voorkant* = front side). In temporal or mental contexts (as in this word), it indicates precedence in time or priority in thought.
*   **Grammatical Behavior:** In Dutch verb conjugation, this prefix is **separable**.
    *   *Infinitive:* **voornemen**
    *   *Present Tense:* Ik **neem** het **voor** (I intend it).
    *   *Past Participle:* **voor**genomen (intended).

#### B. The Root: `nemen`
*   **Type:** Free Morpheme (Base Verb).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *neman*, related to Proto-Germanic **\*nemana*. It is cognate with the English verb **to nim** (archaic/dialectal, as in *nimble*) and the German **nehmen**.
*   **Literal Meaning:** "To take."
*   **Grammatical Class:** It is a **strong verb** (irregular).
    *   *Infinitive:* nemen
    *   *Past Singular:* nam
    *   *Past Participle:* genomen
*   **Semantic Function:** It provides the core action of the word.

---

### 3. Semantic Synthesis (How the meaning is created)

When combined, the morphemes undergo a semantic shift from the physical to the mental/abstract.

*   **Literal Composition:** "To take before."
*   **Figurative Meaning:** To "take" an idea or plan into your mind "before" you act on it.
*   **Resulting Definition:** To intend, to plan, to propose, or to resolve.

This is a classic example of **metaphorical extension** in Germanic languages. Just as English uses "fore-" to indicate time (e.g., *foresee*, *forethought*), Dutch uses *voor-* to indicate mental preparation.

### 4. Grammatical and Syntactic Context

To fully understand the morphology, one must look at how the word behaves in a sentence:

**1. As a Verb (Transitive/Reflexive):**
*   *Ik **neem** voor om te gaan.* (I intend to go.)
*   *Zij **nam** het **voor**.* (She intended it.)
*   *Note:* Because it is a separable verb, the prefix `voor-` detaches and moves to the end of the clause in main sentences.

**2. As a Noun (Nominalization):**
*   Dutch frequently nominalizes infinitives.
*   *Het **voornemen**.* (The intention / The plan).
*   Here, the capitalization changes (in writing), and it takes a definite article (*het*), but the internal morphological structure remains identical.

**3. Reflexive Usage:**
*   It is very common to use this verb reflexively: *zich **voornemen***.
*   *Ik **benoem** mij **voor**...* (Incorrect) -> *Ik **neem** mij **voor**...* (I resolve/intend for myself).
*   This adds a reflexive pronoun (*mij/je/zich*) which acts as a separate syntactic element, not a morpheme within the word itself.

### Summary
The word **`voornemen`** is a **separable compound verb**.
*   **`voor-`** adds the temporal/mental dimension of "beforehand."
*   **`nemen`** provides the action of "taking."
*   Together, they create the concept of **"taking something into your mind beforehand,"** which equates to **intention** or **planning**.
