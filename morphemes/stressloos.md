### 1. Morphological Segmentation

> **`stress`** + **`-loos`**

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### **Morpheme 1: `stress`**
*   **Type:** Free Morpheme (Root/Base)
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun (in its base form)
*   **Meaning:** "Stress," "strain," or "tension."
*   **Etymology:** This is a **loanword** (Lehnwort) borrowed from English. The English word *stress* itself derives from the Old French *estresse* (meaning pressure or distress), which comes from the Latin *strictus* (drawn tight).
*   **Linguistic Status:** Although it is of foreign origin, it is fully integrated into the Dutch lexicon. It functions as a native noun root that can accept Dutch derivational affixes.

#### **Morpheme 2: `-loos`**
*   **Type:** Bound Morpheme (Derivational Suffix)
*   **Part of Speech:** Adjectivalizer (turns a noun into an adjective)
*   **Meaning:** "Without," "lacking," or "free from."
*   **Etymology:** This is a native Dutch suffix derived from Middle Dutch *loos*, meaning "loose" or "free." It is a **cognate** of the English suffix **-less** (e.g., *hopeless*, *careless*) and the German suffix **-los** (e.g., *hoffnungslos*).
*   **Function:** It is a **negative derivational suffix**. It negates the semantic content of the root word and changes the grammatical category from a noun to an adjective.

### 3. Morphological Process
The formation of this word follows a specific derivational rule in Dutch morphology:

1.  **Base:** `stress` (Noun)
2.  **Operation:** Add the suffix `-loos`.
3.  **Result:** `stressloos` (Adjective)
4.  **Semantic Shift:** The meaning shifts from the concept of "stress" to the state of "being without stress."

### 4. Linguistic Notes & Nuances

*   **Productivity:** The suffix `-loos` is highly **productive** in Dutch. This means speakers can freely attach it to many nouns to create new adjectives (e.g., *werkloos* [unemployed], *zinloos* [pointless], *krachteloos* [powerless]).
