From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`gelijk`** is a fascinating example of a lexicalized compound that has undergone significant semantic shift over time.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word consists of two morphemes:

> **`ge-`** + **`lijk`**

---

### 2. Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Prefix: `ge-`
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix (historically).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Proto-Germanic **`*ga-`**.
*   **Function:** In Proto-Germanic and Old Dutch, this prefix indicated association, collectivity, or completion.
*   **Modern Status:** In the word *gelijk*, this prefix is **fossilized**.
    *   In modern Dutch, `ge-` is most commonly recognized as the marker for past participles (e.g., *gemaakt* from *maken*) or collective nouns (e.g., *geboomte* from *boom*).
    *   However, in *gelijk*, it does not indicate a past tense. It is part of the lexical root of the adjective. It historically served to intensify or bind the concept of "likeness" into a state of being.
    *   **Cognates:** Compare with German *gleich* (where the prefix has largely disappeared) or English *alike* (where the prefix is *a-*).

#### B. The Root: `lijk`
*   **Type:** Lexical Root (Noun origin).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Proto-Germanic **`*līkō`**, meaning "body," "form," or "shape."
*   **Meaning:** The core semantic value is "body" or "form."
*   **Cognates:**
    *   **English:** *Like* (similar), *Likeness*, and the adverbial suffix *-ly* (originally meaning "having the body/form of").
    *   **German:** *Leiche* (corpse/body), *gleich* (equal).
    *   **Old English:** *līc* (body).
*   **Semantic Shift:** The meaning evolved from "having the same **body/form**" $\rightarrow$ "having the same **shape**" $\rightarrow$ "being **equal/similar**."

---

### 3. Important Linguistic Distinction: Root vs. Suffix
A common point of confusion in Dutch morphology is distinguishing the root `lijk` in *gelijk* from the suffix `-lijk` found in other words.

*   **In `gelijk`:** `lijk` is the **root**. The word is not "ge- + like-ness." It is a fossilized compound meaning "same-body."
*   **In `vriendelijk` (friendly):** Here, `-lijk` is a **derivational suffix**. It turns the noun *vriend* (friend) into an adjective. It corresponds to the English suffix *-ly*.
    *   *Structure:* `vriend` (root) + `-lijk` (suffix).
*   **In `gelijk`:** You cannot remove `ge-` to find a standalone word *lijk* that means "equal" in modern Dutch (though *lijk* exists as a noun meaning "corpse," which is a semantic cousin).

### 4. Semantic Polysemy (Multiple Meanings)
Because the word is so fundamental, it has developed three distinct meanings in modern usage, all stemming from the concept of "equality":

1.  **Equality (Mathematical/Physical):**
    *   *Example:* "5 is **gelijk** aan 5." (5 is equal to 5.)
    *   *Origin:* Direct retention of the "same form" meaning.
2.  **Correctness (Judgment):**
    *   *Example:* "Je hebt **gelijk**." (You are right.)
    *   *Origin:* Metaphorical extension. If your opinion is "equal" to the truth, you are right.
3.  **Immediacy (Temporal):**
    *   *Example:* "Ik kom **gelijk**." (I'm coming right away.)
    *   *Origin:* Pragmatic extension. "Right now" is treated as "equal to this moment."

### 5. Derivatives
The morphological productivity of this word is seen in its derivatives, which treat the whole word `gelijk` as a new root:

*   **`gelijkheid`** (Equality): `gelijk` + `-heid` (abstract noun suffix).
*   **`gelijken`** (To compare): `gelijk` + `-en` (verb suffix).
*   **`ongelijk`** (Unequal/Disagreement): `on-` (negation prefix) + `gelijk`.

**Conclusion:** Linguistically, *gelijk* is a fossilized compound where the prefix `ge-` and the root `lijk` (body) have merged to create a concept of "sameness" that has expanded to cover correctness and time.
