From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch verb **`verzamelen`** (to collect, to gather) can be broken down into four distinct morphemes.

Here is the morphological segmentation and explanation:

### 1. The Segmentation
**`ver`** + **`zam`** + **`el`** + **`en`**

---

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. `ver-` (Prefix)
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix
*   **Function:** In Dutch, the prefix *ver-* is highly productive and versatile. It often indicates a change of state, a transition, completion, or intensification.
*   **In this context:** When attached to a verb stem, *ver-* often implies that the action is being done thoroughly, systematically, or resulting in a specific state. Here, it transforms the basic act of "joining" into the systematic act of "collecting" or "assembling."
*   **Comparison:** Similar to English *for-* (as in *forbid* or *forget*, though etymologically distinct) or German *ver-* (as in *versammeln*).

#### B. `zam` (Root)
*   **Type:** Bound Root (Lexical Root)
*   **Origin:** This root comes from the Proto-Germanic **`*sam-`**, meaning "together."
*   **Phonological Note:** You may recognize this root in the Dutch word **`samen`** (together). In *verzamelen*, the initial **`s`** has voiced to a **`z`**. In Dutch phonology, the voiceless *s* often becomes the voiced *z* when it occurs between vowels or after a voiced consonant (like the *r* in *ver-*).
*   **Cognates:**
    *   English: *same*, *sum*, *some* (all share the "together/one" concept).
    *   German: *zusammen* (together).

#### C. `-el` (Derivational Suffix)
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix
*   **Function:** This is a verb-forming suffix. It turns the root concept into a verb stem. In Dutch, *-el-* is often found in verbs that denote a repeated action or a process (frequentative).
*   **In this context:** It turns the concept of "together" (*sam*) into the action of "gathering" (*zamelen*).
*   **Note:** In modern synchronic analysis (how the word is used today), speakers often perceive **`zamel`** as a single stem unit. However, etymologically and morphologically, the *-el-* is a distinct suffix attached to the root.

#### D. `-en` (Inflectional Suffix)
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix
*   **Function:** This is the standard marker for the **infinitive** form of a Dutch verb.
*   **Grammar:** It indicates that the verb is in its base form (e.g., "to collect"). It does not change the meaning of the word but marks its grammatical category.
*   **Conjugation:** If you conjugate this verb, the *-en* is dropped (e.g., *ik verzamel* - I collect), but the *-el-* remains part of the stem.

---

### 3. Synthesis: How it works together

1.  **Root (`sam`):** Establishes the core meaning: "together."
2.  **Derivation (`-el`):** Creates a verb meaning "to bring together" (*zamelen*).
3.  **Prefix (`ver-`):** Modifies the verb to imply a completed or systematic collection (*verzamelen*).
4.  **Inflection (`-en`):** Marks the word as an infinitive verb.

**Literal Translation:** "To [make into a state of] [bringing together] [verb]."
**Actual Meaning:** "To collect" or "To gather."

### 4. Related Linguistic Comparisons

*   **German:** *versammeln* (to assemble/gather). The structure is almost identical (*ver-* + *sammel* + *-n*).
*   **English:** There is no direct cognate for the whole word, but the root survives in the word **`sum`** (a gathering of numbers) or **`some`** (a part of a whole). The semantic equivalent is **`gather`** (from Old English *gaderian*, related to *together*).
*   **Dutch Noun:** *De verzameling* (The collection). Here, the verb stem *verzamel-* takes the nominal suffix *-ing*.
