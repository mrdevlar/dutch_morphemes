From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`oplossen`** is a complex verb formed through derivation and inflection. It is a classic example of a **separable compound verb** (*scheidbaar werkwoord*).

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation of the morphemes.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **`op`** + **`los`** + **`en`**

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. `op` (Prefix / Verbal Particle)
*   **Type:** Separable Prefix (Part of a *scheidbaar werkwoord*).
*   **Original Category:** Preposition / Adverb.
*   **Literal Meaning:** "Up," "on," or "upon."
*   **Function:** In this context, `op` acts as a **verbal particle**. It modifies the meaning of the root verb. It adds a sense of direction, completion, or a change of state.
    *   *Semantic contribution:* It implies bringing something to a state of being "loose" or "open," or causing something to go "up" into a solution.
*   **Linguistic Note:** Because it is a separable prefix, it behaves differently depending on the sentence structure. In the infinitive (`oplossen`), it is attached. In a main clause, it detaches and moves to the end of the sentence (e.g., *Ik los het probleem **op***).

#### B. `los` (Lexical Root / Stem)
*   **Type:** Free Morpheme (Root).
*   **Original Category:** Adjective.
*   **Meaning:** "Loose," "free," "detached," or "unbound."
*   **Function:** This is the core semantic unit of the word. It carries the primary lexical meaning.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *los*, cognate with German *los* and English *lose* (in the sense of "to be free from").
*   **Relationship:** The root `los` exists independently as an adjective (e.g., *een losse knoop* — a loose knot). In `oplossen`, it is nominalized/verbalized to mean "to make loose."

#### C. `-en` (Inflectional Suffix)
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix.
*   **Function:** **Infinitive Marker**.
*   **Grammar:** This suffix marks the verb as the dictionary form (the infinitive). It indicates that the verb is not conjugated for person, number, or tense in this specific form.
*   **Phonology:** In modern Dutch, this is often pronounced as a schwa sound /ə/ or a nasalized vowel, depending on the dialect.

### 3. Grammatical Behavior: The Separable Verb
From a syntactic and morphological perspective, `oplossen` is categorized as a **separable verb** (*scheidbaar werkwoord*). This is a crucial feature of Dutch morphology.

*   **In the Infinitive:** The morphemes are joined: *oplossen*.
*   **In the Present Tense (Main Clause):** The prefix `op` separates from the root `los` and moves to the end of the clause.
    *   *Example:* "Ik **los** het probleem **op**." (I solve the problem.)
    *   *Morphological split:* `Ik` + `los` (conjugated root) + `het probleem` + `op` (prefix).
*   **In the Past Participle:** The prefix `op` is reattached, and the ge- infix is inserted between the prefix and the root.
    *   *Example:* "Ik heb het probleem **opgelost**." (I have solved the problem.)
    *   *Structure:* `op` + `ge` + `los` + `t`.

### 4. Semantic Composition (How the meaning is built)
The meaning of `oplossen` is not simply the sum of its parts, but a result of **semantic composition**:

1.  **Literal/Physical Meaning:** To dissolve (a substance).
    *   *Logic:* To make a solid substance "loose" (`los`) into a liquid ("up" or "into" solution `op`).
2.  **Figurative Meaning:** To solve (a problem).
    *   *Logic:* To make a problem "loose" or "detached" (`los`) from its binding constraints ("up" or "open" `op`).
