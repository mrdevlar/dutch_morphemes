From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`regelen`** is a denominal verb (a verb derived from a noun). It can be segmented into two distinct morphemes.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
**`regel`** + **`-en`**

| Morpheme | Type | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **regel** | Root / Stem | Lexical meaning (Noun base) |
| **-en** | Suffix | Grammatical marker (Verbalizer / Infinitive) |

---

### 2. Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Root: `regel`
*   **Category:** Noun (originally), functioning here as the verbal stem.
*   **Meaning:** "Rule," "line," "row," or "strophe."
*   **Etymology:** This word is a borrowing from Latin via Old Dutch/Middle Dutch.
    *   Latin: *regula* (meaning "straight rod," "rule," or "pattern").
    *   Old Dutch: *reghel*.
    *   Cognate: English **"rule"** and German **"Regel"**.
*   **Semantic Shift:** In the transition from noun to verb, the meaning shifted from the *object* (a rule) to the *action* associated with that object.
    *   *Literal:* To make something follow a rule/line.
    *   *Figurative:* To organize, to arrange, to manage, or to fix.

#### B. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Category:** Inflectional/Derivational Suffix.
*   **Function:**
    1.  **Verbalizer:** It converts the noun stem (*regel*) into a verb. In Dutch morphology, adding *-en* to a noun stem is a common way to create a verb (e.g., *de fiets* $\rightarrow$ *fietsen*, *de regen* $\rightarrow$ *regenen*).
    2.  **Infinitive Marker:** In the context of the dictionary form, *-en* marks the verb as an infinitive (the base form used for conjugation).
*   **Phonology:** In standard Dutch pronunciation, this suffix is realized as a schwa sound /ə/ followed by a nasal /n/ (IPA: /ˈreː.ɣə.lə(n)/).

---

### 3. Morphological Process
The formation of this word is an example of **Derivation** (specifically **Denominalization**).

1.  **Base:** *regel* (Noun: "a rule").
2.  **Process:** Add verbal suffix *-en*.
3.  **Result:** *regelen* (Verb: "to regulate/arrange").

Unlike inflection (which changes tense or number without changing the part of speech), this process changes the part of speech from Noun to Verb.

### 4. Conjugation and Stem Stability
To understand the morphology fully, it helps to look at how the stem behaves in the verb paradigm. The stem **`regel-`** remains invariant (unchanging) across tenses, while other suffixes are added to it.

*   **Infinitive:** regel**en**
*   **Present (1st person):** ik regel
*   **Present (3rd person):** hij regel**t**
*   **Past (Weak):** ik regel**de**
*   **Past Participle:** ge**regel**d

This confirms that **`regel`** is the invariant lexical root, and **`-en`** is the specific marker for the infinitive form.

### 5. Semantic Nuances
Because the root implies "following a rule," the verb *regelen* covers a spectrum of meanings depending on context:
1.  **To regulate:** *De temperatuur regelen* (To regulate the temperature).
2.  **To arrange/organize:** *De reis regelen* (To arrange the trip).
3.  **To fix/settle:** *Dat probleem regelen* (To fix that problem).

### Summary
Linguistically, **`regelen`** is a **weak, denominal verb** consisting of the noun root **`regel`** (rule) and the infinitive/verbal suffix **`-en`**. It represents a semantic shift from a static concept (a rule) to a dynamic action (making things follow a rule).
