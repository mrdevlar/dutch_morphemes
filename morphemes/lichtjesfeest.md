From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`lichtjesfeest`** is a **compound noun** formed through derivation and inflection. It is a specific cultural term often associated with the Sinterklaas tradition.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation of the word.

### 1. Visual Segmentation
To understand the structure, we can segment the word into its constituent morphemes (the smallest units of meaning):

> **licht** + **je** + **s** + **feest**

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

| Morpheme | Type | Function | Meaning / Origin |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **licht** | Free Morpheme (Root) | Lexical Noun | "Light" (Proto-Germanic *\*leuhtą*). Refers to illumination, a lamp, or a lantern. |
| **-je** | Bound Morpheme (Suffix) | Derivational (Diminutive) | "Little/Small". Turns the noun into a diminutive. In Dutch, after a stem ending in a voiceless consonant (like *ch* /x/ or *t*), the suffix is *-je*. |
| **-s** | Bound Morpheme (Suffix) | Inflectional (Plural) | "Plural". Marks the diminutive noun as plural. |
| **feest** | Free Morpheme (Root) | Lexical Noun | "Party", "Festival", or "Celebration" (from Old French *feste*). |

### 3. Step-by-Step Construction

1.  **`licht` (Light):** The base noun.
2.  **`licht` + `-je` → `lichtje` (Little light):**
    *   The suffix **-je** is a **diminutive**. It changes the meaning from a general "light" to a "small light," "candle," or "lantern."
    *   *Phonological Note:* In Dutch, the spelling of the diminutive depends on the final sound of the stem. Since *licht* ends in a voiceless sound (/xt/), the suffix remains *-je* (not *-tje*).
3.  **`lichtje` + `-s` → `lichtjes` (Little lights):**
    *   The suffix **-s** is the standard **plural marker** for diminutives ending in *-je*.
    *   *Note:* While some Dutch compounds drop the plural marker of the first element (e.g., *autobus* rather than *auto'sbus*), in this specific cultural term, the plural **-s** is retained to emphasize that there are *many* lights involved.
4.  **`lichtjes` + `feest` → `lichtjesfeest` (Festival of little lights):**
    *   This creates a **compound noun**.
    *   **Head:** `feest` (The core meaning is a "feast/party").
    *   **Modifier:** `lichtjes` (Describes the type of feast).

### 4. Syntactic and Semantic Structure

*   **Compound Type:** Endocentric Compound. The meaning of the whole word is a specific type of the head noun (`feest`).
*   **Semantic Relation:** Possessive/Content. It is a feast *characterized by* or *consisting of* little lights.
*   **Stress Pattern:** In Dutch compounds, the primary stress usually falls on the first element.
    *   **LÍCHTjesfeest** (/ˈlɪxtjəsfeːst/).

### 5. Cultural Context
While the morphology is standard Dutch, the word is culturally specific.
*   **Sinterklaas Tradition:** The *lichtjesfeest* is traditionally associated with the arrival of Sinterklaas (St. Nicholas) in the Netherlands and Belgium (mid-November). Children often carry paper lanterns (`lichtjes`) through the streets to welcome him.
*   **Modern Usage:** The term is also used for modern city events where people walk through streets with lanterns or lights (similar to a "Lantern Festival"), often in the winter season.

### Summary
The word **`lichtjesfeest`** is a four-morpheme construction:
1.  **Root:** *licht* (light)
2.  **Derivational Suffix:** *-je* (diminutive)
3.  **Inflectional Suffix:** *-s* (plural)
4.  **Root:** *feest* (party)

It literally translates to **"little-lights-party."**
