From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`mannenbaan`** is a **compound noun** (*samenstelling*). It is formed by combining two distinct lexical items, one of which contains an inflectional suffix.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes, their functions, and the structural rules governing the word.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three atomic morphemes:

> **man** + **-en** + **baan**

Visually, it is often perceived as two parts (the modifier and the head), but linguistically, it consists of three units:

1.  **man** (Root)
2.  **-en** (Inflectional Suffix)
3.  **baan** (Root/Head)

---

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. `man` (Root / Lexeme)
*   **Type:** Free morpheme (can stand alone as a word).
*   **Category:** Noun.
*   **Meaning:** "Man" (adult male human).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Proto-Germanic *\*mannaz*.
*   **Phonology:** Contains a short vowel /ɑ/ (in standard Dutch).
*   **Orthography Note:** In the singular, it is spelled with one *n*. However, because the vowel is short, Dutch orthography requires the consonant to double when a suffix is added to maintain the short vowel sound (see *-en* below).

#### B. `-en` (Inflectional Suffix)
*   **Type:** Bound morpheme (cannot stand alone).
*   **Category:** Inflectional suffix (Plural marker).
*   **Function:** Marks the noun *man* as plural.
*   **Morphophonology:**
    *   The singular is *man*.
    *   The plural is *mannen*.
    *   **Gemination:** When adding *-en* to monosyllabic nouns ending in a single consonant with a short vowel (like *man*, *kat*, *pot*), the final consonant is doubled (*n* becomes *nn*). This preserves the short vowel quality. If it were spelled *manen*, the vowel might be interpreted as long (/aː/).
*   **Syntactic Role:** In this compound, the plural form *mannen* acts as the **modifier**.

#### C. `baan` (Root / Head)
*   **Type:** Free morpheme.
*   **Category:** Noun.
*   **Meaning:** "Job," "career," "track," or "lane."
*   **Etymology:** From Middle Dutch *baene*, related to the concept of a path or track.
*   **Syntactic Role:** This is the **head** of the compound. In Dutch (and Germanic languages generally), the rightmost element determines the grammatical category and the core meaning of the new word. Therefore, a *mannenbaan* is a type of *baan* (job), not a type of *man*.

---

### 3. Compound Structure (Syntax & Semantics)

**Structure:** `[ [ man + -en ] + baan ]`
**Type:** Endocentric Compound (The meaning is a specific type of the head noun).

*   **Modifier:** *mannen* (Men's / of men)
*   **Head:** *baan* (Job)
*   **Relationship:** Possessive or Attributive. It implies "a job for men" or "a job characterized by men."

**Linking Elements:**
In Dutch compounding, linking elements (like *-s-* or *-en-*) are sometimes inserted between the modifier and the head (e.g., *liefdes*verklaring). However, in *mannenbaan*, **no linking element** is used. This is standard when the first part of the compound is already a plural noun ending in *-en*.

---

### 4. Sociolinguistic & Pragmatic Context

While the morphology is straightforward, the semantics of *mannenbaan* carry specific cultural weight in the Netherlands and Flanders.

*   **Literal Meaning:** A job held by men.
*   **Connotative Meaning:** It is frequently used in sociological and political discourse to describe a career path that is **traditionally male-dominated**, often implying high status, high salary, and leadership roles from which women are historically excluded or face significant barriers (the "glass ceiling").
*   **Usage:** It is often used critically. For example: *"Dit is een typische mannenbaan"* ("This is a typical men's job") suggests the environment is not conducive to women.
