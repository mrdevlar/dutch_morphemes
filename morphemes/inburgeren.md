From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`inburgeren`** is a complex verb formed through derivation and compounding. It is a **separable verb** (*scheidbaar werkwoord*).

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation of its components:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word consists of three distinct morphemes:

> **`in`** + **`burger`** + **`-en`**

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. `in-` (Prefix / Particle)
*   **Category:** Preposition functioning as a separable verbal prefix.
*   **Meaning:** "Into," "in," or "inside."
*   **Function:** It indicates direction, state change, or inclusion. In this context, it transforms the action into "becoming part of" or "entering into."
*   **Linguistic Note:** In Dutch grammar, this is a **separable prefix**. While it appears attached in the infinitive (`inburgeren`), it separates in main clauses (e.g., *Hij **burger** **in*** — "He integrates"). The stress falls on the root, not the prefix (*inBURgeren*), which distinguishes it from inseparable prefixes like *be-* or *ver-*.

#### B. `burger` (Root / Stem)
*   **Category:** Noun (Free morpheme).
*   **Meaning:** "Citizen," "burgher," or "townsman."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *borchere*, which comes from Old High German *burgāri*. It is related to the word *burg* (fortress, town, or castle). Historically, a *burger* was someone who lived within the fortified walls of a town and held specific rights.
*   **Function:** This is the semantic core of the word. It provides the specific identity that the subject is acquiring.

#### C. `-en` (Suffix)
*   **Category:** Verbalizer / Infinitive Marker (Bound morpheme).
*   **Meaning:** N/A (Grammatical function only).
*   **Function:**
    1.  **Derivational:** It turns the noun *burger* into a verb (*burgeren* — "to act as a citizen").
    2.  **Inflectional:** It marks the word as the **infinitive** form of the verb.
*   **Linguistic Note:** This is a standard process in Dutch called **denominalization** (creating a verb from a noun). Similar examples include *school* (school) $\rightarrow$ *schoolen* (to train) or *koffie* (coffee) $\rightarrow$ *koffieën* (to have coffee).

---

### 3. Semantic Composition (How the meaning is built)
The meaning of the whole word is not merely the sum of its parts, but a specific sociopolitical concept:

1.  **`burgeren`**: To act like a citizen.
2.  **`in-`**: To move into that state.
3.  **`inburgeren`**: To undergo the process of becoming a citizen or to integrate culturally and legally into the society of a specific country.

### 4. Grammatical Behavior
Because `in-` is a separable prefix, the word behaves differently depending on its position in a sentence:

*   **Infinitive:** *Hij probeert te **inburgeren**.* (He tries to integrate.)
*   **Main Clause:** *Hij **burger** **in**.* (He integrates.)
*   **Past Participle:** *Hij is **in**ge**burgerd**.* (He has integrated.)
    *   *Note:* The prefix `in-` moves to the front of the participle, and the suffix `-d` is added (standard for weak verbs ending in a vowel sound).
