### 2. Detailed Explanation

#### A. Morphological Status: Free Root
*   **Free Morpheme:** The unit `vak` can stand alone as a complete word. It does not require any other morphemes to be grammatically valid (unlike the English suffix *-s* in *cats*, which cannot stand alone).
*   **Root:** It is the core lexical unit that carries the primary semantic content. There are no affixes (prefixes or suffixes) attached to it in this specific form.
*   **Part of Speech:** It functions primarily as a **noun** (*zelfstandig naamwoord*).

Although there is only one morpheme, it is **polysemous**, meaning it carries multiple related meanings depending on context:
1.  **Educational Subject:** *Ik heb een toets in het vak wiskunde.* (I have a test in the subject math.)
2.  **Trade/Profession:** *Hij is een man van zijn vak.* (He is a man of his trade/profession.)
3.  **Compartment/Slot:** *Het vak in de kast.* (The compartment in the cupboard.)

While the root `vak` is a single morpheme, it interacts with **inflectional morphemes** to indicate grammatical number or diminution. These are added *to* the root, creating new morphological structures:

To understand the morpheme fully, it helps to know its origin:
*   **Origin:** Middle Dutch *vac*, Old Dutch *fak*.
*   **Cognates:** German *Fach* (compartment, subject, trade).

*   **Distinction:** It is etymologically distinct from the word *vakantie* (vacation). While they look similar, *vakantie* comes from the Latin *vacatio* (freedom from duty), whereas *vak* is a native Germanic root referring to a physical or conceptual "space" or "division."
