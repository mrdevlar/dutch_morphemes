From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`vrijwilliger`** (meaning "volunteer") is a fascinating example of transparent Germanic morphology. It is a **derived compound noun** built from four distinct morphemes.

Here is the breakdown of the word, moving from the surface level down to the root morphemes.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented as follows:
> **vrij** + **wil** + **ig** + **er**

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. `vrij` (Free)
*   **Type:** Root / Adjective
*   **Meaning:** "Free," "unconstrained," or "independent."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Proto-Germanic **\*frijaz**.
*   **Cognates:** English *free*, German *frei*, Old Norse *frjá*.
*   **Function:** In this compound, it modifies the concept of "will," indicating that the will is not coerced.

#### B. `wil` (Will)
*   **Type:** Root / Noun Stem
*   **Meaning:** "Will," "desire," or "intention."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Proto-Germanic **\*wiljō**.
*   **Cognates:** English *will*, German *Wille*, Old English *will*.
*   **Function:** This is the semantic core of the word. It refers to the mental faculty of making choices.

#### C. `-ig` (-y / -ing)
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix (Adjectivizer)
*   **Meaning:** "Having the quality of" or "characterized by."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Proto-Germanic **\*-agaz**.
*   **Cognates:** English *-y* (e.g., *hungry*, *thirsty*), German *-ig* (e.g., *königlich*).
*   **Function:** It attaches to the noun *wil* to create the adjective **`willig`** (willing).
    *   *Wil* (noun) + *-ig* → *Willig* (adjective: "willing").

#### D. `-er` (-er)
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix (Agent Noun)
*   **Meaning:** "One who performs the action" or "one characterized by."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Proto-Germanic **\*-araz**.
*   **Cognates:** English *-er* (e.g., *worker*, *teacher*), German *-er* (e.g., *Lehrer*).
*   **Function:** It attaches to the adjective **`vrijwillig`** to create the agent noun **`vrijwilliger`**.
    *   *Vrijwillig* (adjective) + *-er* → *Vrijwilliger* (noun: "one who is voluntary").

---

### 3. Hierarchical Structure (Derivation Steps)
To understand how the word is built, we must look at the order of operations. It is not built all at once; it is a layered process:

1.  **Step 1 (Adjective Formation):**
    *   `wil` + `-ig` = **`willig`**
    *   *Meaning:* Willing.
2.  **Step 2 (Compounding):**
    *   `vrij` + `willig` = **`vrijwillig`**
    *   *Meaning:* Voluntary (literally "free-willing").
3.  **Step 3 (Nominalization):**
    *   `vrijwillig` + `-er` = **`vrijwilliger`**
    *   *Meaning:* Volunteer (literally "one who is free-willing").

### 4. Linguistic Comparison
This word is a **calque** (loan translation) of the concept found in English, though the etymological roots differ.

*   **Dutch:** *Vrijwilliger* (Germanic roots: Free + Will).
*   **English:** *Volunteer* (Latin roots: *Voluntas* = Will).
*   **German:** *Freiwilliger* (Germanic roots: Free + Will).

The Dutch word is structurally almost identical to the German *Freiwilliger*, highlighting the close relationship between the two West Germanic languages.

### 5. Orthographic Note
You may notice the spelling of the root `wil`.
*   In the adjective *willig*, the `l` is doubled.
*   In the noun *vrijwilliger*, the `l` remains doubled.
*   **Reasoning:** In Dutch orthography, when adding a suffix to a monosyllabic root ending in a single vowel and single consonant (like *wil*), the consonant is often doubled to preserve the short vowel sound. However, because *vrijwillig* is treated as a compound where the stress falls on the first syllable of the second part (*vrij-WIL-lig*), the doubling is retained to maintain the phonological integrity of the root *wil*.

### Summary
*   **Word:** `vrijwilliger`
*   **Literal Translation:** "Free-will-er"
*   **Actual Meaning:** Volunteer
*   **Morphological Type:** Compound Derivation (Adj + Suffix)
