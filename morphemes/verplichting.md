From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`verplichting`** (meaning "obligation" or "duty") is a complex word formed through derivational morphology. It consists of three distinct morphemes.

Here is the breakdown and explanation of each component.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented as follows:

> **ver-** + **plicht** + **-ing**

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Root: `plicht`
*   **Category:** Lexical Root (Noun base)
*   **Meaning:** "Duty," "pledge," or "obligation."
*   **Etymology:** This comes from Middle Dutch *plicht*, derived from Old Dutch *plīht*. It is cognate with the German *Pflicht* and the English word **plight** (in the archaic sense of a pledge or condition, as in "to plight one's troth").
*   **Function:** This is the semantic core of the word. It carries the primary meaning of "duty."

#### B. The Prefix: `ver-`
*   **Category:** Derivational Prefix
*   **Function:** In this context, *ver-* serves two main purposes:
    1.  **Verbalization:** It turns the noun *plicht* into a verb (*verplichten* = to oblige). In Dutch, *ver-* is a common prefix used to create verbs from nouns or adjectives, often implying a change of state, completion, or intensification.
    2.  **Inseparability:** In Dutch grammar, *ver-* is an **inseparable prefix**. This is phonologically significant because the stress of the word does not fall on the prefix.
*   **Stress Pattern:** The stress falls on the root: *ver-**PLICHT**-ing*. If *ver-* were separable (like *op-* in *opbellen*), the stress would fall on the prefix. Because the stress is on the root, it confirms *ver-* is inseparable.

#### C. The Suffix: `-ing`
*   **Category:** Derivational Suffix (Nominalizer)
*   **Function:** This suffix converts a verb into a noun (specifically, an abstract noun). It indicates the **action** or the **result** of the action denoted by the verb.
*   **Process:** It attaches to the stem of the verb *verplichten* (to oblige).
    *   *Verplichten* (verb: to oblige) + *-ing* = *Verplichting* (noun: the state of being obliged / obligation).
*   **Productivity:** This is a highly productive suffix in Dutch (similar to English *-ing* or *-tion*). Examples include *lezen* (to read) → *lezing* (lecture/reading) or *werken* (to work) → *werking* (functioning).

### 3. Derivational History (The Word Formation Chain)
To understand the morphology fully, it helps to trace the word's creation step-by-step:

1.  **Base Noun:** *Plicht* (Duty)
2.  **Verb Formation:** *Plicht* + *ver-* + *-en* (infinitive ending) → ***Verplichten*** (To oblige / To bind by duty).
3.  **Noun Formation:** *Verplichten* (stem: *verplicht*) + *-ing* → ***Verplichting*** (Obligation).

### 5. Comparison with English
While English does not have a direct cognate for the full word *verplichting*, the components are recognizable:
*   **Plicht** $\approx$ **Plight** (archaic: a pledge/condition).
*   **-ing** $\approx$ **-ing** (as in *binding* or *obliging*).
*   **Ver-** $\approx$ **For-** or **Per-** (as in *forbid* or *perform*, indicating a change or completion).

Therefore, linguistically, *verplichting* is a **deverbal noun** derived from a **denominal verb**. It represents a classic example of Dutch word-building where a noun is transformed into an action, and then back into an abstract noun representing the concept of that action.
