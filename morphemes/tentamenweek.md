From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`tentamenweek`** is a **compound noun** (samenstelling). It consists of two distinct lexical morphemes joined together to create a new meaning.

### 1. Segmentation
The word can be segmented into two free morphemes:
> **`tentamen`** + **`week`**

There is no linking morpheme (such as an *-s-* or *-en-*) between them, which is common in Dutch compounding (known as a *zero morpheme* in this position).

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

*   **Etymology:** This is a **loanword** from Latin.
    *   It derives from the Latin noun *tentamen* (an attempt, trial, or test), which comes from the verb *tentare* (to try, to test, to handle).
    *   In Dutch, it is fully nativized. It is not treated as a verb or a prefix; it functions as a standalone noun.
*   **Role in Compound:** It acts as the **modifier** (or dependent). It specifies *what kind* of week it is.

#### B. `week` (Head)

*   **Meaning:** Week (a period of seven days).
*   **Etymology:** This is a **Germanic** word.
    *   It stems from Proto-Germanic *\*wikō*.
    *   It is cognate with the English word *week*, the German *Woche*, and the Swedish *vecka*.
*   **Role in Compound:** It acts as the **head** (or head noun). In Dutch endocentric compounds, the semantic category of the whole word is determined by the second element. Therefore, a *tentamenweek* is a type of *week*.

### 5. Linguistic Note on Usage
In the context of Dutch higher education (universities and HBO), `tentamenweek` is a specific cultural-linguistic term. It refers to the specific period in the academic calendar where students take their written or oral examinations. It is distinct from `studieweek` (study week) or `vakantie` (vacation).

Because `tentamen` is a loanword, it retains its Latin plural form in some contexts (*tentamina*), though in modern spoken Dutch, the Dutch plural *tentamens* is also frequently used. However, in the compound `tentamenweek`, the singular form is used as the attributive modifier, which is standard Dutch grammar for compounds (e.g., *auto* + *deur* = *autodeur*, not *auto'sdeur*).
