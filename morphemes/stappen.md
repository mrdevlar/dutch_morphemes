From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`stappen`** can be segmented into two distinct morphemes. However, because Dutch orthography (spelling) interacts with morphology, there is a specific detail regarding the consonant doubling that must be addressed.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation

The word is composed of two morphemes:

> **`stap`** + **`-en`**

*Note on Spelling:* Although the word is spelled with a double **p** (`stappen`), the morphological boundary lies between the root and the suffix. The doubling of the *p* is an **orthographic rule** (spelling convention) used to indicate that the preceding vowel is short, not an indication of a third morpheme.

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Root: `stap-`
*   **Type:** Free Morpheme / Lexical Root
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun or Verb Stem
*   **Meaning:** "Step," "pace," or "footstep."
*   **Phonology:** /stɑp/ (The vowel is short /ɑ/).
*   **Etymology:** This root is of Proto-Germanic origin (*\*stappō*). It is cognate with the English word **step** and the German word **Schritt** (though German uses a different root for the verb *schreiten*).
*   **Function:** This carries the core semantic meaning of the word. It can stand alone as a noun (*de stap* = the step).

#### B. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Type:** Bound Morpheme / Inflectional Suffix
*   **Phonology:** /ə(n)/ (Often realized as a schwa sound, sometimes with a nasalized final /n/).
*   **Function:** This suffix is **polyfunctional**. Its grammatical meaning depends on whether `stappen` is being used as a verb or a noun.

    **Context 1: As a Verb (Infinitive)**
    *   **Meaning:** "To step," "to walk," or "to go out" (slang).
    *   **Grammar:** This is the **Infinitive** marker. In Dutch, the infinitive form of a regular (weak) verb is formed by adding *-en* to the stem.
    *   **Example:** *Ik wil **stappen**.* (I want to step/go out.)
    *   **Grammar Note:** It also serves as the **Plural Present Tense** marker (e.g., *Wij stappen* = We step).

    **Context 2: As a Noun (Plural)**
    *   **Meaning:** "Steps" (plural of *de stap*).
    *   **Grammar:** This is the **Plural** marker. In Dutch, many nouns form their plural by adding *-en*.
    *   **Example:** *Hij maakt grote **stappen**.* (He takes big steps.)

---

### 3. Orthographic vs. Morphological Structure

A crucial linguistic distinction in Dutch is between the **phonological root** and the **orthographic representation**.

*   **Morphological Root:** `stap`
*   **Suffix:** `-en`
*   **Spelling Rule:** If you were to write `stap` + `en` directly, Dutch spelling rules would dictate that the vowel *a* becomes long (pronounced /aː/), changing the pronunciation to *stāpen*.
*   **The Fix:** To preserve the short vowel sound of the root (/ɑ/), the final consonant of the root is doubled before the suffix is added.
*   **Result:** `stap` + `p` (orthographic) + `en` = **`stappen`**.

Therefore, linguistically, there are only **two** morphemes, even though there are three consonants in the middle of the word.

### 5. Semantic Extensions
While the morphology remains the same, the semantics can shift based on context:
1.  **Literal:** Physical movement (*stappen* = to walk/step).
2.  **Social Slang:** Going out to party (*stappen* = to clubbing).
3.  **Metaphorical:** Making progress (*grote stappen maken* = to make great strides).

### Conclusion
The word **`stappen`** is a **monomorphemic root** (`stap`) combined with an **inflectional suffix** (`-en`). The double 'p' is a spelling device to maintain vowel quality, not a morphological addition. The specific grammatical role (verb vs. noun) is determined by the syntactic context, not the morphology itself.
