From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`mede-eigenaar`** (meaning "co-owner") is a complex compound noun formed through derivational morphology.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes, their functions, and their etymological roots.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **`mede`** + **`eigen`** + **`aar`**

Structurally, this is a **compound** (`mede` + `eigenaar`) where the second element (`eigenaar`) is itself a **derived noun** (`eigen` + `aar`).

---

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. `mede-` (Prefix/Combining Form)
*   **Type:** Bound morpheme (in this context), functioning as a prefix.
*   **Meaning:** "Together," "jointly," "also," or "co-".
*   **Function:** It modifies the noun to indicate shared participation or possession. It transforms the concept of a single owner into a shared ownership.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *mede*. It is cognate with the German *mit* and the English word **mid** (as in *midday* or *amid*), stemming from the Proto-Germanic *\*medō*.
    *   *Note:* While it translates to "with" in English, it is **not** etymologically related to the English word "with" (which comes from a different Proto-Germanic root, *\*wiþrō*).
*   **Orthography:** In modern Dutch spelling rules (*Groene Boekje*), `mede-` is typically hyphenated when attached to a word beginning with a vowel (like *eigenaar*) or when the base word is itself a compound, to ensure readability and prevent mispronunciation.

#### B. `eigen` (Root/Stem)
*   **Type:** Adjective (Root).
*   **Meaning:** "Own," "proper," or "characteristic."
*   **Function:** This provides the semantic core of the word, relating to possession or property.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *eigen*. It is cognate with the German *eigen* and the English word **own**. All three stem from the Proto-Germanic *\*eigaz*.

#### C. `-aar` (Suffix)
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix (Agentive Nominalizer).
*   **Meaning:** "One who [verb/adjective]"; a person associated with the root.
*   **Function:** This suffix turns the adjective `eigen` (own) into a noun `eigenaar` (owner). It denotes the agent or the person holding the quality of the root.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *-aer*. It is a variant of the more common agentive suffix *-er* (as in *schrijver* / writer). It is cognate with the English suffix **-er** (as in *owner*, *worker*), though the vowel shift differs.
    *   *Comparison:* English *own* + *-er* = *owner*. Dutch *eigen* + *-aar* = *eigenaar*.

---

### 3. Syntactic and Semantic Composition

The meaning is constructed hierarchically:

1.  **Level 1 (Derivation):** `eigen` (own) + `-aar` (one who) $\rightarrow$ **`eigenaar`** (owner).
2.  **Level 2 (Compounding):** `mede-` (co-) + `eigenaar` (owner) $\rightarrow$ **`mede-eigenaar`** (co-owner).

**Semantic Logic:**
The prefix `mede-` distributes the agency of the noun. If an `eigenaar` is the sole possessor, a `mede-eigenaar` shares that possession with at least one other party.

### 4. Orthographic Note: The Hyphen
You may notice the hyphen in `mede-eigenaar`. In Dutch morphology, the hyphen serves a specific purpose here:
*   **Vowel Collision:** The prefix ends in a vowel (`e`) and the root begins with a vowel (`ei`). The hyphen prevents the vowels from merging visually or phonetically in a way that might confuse the reader.
*   **Compound Clarity:** Since `eigenaar` is already a compound word (`eigen` + `aar`), adding `mede` creates a "compound of a compound." Dutch orthography often mandates a hyphen in these cases to maintain the structural integrity of the base word.
