From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`kenmerk`** is a **compound noun** (*samenstelling*). It is formed by combining two distinct morphemes.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Segmentation
The word can be segmented as follows:
> **ken** + **merk**

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### **Morpheme 1: `ken-`**
*   **Type:** Bound morpheme (specifically, a verbal stem).
*   **Source:** Derived from the verb **`kennen`** (to know, to recognize, to be acquainted with).
*   **Function:** In this compound, it acts as the **modifier**. It specifies the *purpose* or *function* of the second morpheme.
*   **Etymology:** Traces back to the Proto-Germanic *\*kunnaną* (to know, be able to). It is cognate with the English verb "to know" (via the Germanic root *\*knew-*) and the English prefix "ken" (as in "beyond one's ken," meaning beyond one's knowledge).
*   **Note:** In modern Dutch, `ken` cannot stand alone as an independent word; it is only found in derived forms (like *kenmerk*, *herkennen*, *kennis*).

#### **Morpheme 2: `-merk`**
*   **Type:** Free morpheme (can stand alone as a word).
*   **Source:** The noun **`merk`** (mark, sign, brand, brand name).
*   **Function:** This is the **head** of the compound. In Dutch compounds, the head is usually the final element, which determines the grammatical category and the core meaning of the new word. Since `merk` is a noun, `kenmerk` is also a noun.
*   **Etymology:** Traces back to the Proto-Germanic *\*markō* (boundary, sign, mark). It is cognate with the English word **"mark"** and the German word **"Merkmal"** (characteristic).
*   **Meaning:** A distinguishing sign, symbol, or feature.

### 3. Semantic Composition (Meaning Construction)
The meaning of the compound is not simply the sum of its parts ("know" + "mark"), but rather a specific relationship between them.

*   **Literal Interpretation:** "A mark for knowing."
*   **Functional Interpretation:** A feature or sign that allows one to recognize or identify something.
*   **Relationship:** This is a **teleological compound** (purpose-based). The *merk* exists for the purpose of *kennen*. It is a distinguishing characteristic.

### 4. Grammatical Properties
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun (*zelfstandig naamwoord*).
*   **Gender:** Neuter (*het-woord*).
    *   *Singular:* **het** kenmerk
    *   *Plural:* de kenmerk**en**
*   **Derivation:** From this noun, a verb can be derived via conversion (zero-derivation):
    *   **`kenmerken`** (verb): To characterize, to mark.
    *   *Example:* "Dit **kenmerkt** de situatie." (This characterizes the situation.)

### Conclusion
Linguistically, **`kenmerk`** is a **neuter compound noun** consisting of a verbal stem (`ken-`) modifying a nominal head (`-merk`). It semantically encodes the concept of "a distinguishing sign used for recognition."
