From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`opvallen`** is a classic example of a **separable compound verb** (*scheidbaar samengesteld werkwoord*).

Here is the morphological breakdown and linguistic analysis of the word.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **`op`** + **`val`** + **`-en`**

### 2. Morpheme-by-Morpheme Analysis

#### A. `op` (Particle / Preposition)
*   **Category:** In this context, `op` functions as a **verbal particle** (specifically a separable prefix). Historically and etymologically, it is a preposition.
*   **Literal Meaning:** "Up," "on," or "upon."
*   **Function:** It modifies the semantic direction or aspect of the base verb. In many Germanic languages, particles add a specific nuance (directional, completive, or intensifying) to the verb.
*   **Linguistic Note:** While it appears attached in the infinitive form (`opvallen`), it is syntactically separable. In a main clause, it detaches and moves to the end of the sentence (e.g., *Hij valt **op***).

#### B. `val` (Verb Root / Stem)
*   **Category:** Lexical Root (Free Morpheme).
*   **Base Verb:** *vallen* (to fall).
*   **Meaning:** To drop, to descend, or to fall.
*   **Function:** This carries the core lexical meaning of the action.
*   **Phonology:** Note that in the past tense, this root undergoes a vowel change (ablaut) to *viel* (e.g., *hij viel op*), marking it as a strong verb.

#### C. `-en` (Inflectional Suffix)
*   **Category:** Inflectional Affix (Suffix).
*   **Function:** This is the **infinitive marker**. In Dutch, the standard ending for the infinitive form of a verb is *-en*.
*   **Morphophonology:** It does not change the meaning of the verb but marks the grammatical form (non-finite).

---

### 3. Syntactic Behavior: Separability
From a syntactic perspective, `opvallen` belongs to a specific class of verbs known as **separable verbs**.

*   **Infinitive:** *opvallen* (The particle and root are joined).
*   **Main Clause (V2 Word Order):** The finite verb moves to the second position, and the particle `op` is stranded at the end of the clause.
    *   *Example:* "Die kleur **valt** erg **op**." (That color stands out a lot.)
*   **Subordinate Clause:** The verb returns to the end, and the particle re-attaches.
    *   *Example:* "...dat die kleur erg **opvalt**." (...that that color stands out a lot.)

### 4. Semantic Analysis: Compositionality vs. Idiomatization
Linguistically, `opvallen` demonstrates a shift from **compositional meaning** to **idiomatic meaning**.

*   **Literal Composition:** If we sum the meanings literally, it would mean "to fall up" or "to fall on."
*   **Idiomatic Meaning:** The actual meaning is "to stand out," "to be noticeable," or "to strike someone."
    *   *Visual:* Something that catches the eye (literally "falls into the eye").
    *   *Mental:* An idea that strikes the mind (e.g., *Het valt mij op dat...* / "It strikes me that...").
*   **Semantic Bleaching:** The directional meaning of `op` (up) is largely bleached (weakened) in this context. It no longer implies physical upward motion but rather a metaphorical "landing" on attention.

### 5. Etymology and Cognates
*   **`op`**: Derived from Proto-Germanic **\*upp*. Cognate with English **"up"**.
*   **`val`**: Derived from Proto-Germanic **\*fallaną*. Cognate with English **"fall"**.
*   **`-en`**: Derived from Proto-Germanic **\*-aną*. Cognate with English infinitive marker (though English has largely lost it, retaining it in "to fall" vs "fall").
