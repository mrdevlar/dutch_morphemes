From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`bijvoorbeeld`** is a compound word that functions as an adverbial phrase meaning "for example."

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation of its components.

### 1. Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **bij** + **voor** + **beeld**

Alternatively, from a modern lexical perspective, it can be viewed as a compound of a preposition and a noun:

> **bij** + **voorbeeld**
> *(where `voorbeeld` itself is a compound of `voor` + `beeld`)*

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### A. `bij`
*   **Part of Speech:** Preposition.
*   **Literal Meaning:** "at," "by," "near," "with."
*   **Function in Compound:** In this context, it acts as a prepositional prefix that creates an adverbial sense. It implies proximity or inclusion ("in the case of" or "alongside").
*   **Cognates:**
    *   English: **by**
    *   German: **bei**
*   **Linguistic Note:** In Dutch, adding *bij* to a noun often creates a phrase indicating an accompanying circumstance (e.g., *bijna* = near + almost = almost).

#### B. `voor`
*   **Part of Speech:** Preposition.
*   **Literal Meaning:** "for," "before," "in front of."
*   **Function in Compound:** It indicates purpose or representation. Here, it suggests that the "image" is presented *for* the sake of illustration.
*   **Cognates:**
    *   English: **for** (historically related via Proto-Germanic *fur*)
    *   German: **vor**
*   **Linguistic Note:** In the compound *voorbeeld*, *voor* shifts the meaning of *beeld* from a physical picture to a conceptual model or prototype.

#### C. `beeld`
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun.
*   **Literal Meaning:** "image," "picture," "statue," "representation."
*   **Function in Compound:** This is the **head** of the compound. It carries the core semantic weight.
*   **Cognates:**
    *   English: **behold** (related to seeing), **bilde** (archaic/poetic)
    *   German: **Bild**
*   **Semantic Shift:** In this context, *beeld* undergoes a metaphorical shift. It moves from a visual representation (a painting) to a cognitive representation (an instance or model used to explain a concept).

### 3. Syntactic and Semantic Combination

**Level 1: `voor` + `beeld` = `voorbeeld`**
*   **Literal:** "For-image" or "Before-image."
*   **Meaning:** An image presented beforehand as a model.
*   **Modern Meaning:** "Example."
*   **Grammar:** Noun.

**Level 2: `bij` + `voorbeeld` = `bijvoorbeeld`**
*   **Literal:** "At/By an example."
*   **Meaning:** "In the case of an example" $\rightarrow$ "For example."
*   **Grammar:** Adverbial phrase (used to introduce an illustration).

### 4. Comparative Linguistics
To understand the logic of the word, it is helpful to compare it with its Germanic neighbors:

*   **Dutch:** *bijvoorbeeld* (at + for + image)
*   **German:** *zum Beispiel* (to + the + example)
*   **English:** *for example* (for + example)

While English and German use a prepositional phrase structure that remains distinct, Dutch has fused these words into a single orthographic unit (*bijvoorbeeld*), a process known as **lexicalization**.

### Summary
The word **`bijvoorbeeld`** is a **triple compound**. It literally constructs the concept of an example as an "image" (*beeld*) presented "for" (*voor*) a purpose, standing "by" (*bij*) the main statement to illustrate it.
