From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`ermee`** is a **pronominal adverb** (in Dutch: *voornaamwoordelijk bijwoord*). It is a fused construction used to replace a prepositional phrase involving an inanimate object.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation of its components.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into two distinct morphemes:

> **`er`** + **`mee`**

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### **Morpheme 1: `er`**
*   **Category:** Pronominal Adverb / R-Word (*R-woord*).
*   **Function:** In this construction, `er` functions as a **pro-form**. It stands in for a noun phrase (the antecedent) that has already been mentioned or is understood from context.
*   **Meaning:** It roughly translates to "it," "them," or "that."
*   **Linguistic Note:** In Dutch grammar, words like *er*, *daar*, *hier*, and *waar* are often called "R-words" because they contain the phoneme /r/. When combined with a preposition, `er` acts as the pronominal element that refers to the object of the preposition.
*   **Constraint:** `er` is typically used for **inanimate** objects. If the object is a person, Dutch speakers usually prefer *met hem* (with him) or *met haar* (with her) rather than *ermee*.

#### **Morpheme 2: `mee`**
*   **Category:** Preposition (Allomorph).
*   **Base Form:** The underlying preposition is **`met`** (meaning "with").
*   **Function:** It indicates accompaniment, instrumentality, or manner.
*   **Phonological Adaptation (Allomorphy):** This is the most interesting linguistic feature of the word. The standard preposition is *met*. However, when *met* is combined with the R-word *er* to form a pronominal adverb, the final /t/ is dropped and the vowel is lengthened to /eː/.
    *   *met* + *er* $\rightarrow$ **`ermee`** (not *`ermet`*).
    *   This is a specific **allomorph** of the preposition *met* conditioned by the presence of the preceding *er*.
*   **Comparison:**
    *   *op* + *er* $\rightarrow$ *erop* (no change to *op*)
    *   *in* + *er* $\rightarrow$ *erin* (no change to *in*)
    *   *met* + *er* $\rightarrow$ *ermee* (change from *met* to *mee*)

### 3. Syntactic Classification: Pronominal Adverb
Together, `er` and `mee` form a single lexical unit known as a **Pronominal Adverb**.

*   **Purpose:** Dutch syntax generally avoids separating a preposition from its object when the object is a pronoun (unlike English, where we say "with it"). Instead of saying *met het* (with the/it) or *met dat* (with that), Dutch fuses them.
*   **Example:**
    *   *Full phrase:* Ik speel **met het** blok. (I play with the block.)
    *   *Pronominal Adverb:* Ik speel **ermee**. (I play with it.)
*   **Word Order:** In a main clause, the pronominal adverb usually stays together. However, in spoken Dutch, it is very common to separate them for emphasis or rhythm (e.g., *Ik speel er mee*), though in writing, it is standard to keep them as one word: **`ermee`**.

### 4. Etymology and Historical Context
*   **`er`**: Derives from Old Dutch *iri*, related to the German *där* (there) and English *there*. It originally indicated location but evolved to function as a general placeholder.
*   **`mee`**: Derives from Middle Dutch *met*. The shift from *met* to *mee* in this specific construction likely occurred to facilitate smoother phonological flow (avoiding the /tm/ cluster) and to distinguish the pronominal form from the standard preposition.
