From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch verb **`kweken`** (meaning "to breed," "to cultivate," or "to rear") can be analyzed through its morphological structure, etymology, and orthographic rules.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word consists of two distinct morphemes:

| Morpheme | Form | Type | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`kwek-`** | Root | Lexical | Carries the core semantic meaning ("to breed/grow"). |
| **`-en`** | Suffix | Inflectional | Marks the verb as an **infinitive**. |

**Formula:** `kwek` + `en` = `kweken`

---

### 2. Analysis of the Root: `kwek-`
The root is the lexical core of the word.

*   **Semantics:** The root conveys the action of causing something to grow, develop, or come into existence. It is most commonly used regarding plants (*planten kweken*) or animals (*dieren kweken*).
*   **Etymology:**
    *   **Origin:** Middle Dutch *kweken*.
    *   **Proto-Germanic:** Reconstructed as *\*kwēkaną*.
    *   **Cognates:** This root is historically related to the concept of "life" or "quickness." It shares a lineage with the English word **quick** (in the archaic sense of "alive," as in "the quick and the dead") and the Dutch word **kwik** (quicksilver/mercury, historically associated with liveliness or speed).
    *   **Semantic Shift:** The original Proto-Germanic meaning was likely "to make alive" or "to quicken." Over time, in Dutch, this narrowed specifically to the agricultural and biological sense of "breeding" or "cultivating."
*   **Phonology:** The root contains a short vowel /ɛ/ (spelled *e*). In Dutch phonology, a short vowel in a stressed syllable must be followed by a double consonant in the spelling to indicate that the vowel remains short.

### 3. Analysis of the Suffix: `-en`
This is a grammatical morpheme.

*   **Function:** It is the standard **infinitive marker** for Dutch verbs.
*   **Paradigm:** When the verb is conjugated, this suffix is removed or altered:
    *   *Infinitive:* kwek**en**
    *   *Present (1st/2nd/3rd plural):* wij kwek**en**
    *   *Present (3rd singular):* hij kweek**t** (suffix changes to *-t*)
    *   *Past:* hij kweek**te** (suffix changes to *-te*)
    *   *Participle:* ge**kweekt** (prefix *ge-* + stem + *-t*)

### 4. Orthographic Morphology (The "Doubling" Rule)
A crucial linguistic feature of this word in written Dutch is the interaction between the root and the spelling rules, often called the **Stamregel** (Stem Rule).

*   **The Issue:** The root vowel is short (/ɛ/). In Dutch orthography, a short vowel in a closed syllable must be followed by a double consonant to prevent the vowel from being read as long.
*   **The Application:**
    *   In the infinitive **`kweken`**, the syllable is open (*kwe-ken*), so the vowel is naturally short, and only one *k* is needed.
    *   However, in the **stem** used for conjugation (e.g., *kweekt*, *kweekte*), the syllable becomes closed (*kweek-t*). To preserve the short vowel sound, the consonant is doubled to **`kweek-`**.
*   **Linguistic Note:** While the *phonological* root is /kwek/, the *orthographic* stem often appears as *kweek* in inflected forms. This is a morphophonemic alternation specific to Dutch spelling conventions.

### 5. Derivational Morphology
The root `kwek-` is productive and forms the basis for several other words (derivatives):

*   **`kweek`** (Noun): A seedling or a batch of bred animals.
*   **`kweker`** (Noun): A breeder or cultivator (Root + Agentive suffix *-er*).
*   **`kwekerij`** (Noun): A nursery or breeding farm (Root + Locative suffix *-erij*).
*   **`kwekeling`** (Noun): A young plant or seedling (Root + Diminutive suffix *-eling*).

### Summary
From a linguistic standpoint, **`kweken`** is a **monomorphemic lexical root** (`kwek-`) combined with an **infinitive inflectional suffix** (`-en`). Its etymology links it to the ancient concept of "making alive" (cognate with English *quick*), and its spelling demonstrates the Dutch morphophonemic rule regarding short vowels and consonant doubling.
