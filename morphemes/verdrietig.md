From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`verdrietig`** (meaning "sad" or "sorrowful") can be analyzed through **morphology** (the study of word structure) and **etymology** (word history).

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes, moving from the modern synchronic analysis (how the word is structured today) to the diachronic analysis (its historical roots).

### 1. Synchronic Analysis (Modern Structure)
In contemporary Dutch, a speaker perceives this word as a noun base modified by an adjectival suffix.

**Segmentation:** `verdriet` + `-ig`

| Morpheme | Type | Function | Meaning/Contribution |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **verdriet** | **Root (Noun)** | Lexical Base | "Sorrow," "grief," or "distress." |
| **-ig** | **Suffix** | Derivational | Turns the noun into an adjective. Means "having the quality of" or "full of." |

*   **Part of Speech:** Adjective.
*   **Stress Pattern:** Primary stress falls on the first syllable: **`VER`**-driet-ig.

---

### 2. Diachronic Analysis (Historical/Etymological Structure)
If we dig deeper into the history of the word (Middle Dutch and Old High German), the noun base *verdriet* itself is composed of two older morphemes.

**Segmentation:** `ver-` + `driet` + `-ig`

| Morpheme | Type | Origin | Function & Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **ver-** | **Prefix** | Proto-Germanic | A derivational prefix often indicating a change of state, completion, or intensification. In this context, it turns the root into a verb/noun indicating a process of "becoming troubled." |
| **driet** | **Root** | Middle Dutch *drieten* | The core semantic unit. Historically meant "to press," "to worry," or "to annoy." (Cognate with German *verdrießen*). |
| **-ig** | **Suffix** | Proto-Germanic *-agaz* | The standard adjectival suffix (cognate with English *-y* or *-ish*). |

**Note on the Root:** In Modern Dutch, the root `driet` is **bound**. You cannot use the word *driet* on its own; it only exists within the lexicalized noun *verdriet*.

---

### 3. Semantic Composition (How meaning is built)
The meaning of the word is constructed hierarchically:

1.  **`driet`**: The concept of trouble or pressing worry.
2.  **`ver-` + `driet`** $\rightarrow$ **`verdriet`**: The state or result of that worry (Sorrow/Grief).
3.  **`verdriet` + `-ig`** $\rightarrow$ **`verdrietig`**: Possessing the quality of sorrow (Sad).

**Literal Translation:** "Sorrow-full" or "Full of grief."

### 4. Linguistic Comparisons
To understand the morphemes better, it helps to compare them with English equivalents:

*   **The Suffix `-ig`:**
    *   Dutch: *zon* (sun) $\rightarrow$ *zon**ig*** (sunny).
    *   English: *sun* $\rightarrow$ *sun**y***.
    *   Both derive from the same Proto-Germanic root (*-agaz*).
*   **The Noun-Adjective Shift:**
    *   Dutch: *verdriet* (noun) $\rightarrow$ *verdrietig* (adj).
    *   English: *sorrow* (noun) $\rightarrow$ *sorrow**ful*** (adj).
    *   Note that English often uses *-ful* for this specific semantic shift, whereas Dutch prefers *-ig*.

*   **Meaning:** Characterized by sorrow.
