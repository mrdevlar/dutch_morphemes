From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`matig`** is a **derivational adjective**. It is **bimorphemic**, meaning it consists of two distinct morphemes (the smallest units of meaning).

Here is the breakdown and explanation of the morphemes:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented as follows:
> **`mat`** + **`ig`**

*   **`mat`**: The root (or stem).
*   **`ig`**: The derivational suffix.

---

### 2. Analysis of the Root: `mat-`
*   **Form:** In modern Dutch, this appears as the string of letters *m-a-t*.
*   **Status:** **Bound Stem / Allomorph.**
    *   While the letters *m-a-t* exist as a free morpheme in modern Dutch (meaning "mat" or "rug"), in the word *matig*, this form is a **bound stem**. It cannot stand alone with its current meaning.
    *   **Etymological Root:** This stem is historically and semantically derived from the noun **`maat`** (meaning "measure," "limit," or "size").
    *   **Phonological Change:** There is a morphophonological process where the long vowel *aa* in *maat* shortens to *a* when combined with the suffix *-ig*. This is a specific lexicalized rule in Dutch (similar to how *zond* becomes *zondig* from *zonde*).
*   **Meaning:** The core semantic concept is **"measure"** or **"limit."**

### 3. Analysis of the Suffix: `-ig`
*   **Form:** The string of letters *i-g*.
*   **Type:** **Derivational Suffix.**
*   **Function:** It converts a noun (or sometimes a verb/adjective) into an adjective.
*   **Meaning:** It generally conveys the meaning of **"having the quality of,"** **"characterized by,"** or **"full of."**
    *   *Examples:*
        *   *Vriend* (friend) $\rightarrow$ *Vriendelijk* (friendly) [Note: *-lijk* is similar, but *-ig* is distinct].
        *   *Kleur* (color) $\rightarrow$ *Kleurig* (colorful).
        *   *Zonde* (sin) $\rightarrow$ *Zondig* (sinful).
*   **In `matig`:** It adds the quality of "having measure."

---

### 4. Morphophonology (Sound & Spelling Rules)
A key linguistic feature of this word is the vowel change between the base noun and the derived adjective.

*   **Base Noun:** *Maat* (with a long vowel /aː/).
*   **Derived Adjective:** *Matig* (with a short vowel /a/).
*   **Rule:** In Dutch derivational morphology, when adding *-ig* to certain nouns ending in a long vowel, the vowel often shortens to maintain the syllable structure or due to historical sound shifts. This makes the spelling *matig* distinct from the noun *maat*, even though they share the same root.

### 5. Semantic Composition (Meaning Construction)
The meaning of the word is compositional, built from the meanings of its parts:

1.  **`mat`** (from *maat*) = Measure / Limit.
2.  **`-ig`** = Having the quality of.
3.  **`matig`** = Having measure / Having a limit.

**Semantic Shift:**
Over time, the literal meaning "having a measure" shifted to the idiomatic meaning of **"moderate,"** **"temperate,"** or **"not excessive."**
*   *Example:* *Een matige hoeveelheid* (A moderate amount).
*   *Example:* *Matig weer* (Temperate weather).

To understand the morphology fully, it helps to look at the related words in the same family:

Linguistically, **`matig`** is a **derived adjective** formed by attaching the adjectival suffix **`-ig`** to a bound stem **`mat-`** (an allomorph of *maat*). The word literally means "measure-having," which semantically evolved to mean "moderate."
