From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`inrichten`** can be analyzed as a **separable compound verb** (scheidbaar werkwoord).

Here is the morphological breakdown, etymological analysis, and syntactic explanation.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
Strictly speaking, the word consists of three morphemes:

1.  **`in-`** (Prefix)
2.  **`richt-`** (Verbal Stem)
3.  **`-en`** (Infinitive Suffix)

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Prefix: `in-`
*   **Category:** Separable Prefix (Prepositional/Adverbial).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Proto-Germanic **\*in**, cognate with English *in*, German *in*, and Latin *in*.
*   **Function:** It indicates direction, location, or a change of state. In this specific compound, it implies an action happening "inside" a space or bringing something "into" a state of order.
*   **Linguistic Note:** In Dutch, prefixes can be *inseparable* (stress on the root, e.g., *be-ginnen*) or *separable* (stress on the prefix, e.g., **`in`**-richten). In *inrichten*, the stress falls on **`in`**, marking it as separable.

#### B. The Stem: `richt-`
*   **Category:** Verbal Root/Stem.
*   **Base Verb:** *richten*.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old High German *rihten*, from Proto-Germanic **\*rehtjaną* ("to make straight, to set right").
*   **Cognates:** This root is related to the English word **`right`** (as in "straight" or "correct"), **`rectify`**, and **`direct`**.
*   **Core Meaning:** To direct, to aim, to align, or to straighten.
*   **Semantic Shift:** While *richten* alone means "to aim" or "to direct" (e.g., *een camera richten* = to aim a camera), the addition of *in-* shifts the meaning toward organizing or arranging items within a space.

#### C. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Category:** Inflectional Suffix.
*   **Function:** Marks the verb as an **infinitive** (the dictionary form).
*   **Morphology:** In Dutch, the infinitive is formed by adding *-en* to the stem.
    *   *Stem:* *richt*
    *   *Infinitive:* *richt* + *en* = *richten*
    *   *Compound:* *in* + *richten* = *inrichten*
*   **Conjugation:** When the verb is conjugated (e.g., *ik richt*, *hij richt*), this suffix is dropped.

---

### 3. Semantic Composition (How the meaning is built)
The meaning of the compound is not merely the sum of its parts, but a specific idiomatic combination:

*   **`richten`** (to direct/align) + **`in`** (into/inside) $\rightarrow$ **`inrichten`** (to arrange things inside a space).
*   **English Equivalents:** To furnish, to set up, to equip, to arrange.
*   **Context:** It is most commonly used regarding rooms, offices, or systems (e.g., *een kamer inrichten* = to furnish a room; *een systeem inrichten* = to set up a system).

---

### 4. Syntactic Behavior (Separability)
The most distinct linguistic feature of *inrichten* is its behavior in sentence structure. Because it is a **separable verb**, the prefix `in-` detaches from the stem `richten` in main clauses.

**1. Infinitive Form (Dictionary/After 'te'):**
*   *Ik wil de kamer **inrichten**.* (I want to furnish the room.)
*   *...om de kamer **in te richten**.* (Note the "te" splits the prefix and stem.)

**2. Main Clause (Present Tense):**
*   *Ik **richt** de kamer **in**.* (I furnish the room.)
    *   *Stem (`richt`) moves to position 2.*
    *   *Prefix (`in`) moves to the very end of the clause.*

**3. Past Tense:**
*   *Ik **richtte** de kamer **in**.* (I furnished the room.)
    *   *Note: The past tense suffix `-te` is added to the stem `richt`, not the prefix.*
