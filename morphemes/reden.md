From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`reden`** is a **homograph**. This means it is spelled the same way but represents different lexical entries with distinct morphological structures and etymologies.

To provide an accurate analysis, we must distinguish between the three primary ways this word functions in Dutch:
1.  **Verb (Infinitive):** "to reason" or "to argue."
2.  **Noun (Plural):** "reasons" (plural of *rede*).
3.  **Verb (Past Tense):** "rode" (past tense plural of *rijden*, "to ride").

Below is the morphological breakdown for each case.

---

### 1. The Verb (Infinitive): *to reason / to argue*
**IPA:** /ˈreː.də(n)/
**Lexeme:** *reden*

In this context, the word is a regular weak verb.

*   **Morpheme 1: `red-`**
    *   **Type:** Root / Stem (Lexical Morpheme)
    *   **Meaning:** The core semantic content relating to logic, argumentation, or speech.
    *   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *reden*, Old Dutch *rēdan*. It is cognate with the English word **"read"** (originally meaning "to advise" or "to arrange") and **"reason."**
*   **Morpheme 2: `-en`**
    *   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix
    *   **Function:** Marks the **infinitive** form of the verb. In Dutch, almost all infinitives end in *-en* (or *-n* after a vowel).
    *   **Note:** In spoken Dutch, the final *-n* is often dropped (apocope), sounding like /ˈreː.də/.

**Structure:** `red` (Root) + `en` (Infinitive Marker)

---

### 2. The Noun (Plural): *reasons*
**IPA:** /ˈreː.də(n)/
**Lexeme:** *rede* (singular) → *reden* (plural)

Here, the word is a noun functioning as the plural form of *rede*.

*   **Morpheme 1: `red-`**
    *   **Type:** Root / Stem (Lexical Morpheme)
    *   **Meaning:** "Reason," "speech," or "cause."
    *   **Etymology:** Same as the verb above (Middle Dutch *rede*).
*   **Morpheme 2: `-en`**
    *   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix
    *   **Function:** Marks the **plural** number.
    *   **Linguistic Note:** In Dutch, *-en* is one of the two most common plural markers (the other being *-s*). For feminine nouns ending in *-e* (like *rede*), *-n* is typically added to the stem. Since the stem ends in a vowel sound, it is often analyzed as adding *-n* to the singular form *rede*, resulting in *reden*.

**Structure:** `red` (Root) + `en` (Plural Marker)

---

### 3. The Verb (Past Tense): *rode* (from *rijden*)
**IPA:** /ˈreː.də(n)/
**Lexeme:** *rijden* (to ride)

This is the most complex case. *Reden* here is not the infinitive, but the **past tense plural** (and 1st/3rd person singular) of the strong verb *rijden* (to ride/drive).

*   **Morpheme 1: `ree-`**
    *   **Type:** Ablauted Stem (Lexical Morpheme)
    *   **Function:** This represents a vowel change (ablaut) from the present stem *rij-* to the past stem *ree-*. This is characteristic of Germanic **strong verbs** (Class VII).
    *   **Etymology:** From Proto-Germanic *\*rīdaną*.
*   **Morpheme 2: `-d-`**
    *   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix
    *   **Function:** Marks the **past tense**. While strong verbs usually rely on vowel change, this specific verb class adds a *-d-* in the past tense forms.
*   **Morpheme 3: `-en`**
    *   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix
    *   **Function:** Marks the **plural** (or 1st/3rd person singular) agreement.

**Structure:** `ree` (Ablauted Root) + `d` (Past Marker) + `en` (Plural/Person Marker)
