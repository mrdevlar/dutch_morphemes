From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`blijven`** (meaning "to stay" or "to remain") can be analyzed through morphology (the structure of words), phonology (sound patterns), and etymology (historical origin).

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes:

### 1. Morphemic Segmentation
The word consists of two distinct morphemes:

> **`blijf`** + **`-en`**

*   **`blijf`**: The lexical root (stem).
*   **`-en`**: The inflectional suffix (infinitive marker).

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Root: `blijf-` (Lexical Morpheme)
*   **Type:** Free morpheme (in the sense that it carries the core meaning) / Bound root (in the sense that it requires inflection to function as a finite verb).
*   **Function:** It carries the semantic content of the word: the concept of "remaining," "staying," or "continuing."
*   **Phonological Note (Final-Obstruent Devoicing):**
    *   This is a crucial linguistic feature in Dutch. The underlying consonant in the root is voiced **/v/**.
    *   However, in the **infinitive** form (`blijven`), the /v/ is in the middle of the word, so it remains voiced: /blɛivə(n)/.
    *   In the **present tense stem** (e.g., *ik blijf* - "I stay"), the consonant falls at the end of the syllable/word. Dutch phonology dictates that obstruents (stops and fricatives) devoice at the end of a word. Therefore, the /v/ becomes /f/.
    *   **Orthography:** To reflect this phonological rule, the spelling changes from `v` in the infinitive to `f` in the stem (*blijf*).
    *   **Underlying Form:** Linguists often represent the underlying root as **`blijv-`**, noting that the surface form becomes `blijf-` due to devoicing rules.

#### B. The Suffix: `-en` (Inflectional Morpheme)
*   **Type:** Bound morpheme.
*   **Function:** It marks the **infinitive** form of the verb.
*   **Grammar:** In Dutch, almost all verbs take the suffix *-en* (or sometimes just *-n* after a vowel) to form the dictionary/infinitive form. It indicates that the verb is not conjugated for person or number in this specific instance.
*   **Pronunciation:** In standard Dutch, this is often pronounced as a schwa /ə/ followed by a nasal /n/, or sometimes just /n/ in rapid speech.

### 3. Grammatical Classification: Strong Verb
From a morphological perspective, `blijven` is classified as a **strong verb** (sterk werkwoord). This means it does not form its past tense by adding a dental suffix (like *-te* or *-de* in weak verbs). Instead, it uses **ablaut** (vowel gradation).

This affects the root morpheme in different tenses:
*   **Infinitive:** `blijf` + `en` (Root vowel: *ij*)
*   **Past Singular:** `bleef` (Root vowel changes to *ee*)
*   **Past Plural:** `bleven` (Root vowel changes to *e*)
*   **Past Participle:** `ge` + `blev` + `en` (Prefix *ge-*, Root *blev*, Suffix *-en*)

The root morpheme is not static; it undergoes internal vowel changes to indicate tense, which is a hallmark of Germanic strong verbs.
