From a morphological perspective, the Dutch word **`belediging`** (meaning "insult" or "offense") is a complex word formed through derivational morphology. It consists of three distinct morphemes.

Here is the breakdown:

### Visual Segmentation
> **`be-`** + **`ledig`** + **`-ing`**

---

### 1. The Prefix: `be-`
*   **Type:** Bound derivational morpheme (Prefix).
*   **Function:** Verbalizer / Causative.
*   **Explanation:** In Dutch, the prefix *be-* is highly productive. It is often attached to adjectives or nouns to create transitive verbs. It generally implies "to make [adjective]" or "to affect with [noun]."
*   **In this context:** It attaches to the adjective *ledig* (empty) to create the verb *beledigen*.
*   **Semantic Shift:** Literally, *be- + ledig* suggests "to make empty." However, in a social context, this evolved to mean "to empty someone of their dignity or honor."

### 2. The Root: `ledig`
*   **Type:** Free morpheme (Root).
*   **Part of Speech:** Adjective (in its base form).
*   **Meaning:** "Empty," "vacant," or "idle."
*   **Explanation:** This is the semantic core of the word. It survives in modern Dutch as the standalone adjective *ledig* (e.g., *een ledige stoel* = an empty chair).
*   **Phonology:** The root ends in a velar fricative /ɣ/ (written as *g*), which is retained in the derived forms.

### 3. The Suffix: `-ing`
*   **Type:** Bound derivational morpheme (Suffix).
*   **Function:** Nominalizer.
*   **Explanation:** This is one of the most common suffixes in Dutch (and Germanic languages generally). It attaches to a verb stem to create a feminine noun (in traditional grammar) or a neuter noun (in modern usage) that denotes the **action**, **process**, or **result** of that verb.
*   **In this context:** It attaches to the verb stem *beledig-* (from *beledigen*).
    *   *Verb:* *beledigen* (to insult).
    *   *Noun:* *belediging* (the insult / the act of insulting).
*   **Note on Spelling:** When adding *-ing* to a verb ending in *-en*, the *-en* is dropped.
    *   *beledigen* $\rightarrow$ *beledig-* + *-ing* = *belediging*.

---

### Summary of Derivation Chain
The word follows a specific derivational path:

1.  **Adjective:** *ledig* (empty)
2.  **Verb (via prefix):** *be- + ledig + -en* $\rightarrow$ *beledigen* (to make empty / to insult)
3.  **Noun (via suffix):** *beledig- + -ing* $\rightarrow$ *belediging* (an insult)

### Semantic Evolution (Etymology)
From a linguistic semantics perspective, the shift from "empty" to "insult" is a metaphor. Historically, to insult someone was viewed as **stripping them of their honor**, effectively "emptying" them of their social standing or dignity. This is similar to the English idiom "to rob someone of their honor."

### Grammatical Classification
*   **Word Class:** Noun (*de* noun).
*   **Morphological Type:** Complex word (polymorphemic).
*   **Process:** Derivation (changing the word class and meaning, rather than just inflection like tense or number).
