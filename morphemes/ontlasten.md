From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch verb **`ontlasten`** can be analyzed through **morphology** (the study of word structure) and **etymology** (word origins).

Here is the morphological breakdown:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word consists of three distinct morphemes:

> **`ont-`** + **`last`** + **`-en`**

---

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Prefix: `ont-`
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix (Inseparable).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *unt-*, cognate with German *ent-* and English *un-* (specifically the privative sense, as in *undo* or *unburden*).
*   **Function:** It is a **reversative** or **privative** prefix. It indicates removal, reversal, separation, or the cessation of an action.
*   **Semantic Contribution:** It adds the meaning of "off," "away," or "undoing."
*   **Phonology:** In Dutch, inseparable prefixes like *ont-*, *be-*, *ver-*, and *ge-* are **unstressed**. The stress falls on the root syllable.

#### B. The Root: `last`
*   **Type:** Lexical Root (Noun stem).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *last*, cognate with English **`load`** and German **`Last`**.
*   **Meaning:** Burden, weight, cargo, responsibility, or tax.
*   **Semantic Contribution:** This provides the core semantic content of the word. It refers to the "thing" being acted upon.

#### C. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Type:** Inflectional/Derivational Suffix.
*   **Function:**
    1.  **Verbalizer:** It converts the noun root (*last*) into a verb.
    2.  **Infinitive Marker:** In Dutch, the bare infinitive form of a verb almost always ends in *-en* (e.g., *lopen*, *eten*, *ontlasten*).
*   **Semantic Contribution:** It indicates the action of doing something to the root concept.

---

### 3. Semantic Composition (Synthesis)

When you combine these morphemes, the literal meaning is constructed as follows:

*   **`ont-`** (remove/away) + **`last`** (burden/load) + **`-en`** (to make/do)
*   **Literal Meaning:** "To remove the burden" or "To take the load off."

**Extended Meanings:**
Because the "burden" can be physical, psychological, or legal, the verb has several nuances:
1.  **Physical:** To unload (e.g., a truck or a ship).
2.  **Psychological:** To relieve (e.g., *Ik wil je niet ontlasten* — "I don't want to relieve you [of your duties]").
3.  **Legal/Moral:** To exonerate or acquit (removing the burden of guilt).
4.  **Biological:** To discharge (bowels/bladder). This is the source of the noun *ontlasting* (excrement/discharge).

### 4. Linguistic Notes

*   **Stress Pattern:** Because *ont-* is an inseparable prefix, the primary stress falls on the root: **ont-LAST-en**. This distinguishes it from separable verbs (like *op-laden*, where stress is on the prefix).
*   **Conjugation:** It is a **weak verb** (regular).
    *   Past tense: *ontlastte*
    *   Past participle: *ontlast* (Note: The *-en* is dropped in the participle, but the prefix *ont-* remains attached).
*   **Cognate Comparison:**
    *   **Dutch:** *ontlasten*
    *   **German:** *entlasten* (identical structure and meaning).
    *   **English:** *unload* (conceptually identical, though English uses the prefix *un-* + noun *load* + verb suffix *-d*, whereas Dutch uses a prefix + noun root + verbal suffix).
