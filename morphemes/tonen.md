From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`tonen`** (meaning "to show," "to display," or "to exhibit") can be analyzed through its morphological structure, etymology, and orthographic rules.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word consists of two distinct morphemes:

> **`toon-`** + **`-en`**

| Morpheme | Type | Function | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`toon-`** | **Lexical Root (Stem)** | Carries the core semantic meaning. | "Tone," "pitch," "mood," or "display." |
| **`-en`** | **Inflectional Suffix** | Marks the grammatical form. | Infinitive marker (non-finite verb form). |

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Root: `toon-`
*   **Category:** This is a **denominal root**. It originates from the Dutch noun *de toon* (the tone, pitch, or mood).
*   **Derivational Process:** In Dutch, many verbs are created from nouns (denominal verbs). By adding the verb-forming suffix, the noun *toon* is converted into the verb *tonen*.
*   **Semantic Shift:** While the noun *toon* primarily refers to a musical tone or a mood (e.g., *in een goede toon* — in a good mood), the verb *tonen* has specialized to mean "to show" or "to display" (e.g., *een schilderij tonen* — to show a painting). This is a semantic broadening or shift common in language evolution.
*   **Phonology:** The underlying vowel is long /oː/.

#### B. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Category:** **Inflectional Suffix**.
*   **Function:** In Dutch, *-en* is the standard ending for the **infinitive** form of a verb (the dictionary form). It indicates that the verb is not conjugated for person or number.
*   **Pronunciation:** It is typically pronounced as a schwa /ə/ (often elided in rapid speech, sounding like *toon*).
*   **Dual Role:** In the context of *tonen*, this suffix performs a dual function:
    1.  **Derivational:** It turns the noun *toon* into a verb.
    2.  **Inflectional:** It marks the word specifically as the infinitive.

### 3. Orthographic Note (Spelling Rules)
A linguistic analysis of *tonen* must address the spelling difference between the noun and the verb, which is governed by Dutch orthographic rules regarding **open syllables**.

*   **Noun:** *`toon`*
    *   The word is one syllable. The vowel is long. Because the syllable is "closed" by the final consonant *n*, the long vowel is written with a double letter (**oo**).
*   **Verb:** *`tonen`*
    *   When the suffix *-en* is added, the syllable structure changes: *to-nen*.
    *   The first syllable (*to*) is now an **open syllable** (it ends in a vowel sound).
    *   **Rule:** In Dutch spelling, a long vowel in an open syllable is written with a **single letter**.
    *   Therefore, *toonen* is spelled **`tonen`**.

This spelling change does not indicate a change in pronunciation (the vowel remains long /oː/), but it is a morphophonological adjustment required by the writing system.

*   **Origin:** Middle Dutch *tonen*.
*   **Proto-Germanic:** *\*tōnōną*.
*   **Cognates:**

Linguistically, **`tonen`** is a **denominal verb** formed by the lexical root **`toon-`** and the infinitive/verb-forming suffix **`-en`**.
