From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`plannen`** is an interesting case because its surface form is identical for two different parts of speech (noun and verb), and its spelling involves a specific orthographic rule regarding vowel length.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation.

### 1. Morphemic Segmentation
At the morphological level, the word consists of **two morphemes**:

> **`plan`** + **`-en`**

*   **`plan`**: The **Root** (or Lexeme).
*   **`-en`**: The **Suffix** (Inflectional Affix).

**Note on the double 'n':**
Linguistically, the double *n* is **not** a separate morpheme. It is an **orthographic convention** (spelling rule) used to preserve the pronunciation of the vowel. In Dutch, a short vowel in a stressed syllable must be followed by a double consonant when a suffix is added.
*   Underlying form: /plɑn/ + /ən/
*   Surface spelling: **plannen**

---

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Root: `plan`
*   **Type:** Free Root (it can stand alone as a word).
*   **Pronunciation:** /plɑn/ (The 'a' is a short, open back vowel).
*   **Meaning:**
    *   As a noun: "a plan," "a scheme," "a map."
    *   As a verb stem: "to plan," "to intend."
*   **Etymology:** Borrowed from French *plan* (which comes from Latin *planum*, meaning "flat surface" or "ground plan").

#### B. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix (it changes the grammatical function but not the core meaning).
*   **Pronunciation:** /ə(n)/ (A schwa sound, often with a silent final 'n' in casual speech).
*   **Function:** This suffix has three possible grammatical functions depending on the context of the sentence:

    1.  **Plural Marker (Noun):**
        *   *Singular:* `het plan` (the plan)
        *   *Plural:* `de plannen` (the plans)
        *   *Function:* Indicates there is more than one plan.

    2.  **Infinitive Marker (Verb):**
        *   *Example:* `Ik wil plannen` (I want to plan).
        *   *Function:* Marks the verb in its base, unconjugated form.

    3.  **Present Tense Plural (Verb):**
        *   *Example:* `Wij plannen` (We plan).
        *   *Function:* Marks the verb conjugated for the 1st, 2nd, or 3rd person plural.

---

### 3. Phonology and Orthography (The "Double N" Rule)
To fully understand the morphology, one must understand why it is spelled with two *n*s.

*   **The Rule:** In Dutch orthography, a short vowel (like the /ɑ/ in *plan*) is "protected" from becoming long when a suffix is added by doubling the following consonant.
*   **Comparison:**
    *   *Singular:* `plan` (One 'n' signals the short vowel).
    *   *Plural:* `plannen` (If spelled `planen`, the 'a' might be read as a long vowel /aː/).
    *   *Verb Stem:* `plan` (e.g., *ik plan*).
    *   *Verb Infinitive:* `plannen` (e.g., *ik ga plannen*).

Therefore, the second *n* belongs to the **root** in terms of spelling logic, but morphologically, it is part of the boundary between the root and the suffix.

### Conclusion
The word **`plannen`** is a **monomorphemic root** (`plan`) combined with a **single inflectional suffix** (`-en`). The doubling of the consonant is a phonological safeguard in the writing system, not an additional morphological unit. Context determines whether it functions as a plural noun ("plans") or a verb form ("to plan" / "we plan").
