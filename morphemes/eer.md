However, because the word has two distinct meanings with different etymological origins, a complete linguistic analysis requires distinguishing between these two homonyms.

Here is the breakdown:

### 1. Morphological Structure
*   **Word:** `eer`
*   **Morpheme Count:** 1
*   **Morpheme Type:** **Free Root Morpheme**
    *   *Free:* It can stand alone as an independent word.
    *   *Root:* It carries the core lexical meaning and serves as the base for derivation (e.g., *eerlijk*, *vereren*).
*   **Phonology:** /eːr/ (Long 'e' sound followed by 'r').

### 2. Semantic Analysis (Homonymy)

#### A. `eer` (Noun)
*   **Meaning:** Honor, glory, reputation.
*   **Example:** *Hij handelt met eer.* (He acts with honor.)
*   **Grammatical Category:** Common noun (de-woord).
*   **Morphological Status:** In this context, it is a **bound root** when derived (e.g., *eer-bied*), but a **free root** when used alone.

#### B. `eer` (Preposition / Conjunction)
*   **Meaning:** Before (in time).
*   **Example:** *Eer ik ga, eet ik.* (Before I go, I eat.)
*   **Grammatical Category:** Preposition or Subordinating Conjunction.
*   **Morphological Status:** A free root functioning as a function word.
