From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`geboorte`** (meaning "birth") can be analyzed as a **deverbal noun** (a noun derived from a verb). It consists of three distinct morphemes.

Here is the breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word is segmented as follows:
> **`ge-`** + **`boor`** + **`-te`**

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Prefix: `ge-`
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix.
*   **Function:** In this context, `ge-` serves a nominalizing function. It is historically related to the prefix used to form past participles in Dutch (e.g., *ge-maakt*, *ge-zongen*).
*   **Nuance:** While the past participle of the related verb is *geboren* (born), the noun *geboorte* uses `ge-` to mark the word as a noun derived from the verbal concept. It often indicates a completed action or the result of an action.

#### B. The Root/Stem: `boor`
*   **Type:** Lexical Root (Verb Stem).
*   **Source:** This stem comes from the verb **`boren`**.
*   **Semantic Shift:** In modern Dutch, the verb *boren* primarily means "to drill" or "to bore." However, historically and etymologically, it meant "to be born" or "to bear" (related to the English verb *to bear* in the sense of giving birth).
*   **Phonological Note:** This stem preserves the long vowel **`oo`** from the infinitive *boren*. This is distinct from the past participle *geboren*, which uses the short vowel **`o`** due to Germanic ablaut (vowel gradation). The noun *geboorte* retains the infinitive vowel quality.

#### C. The Suffix: `-te`
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix.
*   **Function:** This is a nominalizing suffix used to create abstract nouns from verbs. It indicates the **action** or the **result** of the verb.
*   **Pattern:** This suffix is common in Dutch for forming nouns from verbs (e.g., *liefde* from *lief* + *de*, *zonde* from *zond* + *e*). In *geboorte*, the suffix is realized as `-te`.
*   **Spelling:** The `-t-` is the consonant of the suffix, and the final `-e` is the schwa vowel that typically accompanies this suffix in singular nouns.

### 3. Etymological Context
*   **Proto-Germanic:** The root traces back to **`*buraną`** (to bear, give birth).
*   **Cognates:**
    *   **English:** *Birth* (Note: English uses a different suffix *-th* and a different vowel shift).
    *   **German:** *Geburt* (Note: German uses the suffix *-t* and the vowel *u*).
    *   **Dutch:** *Geboorte* (Retains the long *oo* from the infinitive stem).

### 5. Linguistic Observation: The "Drill" vs. "Birth" Confusion
A common point of confusion for learners is that the modern verb **`boren`** means "to drill."
*   **Verb (Modern):** *boren* (to drill).
*   **Verb (Archaic/Birth):** *geboren worden* (to be born).
*   **Noun:** *geboorte* (birth).

Linguistically, *geboorte* is a "fossilized" form. It preserves the older meaning of the root *boor-* (birth) even though the active verb *boren* has shifted its primary meaning to "drilling" in contemporary usage. The noun remains the standard term for the event of birth.
