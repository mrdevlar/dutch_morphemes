From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`vertrouwd`** is a complex word formed through derivation and inflection. It functions primarily as an adjective (meaning "familiar" or "trusted") but is historically derived from a verb.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **ver-** + **trouw** + **-d**

---

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Root: `trouw`
*   **Type:** Free Morpheme (Lexical Root)
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun / Adjective
*   **Meaning:** Loyalty, fidelity, faithfulness, truth.
*   **Etymology:** This root comes from Old Dutch *trouwa*, which stems from Proto-Germanic **\*trewwō*.
*   **Cognates:** This is a very transparent cognate with English.
    *   English **`true`** (faithful, real).
    *   English **`troth`** (pledge of faith, as in "plight one's troth").
    *   German **`Treue`** (loyalty).
*   **Semantic Note:** In modern Dutch, *trouw* also means "wedding" (the ceremony of pledging loyalty).

#### B. The Prefix: `ver-`
*   **Type:** Bound Morpheme (Derivational Prefix)
*   **Function:** Inseparable verbal prefix.
*   **Meaning:** This prefix often indicates a change of state, a process, or intensification. When attached to *trouw*, it transforms the noun/adjective concept of "loyalty" into the action of "placing trust" or "entrusting."
*   **Verb Formation:** `vertrouwen` (to trust).
*   **Stress Pattern:** In Dutch, inseparable prefixes like *ver-* are unstressed. The stress falls on the root: *ver-**trou**-wen*.
*   **Etymology:** Proto-Germanic **\*far-**. It is distantly related to the English prefix *for-* (as in *forget* or *forgive*), though the meanings have diverged significantly over time.

#### C. The Suffix: `-d`
*   **Type:** Bound Morpheme (Inflectional/Adjectival Suffix)
*   **Function:** Past Participle Marker / Adjectival Suffix.
*   **Grammar Rule:** In Dutch, the past participle of weak verbs is formed by adding *-d* or *-t* to the stem (governed by the *'t kofschip* rule).
    *   The verb stem is *vertrouw*.
    *   The final sound is /v/ (voiced).
    *   Therefore, the voiced suffix **-d** is added (not the voiceless *-t*).
*   **Lexicalization:** While *-d* marks the past participle of the verb *vertrouwen* (e.g., *Ik heb hem vertrouwd* / "I have trusted him"), in the word *vertrouwd*, this participle form has been **lexicalized** as a standalone adjective.
*   **Meaning Shift:**
    *   **Verb:** To trust (active action).
    *   **Participle/Adjective:** Trusted, familiar, well-known (passive state or quality).

---

### 3. Semantic Evolution (The "Story" of the Word)

The meaning of *vertrouwd* has shifted through the combination of these morphemes:

1.  **`trouw`**: The core concept is **loyalty** or **truth**.
2.  **`ver-` + `trouw`**: The act of **making something true/faithful** to you, or placing your faith in something. This creates the verb *vertrouwen* (to trust).
3.  **`vertrouwd`**: The state of having been trusted.
    *   If a person is *vertrouwd*, they are **trusted**.
    *   If a situation or place is *vertrouwd*, it is **familiar** (because it is "trusted" or safe).

### 5. Linguistic Comparison
To understand the morphology better, compare it to the English equivalent:

*   **Dutch:** `ver-` + `trouw` + `-d`
*   **English:** `for-` (archaic) + `true` + `-th` (noun) OR `trust` (verb) + `-ed` (adj/participle).

While English uses the word *trust* (derived from the same Germanic root), Dutch kept the root *trouw* visible within the word *vertrouwd*. This makes the Dutch word morphologically richer in terms of visible etymology compared to the modern English "familiar" or "trusted."
