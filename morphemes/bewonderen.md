From a linguistic perspective, specifically within the field of **morphology** (the study of word structure), the Dutch verb **`bewonderen`** (to admire) can be segmented into three distinct morphemes.

Here is the breakdown:

### 1. Segmentation
The word is composed of the following structure:
> **`be-`** + **`wonder`** + **`-en`**

---

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Prefix: `be-`
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix.
*   **Function:** This is one of the most common verbal prefixes in Dutch (and Germanic languages generally). It serves to **derive** a new verb from a noun or an existing verb.
*   **Semantic Role:**
    *   **Transitivization:** It often turns an intransitive concept into a transitive verb (one that takes a direct object). You do not just "wonder"; you "admire *someone*."
    *   **Aspectual Meaning:** It can imply an action that is directed *upon* an object or covers the object thoroughly. In this specific case, it adds a nuance of active appreciation or regard directed toward the object of the wonder.
*   **Comparison:** Compare *wonderen* (to wonder/be surprised) with *bewonderen* (to admire). The *be-* adds the layer of active, directed admiration.

#### B. The Root: `wonder`
*   **Type:** Lexical Root (Stem).
*   **Origin:** Old Dutch *wundar*, Proto-Germanic *\*wundrą*.
*   **Part of Speech:** Originally a noun (meaning "miracle" or "marvel"), functioning here as the semantic base for the verb.
*   **Meaning:** It carries the core semantic content of "marvel," "surprise," or "awe."
*   **Cognates:** This root is directly cognate with the English noun **wonder** and the German noun **Wunder**.

#### C. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix.
*   **Function:** This is the standard marker for the **infinitive** form of weak verbs in Dutch.
*   **Grammatical Role:** It does not change the core meaning of the word (like the prefix does); rather, it marks the grammatical form of the verb (non-finite, dictionary form).
*   **Variation:** In spoken Dutch, this is often reduced to just *-n* (e.g., *bewonder'n*), but in standard orthography and morphological analysis, it is treated as the full suffix *-en*.

---

### 3. Morphological Process Summary

The formation of this word involves two distinct morphological processes:

1.  **Derivation:** The prefix **`be-`** is attached to the root **`wonder`**. This changes the lexical category and meaning, creating a new verb stem: *bewonder-* (to admire).
2.  **Inflection:** The suffix **`-en`** is attached to the derived stem to mark the infinitive tense/mood.

### 4. Etymological & Semantic Context

To understand the morphology fully, it helps to look at the semantic shift:

*   **Root:** *Wonder* (A miracle/marvel).
*   **Intermediate:** *Wonderen* (To be filled with wonder/surprise).
*   **Derived:** *Bewonderen* (To look at something with wonder/appreciation).

The prefix `be-` is crucial here. Without it, *wonderen* implies a state of surprise. With `be-`, the action becomes directed outwardly toward an object of admiration. This is a classic example of how Germanic prefixes modify the valency (the number of arguments a verb takes) and the semantic focus of a root.
