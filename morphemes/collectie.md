From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`collectie`** (meaning "collection") is a **loanword** derived from Latin via French. Morphologically, it is a **derived noun**.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes, analyzing both the synchronic (current Dutch structure) and diachronic (historical/etymological) layers.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
In modern Dutch morphology, the word is segmented into two morphemes:

> **collect-** + **-ie**

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Root/Stem: `collect-`
*   **Type:** Bound Lexical Root (Stem).
*   **Function:** Carries the core semantic meaning ("gathering" or "assembling").
*   **Status in Dutch:** This is a **bound root**. In contemporary Dutch, you cannot use *collect-* as a standalone word. While the verb *collecteren* exists (meaning to collect money or items), it is less common than the synonym *verzamelen*. The root is primarily found in nouns derived from it (e.g., *collectie*, *collecteur*).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from the Latin past participle *collectus* (from the verb *colligere*, meaning "to gather together").
    *   *Note on Etymology:* Historically, this root itself contains a prefix (*col-*, a variant of *con-* meaning "together") and a root (*-lect-*, from *legere* meaning "to gather/choose"). However, in modern Dutch morphology, speakers do not analyze *col-* as a separate productive prefix; the entire unit *collect-* is treated as the stem.

#### B. The Suffix: `-ie`
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix (Nominalizer).
*   **Function:** Converts the verbal/participial stem into an abstract noun. It indicates the **result** of an action or a **state of being**.
*   **Origin:** Latin *-io* (genitive *-ionis*) $\rightarrow$ Old French *-ie* $\rightarrow$ Dutch *-ie*.
*   **Productivity:** This is a highly productive suffix in Dutch for international vocabulary (e.g., *bibliotheek* vs. *bibliografie*, *politie*, *industrie*, *fotografie*).
*   **Phonology:** It is pronounced as a long /i/ sound (spelled *ie*), and it typically carries the primary stress of the word (col-lec-**TIE**).

### 3. Grammatical Context & Inflection
Once the word is formed via derivation, it enters the Dutch inflectional system:

*   **Plural:** *collectie* + **-s** $\rightarrow$ *collecties*
    *   The suffix *-ie* usually takes the standard Dutch plural marker *-s* (unlike *-ing* words which take *-en*).
*   **Diminutive:** *collectie* + **-tje** $\rightarrow$ *collectietje*
    *   Because the word ends in a vowel sound /i/, the diminutive suffix *-je* is added, often requiring an epenthetic *-t-* to maintain phonotactic rules (though in this specific case, the spelling is *collectietje* to preserve the long vowel sound).

### 5. Linguistic Note on "False Friends"
It is worth noting that while English also has the word *collection*, the Dutch *collectie* is a **cognate** (sharing a common etymological ancestor) rather than a direct translation in all contexts.
*   **Dutch:** *Een collectie van schilderijen* (A collection of paintings).
*   **English:** *A collection of paintings*.
*   **Difference:** In Dutch, *collectie* can also refer to a specific "issue" or "edition" (e.g., a fashion collection), similar to English, but it is strictly a noun. The verb form *collecteren* is a "Dutch-ism" (a verb created from the noun) and is not standard in English (where we use *collect*).

### Conclusion
Linguistically, **`collectie`** is a **monomorphemic stem** (`collect-`) combined with a **nominalizing suffix** (`-ie`). It represents a classic example of **international vocabulary** in Dutch, where the morphological structure is preserved from Latin/French but integrated into Dutch phonology and inflectional rules (such as pluralization with *-s*).
