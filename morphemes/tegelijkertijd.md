From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`tegelijkertijd`** (meaning "simultaneously" or "at the same time") is a complex compound word. It can be broken down into four distinct morphemes.

Here is the morphological segmentation and explanation:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
**`te`** + **`gelijk`** + **`er`** + **`tijd`**

---

### 2. Component Analysis

#### **A. `te`**
*   **Category:** Preposition / Prefix
*   **Meaning:** "At," "to," or "towards."
*   **Function:** In this context, it functions as a locative/temporal marker indicating a specific point or state.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *te*, related to the English preposition "to" and German *zu*.
*   **Note:** In modern Dutch, *te* is often used to form adverbs (e.g., *tegen* vs. *te*), but here it acts as the spatial/temporal anchor for the phrase.

#### **B. `gelijk`**
*   **Category:** Adjective (historically) / Adverbial base
*   **Meaning:** "Equal," "same," or "alike."
*   **Function:** It modifies the concept of time, suggesting that two or more events share the same temporal value.
*   **Etymology:** Cognate with the English word **"like"** (as in "alike") and the German word **"gleich"**.
*   **Lexicalization:** In modern Dutch, *tegelijk* is often treated as a single lexicalized adverb meaning "at the same time." However, morphologically, it is a combination of *te* + *gelijk*.

#### **C. `er`**
*   **Category:** Linking Morpheme (*Tussenvoegsel*)
*   **Meaning:** None (in this specific context).
*   **Function:** This is a **linking element**. It does not carry semantic meaning like the comparative suffix *-er* (as in *snel* → *sneller*). Instead, it serves a phonological and historical function to bridge the two parts of the compound.
*   **Linguistic Note:** In Dutch compounding, linking elements like *-s-*, *-en-*, or *-er-* are common. The *-er-* here is likely a fossilized remnant of an old genitive or dative case marker that helped connect the adverbial phrase to the noun *tijd*. It distinguishes the word from a simple juxtaposition.

#### **D. `tijd`**
*   **Category:** Noun
*   **Meaning:** "Time."
*   **Function:** This is the **head** of the compound. The entire word functions as an adverb, but the core noun is *tijd*.
*   **Etymology:** Cognate with the English word **"tide"** (as in "eventide" or "high tide," which originally referred to a specific time or season, not just the ocean).
*   **Relation:** It is related to the German *Zeit*.

---

### 3. Syntactic and Semantic Structure

*   **Literal Translation:** "At-equal-time"
*   **Semantic Composition:**
    1.  **`tegelijk`** (At the same time / Simultaneously)
    2.  **`tijd`** (Time)
    3.  **`er`** (Linker)
*   **Result:** The word essentially means "at the same time-time." While this sounds redundant in English, in Dutch, it emphasizes the temporal aspect of the simultaneity.

### 4. Comparison with English
To understand the morphology better, compare it to the English equivalent:

*   **Dutch:** `te` (at) + `gelijk` (equal) + `er` (linker) + `tijd` (time)
*   **English:** `at` + `the` + `same` + `time`

The Dutch word is more morphologically dense, packing the preposition (`te`), the adjective (`gelijk`), and the noun (`tijd`) into a single lexical unit.
