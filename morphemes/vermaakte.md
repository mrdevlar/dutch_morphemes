From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`vermaakte`** can be analyzed through **morphology** (the study of word structure). It is a verb form consisting of three distinct morphemes.

Here is the breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented as follows:
> **ver-** + **maak** + **-te**

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. `ver-` (Derivational Prefix)
*   **Type:** Derivational Morpheme (Prefix).
*   **Function:** This prefix changes the lexical meaning of the root verb. It often indicates a change of state, completion, or a specific direction of action.
*   **Etymology:** It stems from Proto-Germanic *\*far-*, cognate with the English prefix *for-* (as in *forget* or *forgive*).
*   **Semantic Shift:** In this specific context, it attaches to the root *maak* (make). While *maken* means "to make," the addition of *ver-* creates the verb *vermaken*, which means "to entertain" or "to amuse." This is a case of **lexicalization**, where the combination creates a meaning that is not strictly compositional (i.e., it does not literally mean "to make away").

#### B. `maak` (Root / Stem)
*   **Type:** Lexical Root (Free Morpheme).
*   **Base Form:** The infinitive of the verb is *maken* ("to make").
*   **Stem Formation:** In Dutch morphology, to form the stem of a verb, the infinitive ending *-en* is removed.
    *   *Maken* $\rightarrow$ Stem: **`maak`**.
*   **Meaning:** To create, to do, or to make.
*   **Phonology:** The stem ends in the phoneme /k/, which is a **voiceless** velar plosive. This phonological feature is crucial for determining the suffix used in the next step.

#### C. `-te` (Inflectional Suffix)
*   **Type:** Inflectional Morpheme (Suffix).
*   **Function:** This suffix marks **Past Tense** (Preterite) and **Singular Number** (1st or 3rd person singular).
*   **Phonological Conditioning (The "T-Vowel" Rule):** Dutch has a rule regarding the past tense suffix for weak (regular) verbs. The choice between *-te* and *-de* depends on the final sound of the stem:
    *   If the stem ends in a **voiceless** consonant (t, k, f, s, ch, p), the suffix is **-te**.
    *   If the stem ends in a **voiced** consonant or vowel, the suffix is **-de**.
    *   Since the stem `maak` ends in /k/ (voiceless), the suffix becomes **-te**.
*   **Pronunciation:** In standard Dutch, this is pronounced as a schwa followed by a voiceless /t/ (/tə/).

### 3. Grammatical Context
*   **Infinitive:** *vermaken* (to entertain)
*   **Present Tense:** *vermaakt* (entertains)
*   **Past Tense (Singular):** *vermaakte* (entertained)

*   **Past Participle:** *vermaakt* (entertained)
