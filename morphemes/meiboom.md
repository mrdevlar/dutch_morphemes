From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`meiboom`** is a **compound noun** consisting of two distinct free morphemes.

Here is the morphological breakdown and analysis:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented as follows:
> **`mei`** + **`boom`**

### 2. Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. Morpheme 1: `mei`
*   **Type:** Free morpheme (can stand alone as an independent word).
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun (Proper Noun / Temporal Noun).
*   **Meaning:** "May" (the fifth month of the year).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *meie*, Old Dutch *meio*, tracing back to the Latin *Maius* (named after the Roman goddess Maia).
*   **Function in Compound:** **Modifier.** It specifies the *type* or *occasion* associated with the head noun. It answers the question "Which kind of pole?"

#### B. Morpheme 2: `boom`
*   **Type:** Free morpheme.
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun.
*   **Meaning:** "Tree."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *boom*, Old Dutch *bōm*, Proto-Germanic *\*bōmaz*.
*   **Function in Compound:** **Head.** In Dutch compounding, the rightmost element is typically the head. Therefore, a *meiboom* is a type of *boom*.
*   **Semantic Shift:** While *boom* literally means "tree," in this specific compound, the meaning undergoes a slight semantic narrowing. It refers to a **wooden pole** or **post** (often stripped of branches) rather than a living, growing tree. This is a common metonymic shift in Germanic languages where the material (wood/tree) represents the object made from it.

### 3. Compound Structure (Syntax & Morphology)
*   **Structure:** Endocentric Compound (Noun + Noun = Noun).
    *   The compound belongs to the category of the head (*boom*).
    *   The structure is `[Modifier] + [Head]`.
*   **Linking Morphemes:** There is **no linking element** (such as *-s-*, *-en-*, or *-e-*).
    *   In Dutch, many compounds require a linking morpheme (e.g., *appel* + *s* + *taart* = *appeltaart*). However, *meiboom* is a direct juxtaposition. This often occurs when the first element is a proper noun (like a month name) or when the phonological flow allows for direct combination without ambiguity.
*   **Pluralization:** The plural suffix attaches to the head morpheme.
    *   Singular: *meiboom*
    *   Plural: *meiboom**en*** (The *-en* suffix attaches to *boom*, not *mei*).

### 4. Phonology
*   **IPA:** /ˈmei.bom/
*   **Stress:** Primary stress falls on the first syllable of the first morpheme (**mei**-boom). This is standard for Dutch compounds.
*   **Vowel Length:**
    *   *mei*: Diphthong /ɛi/ (long).
    *   *boom*: Long vowel /oː/.

### 5. Cultural Semantics
Linguistically, this word is a **cultural lexical item**. It refers specifically to the tradition of erecting a decorated pole on May 1st (Labour Day / Spring Festival).
*   **Synonyms/Related terms:** *Mei* (the month), *Meiviering* (May celebration).
*   **Contrast:** Unlike the English "Maypole" (which is often written as two words or hyphenated), Dutch orthography mandates the closed compound form (*meiboom*).
