From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`regulier`** is a classic example of a **loan derivation** (ontleende afleiding). It is not formed from native Germanic roots but is borrowed from Latin via French, adapted into Dutch morphology.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into two morphemes:

> **`regul-`** + **`-ier`**

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Root: `regul-`
*   **Type:** Bound Root (Lexical Root).
*   **Origin:** Latin *regula* (meaning "rule," "straightedge," or "pattern").
*   **Status in Dutch:** This is a **bound morpheme**. In modern Dutch, the root `regul-` cannot stand alone as an independent word.
    *   *Contrast:* The native Dutch word for "rule" is **`regel`**. However, *regel* and *regulier* are etymological doublets (they share the same ancestor but entered the language differently).
    *   *Phonology:* The vowel change from *e* (in *regel*) to *u* (in *regul-*) reflects the preservation of the Latin/French vowel quality rather than the Germanic vowel shift.
*   **Semantic Contribution:** It carries the core meaning of "rule," "standard," or "order."

#### B. The Suffix: `-ier`
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix (Adjectival).
*   **Origin:** Latin *-arius* (via Old French *-ier*).
*   **Function:** It converts the noun/root into an adjective. It indicates "pertaining to" or "characterized by."
*   **Productivity:** This suffix is common in Dutch for words of Latin/French origin (e.g., *militair*, *particulier*, *administratief* [note: -ief is similar], *universitair*).
*   **Semantic Contribution:** It adds the meaning of "having the nature of" or "following the standard of."

### 3. Etymological Path (Diachronic View)
To understand the morphology fully, one must look at the historical evolution:

1.  **Latin:** *regula* (noun) $\rightarrow$ *regulāris* (adjective, "according to a rule").
2.  **French:** *régulier* (The Latin *-aris* evolved into French *-ier*).
3.  **Dutch:** *regulier* (Borrowed directly from French or Latin during the period of high cultural borrowing, roughly 17th–19th century).

### 4. Synchronic vs. Diachronic Analysis
*   **Diachronic (Historical):** The word is clearly composed of *regula* + *-aris*.
*   **Synchronic (Modern Dutch):** For a modern Dutch speaker, the word is often perceived as a single, unanalyzable lexical unit (a monomorphemic loanword) because the root `regul-` is not productive. However, linguistically, it retains the structure of **Root + Suffix**.

### 5. Comparison with Native Synonyms
To highlight the morphological difference, compare *regulier* with the native Germanic equivalent:

| Word | Morphology | Origin | Meaning Nuance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Regulier** | `regul-` + `-ier` | Latin/French | Formal, standard, official, "regular" (e.g., regular army). |
| **Regelmatig** | `regel` + `-matig` | Native Germanic | Recurring, frequent, "regular" (e.g., regular visits). |

*   **`regel`**: Native noun (Rule).
*   **`-matig`**: Native suffix (meaning "having the measure/character of").

### Summary
*   **Word:** `regulier`
*   **POS:** Adjective
*   **Morphemes:** `regul-` (Root: Rule/Standard) + `-ier` (Suffix: Pertaining to).
*   **Classification:** Loanword with internal derivational structure.
*   **Stress:** re-**GU**-li-er (Stress falls on the penultimate syllable, typical for this suffix pattern).
