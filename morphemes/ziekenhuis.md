From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`ziekenhuis`** (hospital) is a **compound noun** (*samengesteld zelfstandig naamwoord*). It is formed by combining two distinct lexical units.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three morphemes:

> **ziek** + **-en** + **huis**

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. `ziek` (Root/Stem)
*   **Part of Speech:** Adjective.
*   **Meaning:** "Sick" or "ill."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *sieck*, Old Dutch *siek*. It is cognate with the English word **sick** and the German *siech* (archaic/dialectal for sick/ailing).
*   **Function:** This is the semantic root describing the condition of the people associated with the building.

#### B. `-en` (Inflectional Suffix)
*   **Type:** Plural marker / Substantivizing suffix.
*   **Function:** In this specific context, the suffix serves two purposes:
    1.  **Pluralization:** It marks the adjective as plural.
    2.  **Substantivization:** It turns the adjective (*ziek*) into a noun phrase meaning "the sick people" (*de zieken*).
*   **Linguistic Note:** In Dutch grammar, when an adjective is used as a noun to refer to a group of people, it typically takes the plural *-en* ending (e.g., *de rijken* = the rich, *de armen* = the poor). In the compound *ziekenhuis*, this plural form is retained to indicate that the house is for *multiple* sick people, not just one.

#### C. `huis` (Head/Root)
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun.
*   **Meaning:** "House" or "building."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *huus*, Old Dutch *hūs*. It is cognate with the English word **house** and the German *Haus*.
*   **Function:** This is the **head** of the compound. In Dutch (and Germanic) compounding, the final element determines the grammatical gender and the general category of the word.
    *   *Huis* is a neuter noun (*het* woord). Therefore, *het ziekenhuis* is also neuter.

### 3. Semantic Composition (How the meaning is built)
The word is an **endocentric compound**, meaning the meaning of the whole is a specific type of the head noun.

*   **Literal Translation:** "Sick-people-house" or "House of the sick."
*   **Semantic Shift:** While literally a house for sick people, the term has undergone semantic narrowing to refer specifically to a medical institution (hospital) rather than a private home where someone might be sick.

### 4. Phonological Note
*   **Linking Sound:** There is no additional linking sound (like *-s-* or *-n-*) inserted between the two parts. The plural *-n* of *zieken* flows directly into the *h* of *huis*.
*   **Pronunciation:** /ˈzi.kə(n)ˌɦœys/. Note that the *k* in *ziek* is pronounced clearly, and the *h* in *huis* is pronounced (unlike in some English loanwords where 'h' might be silent).

**Total Meaning:** A building designated for the care of sick people (**Hospital**).
