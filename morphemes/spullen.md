From a morphological perspective, the Dutch word **`spullen`** can be broken down into two distinct morphemes.

Here is the linguistic breakdown:

### 1. Segmentation
**`spul`** + **`-en`**

---

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### **Morpheme 1: `spul`**
*   **Type:** Free Morpheme (Root / Lexeme)
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun
*   **Meaning:** "Stuff," "material," "belongings," or "gear."
*   **Number:** Singular (though often treated as a mass noun).
*   **Phonology:** The vowel **`u`** is short (IPA: /ʏ/).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *spul*, related to Low German *spul*. It is cognate with the English word *spel* (in the sense of "game" or "play," though the meaning has shifted significantly in Dutch to mean "things").

#### **Morpheme 2: `-en`**
*   **Type:** Bound Morpheme (Inflectional Suffix)
*   **Function:** Plural Marker
*   **Meaning:** Indicates plurality (more than one).
*   **Orthographic Note:** In Dutch orthography, when a singular noun ends in a single consonant and contains a **short vowel** in a stressed syllable, that consonant is doubled before adding the plural suffix `-en`.
    *   *Root:* `spul` (short `u`)
    *   *Suffix:* `-en`
    *   *Result:* `spul` + `en` → **`spullen`** (The `l` is doubled to preserve the short vowel sound).
    *   *Contrast:* If the vowel were long, it would be spelled differently (e.g., *muur* [wall] → *muren* [walls]).

---

### 3. Linguistic Context & Usage

**1. Inflectional Morphology**
This is a case of **regular pluralization**. The word follows the standard Dutch pattern for neuter nouns ending in a consonant cluster or single consonant with a short vowel.

**2. Semantic Shift (Pluralia Tantum)**
While `spul` exists as a singular noun (e.g., *dat hele spul* — "that whole stuff"), the form **`spullen`** is frequently used as a **pluralia tantum** (a noun that is only used in the plural form) when referring to personal belongings.
*   *Example:* "Ik moet mijn **spullen** pakken." (I have to pack my stuff/things.)
*   In this context, it functions similarly to the English word "belongings" or "gear," which are inherently plural in concept.

**3. Diminutive Formation**
To further prove that `spul` is the root, one can form the diminutive:
*   `spul` + `-je` (diminutive suffix) → **`spulletje`** (a little bit of stuff / a small item).
*   Note the consonant doubling here as well (`l` becomes `ll`) to maintain the short vowel.
