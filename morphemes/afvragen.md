From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`afvragen`** is a classic example of a **separable compound verb** (*scheidbaar werkwoord*).

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation of its components.

### 1. Morphological Breakdown

Strictly speaking, the word consists of **three morphemes**:

1.  **`af`** (Prefix / Particle)
2.  **`vraag`** (Root / Stem)
3.  **`-en`** (Infinitive Suffix)

However, in standard morphological analysis of Dutch verbs, it is often treated as a binary compound of the prefix and the base verb.

#### A. The Prefix: `af`
*   **Type:** Separable Prefix (also known as a verbal particle).
*   **Origin:** Preposition/Adverb.
*   **Literal Meaning:** "Off," "away," "down," or "from."
*   **Function:** In this context, it acts as a **derivational morpheme**. It changes the meaning of the base verb.
    *   *Literal spatial sense:* Moving something away (e.g., *afwassen* = to wash off/dishes).
    *   *Abstract sense in `afvragen`:* It implies directing the action "away" from an external recipient and turning it inward. It creates an idiomatic meaning distinct from the sum of its parts.

#### B. The Root: `vraag`
*   **Type:** Free Morpheme (Lexical Root).
*   **Meaning:** "Ask" or "Question."
*   **Etymology:** From Old Dutch *vragen*, related to German *fragen* and English *ask* (cognates via Proto-Germanic *\*fraugan-*).
*   **Function:** This carries the core semantic load of the action (the act of inquiring).

#### C. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Type:** Inflectional Morpheme.
*   **Function:** Marks the **infinitive** form of the verb.
*   **Grammar:** In Dutch, the infinitive ending is almost always *-en* (or *-n* after a vowel). It indicates that the verb is in its base, unconjugated form.

---

### 2. Semantic Analysis (Meaning)

The combination of these morphemes creates a semantic shift known as **idiomatization**.

*   **`vragen` (to ask):** Usually implies a transitive action directed at another person (e.g., *Ik vraag hem* = I ask him).
*   **`af` (off/away):** Implies a separation or redirection.
*   **`afvragen` (to wonder/question oneself):** The combination creates a reflexive meaning. The "question" is sent "away" from the speaker's mouth and directed back into their own mind.
    *   *English Equivalent:* "To wonder" or "To ask oneself."
    *   *Note:* It is almost exclusively used reflexively with the pronoun *zich* (e.g., *Ik vraag **me** af* = I wonder).

### 3. Syntactic Behavior (Separability)

From a syntactic perspective, `afvragen` is a **separable verb**. This is a crucial feature of Dutch morphology.

*   **In the Infinitive:** The morphemes are joined: *Ik wil **afvragen**.* (I want to wonder).
*   **In a Main Clause:** The prefix `af` separates from the root `vragen` and moves to the end of the clause.
    *   *Example:* *Ik **vraag** me **af** waarom...* (I wonder why...).
    *   *Structure:* Subject + Conjugated Verb (`vraag`) + Reflexive Pronoun (`me`) + **Separated Prefix** (`af`).

This separation proves that `af` is not a bound prefix in the same way the English prefix *un-* is (as in *undo*). In Dutch, `af` retains its status as an independent syntactic unit that can be displaced.

### 4. Summary Table

| Morpheme | Type | Meaning | Role in `afvragen` |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`af`** | Prefix / Particle | Off / Away | Modifies direction to "inward/reflexive" |
| **`vraag`** | Root | Ask | Core action (to inquire) |
| **`-en`** | Suffix | (Infinitive marker) | Marks the verb as non-conjugated |

### 5. Linguistic Takeaway
The word `afvragen` illustrates the **agglutinative** tendencies of Dutch verb formation (combining distinct morphemes to create new meanings) while maintaining **Germanic separability** in syntax. It demonstrates how a spatial preposition (`af`) can be grammaticalized to create an abstract, psychological state (wondering) from a concrete action (asking).
