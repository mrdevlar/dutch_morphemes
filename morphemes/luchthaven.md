From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`luchthaven`** (meaning "airport") is a classic example of a **compound noun** (*samengesteld zelfstandig naamwoord*). It consists of two distinct roots joined together to create a new semantic unit.

Here is the morphological breakdown and analysis:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into two free morphemes:

> **lucht** + **haven**

There are no bound morphemes (such as prefixes, suffixes, or inflectional endings) in this specific word. It is a direct juxtaposition of two independent words.

---

### 2. Analysis of Component Morphemes

#### A. `lucht`
*   **Morpheme Type:** Free Root Morpheme.
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun.
*   **Literal Meaning:** "Air," "sky," or "atmosphere."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *lucht*, from Old Dutch *luf*. It is cognate with the German *Luft* and the English dialect/archaic word *loft*. It stems from the Proto-Germanic **\*luftuz*.
*   **Function in Compound:** It acts as the **modifier** (or determiner). It specifies the domain or medium in which the "haven" operates.

#### B. `haven`
*   **Morpheme Type:** Free Root Morpheme.
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun.
*   **Literal Meaning:** "Harbor," "port," or "haven."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *haven*, from Old Dutch *hava*. It is cognate with the German *Hafen* and the English *haven*. It stems from the Proto-Germanic **\*hafnō*.
*   **Function in Compound:** It acts as the **head** of the compound. In Dutch (and Germanic languages generally), the head of a compound is the final element. This means the word is fundamentally a type of *haven*.

---

### 3. Syntactic and Semantic Structure

#### Compound Type: Endocentric
This is an **endocentric compound**. The meaning of the whole word is a hyponym (a specific type) of the head word.
*   A *luchthaven* is a type of *haven*.
*   Just as a *fietsenmaker* (bicycle maker) is a type of *maker*, a *luchthaven* is a type of *haven*.

#### Semantic Relationship: Metaphorical Extension
The semantics rely on a metaphorical extension of the word *haven*.
*   **Original Semantics:** A *haven* is traditionally a place where ships dock on water.
*   **Compound Semantics:** By adding *lucht* (air), the concept is transferred to aviation. It is a "harbor for air [vehicles]."
*   **Comparison:** This parallels the English word **airport** (*air* + *port*). Interestingly, the German equivalent is *Flughafen* (*flight* + *harbor*), showing a slight variation in which root is chosen to modify the harbor concept.

#### Orthography and Phonology
*   **Spelling:** It is a **closed compound** (written as one word without a space or hyphen).
*   **Linking Element:** Unlike many Dutch compounds that require a linking element (like *-s-* in *liefdes*verklaring or *-e-* in *kinder*boek), *luchthaven* uses **zero-linking**. The roots are placed directly next to each other.
*   **Stress Pattern:** In Dutch compounds, the primary stress typically falls on the **first element**.
    *   Pronunciation: **LUCHT**-haven (not lucht-HAVEN).
    *   This distinguishes it from a phrase where the stress might fall on the noun (e.g., *een haven voor lucht* would stress *haven*).

### Conclusion
Linguistically, **`luchthaven`** is a transparent compound formed by two native Germanic roots. It follows standard Dutch morphological rules for compounding (Head-final structure, primary stress on the modifier) and demonstrates how language creates new vocabulary by combining existing concepts (Air + Harbor) to describe modern technology.
