From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch verb **`richten`** can be analyzed through both **synchronic** (current structure) and **diachronic** (historical/etymological) morphology.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes:

### 1. Synchronic Analysis (Current Structure)
In modern Dutch grammar, the word is analyzed as a verb stem combined with an infinitive marker.

| Morpheme | Form | Type | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`richt`** | *richt* | **Lexical Root / Stem** | Carries the core semantic meaning (to aim, to direct, to align). |
| **`-en`** | *-en* | **Inflectional Suffix** | Marks the verb as the **infinitive** form (or plural present tense). |

*   **Pronunciation:** /ˈrɪxtə(n)/
*   **Grammar:** Regular weak verb (though historically strong, it conjugates regularly in modern Dutch: *ik richt, jij richt, wij richten*).

### 2. Diachronic Analysis (Etymological Structure)
To understand the internal structure of the stem **`richt`**, we must look at its history. The stem itself is not a primitive root but is derived from an adjective.

| Morpheme | Form | Origin | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`recht`** | *recht* | **Adjective Root** | Meaning "straight," "right," or "correct." |
| **`-t`** | *-t* | **Derivational Suffix** | A historical **causative/factitive** marker. It turns the adjective into a verb meaning "to make [adjective]." |
| **`-en`** | *-en* | **Infinitive Suffix** | Standard Dutch verb ending. |

**Historical Evolution:**
*   **Proto-Germanic:** *\*rehtjaną* (to make straight).
*   **Old Dutch:** *rihten*.
*   **Modern Dutch:** *richten*.

Therefore, etymologically, *richten* literally means **"to make straight."**

---

### 3. Semantic Analysis (Meaning)
The meaning of the morphemes has shifted over time from the literal physical sense to abstract concepts:

1.  **Literal (Physical):** To align something physically so it is straight.
    *   *Example:* *De schuur richten* (To straighten the shed).
2.  **Directional:** To point something toward a target.
    *   *Example:* *Het geweer richten* (To aim the rifle).
3.  **Abstract:** To direct attention, effort, or communication.
    *   *Example:* *Zich richten tot de koning* (To address the king).
    *   *Example:* *Zich richten op de toekomst* (To focus on the future).

### 4. Related Morphological Families
The root **`richt-`** is highly productive in Dutch. It appears in various derived forms, demonstrating how this morpheme functions within the language:

*   **`richting`** (*richt* + *-ing*): Noun suffix *-ing* creates a noun meaning "direction."
*   **`inrichten`** (*in-* + *richt* + *-en*): Prefix *in-* adds the meaning of "setting up" or "furnishing" (literally: to arrange inside).
*   **`her-richten`** (*her-* + *richt* + *-en*): Prefix *her-* adds the meaning of "re-" (to re-align or re-aim).
*   **`richtlijn`** (*richt* + *lijn*): Compound noun meaning "guideline" (literally: straight line).

### 5. Cognates (Comparative Linguistics)
The morpheme **`richt`** shares a common ancestor with words in other Germanic languages, tracing back to the Proto-Indo-European (PIE) root **`*reg-`** (to move in a straight line).

*   **English:** *Right* (adjective), *Rector* (one who rules/straightens), *Regulate*.
*   **German:** *Richten* (identical meaning and form).
*   **Latin:** *Regalis* (Royal/Kingly - one who rules straight).
