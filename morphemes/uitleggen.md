From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`uitleggen`** (meaning "to explain" or "to lay out") is a **separable compound verb** (*scheidbaar werkwoord*).

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation of its components.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation

The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **uit** + **leg** + **-en**

### 2. Detailed Explanation of Morphemes

#### A. `uit-` (Separable Prefix / Particle)
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix (specifically a separable verbal particle).
*   **Literal Meaning:** "Out," "out of," or "from."
*   **Function:** In this context, it acts as a **resultative** or **directional** prefix. It modifies the meaning of the root verb to indicate that the action is being brought "out" into the open or made clear.
*   **Syntactic Behavior:** This is a **separable** prefix. In a main clause, it detaches from the verb and moves to the end of the sentence.
    *   *Infinitive:* `uitleggen`
    *   *Main Clause:* "Ik **leg** het **uit**." (I explain it.)
    *   *Subordinate Clause:* "...dat ik het **uitleg**." (...that I explain it.)

#### B. `leg-` (Root / Stem)
*   **Type:** Lexical Root (Free Morpheme).
*   **Literal Meaning:** "To lay," "to put," or "to place."
*   **Function:** This is the core semantic component of the word. It provides the physical action upon which the prefix acts.
*   **Orthographic Note:** You will notice the spelling in the infinitive is *leggen* (with a double 'g'), but the morphological stem is just **`leg`**.
    *   In Dutch orthography, when a stem has a short vowel (like the 'e' in *leg*) and is followed by a vowel-initial suffix (like *-en*), the final consonant is doubled to preserve the short vowel sound.
    *   Therefore, morphologically it is **`leg`** + **`-en`**, but orthographically it appears as **`leggen`**.

#### C. `-en` (Infinitive Suffix)
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix.
*   **Function:** This marks the verb as being in the **infinitive** form (the dictionary form). It does not change the core meaning of the word but indicates its grammatical state (non-finite).
*   **Variation:** This suffix changes based on tense and person (e.g., *leg* for "I lay," *legde* for "laid," *gelegd* for "laid" [participle]).

### 3. Semantic Composition (Meaning)

The meaning of `uitleggen` is largely **compositional**, meaning it is derived from the sum of its parts, though it has become an idiom over time.

*   **Literal:** "To lay out" (e.g., laying out a map or laying out clothes).
*   **Figurative:** "To explain" (laying out thoughts or information clearly for someone else to see).

### 5. Related Forms (Morphological Paradigm)
To fully understand the morphology, it helps to see how the morphemes behave in other forms of the verb:

*   **Present Tense:** *Ik **leg** **uit**.* (Prefix separates)
*   **Past Tense:** *Ik **legde** **uit**.* (Stem changes to *legde*)
*   **Past Participle:** *Ik heb het **uit** **ge** **legd**.* (Prefix *uit-* stays at front, *ge-* is inserted before the stem, *-d* is the participle suffix).

This structure highlights that `uit-` is a separable prefix, while `leg` is the stable root, and `-en` is the specific marker for the infinitive form you asked about.
