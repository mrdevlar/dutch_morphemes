### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Root: `schrik`
*   **Category:** Free Morpheme / Lexical Root.
*   **Semantic Meaning:** This root denotes "fright," "shock," or "sudden fear."
*   **Part of Speech Flexibility:** In Dutch, this root functions as both a noun and a verb stem.
    *   *Noun:* **De schrik** (The fright).
    *   *Verb Stem:* **schrik-** (as seen in *ik schrik*, *jij schrikt*).
*   **Etymology:**
    *   **Middle Dutch:** *schrik*
    *   **Old Dutch:** *skrik*
    *   **Proto-Germanic:** *\*skrik-* (related to jumping or starting).
    *   **Cognates:** German *Schreck* (noun) / *schrecken* (verb); English *shriek* (phonetically related via Grimm's Law, though semantically shifted); Old High German *skrik*.
*   **Linguistic Note:** This is a **zero-derivation** (or conversion) relationship in the lexicon. The noun *schrik* and the verb stem *schrik* are identical in form, differing only in syntactic context.

#### B. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Category:** Inflectional Suffix.
*   **Function:** In Dutch grammar, *-en* is the standard marker for the **infinitive** form of a verb. It indicates that the verb is not conjugated for person, number, or tense in this specific form.
*   **Pronunciation:** In many dialects and standard speech, the *-en* is often reduced to a schwa sound /ə/ or even elided entirely in rapid speech (e.g., *schrik'n*), though it remains morphologically present.
*   **Comparison:** Compare with *lopen* (to walk), *werken* (to work), *zien* (to see - irregular but ends in infinitive marker).

### 3. Morphosyntactic Properties (Verb Class)

While the surface morphology is simply Root + Suffix, the internal morphology of the verb reveals it is a **Strong Verb (Sterk werkwoord)**. This is crucial for a complete linguistic explanation.

*   **Verb Class:** Strong Verb (Class 4 in traditional Dutch grammar).
*   **Ablaut (Vowel Gradation):** The verb changes its root vowel to indicate tense, rather than adding a dental suffix (like *-te* or *-de* in weak verbs).
    *   **Infinitive:** *schrikken* (Root vowel: **i**)
    *   **Past Singular:** *schrok* (Root vowel: **o**)
    *   **Past Participle:** *geschrokken* (Root vowel: **o** + prefix *ge-* + suffix *-en*)
*   **Valency (Transitivity):**
    *   **Intransitive/Reflexive:** Most commonly used as *zich schrikken* (to get startled).
    *   **Transitive:** Rarely used transitively in modern Dutch (e.g., *Hij schrikte mij* is archaic or dialectal). The transitive meaning "to frighten someone" is usually expressed by the prefixed verb **`verschrikken`**.
