From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`praktisch`** can be analyzed through morphology (the study of word structure), etymology (word origins), and semantics (meaning).

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word consists of two distinct morphemes:

> **`praktik`** + **`-isch`**

*   **`praktik`**: The lexical root (stem).
*   **`-isch`**: A derivational suffix.

---

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Root: `praktik-`
*   **Type:** Free morpheme (when standing alone as a noun stem) / Bound morpheme (in this specific derivation).
*   **Source Noun:** This stem is derived from the Dutch noun **`praktijk`** (meaning "practice" or "the actual application of an idea").
*   **Morphophonemic Adjustment:** Notice the spelling change. The noun is spelled *praktijk*, but when forming the adjective, the final *-j* is dropped to create the stem *praktik-*. This is a common process in Dutch morphology to ensure phonological flow before adding a vowel-initial suffix.
*   **Meaning:** It carries the core semantic concept of "action," "exercise," or "actual use" rather than theory.

#### B. The Suffix: `-isch`
*   **Type:** Derivational suffix (specifically, an adjectivalizing suffix).
*   **Function:** It transforms a noun (or noun stem) into an adjective. It indicates "pertaining to," "characterized by," or "of the nature of."
*   **Etymology:** This suffix has a complex history:
    1.  **Greek:** Originated as *-ikos* (e.g., *praktikos*).
    2.  **Latin:** Evolved into *-icus*.
    3.  **Germanic:** Entered the Germanic languages (including Dutch and German) as *-isch*.
*   **Cognates:** It is directly cognate with the English suffix **-ic** (as in *practical*, *poetic*, *musical*) and the German suffix **-isch** (as in *praktisch*, *musikalisch*).

---

### 3. Semantic Analysis (Meaning)

The combination of these morphemes creates a specific semantic field that has evolved over time:

1.  **Literal Meaning:** "Related to practice."
    *   *Example:* *Een praktische oplossing* (A solution related to actual practice/theory).
2.  **Extended Meaning:** "Useful," "convenient," or "feasible."
    *   In modern Dutch, *praktisch* is often used to describe something that works well in real life, not just in theory.
    *   *Example:* *Dit is een praktisch meubelstuk* (This is a useful/convenient piece of furniture).
3.  **Adverbial Shift (Grammaticalization):**
    *   Uniquely in Dutch, *praktisch* is frequently used as an adverb meaning **"almost"** or **"virtually."**
    *   *Example:* *Het is praktisch klaar* (It is **practically/almost** ready).
    *   *Linguistic Note:* This is a semantic bleaching where the word loses its concrete meaning of "useful" and gains a scalar meaning of "near completion."
