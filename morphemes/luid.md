### 1. Morphological Structure
**`luid`** is a **monomorphemic** word. This means it consists of a single morpheme.

*   **Morpheme:** `luid`
*   **Type:** Free lexical morpheme (Root)
*   **Category:** Adjective (can also function as an adverb)

There are no prefixes or suffixes attached to this specific form. It is the base form (lemma) of the word.

### 2. Etymological Breakdown
While the word is a single morpheme in Modern Dutch, its history reveals a deeper structure rooted in Proto-Indo-European (PIE).

*   **Modern Dutch:** `luid` (loud)
*   **Middle Dutch:** `ludet`, `luid`
*   **Old Dutch:** `hlūd`
*   **Proto-Germanic:** `*hlūdaz`
*   **Proto-Indo-European (PIE):** `*kleu-` (meaning "to hear")

**The Semantic Shift:**
The original PIE root `*kleu-` relates to hearing. In Proto-Germanic, the meaning shifted from the passive "heard" (audible) to the active quality of the sound itself ("loud" or "clear"). This is a common semantic shift where the capability of being heard transforms into the intensity of the sound.
