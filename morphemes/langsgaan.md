From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`langsgaan`** is a **separable compound verb** (*scheidbaar werkwoord*).

To understand its morphology, we must look at it on two levels: the primary compound structure and the internal etymology of the prefix.

Here is the breakdown:

### 1. Primary Morphological Segmentation
At the surface level, the word consists of two distinct morphemes joined together to form the infinitive:

> **`langs`** + **`gaan`**

*   **`langs`**: A prepositional particle (directional adverb).
*   **`gaan`**: The verbal root (the semantic head).

### 2. Deep Dive into Morphemes

#### A. The Particle: `langs`
While modern Dutch treats `langs` as a single lexical unit (a preposition meaning "along" or "past"), etymologically and morphologically, it can be further decomposed:

*   **`lang`**: An adjective meaning "long."
*   **`-s`**: An adverbial suffix (historically a genitive ending).

**Linguistic Function:**
In this compound, `langs` functions as a **directional prefix**. It modifies the verb `gaan` to indicate the trajectory of the movement.
*   *Literal sense:* Moving "along" a length.
*   *Idiomatic sense:* Passing by a location or visiting someone.

#### B. The Verb: `gaan`
*   **Form:** Infinitive of the verb *to go*.
*   **Function:** This is the **head** of the compound. It carries the core grammatical information (tense, person, number) and the primary action.
*   **Etymology:** From Old Dutch *gān*, related to German *gehen* and English *go*.

### 3. Syntactic and Phonological Properties

#### Separability (*Scheidbaarheid*)
The most defining linguistic feature of `langsgaan` is that it is a **separable verb**.
*   **Infinitive:** Written as one word (`langsgaan`).
*   **Conjugated:** The prefix `langs` separates from the verb and moves to the end of the clause.
    *   *Example:* "Ik **ga** vandaag even **langs**." (I am dropping by today.)
*   **Past Participle:** The prefix remains separated, and the ge- prefix is inserted between them.
    *   *Example:* "Ik ben **langs** **ge** **gaan**." (I dropped by.)

#### Stress Pattern (*Klemtoon*)
In Dutch separable verbs, the primary stress falls on the **prefix**, not the verb root.
*   **`LÁNGS`-gaan** (Not: lang-SGAAN)
*   This distinguishes it from inseparable verbs (like *begrijpen*), where the stress falls on the root.

### 4. Semantic Composition (Meaning)
The meaning of the compound is not purely additive; it has undergone semantic bleaching and specialization.

1.  **`lang`** (long) + **`-s`** (along) + **`gaan`** (go)
2.  **Literal:** To go along (a path).
3.  **Idiomatic:** To visit someone briefly or to pass a specific location.

### Conclusion
**`Langsgaan`** is a morphological compound of the directional particle **`langs`** and the verb **`gaan`**. While `langs` itself contains the historical morphemes `lang` + `-s`, in modern syntax it functions as a single separable prefix that dictates the direction of the movement, creating the specific meaning "to drop by" or "to pass."
