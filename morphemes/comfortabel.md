From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`comfortabel`** is an example of **derivational morphology** involving a loanword adaptation.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes, followed by an explanation of their function, origin, and status within the Dutch language system.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation

The word can be segmented synchronically (as it exists in modern Dutch) as follows:

> **`comfort`** + **`-abel`**

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Root: `comfort`
*   **Type:** Free Morpheme (Noun) / Bound Root (in this specific derivation).
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun (*het comfort*).
*   **Meaning:** Physical ease, well-being, lack of hardship.
*   **Status:** In modern Dutch, *comfort* functions as an independent word. Therefore, in the derivation of *comfortabel*, it acts as the **base** or **stem**.
*   **Etymology:** This is an internationalism (loanword). It entered Dutch via French (*confort*) and English (*comfort*), ultimately deriving from the Latin verb *comfortare* (to strengthen, console).
    *   *Diachronic Note:* Historically, *comfort* itself can be broken down further into the Latin prefix **`com-`** (intensive, "together") + **`fort`** (from *fortis*, meaning "strong"). However, in modern Dutch morphology, speakers do not actively parse *com-* and *fort*; they perceive *comfort* as a single unit.

#### B. The Suffix: `-abel`
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix.
*   **Function:** Adjective-forming. It converts a noun (or verb stem) into an adjective.
*   **Meaning:** "Having the quality of," "capable of," or simply marking the word as an adjective describing a state.
*   **Origin:** Latin *-abilis* (via French *-able*).
*   **Dutch Orthography:** In English, this suffix is spelled *-able* (e.g., *comfortable*). In Dutch, it is standardized as **-abel** (e.g., *mobiel*, *flexibel*, *comfortabel*).
*   **Productivity:** This suffix is highly productive in Dutch for international vocabulary (words borrowed from Latin, French, or English). It is the international counterpart to the native Dutch suffix *-baar* (e.g., *leesbaar* vs. *leesabel*—though *leesabel* is rare, *acceptabel* is common).

### 3. Linguistic Classification

#### Synchronic vs. Diachronic
*   **Synchronic (Current State):** For a modern Dutch speaker, the word is composed of two morphemes: the noun *comfort* and the adjectival suffix *-abel*. The internal structure of *comfort* is opaque (not analyzed).
*   **Diachronic (Historical):** If we trace the etymology back to Latin, the structure is deeper:
    *   `com-` (prefix) + `fort` (root: strong) + `-abilis` (suffix).
    *   Meaning: "Capable of being strengthened" $\rightarrow$ "Capable of being consoled" $\rightarrow$ "Comfortable."

#### Loanword Adaptation
*   **Phonology:** The stress in Dutch falls on the syllable immediately preceding the suffix: *com-for-**TA**-bel*.
*   **Spelling:** The word follows Dutch spelling rules for internationalisms. While English drops the 'a' in the suffix (*comfortable*), Dutch retains the 'a' (*comfortabel*) to align with the Latin *-abilis* and French *-able* spelling conventions common in Dutch academic and formal registers.

### 4. Comparison with Native Morphology
To understand the "foreignness" of this morphology, it helps to compare it with a native Dutch equivalent:

*   **Loanword:** *Comfortabel* (Latin/French root + International suffix *-abel*).
*   **Native Equivalent:** *Gemakkelijk* (Native root *gemak* + Native suffix *-elijk*).

Both mean "comfortable," but *gemakkelijk* uses native Germanic morphology, whereas *comfortabel* uses Romance/Latin morphology adapted into Dutch.
