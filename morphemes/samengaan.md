From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`samengaan`** is a compound verb formed through **derivational morphology**. It consists of two distinct morphemes that combine to create a specific semantic meaning.

Here is the breakdown and explanation of the morphemes:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented as follows:
> **samen** + **gaan**

### 2. Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Prefix: `samen`
*   **Category:** Adverb functioning as a **separable verbal prefix**.
*   **Meaning:** "Together," "jointly," or "in union."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *samun*, which stems from the Proto-Germanic **\*samana**. This is cognate with the English word **same** and the German word **zusammen**.
*   **Grammatical Function:** In this context, *samen* modifies the action of the verb. It indicates that the action is performed by multiple subjects acting in unison or that two entities are merging.
*   **Linguistic Note:** In Dutch grammar, *samen* is classified as a **separable prefix** (*scheidbaar voorvoegsel*). This means that in a main clause sentence, the prefix splits from the verb stem and moves to the end of the clause (e.g., *Wij **gaan** morgen **samen***). However, in the infinitive form (`samengaan`), it is written as a single word.

#### B. The Verb Stem: `gaan`
*   **Category:** Strong (irregular) Verb.
*   **Meaning:** "To go," "to walk," or "to move."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *gān*, stemming from the Proto-Germanic **\*gangan**. This is cognate with the English verb **go** and the German verb **gehen**.
*   **Grammatical Function:** This is the lexical core of the word. It carries the primary action.
*   **Conjugation:** It is an irregular verb (*gaan* - *ging* - *gegaan*).

### 3. Morphological Process: Separable Compounding
The formation of `samengaan` is an example of **prefixation** combined with **compounding**.

*   **Type:** Separable Verb (*Scheidbaar Werkwoord*).
*   **Evidence of Separability:** The strongest linguistic evidence that these are two distinct morphemes is seen in the past participle. In Dutch, the participial prefix *ge-* is inserted **between** the separable prefix and the verb stem.
    *   Infinitive: *samen* + *gaan*
    *   Past Participle: *samen* + **ge** + *gaan* $\rightarrow$ **`samengegaan`**
    *   *If `samen` were an inseparable prefix (like `be-` or `ver-`), the `ge-` would not be inserted (e.g., `begaan`, not `begegaan`).*

### 4. Semantic Composition (Meaning)
The meaning of the compound is not always simply the sum of its parts; it has developed specific idiomatic nuances:

1.  **Literal:** To go somewhere together (e.g., walking side-by-side).
2.  **Romantic:** To be in a relationship (e.g., *Ze gaan samen* = They are dating).
3.  **Compatibility:** To match or fit well together (e.g., *Deze kleuren gaan samen* = These colors go well together).
4.  **Merger:** To combine or merge (e.g., *Twee bedrijven gaan samen* = Two companies merge).

### Conclusion
Linguistically, **`samengaan`** is a **separable compound verb**. It is composed of the adverbial prefix **`samen`** (indicating collectivity) and the verb stem **`gaan`** (indicating movement/action). The separation of these morphemes in conjugation (e.g., *samen-ge-gaan*) confirms their status as distinct units within the Dutch morphological system.
