From a linguistic and morphological perspective, the Dutch word **`inmiddels`** is a compound adverb that has undergone lexicalization (it is now treated as a single word rather than a phrase).

Here is the breakdown of its morphemes, moving from the surface structure to the deeper etymological structure.

### 1. Surface Segmentation
At a modern syntactic level, the word is often analyzed as a compound of a preposition and a fossilized adverbial form:

> **`in`** + **`middels`**

### 2. Deep Morphological Analysis
To understand the internal structure fully, we must look at the etymology. The component `middels` is itself derived from a noun root plus an inflectional suffix. Therefore, the most accurate morphological breakdown is:

> **`in`** + **`middel`** + **`-s`**

---

### 3. Morpheme-by-Morpheme Explanation

#### A. `in` (Preposition)
*   **Type:** Free morpheme (Preposition).
*   **Meaning:** "In," "into."
*   **Function:** In this specific compound, it functions temporally rather than spatially. It sets the frame of reference for the time period being discussed.
*   **Linguistic Note:** This is cognate with the English preposition "in" and the German "in."

#### B. `middel` (Noun Root)
*   **Type:** Free morpheme (Noun).
*   **Meaning:** "Middle," "midst," or "means."
*   **Function:** This is the semantic core of the word. It refers to the center point of a duration of time.
*   **Cognates:** English "middle," German "Mittel."
*   **Context:** In older Dutch, *middel* referred to the "middle" of a time span.

#### C. `-s` (Inflectional Suffix)
*   **Type:** Bound morpheme (Suffix).
*   **Function:** **Adverbial Genitive.**
*   **Explanation:** This is the most linguistically significant part of the word. In Old and Middle Dutch, the genitive case (often marked by *-s*) was frequently used to turn nouns into adverbs of time or manner.
    *   *Example:* *des morgens* (of the morning $\rightarrow$ in the morning).
    *   *In `inmiddels`:* The *-s* indicates the genitive plural of *middel* (historically *middelen*). It essentially means "in the [time] of the middles."
*   **Status:** This suffix is **fossilized**. In modern Dutch, you cannot productively add *-s* to a noun to make an adverb (e.g., you cannot say *`huis`* + *`-s`* to mean "at home"). It survives only in specific lexicalized adverbs like *inmiddels*, *intussen*, *sinds*, and *tijdens*.

---

### 4. Historical & Comparative Context

**Etymology:**
The word evolved from the Middle Dutch phrase *in den middelen* ("in the middles"). Over time, the article *den* was dropped, and the genitive plural ending *-en* shifted to *-s* (a common phonological shift in Dutch adverbs), resulting in *inmiddels*.

**English Comparison:**
The English equivalent is **"meanwhile."**
*   **Mean:** (Middle) $\approx$ Dutch *middel*
*   **While:** (Time) $\approx$ Dutch *tijd* (though *inmiddels* uses the genitive *-s* to imply time, whereas English uses the noun *while*).

**Semantic Shift:**
Originally, *inmiddels* meant strictly "in the meantime" (during the intervening time). However, in modern usage, it has developed a secondary meaning of **"by now"** or **"in the meantime (implying a change of state)."**
*   *Example:* "Hij is **inmiddels** vertrokken." (He has **by now** left / He has left **in the meantime**.)
