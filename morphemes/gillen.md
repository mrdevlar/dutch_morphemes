### 2. Detailed Analysis

#### Morpheme 1: `gill-`
*   **Type:** **Lexical Morpheme** (Root/Stem)
*   **Status:** **Free Morpheme** (in specific contexts, e.g., the imperative *"Gil!"* / "Scream!")
*   **Meaning:** This carries the core semantic content of the word: the act of screaming, shrieking, or yelling loudly.
*   **Etymology:** This root is **onomatopoeic** (sound-symbolic). It mimics the harsh, high-pitched sound of a scream. It is cognate with the German *gellen* and Old High German *gellen*.
*   **Orthography vs. Phonology:**
    *   **Spelling:** The double `l` is an orthographic convention in Dutch. It indicates that the preceding vowel `i` is **short** (/ɪ/). If the word were spelled *gilen*, the `i` would be pronounced as a long vowel (/iː/).
    *   **Phonology:** The phonemic representation of the stem is /ɣɪl/.

#### Morpheme 2: `-en`
*   **Type:** **Inflectional Morpheme** (Suffix)
*   **Status:** **Bound Morpheme** (cannot stand alone)
*   **Function:**
    1.  **Infinitive Marker:** In this specific form, `-en` marks the verb as the **infinitive** (the dictionary form).
    2.  **Plural Marker:** This same suffix is also used for the **plural present tense** (e.g., *wij gillen* / "we scream") and the **plural past tense** (e.g., *wij gilden* / "we screamed").
*   **Phonology:** In standard pronunciation, this is often realized as a schwa sound /ə/ followed by /n/, or simply /n/ in rapid speech (/ˈɣɪ.lə(n)/).

*   **Word Class:** Verb (Transitive/Intransitive).
*   **Verb Class:** In modern Standard Dutch, *gillen* is conjugated as a **weak verb** (regular past tense: *gilde*, past participle: *gegil*), although historically it had strong verb characteristics.
