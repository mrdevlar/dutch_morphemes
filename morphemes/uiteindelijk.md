From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`uiteindelijk`** (meaning "eventually," "finally," or "in the end") is a complex word formed through compounding and derivation.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation of its components.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **uit** + **eind** + **elijk**

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### **Morpheme 1: `uit`**
*   **Type:** Prefix / Preposition / Adverb (in this context, a bound morpheme within a compound).
*   **Literal Meaning:** "Out," "out of," "from."
*   **Function:** It acts as a directional or completive prefix. In this compound, it modifies the concept of the "end" to imply the "outermost" or "final" point of a process.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *ut*, which comes from Proto-Germanic **\*ut*. It is cognate with the English preposition "out" and the German *aus*.

#### **Morpheme 2: `eind`**
*   **Type:** Noun Stem.
*   **Base Word:** *einde* (noun, meaning "end").
*   **Literal Meaning:** "End," "limit," "boundary."
*   **Function:** This is the semantic core (root) of the word. In Dutch morphology, when the noun *einde* is used in compounds, the final schwa vowel (*-e*) is typically dropped, leaving the stem *eind-*.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *end*, from Proto-Germanic **\*andaz*. It is cognate with the English word "end" and the German *Ende*.

#### **Morpheme 3: `-elijk`**
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix.
*   **Base Suffix:** *-lijk* (equivalent to English *-ly*).
*   **Literal Meaning:** "Like," "characterized by," "pertaining to."
*   **Function:** This suffix converts the noun compound (*uiteind*) into an adjective or adverb. It indicates that the action or state is related to the "final end."
*   **Phonological Note:** While the core suffix is *-lijk*, it often appears as *-elijk* (with an epenthetic schwa /ə/) when attached to certain stems to maintain phonotactic flow (ease of pronunciation).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *-līk*, from Proto-Germanic **\*-līkaz*. It is cognate with the English suffix *-ly* (as in *friend* + *ly* = *friendly*) and German *-lich*.

### 3. Semantic Composition (How the meaning builds)

The meaning of the word evolves through the combination of these parts:

1.  **`uit` + `einde`** (Historical Compound): Literally "out-end." This conceptually refers to the "outermost limit" or the "result" of a process.
2.  **`uiteind`** (Modern Stem): The compound noun form, meaning "the final result" or "the conclusion."
3.  **`uiteind` + `elijk`**: "Pertaining to the final result."

**Semantic Shift:**
Over time, the literal spatial meaning ("at the outer end") shifted to a temporal meaning.
*   **Literal:** At the outer boundary.
*   **Temporal:** At the final point in time.
*   **Idiomatic:** "Eventually" or "Finally" (indicating that something happens after a delay or a series of events).

### 4. Linguistic Comparison

*   **English:** The closest cognate in structure is not *eventually* (which comes from Latin *eventus*), but rather the phrase **"in the end"** or the German **"letztendlich"** (*letzt* = last, *end* = end, *lich* = ly).
*   **German:** *schließlich* (finally) or *letztendlich*.
*   **Dutch:** *uiteindelijk*.
