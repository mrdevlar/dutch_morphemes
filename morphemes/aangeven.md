From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`aangeven`** is a complex verb formed through **derivational morphology**. It is a classic example of a **separable verb** (*scheidbaar werkwoord*).

Here is the breakdown of its morphemes, their grammatical functions, and their semantic contributions.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into two distinct morphemes:

> **`aan`** + **`geven`**

*   **`aan`**: A verbal particle (functioning as a prefix in the infinitive).
*   **`geven`**: The lexical root (base verb).

---

### 2. Analysis of the Root: `geven`
*   **Morpheme Type:** Free morpheme / Lexical Root.
*   **Part of Speech:** Verb (Strong Verb, Class 3).
*   **Meaning:** "To give."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *geven*, from Proto-Germanic *\*gebaną*, ultimately from the Proto-Indo-European root *\*gʰebʰ-* (to take, to give).
*   **Conjugation:** It is an irregular (strong) verb.
    *   Infinitive: *geven*
    *   Past Tense: *gaf*
    *   Past Participle: *gegeven*

### 3. Analysis of the Prefix: `aan`
*   **Morpheme Type:** Bound morpheme (in this context) / Verbal Particle.
*   **Origin:** Historically a preposition/adverb meaning "on," "at," "to," or "towards."
*   **Function:** In this compound, it acts as a **directional** or **aspectual** prefix. It modifies the core meaning of "giving" by adding a specific directionality or completion.
*   **Syntactic Behavior:** This is the defining feature of the word. In Dutch grammar, `aan` is a **separable prefix**.
    *   **Infinitive:** *aangeven* (stays together).
    *   **Main Clause:** The prefix separates and moves to the end of the sentence.
        *   *Ik **geef** het **aan**.* (I give it up / I report it.)
    *   **Past Participle:** The prefix `aan` remains at the front, and the standard participle marker `ge-` is inserted between the prefix and the stem.
        *   *aangegeven* (Structure: `aan` + `ge` + `geven`).

---

### 4. Semantic Composition (Polysemy)
Linguistically, `aangeven` is a case of **semantic shift** or **idiomatization**. The meaning is not simply the sum of "on" + "give." Instead, the combination has developed specific, distinct meanings (polysemy) depending on context:

1.  **To Report / To Turn In:**
    *   *Logic:* Giving information *to* an authority.
    *   *Example:* *De dief is aangegeven bij de politie.* (The thief was reported to the police.)
2.  **To Indicate / To Point Out:**
    *   *Logic:* Giving a signal *towards* a specific object or direction.
    *   *Example:* *Kunt u de fout in de tekst aangeven?* (Can you point out the error in the text?)
3.  **To Switch On:**
    *   *Logic:* Giving power/energy *to* a device (often used for lights or appliances).
    *   *Example:* *Zet het licht maar aan.* (Turn on the light.)
4.  **To State / To Declare:**
    *   *Logic:* Giving information formally.
    *   *Example:* *Hij gaf zijn naam aan.* (He stated/gave his name.)

### 5. Summary of Linguistic Features

| Feature | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Word Class** | Verb (Transitive) |
| **Morphological Type** | Derivational Compound (Prefix + Root) |
| **Verb Type** | Separable Verb (*Scheidbaar Werkwoord*) |
| **Stress Pattern** | Primary stress falls on the prefix: **`aan`**geven (unlike `ge`ven). |
| **Participle Formation** | Infixation of *ge-*: *aan* + *ge* + *geven* = *aangegeven*. |

### Conclusion
Linguistically, **`aangeven`** demonstrates how Dutch utilizes **prepositional prefixes** to expand the semantic range of a core verb. While the root **`geven`** provides the core action (transfer), the prefix **`aan`** provides the vector (direction/towards a target), resulting in a verb that covers reporting, indicating, and activating.
