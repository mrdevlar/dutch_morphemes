From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`afstuderen`** is a **separable verb** (scheidbaar werkwoord). It is composed of three distinct morphemes that combine to create a specific meaning related to academic completion.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented as follows:

> **`af`** + **`studeer`** + **`en`**

---

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### **A. `af-` (Separable Prefix)**
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix (Prepositional/Adverbial).
*   **Literal Meaning:** "Off," "away," "down," or "from."
*   **Grammatical Function:** In this context, it functions as a **completive aspect marker**.
*   **Semantic Contribution:**
    *   The base verb *studeren* implies an ongoing process (studying).
    *   The prefix *af-* adds the meaning of **finality** or **completion**. It transforms the activity into a bounded event.
    *   Therefore, *afstuderen* does not mean "to study off," but rather "to finish studying" or "to graduate."
*   **Syntactic Behavior:** This is a **separable verb**. In a main clause, the prefix detaches and moves to the end of the sentence.
    *   *Infinitive:* Ik wil **afstuderen**. (I want to graduate.)
    *   *Main Clause:* Ik **studeer** morgen **af**. (I graduate tomorrow.)

#### **B. `studeer-` (Verb Stem)**
*   **Type:** Lexical Root / Verb Stem.
*   **Origin:** Derived from the Latin verb *studere* (to be eager, to apply oneself to), which entered Dutch via Middle Dutch and French (*étudier*).
*   **Meaning:** To study, to engage in academic learning.
*   **Morphology:** This is the core of the word. In Dutch conjugation, this stem remains relatively stable (e.g., *ik studeer*, *jij studeert*, *hij studeerde*). The *-er-* inside the stem is part of the root, not a suffix (unlike in English *teacher*).

#### **C. `-en` (Infinitive Suffix)**
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix.
*   **Function:** Marks the verb as an **infinitive** (the base form of the verb).
*   **Phonology:** In spoken Dutch, this is often reduced to a schwa sound /ə/ or even elided in rapid speech, but it is morphologically distinct.
*   **Note:** In the conjugated forms (like *studeer*), this suffix is dropped. It only appears in the infinitive (*studeren*) and the plural present tense (*wij studeren*).

---

### 3. Linguistic Summary

| Morpheme | Type | Meaning/Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`af-`** | Prefix | Indicates completion / separation (Completive Aspect). |
| **`studeer-`** | Stem | The core action (to study). |
| **`-en`** | Suffix | Marks the infinitive form. |

### 4. Key Linguistic Concepts at Play

**1. Telicity (Boundedness):**
Linguistically, *studeren* is an **atelic** verb (an activity without a natural endpoint; you can study forever). By adding the prefix *af-*, the verb becomes **telic** (an event with a natural endpoint). You cannot "afstuderen" continuously; once you have done it, the action is complete.

**2. Separable Verbs (Scheidbare Werkwoorden):**
This is a unique feature of Germanic languages (Dutch and German). The prefix *af-* is semantically bound to the verb but syntactically separable. This distinguishes it from inseparable prefixes (like *be-* in *bestuderen*), which never move to the end of the sentence.

**3. Lexicalization:**
While *af* and *studeren* are analyzable, the combination *afstuderen* is highly lexicalized. It has a specific cultural meaning ("to graduate") that is not simply the sum of "off" + "study." In English, we use a different root entirely ("graduate"), whereas Dutch modifies the existing verb for "study."
