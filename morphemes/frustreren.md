### 2. Deep Etymological Breakdown (Historical Morphology)
While the Dutch grammatical stem is *frustrer-*, the word itself is a loanword (specifically from French/Latin). If we analyze the internal structure of the stem, we can identify older morphemes that have been fossilized in the Dutch language.

*   **Segmentation:** `frustr` + `er` + `en`

| Morpheme | Origin | Type | Meaning/Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **frustr-** | Latin (*frustra*) | **Root** | "In vain," "by mistake," or "deception." |
| **-er-** | Latin (*-are*) / French (*-er*) | **Derivational Suffix** | Verbalizer (turns the root into a verb). |
| **-en** | Proto-Germanic | **Inflectional Suffix** | Dutch infinitive marker. |

#### Detailed Explanation of Morphemes:

**A. The Root: `frustr-`**
*   **Origin:** Latin *frustra* (adverb meaning "in vain" or "to no purpose").
*   **Semantic Shift:** In Latin, the verb *frustrari* meant "to deceive" or "to disappoint." Over time, the meaning shifted from active deception to the psychological state of being thwarted or disappointed, which is the modern meaning in both Dutch and English.

**B. The Verbalizer: `-er-`**
*   **Origin:** This corresponds to the Latin first-conjugation infinitive ending *-are* or the French infinitive ending *-er*.
*   **Integration:** In Dutch, this is not a productive suffix (you cannot take a random Dutch noun and add *-er* to make a verb, e.g., *tafel* $\neq$ *taferen*). It is a **fossilized suffix** specific to Romance loanwords (e.g., *studeren*, *reageren*, *organiseren*).
*   **Function:** It signals that the word is a verb derived from a foreign root.

**C. The Infinitive Marker: `-en`**
*   **Origin:** Proto-Germanic *-an*.
*   **Function:** This is the standard Dutch ending for the infinitive form of verbs. It is fully productive in Dutch (e.g., *lopen*, *werken*, *zien*).

*   **The Double 'r':** You will notice the word is spelled with a double *r* (`frustreren`).
    *   **Reason:** In Dutch orthography, a short vowel sound (like the *u* in *frustreren*, pronounced /ʏ/) must be followed by a double consonant to indicate that the vowel is short and not long.
    *   **Pronunciation:** The *u* is pronounced as a close-mid front rounded vowel /ʏ/ (similar to the German *ü* or the 'u' in "fur" but rounded).
    *   **Morphophonemic Boundary:** The double *r* sits on the boundary between the root (*frustr-*) and the verbalizer (*-er-*).
        *   *frustr* (ends in r) + *er* (starts with r) = *frustrer*.
*   **Stress:** The stress falls on the syllable containing the verbalizer suffix: *frus-TRER-en*. This is typical for Dutch verbs ending in *-eren*.
