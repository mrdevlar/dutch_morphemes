From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`passen`** is a verb in its infinitive form. It is a **polymorphemic** word, meaning it consists of more than one morpheme (the smallest unit of meaning).

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into two distinct morphemes:

> **`pas`** + **`-en`**

| Morpheme | Type | Status | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`pas-`** | Lexical Root | Free Morpheme | Carries the core semantic meaning ("to fit/match"). |
| **`-en`** | Inflectional Suffix | Bound Morpheme | Marks the word as an infinitive verb. |

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Stem: `pas-`
*   **Category:** Lexical Root (Free Morpheme).
*   **Meaning:** This morpheme carries the primary semantic load. In the context of the verb *passen*, it relates to concepts of **fitting, matching, adjusting, or guarding**.
*   **Phonology & Orthography:**
    *   **Pronunciation:** /pɑs/ (The vowel is short /ɑ/).
    *   **Spelling Rule:** In Dutch orthography, a short vowel in a stressed syllable must be followed by a double consonant if the syllable is closed. However, in the infinitive *passen*, the double 's' is technically part of the stem representation to preserve the short vowel quality before the vowel-initial suffix *-en*. If the stem were written as *pasen*, it would imply a long vowel /aː/ (like in *pazen*). Therefore, the spelling *passen* signals the phonological reality of the stem *pas-*.
*   **Etymology:** The verb *passen* (to fit) derives from Middle Dutch *passen*, which is related to the noun *pas* (measure, fit). It is cognate with the German *passen*. It is distinct from the noun *pas* (step/pace), which comes from French.

#### B. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Category:** Inflectional Suffix (Bound Morpheme).
*   **Function:** This is the standard **infinitive marker** for Dutch verbs.
*   **Grammar:** It indicates that the verb is in its base, non-finite form. It does not indicate tense, person, or number on its own, but it allows the verb to function as a noun (nominalization) or to be used with modal verbs (e.g., *ik wil passen* - "I want to fit").
*   **Conjugation:** This suffix is also used for the **present tense plural** (e.g., *wij passen* - "we fit").

### 3. Morphophonology (Sound Changes)
While the word *passen* itself is regular, the stem `pas-` undergoes predictable phonological changes when other suffixes are added, governed by Dutch morphophonological rules.

*   **Voicing Assimilation:** The stem ends in a voiceless fricative /s/.
    *   **Past Tense:** When adding the past tense suffix *-te* (for weak verbs), the /s/ remains voiceless, but the suffix adds a /t/, creating the cluster /st/.
        *   *Stem:* `pas-` + *Past Suffix* `-te` → **`paste`** (pronounced /pɑstə/).
    *   **Past Participle:** The prefix *ge-* and suffix *-t* are added.
        *   *Prefix* `ge-` + `pas-` + *Suffix* `-t` → **`gepast`**.

### 4. Polysemy and Context
Linguistically, it is important to note that *passen* is polysemous (has multiple meanings) depending on context, though the morphological structure remains the same:

1.  **To fit:** *De schoenen passen niet.* (The shoes do not fit.)
2.  **To match:** *Dat past bij de kleur.* (That matches the color.)
3.  **To watch/guard:** (Usually in the separable compound *oppassen*). *Ik moet op de kinderen passen.* (I have to watch the children.)
4.  **To pay:** (Archaic or dialectal, mostly *betalen* is used now, but *passen* can mean to pay a bill in specific contexts).

### 5. Summary
From a morphological standpoint, **`passen`** is a **weak verb** consisting of a **lexical root** (`pas-`) and an **inflectional infinitive suffix** (`-en`). It is a regular verb in Dutch, meaning its morphological structure follows standard rules for conjugation without requiring stem vowel changes (ablaut) like strong verbs (e.g., *zingen* → *zong*).
