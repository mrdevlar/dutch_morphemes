From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`communiceren`** (to communicate) can be analyzed on two levels: **synchronic** (how it functions in modern Dutch morphology) and **diachronic** (its historical etymological structure).

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes.

### 1. Synchronic Analysis (Modern Dutch Morphology)
In contemporary Dutch, the word is treated as a single lexical unit borrowed from Latin/French. It is not typically analyzed by Dutch speakers as having separable prefixes.

*   **Word:** `communiceren`
*   **Part of Speech:** Verb (Infinitive)
*   **Morphological Structure:** `communic-` + `-eren`

| Morpheme | Type | Function | Explanation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **communic-** | **Root / Stem** | Lexical Meaning | This carries the core semantic meaning of "sharing" or "connecting." In Dutch, this is treated as an indivisible root because the internal Latin structure is no longer productive. |
| **-eren** | **Suffix** | Grammatical Marker | This is the **infinitive suffix** specific to verbs of Latin or French origin in Dutch. It marks the verb as non-finite (the dictionary form). |

**Linguistic Note on `-eren`:**
In Dutch morphology, verbs ending in `-eren` belong to a specific class of **weak verbs** (regular verbs). Unlike native Dutch verbs (e.g., *lopen* -> *loopt*), these verbs do not undergo vowel changes in the past tense (e.g., *communiceren* -> *communiceerde*).

### 2. Diachronic Analysis (Etymology)
To understand the internal composition of the root `communic-`, we must look at its Latin origin (*communicare*). While Dutch speakers do not actively parse these parts, linguists analyze them to understand the word's semantic history.

*   **Latin Source:** *communicare*
*   **Etymological Structure:** `com-` + `mun` + `-ic-` + `-are`

| Morpheme | Origin | Meaning | Explanation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **com-** | Latin Prefix | "Together" / "With" | Indicates a joint action or shared state. (Cognate with English *com-* in *combine*). |
| **mun** | Latin Root | "Common" / "Duty" | Derived from *munus* (duty, gift, service) or *communis* (common). It implies something shared among a group. |
| **-ic-** | Latin Suffix | "Pertaining to" | A suffix used to form adjectives or verbs related to the root. |
| **-are** | Latin Suffix | "To do/make" | The standard infinitive ending for 1st conjugation Latin verbs. |

**Evolution into Dutch:**
1.  **Latin:** *communicare*
2.  **French:** *communiquer* (The `-are` ending shifted to French `-er`).
3.  **Dutch:** *communiceren* (The French `-er` was adapted to the Dutch phonological pattern `-eren`).

### 3. Grammatical Behavior
Understanding the morphemes helps explain the word's grammatical behavior in Dutch sentences:

*   **Infinitive:** `communiceren` (Stem + `-eren`)
*   **Present Tense:** `ik communiceer` (Stem + `-e`)
    *   *Note:* The `-n` is dropped in the first person singular.
*   **Past Participle:** `gecommuniceerd`
    *   *Note:* Unlike verbs with inseparable prefixes (like *be-`, `ver-`), verbs ending in `-eren` **do** typically take the `ge-` prefix in the past participle, though the stress remains on the stem (*com-mu-ni-CEER-d*).
*   **Noun Derivation:** `communicatie`
    *   Here, the suffix changes from the verbal `-eren` to the nominal `-atie` (from Latin *-atio*).

### Summary
*   **Dutch Morphology:** `communic-` (Root) + `-eren` (Infinitive Suffix).
*   **Etymology:** `com-` (Together) + `mun` (Common) + `-ic-` + `-are` (Verb).
*   **Key Takeaway:** While the word is etymologically complex, in modern Dutch linguistics, it is treated as a **monomorphemic stem** (`communic-`) combined with a **derivational/inflectional suffix** (`-eren`). The internal Latin prefix `com-` is **fossilized** and not separable in Dutch grammar.
