From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`stellen`** can be analyzed through **morphology** (the study of word structure). Below is a breakdown of its morphemes, including a synchronic (modern) analysis and a brief diachronic (historical) note.

### 1. Morphemic Segmentation
In modern Dutch, the word is segmented into two primary morphemes:

> **`stel`** + **`-en`**

*(Note: The double 'l' in the spelling is an orthographic feature, explained below.)*

### 2. Detailed Breakdown

#### A. The Root (Lexical Morpheme): `stel-`
*   **Type:** Free/Bound Root (Lexical).
*   **Function:** Carries the core semantic meaning of the word.
*   **Meaning:** "To put," "to place," "to set," or "to establish."
*   **Phonology:** The vowel is a short /ɛ/ (as in *bed*).
*   **Conjugation:** This root appears in various forms:
    *   *Ik **stel*** (I put)
    *   *Hij **stelt*** (He puts)
    *   *Wij **stellen*** (We put)
    *   *Past Tense:* ***Stel**de*

#### B. The Suffix (Inflectional Morpheme): `-en`
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix.
*   **Function:** Marks the **infinitive** form of the verb.
*   **Grammar:** In Dutch, the infinitive is the citation form of the verb (the form found in dictionaries). It does not indicate tense, person, or number on its own; it simply signals "verb."
*   **Comparison:**
    *   *lopen* (to walk)
    *   *werken* (to work)
    *   *stellen* (to put)

---

### 3. Orthographic & Phonological Note: The Double 'L'
You may notice the spelling is **`stell`**en, not *`stel`en*. This is a crucial part of Dutch morphological orthography.

*   **The Rule:** In Dutch, if a stem has a **short vowel** and ends in a **single consonant**, that consonant is doubled when a vowel-initial suffix (like *-en*) is added.
*   **The Purpose:** This doubling preserves the shortness of the vowel.
    *   **`stellen`** (short /ɛ/): To put/place.
    *   **`stelen`** (long /eː/): To steal.
*   **Linguistic Classification:** This is an **orthographic gemination**. It is not a separate morpheme; it is a spelling convention triggered by the phonological properties of the root `stel-`.

### 4. Diachronic Perspective (Etymology)
While the modern analysis treats `stel-` as a single root, a historical linguistic perspective reveals a more complex structure.

*   **Proto-Germanic:** *\*stalljaną*
*   **Breakdown:**
    1.  **Root:** *\*stal-* (related to *staan* / *stand*).
    2.  **Causative Suffix:** *-j-* (This suffix indicated "to make [something] do the action").
    3.  **Infinitive Suffix:** *-an*.
*   **Evolution:** The *-j-* caused the preceding consonant (*l*) to double (gemination) in Old Dutch. Over time, the causative meaning became fossilized.
*   **Modern Status:** In Modern Dutch, the causative suffix is no longer productive. We no longer add *-j-* to verbs to make them causative. Therefore, synchronically, we treat `stell-` as a single lexical unit.
