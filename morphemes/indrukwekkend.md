From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`indrukwekkend`** is a rich example of Germanic compounding and derivation. It is a compound adjective formed from a noun and a present participle.

Here is the morphological breakdown, analysis, and explanation of the word.

### 1. Visual Segmentation
To understand the structure, we can segment the word into its constituent morphemes (the smallest units of meaning):

> **in** - **druk** - **wek** - **k** - **end**

However, for a more functional linguistic analysis, it is best viewed as a compound of two larger units, which are themselves derived:

> **[ indruk ]** + **[ wekkend ]**

### 2. Morpheme-by-Morpheme Analysis

Here is the deep breakdown of the four primary morphemes involved:

#### A. `in-` (Prefix)
*   **Type:** Bound morpheme (Prefix).
*   **Origin:** Preposition *in* (Germanic *in*).
*   **Meaning:** "In," "into," or "inside."
*   **Function:** It indicates direction or location. In this context, it suggests something being pressed *into* the mind or consciousness.

#### B. `druk` (Root)
*   **Type:** Bound morpheme (Root).
*   **Origin:** Verb *drukken* (to press, push, or stamp).
*   **Meaning:** "Press" or "pressure."
*   **Function:** In the noun *indruk*, this root has been nominalized. It refers to the "mark" or "imprint" left by a press.
*   **Semantic Shift:** Physically, it is a stamp; metaphorically, it is a mental "impression."

#### C. `wek` (Root)
*   **Type:** Bound morpheme (Root).
*   **Origin:** Verb *wekken* (to wake, rouse, or arouse).
*   **Meaning:** "Wake" or "stir."
*   **Function:** This is the core action of the adjective. It implies causing something to become active or noticeable.
*   **Note on Spelling:** The root is *wek*. Because the vowel 'e' is short, Dutch orthography requires the consonant to double when a suffix is added (see below).

#### D. `-end` (Suffix)
*   **Type:** Bound morpheme (Suffix).
*   **Origin:** Present participle marker.
*   **Meaning:** Equivalent to the English "-ing" (e.g., *making*, *causing*).
*   **Function:** It turns the verb *wekken* into an adjective (*wekkend* = "causing" or "rousing").
*   **Grammar:** This is a **derivational suffix** that creates an active participle adjective.

### 3. Orthographic Note: The Double 'k'
You may notice the double **`kk`** in the middle of the word (*indrukwekkend*).
*   **Rule:** In Dutch, if a verb stem has a short vowel in a stressed syllable (like *wek*), the final consonant must be doubled when adding a suffix that begins with a vowel (like *-end*).
*   **Reason:** This preserves the short vowel sound. If it were written *wekend*, the 'e' might be pronounced as a long vowel (like in *weken*).
*   **Morphological status:** The second 'k' is not a separate morpheme; it is an orthographic marker required by the phonology of the root `wek` + suffix `-end`.

### 4. Syntactic Structure (Compound Analysis)
Linguistically, the word is formed via **Endocentric Compounding**.

1.  **First Component:** *Indruk* (Noun).
    *   Derived from *in-* + *drukken*.
    *   Meaning: "Impression."
2.  **Second Component:** *Wekkend* (Adjective/Participle).
    *   Derived from *wekken* + *-end*.
    *   Meaning: "Causing" or "Arousing."
3.  **Combination:** *Indruk* + *wekkend*.
    *   Literal translation: "Impression-causing."
    *   Head of the compound: The second element (*wekkend*) determines the part of speech (Adjective).

### 5. Semantic Evolution
The meaning of the word has shifted from the literal to the figurative:

*   **Literal:** "Waking a press." (Nonsensical physically).
*   **Metaphorical:** "Arousing an impression."
*   **Idiomatic:** "Impressive," "striking," or "moving."

This follows a common pattern in Dutch where verbs of perception or creation are combined with nouns to form adjectives describing the *effect* of an action (e.g., *aandachtwekkend* = attention-waking = attention-grabbing).

It is helpful to compare this to the English equivalent to see the difference in morphological strategy:

### Summary
The word **`indrukwekkend`** is a transparent Germanic compound. It literally means **"causing an impression"** (*in* + *press* + *wake* + *-ing*). It is constructed by taking the noun for "impression" (*indruk*) and combining it with the active participle of the verb "to arouse" (*wekkend*), creating an adjective that describes something capable of arousing a strong mental impression.
