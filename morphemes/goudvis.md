From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`goudvis`** (meaning "goldfish") is a classic example of a **nominal compound**.

Here is the morphological breakdown and analysis:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word consists of two distinct morphemes joined together:

> **goud** + **vis**

There is **no interfix** (linking element) between them. In Dutch, compounds are typically written as a single closed word (unlike English, which often uses a space or hyphen, e.g., "gold fish").

---

### 2. Analysis of Morphemes

#### Morpheme A: `goud`
*   **Type:** Free morpheme (can stand alone as an independent word).
*   **Lexical Category:** Noun (*substantive*).
*   **Meaning:** "Gold" (the chemical element/metal).
*   **Function in Compound:** **Modifier**.
    *   In this structure, the noun *goud* functions attributively to describe the head noun. It specifies the *type* or *characteristic* of the fish.
    *   **Semantic Note:** This is a case of **metonymy**. The fish is not made of gold; rather, the name derives from the fish's golden/orange coloration.

#### Morpheme B: `vis`
*   **Type:** Free morpheme.
*   **Lexical Category:** Noun (*substantive*).
*   **Meaning:** "Fish".
*   **Function in Compound:** **Head**.
    *   In Dutch (and Germanic languages generally), the **right-hand element** is the semantic head. This means the compound is a *type* of fish, not a type of gold.
    *   The grammatical properties of the whole word (gender, pluralization) are determined by this morpheme.

---

### 3. Linguistic Features of the Compound

#### A. Endocentric Structure
`Goudvis` is an **endocentric compound**. This means the meaning of the whole word is a hyponym (a specific subtype) of the head.
*   A *goudvis* is a *vis* (fish).
*   It is *not* a *goud* (gold).

#### B. Stress Assignment
In Dutch morphology, compounds typically receive **primary stress on the first element**.
*   **Pronunciation:** /ˈɣʌu̯d.vɪs/
*   **Stress:** **GÓUD**-vis (not *goud-VÍS*).
*   **Linguistic Significance:** This stress pattern distinguishes the compound noun from a noun phrase. If you said *goud vis* with stress on *vis*, it would imply a fish made of gold (a phrase), whereas *GÓUDvis* implies the specific species.

#### C. Pluralization (Inflection)
When pluralizing a Dutch compound noun, the inflectional suffix attaches to the **head** (the last element).
*   **Singular:** *goudvis*
*   **Plural:** *goudvis**sen***
*   **Morphology:** *vis* + *-en* (plural suffix) + *-n* (epenthetic consonant due to phonotactics).
*   The modifier (*goud*) remains unchanged.

#### D. Etymology
*   **`goud`**: Derived from Proto-Germanic **\*gulþą*. Cognate with English *gold* and German *Gold*.
*   **`vis`**: Derived from Proto-Germanic **\*fiskaz*. Cognate with English *fish* and German *Fisch*.
*   The compound itself is a direct semantic translation of the concept, likely solidified in the language when the species (*Carassius auratus*) became popular in Dutch culture.
