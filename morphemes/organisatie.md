From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`organisatie`** (meaning "organization") is a complex noun formed through derivational morphology. It is a loanword that has been fully integrated into the Dutch language.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes, their functions, and their etymological origins.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **organ** + **is** + **atie**

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Root: `organ-`
*   **Type:** Free morpheme (Root)
*   **Meaning:** "Organ," "tool," "instrument," or "functional part."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from the Ancient Greek word **ὄργανον** (*organon*), meaning "tool" or "instrument."
*   **Function:** This is the semantic core of the word. It provides the base meaning related to structure, function, or a body part. In Dutch, this root also exists as the standalone noun *orgaan* (organ).

#### B. The Verbalizer: `-is-`
*   **Type:** Bound morpheme (Derivational Suffix)
*   **Meaning:** "To make," "to do," or "to become."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from the Ancient Greek verbal suffix **-ίζειν** (*-izein*).
*   **Function:** This morpheme turns the noun root (*organ*) into a verb stem.
    *   In Dutch, when combined with the infinitive ending *-eren*, it creates the verb **`organiseren`** (to organize).
    *   In the noun form *organisatie*, this *-is-* remains as part of the stem to which the nominal suffix attaches.
*   **Linguistic Note:** In synchronic (modern) Dutch analysis, *-is-* is often viewed as part of a larger suffix complex *-isatie*, but etymologically and structurally, it acts as the bridge between the root and the action.

#### C. The Nominalizer: `-atie`
*   **Type:** Bound morpheme (Derivational Suffix)
*   **Meaning:** "The action of," "the process of," or "the result of."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from the Latin suffix **-atio** (genitive *-ationis*), which indicates an action or process.
*   **Function:** This suffix nominalizes the verb stem. It turns the action of "organizing" (*organiseren*) into the concept or entity of "organization" (*organisatie*).
*   **Phonology:** In Dutch, this is pronounced as a schwa followed by a voiceless alveolar fricative and a long 'a' sound: /ˈɔr.ɡa.niˌzaː.tsi/.

### 3. Derivational History (Diachronic View)
The formation of this word follows a specific path of linguistic borrowing and adaptation:

1.  **Greek:** *organon* (tool) + *-izein* (to make) $\rightarrow$ *organizein* (to put in order).
2.  **Latin:** Adapted as *organizo* + *-atio* $\rightarrow$ *organizatio*.
3.  **French:** Evolved into *organisation*.
4.  **Dutch:** Borrowed from French as *organisatie*.

### 4. Synchronic vs. Diachronic Analysis
There is a slight nuance in how linguists analyze this word depending on whether they look at the history (diachronic) or the current system (synchronic):

*   **Diachronic (Historical):** As explained above, it is clearly **organ** + **is** + **atie**.
*   **Synchronic (Modern Dutch):** In modern Dutch morphology, speakers often perceive **-isatie** as a single, unbreakable suffix attached to the root *organ-*. This is because *-isatie* is a productive suffix in Dutch used for many loanwords (e.g., *realisatie*, *visualisatie*, *digitalisatie*).
    *   However, the verb form *organiseren* reveals the internal structure, confirming that *-is-* belongs to the verb stem and *-atie* is the noun ending.

### 5. Orthographic Note (Spelling)
A notable linguistic feature of this word in Dutch is the use of the letter **`s`** instead of **`z`**.
*   **English:** *Organiz**a**tion* (uses 'z').
*   **Dutch:** *Organis**a**tie* (uses 's').

This is due to Dutch orthographic rules regarding the distribution of /s/ and /z/ sounds. In Dutch, the sound /z/ is generally not written as 'z' when it follows a vowel in this specific morphological context (often derived from French spelling conventions where 's' represents the /z/ sound between vowels).
