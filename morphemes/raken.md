From a linguistic (morphological) perspective, the Dutch word **`raken`** is a simple verb consisting of two morphemes. It is not a compound word, nor does it contain derivational prefixes or suffixes.

Here is the breakdown:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
**`raak`** + **`-en`**

### 2. Detailed Explanation of Morphemes

#### A. `raak-` (The Root / Stem)
*   **Type:** Lexical Morpheme (Free/Bound depending on context).
*   **Function:** This is the **verbal stem**. It carries the core semantic meaning (lexical content) of the word.
*   **Meaning:** To hit, to touch, to reach, or to affect.
*   **Linguistic Note:**
    *   In the context of the infinitive *raken*, `raak` is a **bound morpheme** because it cannot stand alone as a verb in Dutch (you cannot say "Ik raak" to mean "I hit" without conjugation, though the 1st person singular present tense is identical to the stem).
    *   However, `raak` can function as a **free morpheme** in other contexts. For example, as an adjective (*"Dat is raak"* = "That is accurate/hit") or as an interjection (*"Raak!"* = "Hit!").
    *   **Etymology:** It stems from Old Dutch *rāken* and Proto-Germanic *\*rēkaną*. It is cognate with the English verb **reach** and the German dialectal *raken*.

#### B. `-en` (The Inflectional Suffix)
*   **Type:** Grammatical Morpheme (Bound).
*   **Function:** This is the **infinitive marker**.
*   **Meaning:** It indicates that the verb is in its base, unconjugated form (the dictionary form).
*   **Linguistic Note:**
    *   In Dutch morphology, `-en` is the standard suffix for the infinitive of regular (weak) verbs.
    *   Phonetically, in many dialects and colloquial speech, this is often reduced to just `-n` (e.g., *raken* pronounced as *raakn*), but morphologically it remains the suffix `-en`.
    *   This suffix is **inflectional**, meaning it changes the grammatical form of the word (tense/mood) without changing its core part of speech or meaning.

### 4. Morphological Process
The formation of this word is an example of **inflection**.
*   **Base:** The lexeme is *RAKEN*.
*   **Stem:** *raak-*
*   **Process:** The infinitive suffix *-en* is attached to the stem to create the citation form used in dictionaries.

**Conjugation Context:**
To see the stem `raak-` in action, look at how the suffix changes in the present tense:
*   *Ik raak* (Stem + Null suffix for 1st person singular)
*   *Jij raakt* (Stem + `-t` for 2nd person singular)
*   *Wij raken* (Stem + `-en` for 1st person plural)

In all these forms, `raak-` remains the invariant lexical core.
