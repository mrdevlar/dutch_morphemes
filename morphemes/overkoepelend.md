From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`overkoepelend`** is a complex word formed through derivation and compounding. It functions primarily as an adjective (derived from a present participle).

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation of the morphemes.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **over** + **koepel** + **end**

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. `over-` (Prefix)
*   **Type:** Separable/Inseparable Prefix (in this specific context, it functions as an **inseparable** prefix).
*   **Etymology:** From Proto-Germanic *\*uber*, cognate with English *over* and German *über*.
*   **Function:** It modifies the meaning of the root verb. In this construction, it adds a sense of "superordination," "covering from above," or "transcending."
*   **Linguistic Note:** The prefix *over-* is "two-way" in Dutch. In some verbs (e.g., *overstappen* - to transfer), it is separable. In others (e.g., *overkomen* - to happen), it is inseparable. In *overkoepelen*, it is treated as inseparable (stress falls on the root, not the prefix).

#### B. `koepel` (Root / Lexeme)
*   **Type:** Noun Root (used here as the base for a denominal verb).
*   **Meaning:** "Dome" or "cupola" (the rounded roof of a building).
*   **Etymology:** Borrowed from Latin *cuppula* (diminutive of *cupa*, meaning "tub" or "vat"), likely entering Dutch via French (*coupole*) or Italian (*cupola*).
*   **Function:** This is the semantic core of the word. It provides the imagery of a structure that covers and protects everything underneath it.
*   **Derivation:** The noun *koepel* is converted into a verb stem (*koepelen* - to dome/cover with a dome), which is then modified by the prefix.

#### C. `-end` (Suffix)
*   **Type:** Present Participle Suffix / Adjectival Suffix.
*   **Function:** This suffix turns the verb *overkoepelen* (to umbrella/overarch) into an adjective. It corresponds to the English *-ing* suffix.
*   **Grammar:** In Dutch, the present participle is formed by taking the verb stem (infinitive minus *-en*) and adding *-end*.
    *   Verb: *overkoepel**en***
    *   Stem: *overkoepel-*
    *   Adjective: *overkoepel**end***
*   **Usage:** It describes something that *is currently performing* the action of covering or encompassing.

### 3. Semantic Composition (Meaning Construction)
The meaning of the word is constructed metaphorically through the combination of these morphemes:

1.  **Literal:** "Over-dome-ing."
2.  **Metaphorical:** Just as a physical dome covers a building, an "overkoepelend" entity covers a range of sub-entities.
3.  **Current Meaning:** "Umbrella," "Overarching," "Supervisory," or "Comprehensive."
    *   *Example:* **Een overkoepelende organisatie** (An umbrella organization).

### 4. Syntactic and Grammatical Context
*   **Word Class:** Adjective (derived from a verb).
*   **Inflection:** As an adjective, it can be inflected for gender and number when used attributively (before a noun).
    *   *Neuter singular:* Een overkoepelend **orgaan** (no -e).
    *   *Common/Plural:* Een overkoepelend**e** organisatie (with -e).
*   **Stress Pattern:** The primary stress falls on the root syllable: *over-KOE-pel-end*. This confirms that *over-* is acting as an inseparable prefix in this specific lexical item.

### 5. Cognates and Comparisons
*   **English:** There is no direct single-word cognate. The closest functional equivalent is **"umbrella"** (used as an adjective, e.g., *umbrella organization*) or **"overarching."**
