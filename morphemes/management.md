From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **"management"** is a fascinating case because it is a **loanword** (specifically an Anglicism) that has been integrated into the Dutch morphological system.

While it looks identical to the English word, its analysis requires distinguishing between its **etymological structure** (where it came from) and its **synchronic structure** (how it functions in modern Dutch).

Here is the morphological breakdown:

### 1. Segmentation
**`manage`** + **`-ment`**

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Root: `manage`
*   **Type:** Bound/Free Root (depending on analysis).
*   **Origin:** English (ultimately from Italian *maneggiare* "to handle," via French *manège*).
*   **Meaning:** To handle, direct, control, or organize.
*   **Status in Dutch:**
    *   Historically, this was not a Dutch morpheme.
    *   However, in modern business Dutch, the verb **"managen"** has been created. Therefore, `manage` is increasingly treated as a **Dutch verb stem** (e.g., *hij managt*, *ge-manageerd*).
    *   In the noun "management," it functions as the semantic core indicating the *action* or *agent*.

#### B. The Suffix: `-ment`
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix.
*   **Origin:** French *-ment* (from Latin *-mentum*).
*   **Function:** **Nominalizer**. It turns a verb (action) into a noun (the result, process, or collective body of that action).
*   **Status in Dutch:**
    *   This is a **highly productive** suffix in Dutch. It is not limited to loanwords; it appears in native or fully integrated words like *argument*, *document*, *moment*, *instrument*, and *element*.
    *   Because `-ment` is a recognized Dutch suffix, Dutch speakers intuitively understand the word structure even if they don't use the verb *managen*.

### 3. Linguistic Nuances

#### Loanword Integration (Anglicism)
Strictly speaking, "management" is a **borrowed word**. In early stages of borrowing, the word is treated as a single, unanalyzable unit (monomorphemic). However, because the suffix `-ment` is so common in Dutch, the word is **transparently analyzed** by speakers. They perceive it as "the thing related to managing."

#### Inflection (Pluralization)
In Dutch morphology, nouns must be inflected for number.
*   **Singular:** *management*
*   **Plural:** *managements*
    *   Here, Dutch adds the plural suffix **`-s`** to the end of the loanword.
    *   This proves that the word is fully integrated into the Dutch grammatical system, even if the root is foreign.

#### Pronunciation
Linguistically, the word has undergone **phonological adaptation**:
*   **English:** /ˈmæn.ɪ.dʒmənt/ (with a "flat A" and a soft 'g' /dʒ/).
*   **Dutch:** Often /mɛˈnɑːʒ.mənt/ or /mɛˈnɪ.dʒmənt/.
    *   The stress often shifts to the second syllable in Dutch (*ma-NA-ge-ment*), whereas English stresses the first (*MA-nage-ment*).
    *   The 'g' is often pronounced as a hard /g/ or a soft /ʒ/ depending on the speaker's level of anglicization.

### Conclusion
In Dutch linguistics, **"management"** is best described as a **loan noun** composed of a **foreign root** (`manage`) and a **productive Dutch suffix** (`-ment`). While it retains its English spelling, it follows Dutch rules for pluralization (`-s`) and stress patterns, making it a fully integrated part of the modern Dutch lexicon.
