From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`beslissing`** (meaning "decision") can be broken down into three distinct morphemes.

Here is the morphological segmentation:

### **`be`** + **`slis`** + **`ing`**

Below is a detailed explanation of each component, including their grammatical function, etymology, and the orthographic rules that affect their spelling.

---

### 1. The Prefix: `be-`
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix (Bound Morpheme).
*   **Function:** In Dutch, the prefix *be-* is highly productive. It typically transforms a noun or an intransitive verb into a transitive verb. It often implies "covering," "affecting," or "completing" an action.
*   **In this word:** It attaches to the root *slis* to create the verb *beslissen* ("to decide" or "to settle"). It adds the sense of bringing a matter to a close or affecting the outcome of a situation.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from the Proto-Germanic *\*bi-*, related to the English prefix *be-* (as in *befriend* or *besmirch*) and the German *be-*.

### 2. The Root: `slis`
*   **Type:** Lexical Root (Bound Morpheme).
*   **Function:** This is the core semantic unit of the word. It carries the primary meaning.
*   **Status:** In modern Dutch, *slis* is a **bound root**, meaning it does not exist as a standalone word. You cannot say "Ik slis" (I decide/close). It only appears within derived words like *beslissen* or *beslissing*.
*   **Etymology:** Historically, this root is related to the verb *sluiten* ("to close" or "to shut"). The semantic shift moved from "closing" a matter to "settling" or "deciding" it.
    *   *Note on Spelling:* The root ends in the consonant **`s`**.

### 3. The Suffix: `-ing`
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix (Nominalizer).
*   **Function:** This suffix converts a verb into a noun (specifically, a deverbal noun). It indicates the **action** of the verb or the **result** of that action.
*   **In this word:** It turns the verb *beslissen* (to decide) into the noun *beslissing* (the decision).
*   **Etymology:** Common Germanic suffix (cognate with English *-ing* as in *building* or *finding*, and German *-ung* as in *Entscheidung*).

---

### Orthographic Note: The Double "S"
You may notice that the word is spelled with a double **`ss`** (`besliss`ing), but the root is analyzed as `slis`. Why is there an extra `s`?

This is due to a Dutch orthographic rule regarding **vowel length**:
1.  The root vowel **`i`** in *slis* is a **short vowel**.
2.  When a suffix starting with a vowel (like *-ing*) is added to a root ending in a single consonant after a short vowel, the consonant must be **doubled** to preserve the short sound of the vowel.
3.  **Calculation:** *slis* (short i) + *-ing* = *slissing* (to keep the 'i' short).
4.  **Add Prefix:** *be-* + *slissing* = **`beslissing`**.

If the spelling were *besising*, the 'i' would be read as a long vowel (like in *bieten*), which would change the pronunciation and the etymological link to the root.

### Comparative Linguistics
*   **English:** The cognate is not *beslissing*, but rather **decision**. English uses the Latin root *decidere* (to cut off), whereas Dutch uses the Germanic root *slis* (to close).
*   **German:** The cognate is **Entscheidung**. It follows a similar structure: *ent-* (prefix) + *scheid* (root, related to *scheiden* "to separate") + *-ung* (suffix). Both Dutch and German use a prefix + root + nominalizer structure to form this concept.
