From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`stilstaan`** is a classic example of a **separable compound verb** (*scheidbaar werkwoord*).

Here is the morphological, syntactic, and semantic breakdown of the word.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word consists of two distinct free morphemes (roots that can stand alone as words):

> **`stil`** + **`staan`**

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### Morpheme A: `stil`
*   **Part of Speech:** Adjective / Adverb.
*   **Meaning:** "Still," "quiet," "motionless," or "calm."
*   **Function:** In this compound, it functions as an **adverbial modifier**. It modifies the action of the verb to indicate the *manner* in which the standing occurs.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *stil*, from Old Dutch *stil*. It is cognate with the English word **still** and the German word **still**.
*   **Proto-Germanic Root:** *\*stilla-* (quiet, still).

#### Morpheme B: `staan`
*   **Part of Speech:** Verb (Infinitive).
*   **Meaning:** "To stand."
*   **Function:** This is the **head** of the compound. It carries the primary grammatical weight (tense, person, number) and the core lexical meaning of the action.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *staen*, from Old Dutch *stān*. It is cognate with the English word **stand** and the German word **stehen**.
*   **Proto-Germanic Root:** *\*stāną* (to stand).

### 3. Syntactic Behavior (The Separable Verb)
Linguistically, the most important feature of `stilstaan` is its behavior in sentence structure. Unlike English compounds (e.g., "standstill"), Dutch separable verbs split apart when conjugated in a main clause.

*   **Infinitive Form:** `stilstaan` (e.g., *Ik wil **stilstaan**.* / I want to stand still.)
*   **Conjugated Form:** The prefix `stil` moves to the end of the clause, and `staan` is conjugated.
    *   *Ik **sta** **stil**.* (I stand still.)
    *   *Jij **staat** **stil**.* (You stand still.)
*   **Stress Pattern:** In the infinitive form, the primary stress falls on the first morpheme: **`STIL`**staan.

### 4. Semantic Composition
The meaning of the compound is **endocentric**, meaning the meaning of the whole is a specific type of the head word (`staan`).

*   **Literal Meaning:** To remain in a standing position without moving.
*   **Figurative/Idiomatic Meanings:**
    1.  **To stop/pause:** Often used for traffic or machinery (e.g., *De trein staat stil* / The train is stopped).
    2.  **To reflect/dwell:** Used metaphorically with the preposition *bij* (e.g., *Bij dit punt wil ik even stilstaan* / I would like to pause/dwell on this point for a moment).

### Conclusion
Linguistically, `stilstaan` is a **verb-adverb compound**. It demonstrates the Germanic tendency to create new verbs by combining a directional or manner-based particle/adjective with a base verb. Its status as a **separable verb** is a defining characteristic of Dutch syntax, distinguishing it from English, where the equivalent concept ("standstill") is usually a noun or requires a phrasal construction ("stand still").
