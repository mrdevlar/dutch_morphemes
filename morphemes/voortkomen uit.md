### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. `voort-` (The Directional Particle)
*   **Classification:** In Dutch morphology, this is analyzed as a **separable prefix** (scheidbaar voorvoegsel) or a verbal particle.
*   **Etymology:** It is historically derived from the preposition/adverb *voor* (before/for) combined with a directional suffix *-t*. This *-t* indicates movement away from a reference point or continuation. It is cognate with the English "forth" (as in *set forth*).
*   **Function:** It modifies the verb *komen* to indicate a specific direction of movement: not just "coming," but "coming forth" or "moving forward/out."
*   **Distinction:** It is crucial to distinguish this from *voor-* (without the *t*).
    *   *Voorkomen* (voor + komen) = To prevent or to occur.
    *   *Voortkomen* (voort + komen) = To originate or stem from.

#### B. `-komen` (The Verb Stem)
*   **Root:** `kom-`
*   **Suffix:** `-en` (Infinitive marker).
*   **Classification:** Strong verb (Class 4).
*   **Function:** Provides the core semantic action of motion or arrival.
*   **Conjugation:** In the present tense, the stem changes slightly (e.g., *ik kom*, *jij komt*).

#### C. `uit` (The Preposition)
*   **Classification:** Preposition (voorzetsel).
*   **Function:** It governs the object that follows (the source). It completes the semantic meaning of the verb.
*   **Syntactic Role:** In Dutch grammar, `voortkomen` is often classified as a **prepositional verb** (*voorzetselwerkwoord*) when used with `uit`. The preposition is "inseparable" from the meaning of the verb in this specific context, even though the verb itself is separable.

### 3. Syntactic Behavior (Separable Verbs)

The verb **`voortkomen`** is a **separable verb** (*scheidbaar werkwoord*). This means that in a main clause, the prefix `voort` splits off and moves to the end of the sentence.

*   **Infinitive:** `voortkomen`
*   **Main Clause:** `Hij komt [uit de school] **voort**.` (He originates from the school.)
*   **Subordinate Clause:** `...dat hij **voortkomt** uit de school.` (...that he originates from the school.)

However, when combined with the preposition `uit`, the phrase functions as a unit meaning "to originate from."

*   **Example:** `Dit probleem komt voort uit een misverstand.`
    *   *Literal:* This problem comes forth out of a misunderstanding.
    *   *Idiomatic:* This problem stems from a misunderstanding.

### 4. Semantic Composition (How meaning is built)

The meaning of the phrase is compositional but idiomatic:

1.  **`komen`** (Come) + **`voort`** (Forth) = To emerge, to proceed.
2.  **`uit`** (Out of) = Indicates the source or origin.
3.  **Combined:** To emerge *from* a source.

In modern Dutch, `voortkomen uit` is a fixed collocation used to describe **lineage, causality, or derivation**. It is more formal than simply saying `komen uit` (which usually refers to physical location, e.g., *Ik kom uit Nederland* = I come from the Netherlands). `Voortkomen uit` implies a logical or genealogical connection.

### 5. Summary of Linguistic Features

*   **Word Class:** Prepositional Verb Phrase.
*   **Morphology:** Agglutinative tendencies (prefix + root + suffix) within the verb, combined with a preposition.
*   **Syntax:** Separable verb structure (`voort` moves to clause end).
*   **Register:** Formal / Written Dutch.
*   **False Friend Warning:** Do not confuse with `voorkomen` (to prevent/occur). The presence of the **`t`** in `voort` changes the etymology and meaning entirely.

### Conclusion
Linguistically, "`voortkomen uit`" is a complex predicate where the directional particle **`voort`** modifies the motion verb **`komen`**, and the preposition **`uit`** specifies the origin. Together, they form a lexicalized unit meaning "to originate from."
