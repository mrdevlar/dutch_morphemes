From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`andere`** can be analyzed as a morphologically complex word consisting of two distinct morphemes. It is an inflected form of the adjective *ander* ("other").

Here is the breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation

| Morpheme | Form | Type | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Root** | `ander-` | Free / Lexical | Carries the core semantic meaning ("other"). |
| **Suffix** | `-e` | Bound / Inflectional | Marks grammatical agreement (gender, number, definiteness). |

**Total Morphemes:** 2

---

### 2. Analysis of the Root: `ander-`

*   **Lexical Category:** Adjective (can also function as a pronoun).
*   **Semantic Meaning:** "Other," "different," "another."
*   **Etymology:**
    *   **Old Dutch:** *ander*.
    *   **Proto-Germanic:** *\*antharaz*.
    *   **Proto-Indo-European (PIE):** Reconstructed as *\*h₂ent-ros* (related to the root *\*h₂ent-*, meaning "front" or "forehead," implying "the other side" or "opposite").
    *   **Cognates:** English *other*, German *ander*, Old Norse *annarr*, Latin *alter* (distantly related).
*   **Morphological Status:** This is the **stem** of the word. In isolation (e.g., after the indefinite article *een* with a neuter noun), it appears as *ander* (e.g., *een ander idee*).

### 3. Analysis of the Suffix: `-e`

*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix (specifically, adjectival inflection).
*   **Function:** In Dutch grammar, adjectives must agree with the noun they modify regarding **gender**, **number**, and **definiteness**. This is known as *adjectival inflection*.
*   **The "E-ending" Rule:** The suffix *-e* is added to the adjective stem in the following contexts:
    1.  **Plural:** *de andere mensen* (the other people).
    2.  **Common Gender (De-words) Singular:** *de andere man* (the other man).
    3.  **Neuter Gender (Het-words) Plural:** *de andere boeken* (the other books).
    4.  **Neuter Gender Singular (Definite):** *het andere kind* (the other child).
*   **The "Zero-Suffix" Context:** The *-e* is **omitted** only when the noun is **singular, neuter, and indefinite** (preceded by *een* or no article).
    *   *Example:* *een ander kind* (an other child) vs. *het andere kind* (the other child).

### 4. Syntactic and Morphological Context

From a morphosyntactic perspective, *andere* represents **weak inflection**.

*   **Strong Inflection:** Occurs when there is no determiner (article) preceding the adjective (e.g., *andere mensen* — "other people"). Even here, the *-e* is present because of the plural nature.
*   **Weak Inflection:** Occurs when a definite determiner (like *de*, *het*, *deze*, *die*) precedes the adjective. The determiner carries the grammatical weight, and the adjective takes the default *-e* ending.

In the word *andere*, the presence of the *-e* indicates that the noun it modifies is either plural, definite, or common gender.

### 5. Summary

*   **Word:** `andere`
*   **Structure:** `[ander]` (Root) + `[e]` (Inflectional Suffix)
*   **Meaning:** "Other" (inflected form).
*   **Linguistic Note:** The word is a classic example of Dutch **agreement morphology**. The root provides the lexical meaning, while the suffix provides the grammatical information required to link the adjective to the specific noun phrase it modifies.
