From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`overgedragen`** is the **past participle** (*voltooid deelwoord*) of the separable verb **`overdragen`** (to transfer, to hand over, to transmit).

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation of its constituent morphemes.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into four distinct morphemes:

> **over** + **ge** + **draag** + **en**

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### **A. `over` (Prefix / Separable Particle)**
*   **Type:** Separable Prefix (*scheidbaar voorvoegsel*).
*   **Etymology:** From Proto-Germanic *\*uber*, related to English *over* and German *über*.
*   **Function:** It modifies the semantic meaning of the root verb.
    *   The root *dragen* means "to carry" or "to bear."
    *   The prefix *over* adds the sense of "across," "beyond," or "from one side to another."
    *   Combined, *overdragen* implies "carrying something across" (transferring ownership, rights, or data).
*   **Syntax:** In Dutch, this is a **separable verb**. In the infinitive, it is written as one word (*overdragen*), but in main clauses, the prefix splits off (*Ik draag het over*). However, in the past participle, the prefix remains attached to the front of the word.

#### **B. `ge` (Infix / Participle Marker)**
*   **Type:** Derivational/Inflectional Infix (*deelwoordvoegsel*).
*   **Function:** This is the standard marker for the **past participle** in Dutch.
*   **Morphophonological Rule:** This is the most linguistically interesting part of this word.
    *   For regular (weak) verbs, *ge-* is placed at the very beginning (e.g., *maken* $\rightarrow$ *gemaakt*).
    *   For **separable verbs** (like *overdragen*), the *ge-* is inserted **between** the prefix and the stem.
    *   Therefore: *over* + *ge* + *dragen* $\rightarrow$ *overgedragen*.
    *   *Note:* If the prefix is inseparable (e.g., *ver* in *verkopen*), the *ge-* is omitted entirely (*verkocht*).

#### **C. `draag` (Root / Stem)**
*   **Type:** Lexical Root (*stam*).
*   **Etymology:** From Proto-Germanic *\*dragana*, related to English *drag* and German *tragen*.
*   **Verb Class:** This is a **Strong Verb** (*sterk werkwoord*).
    *   Strong verbs form their past tense and participle through vowel gradation (ablaut) rather than just adding a dental suffix (*-t* or *-d*).
    *   **Infinitive:** *dragen* (vowel: *aa*)
    *   **Past Tense:** *droeg* (vowel: *oe*)
    *   **Past Participle:** *gedragen* (vowel: *aa*)
*   **Spelling:** The stem retains the long vowel *aa* from the infinitive, followed by the voiced consonant *g*.

#### **D. `en` (Suffix)**
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix (*uitgang*).
*   **Function:** This suffix marks the **past participle form for strong verbs**.
*   **Contrast:**
    *   **Weak verbs** (regular) typically take *-t* or *-d* (e.g., *werken* $\rightarrow$ *gewerkt*).
    *   **Strong verbs** (irregular) typically take *-en* (e.g., *zien* $\rightarrow$ *gezien*, *dragen* $\rightarrow$ *gedragen*).
*   **Note:** While *en* is also the infinitive ending, in this specific context (following the *ge-* and the stem), it functions specifically as the participle ending for strong verbs.

### 4. Linguistic Context & Usage
*   **Grammatical Category:** Past Participle (*Voltooid Deelwoord*).
*   **Usage:** Used in the perfect tenses (*Ik heb het overgedragen* - I have transferred it) or as an adjective (*een overgedragen ziekte* - a transmitted disease).
*   **Word Order Rule:** The placement of *ge-* is a classic example of Dutch morphological rules regarding **separable compound verbs**. The structure follows the formula:
    $$ \text{Prefix} + \text{ge} + \text{Stem} + \text{Participle Suffix} $$

This structure distinguishes Dutch from English, where the prefix usually remains attached to the participle (e.g., *transfer* $\rightarrow$ *transferred*, not *trans-ferred* with an infix).
