From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`doorwerken`** is a complex verb formed through derivation and inflection. It is a classic example of a **separable compound verb** (*scheidbaar werkwoord*).

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation of its components.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **door** + **werk** + **-en**

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. `door` (Prefix / Verbal Particle)
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix (in the infinitive form) / Separable Particle (in conjugated forms).
*   **Origin:** Preposition (*door* = "through", "by", "during").
*   **Function:** In this context, it acts as a **verbal particle** that modifies the meaning of the root verb. It adds an aspectual meaning to the action.
*   **Semantics:** It conveys the idea of **continuation**, **penetration**, or **completion**.
    *   *Continuation:* Continuing to work despite an interruption or into late hours.
    *   *Penetration:* Working through a specific problem or obstacle until it is resolved.
*   **Linguistic Note:** In Dutch morphology, this is a **separable prefix**. While it appears attached in the infinitive (`doorwerken`), it separates from the stem in main clause conjugations (e.g., *Ik werk **door***).

#### B. `werk` (Root / Stem)
*   **Type:** Lexical Root (Free Morpheme).
*   **Origin:** Old Dutch *werch*, related to English *work* and German *Werk*.
*   **Function:** This is the semantic core of the word. It carries the primary lexical meaning of the action.
*   **Semantics:** "To work," "labor," or "to exert effort."
*   **Morphology:** This is the **stem** of the verb. When conjugated, this stem remains constant (e.g., *werk*, *werkte*, *gewerkt*).

#### C. `-en` (Inflectional Suffix)
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix (Bound Morpheme).
*   **Function:** Marks the **infinitive** form of the verb.
*   **Semantics:** It carries no lexical meaning itself; its sole purpose is grammatical. It indicates that the verb is in its base, unconjugated form (similar to the English "to" in "to work," though Dutch uses the suffix instead of a particle).
*   **Phonology:** In this specific word, the `-en` is often pronounced as a schwa sound /ə/ or is elided in rapid speech (*werk'n*).

### 3. Syntactic Behavior (The "Separable" Rule)
From a syntactic perspective, `doorwerken` belongs to a specific class of Dutch verbs known as **scheidbare werkwoorden** (separable verbs). This is crucial for understanding how the morphemes interact in a sentence.

*   **Infinitive:** The morphemes are fused.
    *   *Ik wil **doorwerken**.* (I want to work through/continue working.)
*   **Main Clause (Present Tense):** The prefix `door` separates and moves to the end of the clause.
    *   *Ik **werk** vandaag **door**.* (I work through today.)
    *   *Structure:* Subject + Conjugated Stem + ... + Separated Particle.
*   **Past Participle:** The prefix `door` remains attached, but the participial marker `ge-` is inserted between the prefix and the stem.
    *   *Ik heb **doorgewerkt**.* (I have worked through.)
    *   *Structure:* `door` + `ge` + `werk` + `t`.

### 4. Semantic Composition
The meaning of the whole is not merely the sum of its parts, but a specific idiomatic combination:

*   **Literal:** "Work through."
*   **Idiomatic Usage:**
    1.  **Overtime:** To continue working past normal hours (e.g., *Ik moet vanavond doorwerken* = I have to work late tonight).
    2.  **Persistence:** To work until a task is finished or a problem is solved (e.g., *We moeten dit probleem doorwerken* = We need to work through this problem).
