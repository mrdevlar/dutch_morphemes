From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch verb **`krijgen`** (meaning "to get," "to receive," or "to obtain") can be analyzed through **synchronic morphology** (how the word is structured in modern Dutch) and **diachronic morphology** (its historical etymology).

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes.

### 1. Synchronic Morphological Breakdown
In modern Dutch, the word is composed of two distinct morphemes:

| Morpheme | Segment | Type | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Root** | `krijg-` | **Lexical Morpheme** | Carries the core semantic meaning ("to get/receive"). |
| **2. Suffix** | `-en` | **Inflectional Morpheme** | Marks the verb as the **infinitive** form. |

**Structure:** `[krijg] + [en]`

#### Detailed Explanation:

*   **The Root (`krijg-`):**
    *   This is a **bound root** in this specific context. While it can stand alone as an imperative command (*"Krijg!"* — "Get!"), in the infinitive form, it is the stem to which grammatical endings are attached.
    *   It is a **strong verb stem**. This means it does not form its past tense by adding *-te* or *-de* (weak verbs), but by changing its internal vowel (ablaut).
    *   **Phonology:** The grapheme `ij` represents the diphthong /ɛi/. The `g` represents a voiced velar/uvular fricative /ɣ/ (in standard Dutch).

*   **The Suffix (`-en`):**
    *   This is the standard **infinitive marker** for Dutch verbs.
    *   It is a **grammatical morpheme** because it does not add lexical meaning but indicates the grammatical form of the word (non-finite verb).
    *   *Note:* In Dutch, `-en` is also the plural marker for nouns (e.g., *hond* → *honden*). However, in the context of *krijgen*, it is unambiguously verbal.

### 2. Diachronic Perspective (Etymology)
To fully understand the morphology, it is helpful to look at the historical layers of the root `krijg-`.

*   **Proto-Germanic Origin:** The word derives from the Proto-Germanic verb **\*krijaną**.
*   **Semantic Shift:**
    *   Originally, the root was associated with **"striving," "fighting," or "warring."**
    *   This is cognate with the German noun *Krieg* (war) and the verb *kriegen* (which in German can mean "to get" but historically relates to conflict/striving).
    *   Over time, the meaning shifted from "to strive for" to "to succeed in obtaining" to simply "to receive."
*   **Internal Structure:** Historically, the root may have contained a suffix *-jan-* (a causative or iterative suffix in Proto-Germanic), but in Modern Dutch, this is no longer analyzable. The root `krijg-` is now **monomorphemic** (it cannot be broken down further within the current language system).

### 3. Morphophonological Behavior (Conjugation)
Because `krijgen` is a **strong verb**, the root morpheme `krijg-` undergoes vowel changes (ablaut) depending on the tense. This demonstrates that the root is stable, but its phonological realization changes.

*   **Infinitive:** `krijg` + `en` (Present stem: *krijg-*)
*   **Past Singular:** `kreeg` (Vowel change: *ij* → *ee*)
*   **Past Plural:** `kregen` (Vowel change: *ij* → *e*)
*   **Past Participle:** `ge` + `kreg` + `en` (Prefix *ge-* + Root *kreg-* + Suffix *-en*)

### Summary
From a strictly modern linguistic perspective, **`krijgen`** is a **bimorphemic** word consisting of a **lexical root** (`krijg-`) and an **inflectional suffix** (`-en`). While the root has a complex history related to "striving" or "war," it functions today as an indivisible unit of meaning within the Dutch verb system.
