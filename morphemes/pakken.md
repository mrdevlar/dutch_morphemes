From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`pakken`** is a verb in its infinitive form. It can be segmented into two distinct morphemes.

Here is the breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Segmentation
**`pak`** + **`en`**

---

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### **Morpheme 1: `pak`**
*   **Type:** Free Morpheme / Root (Lexeme)
*   **Phonology:** /pɑk/
*   **Meaning:** This is the lexical core of the word. It carries the primary semantic meaning of "to grab," "to take," "to hold," or "to catch."
*   **Etymology:** It stems from Middle Dutch *pakken* and Middle Low German *pakken*. It is cognate with the noun *pak* (meaning a package, bundle, or suit of clothes), suggesting the original sense of "gathering things together" or "seizing."
*   **Grammatical Function:** In this context, it acts as the **verbal stem**.
    *   *Note:* In the present tense singular (*ik pak* - I grab), the stem stands alone. In the plural (*wij pakken* - we grab) or infinitive, it combines with a suffix.

#### **Morpheme 2: `en`**
*   **Type:** Bound Morpheme / Suffix
*   **Phonology:** /ə(n)/ (The 'n' is often elided in casual speech, sounding like /ə/)
*   **Meaning:** This morpheme carries no lexical meaning on its own.
*   **Grammatical Function:** It is the **Infinitive Marker**. In Dutch morphology, the suffix *-en* is the standard ending used to create the dictionary form (infinitive) of a verb. It also serves as the ending for the plural present tense (e.g., *wij pakken*).

---

### 3. Orthographic Note: The Double 'k'
A crucial linguistic detail regarding *pakken* is the spelling of the root. You might ask: *Why is there a double 'k' if the root is just `pak`?*

*   **The Short Vowel Rule:** In Dutch orthography, a short vowel (like the /ɑ/ in *pak*) must be followed by a double consonant when a vowel-initial suffix (like *-en*) is added.
*   **Purpose:** If the word were spelled *paken*, a Dutch reader would pronounce the 'a' as a long vowel (/aː/), changing the pronunciation to match words like *naden* (to sew).
*   **Linguistic Classification:** The second 'k' is **not a morpheme**. It is an **orthographic device** used to preserve the phonological quality (shortness) of the vowel in the root morpheme. Phonologically, the root remains /pɑk/.
