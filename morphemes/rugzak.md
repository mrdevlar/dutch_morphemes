From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`rugzak`** is a classic example of a **nominal compound** (a noun formed by combining two other nouns).

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word consists of two **free morphemes** (units of meaning that can stand alone as words):

> **`rug`** + **`zak`**

There is no **interfix** (linking element) in this specific compound. In Dutch, compounds sometimes require a linking *-s-* (e.g., *liefdesbrief*) or *-en-* (e.g., *kinderwagen*), but *rugzak* joins the two roots directly.

---

### 2. Analysis of Morphemes

#### Morpheme A: `rug`
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun (Substantive).
*   **Literal Meaning:** "Back" (the rear part of the human torso).
*   **Grammatical Role:** **Modifier** (Determiner).
    *   In Dutch compounds, the first element modifies the second. It specifies *what kind* of sack this is.
*   **Etymology:**
    *   Derived from Middle Dutch *rugge*.
    *   Cognate with German *Rücken* and English *ridge* (via Proto-Germanic *\*rugō*).
*   **Phonology:** In the compound, the primary stress falls on this first syllable (**`RUG`**-zak). This is a standard rule in Dutch/Germanic compounds (left-headed stress).

#### Morpheme B: `zak`
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun (Substantive).
*   **Literal Meaning:** "Bag," "sack," or "pouch."
*   **Grammatical Role:** **Head** (The Core).
    *   The head determines the grammatical category of the whole word (it remains a noun) and the general semantic category (it is a type of bag).
*   **Etymology:**
    *   Derived from Middle Dutch *sack*.
    *   Cognate with German *Sack* and English *sack* (via Proto-Germanic *\*sakka-*).
*   **Pluralization:** The plural of the compound is *rugzakken*. The plural suffix *-en* attaches to the head (*zak* $\rightarrow$ *zakken*), confirming that *zak* is the head of the compound. Note the doubling of the *k* to preserve the short vowel sound of *zak*.

---

### 3. Syntactic and Semantic Structure

*   **Compound Type:** **Endocentric**.
    *   An endocentric compound is one where the meaning of the whole is a hyponym (a specific type) of the head. A *rugzak* is a specific type of *zak*.
*   **Semantic Relationship:** **Locative / Purpose**.
    *   The relationship between the modifier and the head is not explicitly marked by a preposition in the morphology, but is understood contextually.
    *   *Rug* + *Zak* $\approx$ "A sack [for/on] the back."
*   **Orthography:** It is written as a **closed compound** (one single word), which is the standard convention for Dutch nouns formed this way.

Linguistically, **`rugzak`** is a transparent compound noun where the semantic weight is carried by the head noun **`zak`**, while **`rug`** restricts the scope to a specific location or usage. It follows standard Germanic morphological rules regarding stress placement (first element) and pluralization (suffix on the head).
