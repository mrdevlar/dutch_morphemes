From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`zoemen`** (meaning "to buzz" or "to hum") can be analyzed as a **monomorphemic root** combined with an **inflectional suffix**.

Here is the morphological breakdown:

### 1. Segmentation
The word consists of two morphemes:
> **`zoem`** + **`-en`**

---

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### **A. `zoem` (Root / Stem)**
*   **Type:** Lexical Root (Free/Bound Root).
*   **Function:** This carries the core semantic meaning of the word. It refers to the specific sound or action of a low, continuous vibrating noise (like a bee, a fly, or an electric transformer).
*   **Etymology/Semantics:** This is an **onomatopoeic** morpheme. The sound of the word imitates the sound it describes.
    *   The vowel combination `oe` represents the long /uː/ sound (similar to the 'oo' in English "moon").
    *   The `m` represents the bilabial nasal consonant.
*   **Status:** In modern Dutch, `zoem` does not typically function as an independent word (you cannot say *"Ik hoor een zoem"* in standard usage; you would say *"Ik hoor een zoemgeluid"*). Therefore, it acts as a **bound root** that requires a verbal ending to function syntactically.

#### **B. `-en` (Suffix)**
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix.
*   **Function:** This is the standard marker for the **infinitive** form of a Dutch verb.
*   **Grammar:**
    *   **Infinitive:** *zoemen* (to buzz).
    *   **Plural Present:** *wij/jullie/zij zoemen* (we/you/they buzz).
    *   **Imperative:** *Zoem!* (Buzz! — Note: The `-en` is dropped here).
*   **Phonology:** In spoken Dutch, this suffix is often reduced to a schwa sound /ə/ or even elided entirely in rapid speech (e.g., *Ik ga zoem* instead of *Ik ga zoemen*).

---

### 3. Morphological Classification
*   **Word Class:** Verb (Werkwoord).
*   **Verb Type:** Weak verb (Regelmatig werkwoord).
    *   Because the root ends in a voiced consonant (`m`), the past tense adds `-de` rather than `-te`.
    *   *Example:* *Ik zoemde* (I buzzed).
*   **Derivation:** There is no derivational morphology here (no prefixes or suffixes changing the part of speech). It is purely inflectional morphology applied to an onomatopoeic root.

### 4. Related Forms (Derivational Morphology)
While `zoemen` itself is just Root + Inflection, the root `zoem-` can take other morphemes to create new words:
*   **`zoem-ig`** (Adjective): Buzzing / buzzy (Suffix `-ig` turns the root into an adjective).
*   **`zoem-er`** (Noun): A buzzer / a person or thing that buzzes (Suffix `-er` turns the root into an agent noun).
*   **`ge-zoem`** (Noun): The buzzing (Prefix `ge-` + Root turns the verb into a verbal noun/gerund).
