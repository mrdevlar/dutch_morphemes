From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`presentatie`** (meaning "presentation") can be analyzed through two lenses: **synchronic** (how the word is structured in modern Dutch) and **diachronic** (its historical etymological roots).

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes.

### 1. Synchronic Analysis (Modern Dutch Structure)
In contemporary Dutch morphology, the word is viewed as a derivation from the verb *presenteren* (to present). It consists of two primary morphemes:

| Morpheme | Segment | Type | Function/Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Root/Stem** | `present-` | Free/Bound Root | Carries the core lexical meaning ("to show," "to offer," "to be before"). |
| **Suffix** | `-atie` | Derivational Suffix | Nominalizes the root; turns the action into a noun indicating a process or result. |

*   **Word:** `present` + `atie` = **`presentatie`**
*   **Pronunciation:** /pre.zɛn.**taː**.ti/
*   **Stress:** The stress falls on the syllable immediately preceding the suffix (the penultimate syllable): *prezen-TA-tie*.

### 2. Diachronic Analysis (Etymological Roots)
To understand the internal structure of the root `present-`, we must look at its Latin origins. The word entered Dutch via French (*présentation*) and Latin (*praesentatio*).

| Morpheme | Segment | Origin | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Prefix** | `prae-` | Latin | "Before," "in front of." |
| **Root** | `sent-` | Latin (*esse*) | "To be." (Note: *Praesens* is the present participle of *praeesse*, "to be before"). |
| **Suffix** | `-atio` | Latin | Noun-forming suffix indicating action or process. |

*   **Historical Construction:** *prae-* (before) + *esse* (to be) $\rightarrow$ *praesens* (present/being before) $\rightarrow$ *praesentare* (to place before/show) $\rightarrow$ *praesentatio*.
*   **Dutch Adaptation:** The Latin *prae-* and *esse* fused into the stem `present-`. The Latin *-atio* became the Dutch suffix `-atie`.

### 3. Grammatical Properties
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun (*Zelfstandig naamwoord*).
*   **Gender:** Common gender (*De-woord*).
    *   *Example:* **De** presentatie was goed. (The presentation was good.)
*   **Plural:** Formed by adding the plural suffix `-s`.
    *   *Example:* `presentaties`.
*   **Derivational Family:**
    *   **Verb:** *presenteren* (to present).
    *   **Agent Noun:** *presentator* (presenter).
    *   **Adjective:** *present* (present/available - though often used in specific contexts like *aanwezig*).

### 4. Linguistic Notes on the Suffix `-atie`
The suffix `-atie` is a **loan suffix** (from Latin/French). It is highly productive in Dutch for creating nouns from verbs of Latin origin.
*   **Pattern:** Verb stem + `-atie` = Noun (Action/Result).
*   **Examples:**
    *   *informeren* $\rightarrow$ *informatie*
    *   *opereren* $\rightarrow$ *operatie*
    *   *discuteren* $\rightarrow$ *discussie* (Note: spelling variation, but same root logic)
    *   *reclameren* $\rightarrow$ *reclame* (phonetic shift)

### Summary
Linguistically, **`presentatie`** is a **nominalized loanword**.
1.  **Morphologically:** It is composed of the stem **`present-`** and the nominalizing suffix **`-atie`**.
2.  **Etymologically:** It preserves the Latin structure of "being before" (*prae-esse*) turned into a noun of action (*-atio*).
3.  **Functionally:** It denotes the **process** or **result** of the verb *presenteren*.
