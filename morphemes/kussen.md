The Dutch word **`kussen`** is a **homograph**, meaning it is spelled the same but has two distinct meanings with different morphological structures and etymologies. To analyze it linguistically, we must treat these as two separate lexical items.

Here is the morphological breakdown for both meanings.

---

### 1. The Verb: *kussen* ("to kiss")

In this context, the word is an **infinitive verb**.

*   **Morphological Structure:** `kus` + `-en`
*   **Morpheme Breakdown:**
    1.  **`kus` (Root / Free Morpheme):**
        *   **Function:** This is the lexical root carrying the core semantic meaning ("kiss").
        *   **Phonology:** It contains a short vowel sound (/ʏ/).
        *   **Paradigm:** In other conjugated forms, this root appears without the doubling (e.g., *ik **kus***, *hij **kust***).
    2.  **`-en` (Inflectional Suffix):**
        *   **Function:** This is the standard Dutch **infinitive marker**. It indicates the base form of the verb (equivalent to "to" in English infinitives).
        *   **Type:** Inflectional (does not change the part of speech, only the grammatical form).

*   **Orthographic Note (The Double 's'):**
    *   You might notice the spelling is *kuss*en, not *kus*en.
    *   **Linguistic Reason:** This is due to Dutch orthographic rules regarding vowel length. In Dutch, a short stressed vowel (like the /ʏ/ in *kus*) must be followed by a double consonant in an open syllable to prevent the vowel from being read as long.
    *   **Morphological Reality:** Morphologically, the boundary is between *kus* and *-en*. The extra *s* is purely orthographic to preserve the vowel quality, not a separate morpheme.

---

### 2. The Noun: *kussen* ("pillow" / "cushion")

In this context, the word is a **neuter noun** (*het kussen*).

*   **Morphological Structure:** `kussen` (Monomorphemic)
*   **Morpheme Breakdown:**
    1.  **`kussen` (Root / Free Morpheme):**
        *   **Function:** In modern synchronic Dutch (how the language is used today), this is treated as a **single, indivisible morpheme**.
        *   **Reasoning:** Unlike the verb, the *-en* here is **not** a productive suffix. You cannot take another root and add *-en* to make a pillow. Furthermore, the plural form is *kussens* (adding *-s* to the end), which suggests the stem of the noun is *kussen-*.
        *   **Type:** Lexical (content word).

*   **Historical/Diachronic Note:**
    *   Etymologically, the noun is related to the verb. Both stem from the Proto-Germanic root *\*kuss-*.
    *   Historically, the noun may have been derived from the verb (perhaps implying "something you press against" or "soft thing"), but in modern morphology, it is lexicalized as a standalone noun.

### Conclusion
From a strictly linguistic (synchronic) perspective:
*   If **`kussen`** is a **verb**, it is **bimorphemic** (Root + Infinitive Suffix).
*   If **`kussen`** is a **noun**, it is **monomorphemic** (Single Root).

The double **s** in both cases is an orthographic device to ensure the vowel is pronounced short, rather than a morphological marker itself.
