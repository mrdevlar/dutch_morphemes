From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`ondernemer`** (meaning "entrepreneur") is a **deverbal noun**. It is derived from a verb that has itself been formed by adding a prefix to a root verb.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation of the word.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation

The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **onder-** + **nem-** + **-er**

Or, viewed hierarchically as a derivation from a verb:

1.  **Verb Base:** *ondernemen* (to undertake)
2.  **Stem:** *ondernem-*
3.  **Suffix:** *-er*

Therefore, the internal structure is:
**[ [ onder- [ nem- ] ] -er ]**

---

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Prefix: `onder-`
*   **Form:** *onder-*
*   **Origin:** Proto-Germanic *\*under-* (cognate with English *under*, German *unter*).
*   **Literal Meaning:** "Under."
*   **Semantic Function:** In this specific context, the prefix does not indicate spatial position (i.e., "below"). Instead, it functions similarly to the English prefix *under-* in the word **undertake**. It implies taking something **upon oneself** or taking responsibility for something.
*   **Stress:** In this word, the prefix carries the primary stress (*ON-der-ne-mer*), which is common for separable and inseparable prefixed verbs in Dutch when nominalized.

#### B. The Root: `nem-`
*   **Form:** *nem-*
*   **Origin:** Proto-Germanic *\*nemaną*.
*   **Base Verb:** *nemen* (to take).
*   **Morphological Note:** *Nemen* is an irregular (strong) verb in Dutch.
    *   Infinitive: *nemen*
    *   Present Stem: *neem-* (vowel lengthening)
    *   Past Stem: *nam-*
    *   Past Participle: *genomen*
    *   **Derivational Stem:** *nem-* (used here for the noun formation).
*   **Etymological Cognates:** This root is a fascinating cognate of the English word **name**. Both derive from the Proto-Indo-European root *\*h₁neh₃-* (to take, to name). In ancient usage, "naming" was a form of "taking" or "claiming" an identity.
    *   Dutch: *nemen*
    *   German: *nehmen*
    *   English: *nim* (archaic/dialect, as in "nimble") / *name* (semantic shift).

#### C. The Suffix: `-er`
*   **Form:** *-er*
*   **Origin:** Proto-Germanic *\*-arjaz*.
*   **Function:** **Agentive Suffix**. It turns a verb into a noun denoting the person who performs the action of the verb.
*   **Cognates:** This is directly cognate with the English agentive suffix **-er** (e.g., *teach* → *teacher*, *work* → *worker*).
*   **Phonology:** In Dutch, this suffix is typically pronounced as a schwa /ə/ (a neutral vowel sound) in unstressed positions.

---

### 3. Semantic Development (Meaning Shift)

The meaning of the word has evolved through a process of semantic specialization:

1.  **Literal Construction:** "One who takes under/upon."
2.  **Historical Meaning:** Historically, an *ondernemer* was simply someone who "undertook" a task or a project.
3.  **Modern Meaning:** In modern Dutch, the meaning has narrowed specifically to the business context. An *ondernemer* is someone who organizes and operates a business, taking on financial risks.
    *   *Note:* This is distinct from the English word **undertaker**. While *ondernemer* means "entrepreneur," the English "undertaker" has shifted to mean a "funeral director" (someone who undertakes the care of the dead). In Dutch, a funeral director is an *uitvaartondernemer* (literally "funeral entrepreneur").

### 4. Syntactic and Phonological Notes

*   **Word Class:** Noun (Common gender: *de ondernemer*).
*   **Plural:** *ondernemers* (adding the plural suffix *-s* to the agent noun).
*   **Pronunciation:** /ˈɔndərˌneː.mər/
    *   **ON-der:** Primary stress on the first syllable.
    *   **-nem-:** Long vowel /eː/ (spelled *e* because it is in an open syllable before the schwa of the suffix).
    *   **-er:** Unstressed schwa /ər/.
