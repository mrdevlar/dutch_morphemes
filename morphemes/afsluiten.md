From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`afsluiten`** is a complex verb formed through derivational and inflectional morphology. It is classified as a **separable compound verb** (*scheidbaar werkwoord*).

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation of its components.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **`af`** + **`sluit`** + **`en`**

---

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### **Morpheme 1: `af-`**
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix (Verbal Particle).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *af*, cognate with German *ab* and English *off*.
*   **Function:**
    *   **Directional/Separative:** It indicates movement away from a source or a state of separation.
    *   **Aspectual:** It often adds a sense of **completeness** or **termination** to the action of the verb.
*   **Syntactic Behavior:** In Dutch grammar, this is a **separable prefix**. In a main clause, it detaches from the stem and moves to the end of the sentence (e.g., *Ik **sluit** de deur **af***). In a subordinate clause or infinitive construction, it remains attached (e.g., *...dat ik de deur **afsluit***).
*   **Semantic Contribution:** It transforms the general act of "closing" into "closing off," "shutting down," or "finalizing."

#### **Morpheme 2: `sluit`**
*   **Type:** Verbal Root (Stem).
*   **Base Lemma:** *sluiten* (to close/shut).
*   **Etymology:** From Middle Dutch *slūten*, Old Dutch *slūtan*, Proto-Germanic *\*slūtaną*. It is distantly related to the English word **shut** (via the Germanic sound shift where *sl-* became *sh-* in English, though *sluiten* is more directly cognate with the archaic English *slute*).
*   **Function:** This carries the core lexical meaning of the word.
*   **Meaning:** To fasten, to close, to secure, or to join together.
*   **Morphological Note:** In the infinitive form, the stem appears as *sluit-* before the infinitive suffix is added.

#### **Morpheme 3: `-en`**
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix.
*   **Function:** **Infinitive Marker**.
*   **Grammar:** This is the standard ending for the infinitive form of regular and irregular Dutch verbs (e.g., *lopen*, *werken*, *sluiten*). It indicates that the verb is in its base, unconjugated form.

---

### 3. Semantic Composition (Meaning Construction)
When combined, the morphemes create a specific semantic field that is broader than the sum of its parts:

1.  **Literal Meaning:** To close something off physically (e.g., *een afsluiting* = a barrier/fence).
2.  **Abstract Meaning:** To conclude or finalize an agreement (e.g., *een contract afsluiten* = to conclude a contract).
3.  **Technical Meaning:** To disconnect or shut down a system (e.g., *de stroom afsluiten* = to cut off the power).

The prefix `af-` is crucial here; without it, *sluiten* simply means "to close" (like a book or a door). With `af-`, it implies **isolation** or **finality**.

### 4. Related Morphological Forms
To fully understand the morphology of `afsluiten`, it helps to look at how the morphemes behave in other grammatical contexts:

*   **Past Participle:** *afgesloten*
    *   Structure: `af` + `ge` + `sluit` + `en`
    *   Note: The participial prefix `ge-` is inserted **between** the separable prefix `af-` and the stem `sluit-`. This is a hallmark of Dutch separable verbs.
*   **Imperative:** *Sluit af!*
    *   Structure: The prefix `af` separates and follows the stem.
