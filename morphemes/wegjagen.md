From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`wegjagen`** is a **separable compound verb** (*scheidbaar samengesteld werkwoord*).

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation of its constituent morphemes.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **`weg`** + **`jag`** + **`en`**

---

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. `weg` (Adverbial Particle / Separable Prefix)
*   **Type:** Free morpheme functioning as a directional particle.
*   **Literal Meaning:** "Away," "off," or "gone."
*   **Function:** In this context, it acts as a **separable prefix**. It modifies the direction or result of the action performed by the main verb. It indicates that the object of the verb is being moved away from the speaker or a specific location.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *wēg* (related to the English word *way*). In modern Dutch, it has shifted from a noun meaning "path" to an adverb meaning "away."
*   **Syntactic Behavior:** Because it is a separable prefix, it detaches from the verb stem in main clauses (e.g., *Ik **jaag** de hond **weg*** — "I chase the dog away").

#### B. `jag` (Lexical Root)
*   **Type:** Bound morpheme (Root).
*   **Meaning:** "To hunt," "to chase," or "to pursue."
*   **Function:** This is the core semantic unit of the word. It carries the primary action.
*   **Etymology:** From Middle Dutch *jagen*, related to the German *jagen*. It is cognate with the English word *yoke* (in the sense of driving cattle) or *hag* (in older dialects), but most closely related to the concept of hunting.
*   **Verb Class:** Historically, this is a **strong verb** (Class 2 in some classifications).
    *   Infinitive: *jag-en*
    *   Past Tense: *joeg* (vowel change *a* → *oe*)
    *   Past Participle: *ge-jaag-d* (vowel change *a* → *aa*)

#### C. `en` (Infinitive Suffix)
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix (Bound morpheme).
*   **Function:** This is the standard marker for the **infinitive** form of Dutch verbs. It indicates that the verb is in its base, unconjugated form.
*   **Grammar:** It does not carry lexical meaning (like "chase" or "away") but serves a purely grammatical function to identify the word class as a verb in its dictionary form.

---

### 3. Semantic Composition (Meaning Construction)
The meaning of the compound is **compositional**, meaning the total meaning is derived from the sum of its parts:

*   **`jagen`** (to chase) + **`weg`** (away) = **`wegjagen`** (to chase away / to drive away / to expel).

This is distinct from a non-compositional idiom where the meaning would be unpredictable. Here, the logic is transparent: you are performing the action of chasing with the directional goal of moving something away.

### 4. Syntactic Morphology (Separability)
From a morphosyntactic perspective, `wegjagen` is a classic example of a **Tobler-Mussafia** type structure in Dutch (though that term is usually for clitics, the concept of separation applies here).

*   **Infinitive/Participle:** The morphemes stay together.
    *   *Ik wil de hond **wegjagen**.* (I want to chase the dog away.)
    *   *Ik heb de hond **weggejaagd**.* (I have chased the dog away.)
*   **Finite Verb (Main Clause):** The prefix `weg` separates and moves to the end of the clause (V2 word order).
    *   *Ik **jaag** de hond **weg**.* (I chase the dog away.)
