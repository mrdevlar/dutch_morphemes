From a morphological perspective, the Dutch verb **`bezoeken`** (meaning "to visit") can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

1.  **`be-`** (Prefix)
2.  **`zoek`** (Root/Stem)
3.  **`-en`** (Suffix)

Here is a detailed linguistic breakdown of each component:

### 1. The Prefix: `be-`
*   **Type:** Derivational Morpheme.
*   **Function:** This is a highly productive verbal prefix in Dutch (and Germanic languages in general). Its primary function here is **transitivization**.
*   **Semantic Effect:** The prefix `be-` often takes a root that implies an action or state and turns it into a transitive verb that acts *upon* an object. It can imply "to affect," "to cover," or "to direct an action toward."
*   **In this context:** It modifies the root meaning of "searching." While *zoeken* is the act of searching generally, *bezoeken* implies directing that search toward a specific target (a person, a place, or an institution). It shifts the meaning from "to look for" to "to seek out and arrive at."
*   **Etymology:** Cognate with the English prefix *be-* (as in *befriend*, *beset*, *bedevil*) and German *be-*.

### 2. The Root: `zoek`
*   **Type:** Lexical Root (Free Morpheme).
*   **Meaning:** "To search," "to seek," or "to look for."
*   **Grammatical Form:** This is the **stem** of the verb. In Dutch, the infinitive is formed by adding *-en* to the stem.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *sōken*, Old Dutch *sōkōn*, and Proto-Germanic *\*sōkōną*.
*   **Cognates:** It is directly related to the English verb **`seek`**.
    *   *Dutch:* Zoeken
    *   *English:* Seek
    *   *German:* Suchen
*   **Phonology:** Note that the vowel `oe` represents the sound /uː/ (like the 'oo' in 'moon').

### 3. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Type:** Inflectional Morpheme.
*   **Function:** This is the standard marker for the **infinitive** form of regular Dutch verbs.
*   **Grammar:** It indicates that the verb is in its base form (the form you would find in a dictionary). It does not carry tense, person, or number information on its own; those are added through conjugation (e.g., *ik bezoek*, *wij bezoeken*).
*   **Variation:** In some dialects or rapid speech, this may be reduced to just *-n*, but in standard orthography and morphology, it is analyzed as *-en*.

---

### Summary of Morphological Structure

| Morpheme | Type | Meaning/Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **be-** | Derivational Prefix | Transitivizes the verb; implies acting upon an object. |
| **zoek** | Root | "To search/seek" (Cognate with English *seek*). |
| **-en** | Inflectional Suffix | Marks the infinitive form. |

### Semantic Shift (Derivation)
The combination of these morphemes creates a specific semantic shift known as **narrowing**:

1.  **`Zoeken`** (Root + Infinitive ending): To search (general action, intransitive or transitive, but implies the *process* of looking).
2.  **`Bezoeken`** (Prefix + Root + Infinitive ending): To visit (specific action, transitive, implies the *completion* of the search by arriving at the destination).

Therefore, linguistically, *bezoeken* literally translates to "to have searched out" or "to seek out," which evolved into the modern meaning of "to visit."
