From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`houden`** is a verb in its infinitive form. To analyze its morphemes, we must distinguish between its lexical content (meaning) and its grammatical function (form).

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation.

### 1. Morphemic Breakdown

The word **`houden`** consists of two morphemes:

| Morpheme | Type | Function | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`houd-`** | **Root / Stem** | Lexical | Carries the core semantic meaning ("to hold," "to keep"). |
| **`-en`** | **Suffix** | Inflectional | Marks the verb as an **infinitive** (the dictionary form). |

### 2. Detailed Explanation of Morphemes

#### A. The Root: `houd-`
*   **Classification:** This is a **bound root** in this specific context. While it carries the main meaning, it cannot stand alone as a finite verb in modern Dutch without inflection (e.g., you cannot say "Ik houd" without a subject, though "Houd!" is a valid imperative).
*   **Semantics:** The root carries polysemy (multiple related meanings). Depending on context, `houd-` can mean:
    1.  *To hold physically* (e.g., *Ik houd de bal* – I hold the ball).
    2.  *To keep/maintain* (e.g., *Houd de deur dicht* – Keep the door closed).
    3.  *To love/like* (e.g., *Ik hou van jou* – I love you).
*   **Morphophonology:** In the spelling, the root ends in a **`d`**. However, due to the Dutch phonological rule of **final devoicing** (*eindklankverscherping*), this `d` is pronounced as a voiceless **[t]** when it appears at the end of a syllable or word (e.g., *houd* is pronounced /ɦʌut/). Despite the sound change, the morpheme remains orthographically and morphologically `houd-`.

#### B. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Classification:** This is an **inflectional suffix**.
*   **Function:** It marks the **infinitive** mood. In Dutch, the infinitive is the citation form of the verb (the form you find in a dictionary).
*   **Comparison:**
    *   *Infinitive:* `houd` + `en` = **houden** (to hold)
    *   *Present Stem:* `houd` (used for conjugation, e.g., *wij houden* - we hold)
    *   *Imperative:* `houd` (Hold!)
*   **Note:** In some dialects or colloquial speech, this suffix may be reduced or dropped (e.g., *ik ga houden* vs. *ik ga hou*), but in standard morphology, `-en` is the obligatory infinitive marker.

### 3. Morphological Class: Strong Verb (Ablaut)

To fully understand the morpheme `houd-`, one must recognize that **`houden`** is a **strong verb** (Class 2 in Dutch historical grammar). This means the root vowel changes (ablaut) to indicate tense, rather than just adding a suffix like `-t` or `-te`.

This confirms that `houd-` is the stable lexical unit, even when its internal vowel changes:

*   **Infinitive:** `houd` + `en` (Vowel: **ou**)
*   **Present Stem:** `houd` (Vowel: **ou**)
*   **Past Singular:** `hield` (Vowel changes to **ie**)
*   **Past Plural:** `hielden` (Vowel changes to **ie**)
*   **Past Participle:** `ge` + `houd` + `en` (Vowel returns to **ou**)

The existence of the Past Participle **`gehouden`** is the strongest proof of the morphemic structure. Here we see:
1.  **`ge-`**: A prefix marking the participle.
2.  **`houd`**: The root (with the original vowel restored).
3.  **`-en`**: The participle suffix.

### 4. Etymological Context

From a diachronic (historical) linguistic perspective, the morpheme `houd-` is stable across Germanic languages, tracing back to the Proto-Germanic root **`*haldaną`**.

*   **Old Dutch:** *holdan*
*   **Old English:** *healdan* (Modern English: *hold*)
*   **German:** *halten*
*   **Dutch:** *houden*

The Dutch morpheme `houd-` is cognate with the English root `hold-`. The `-en` suffix is the standard Germanic infinitive ending (cognate with English `-en` in *to holden* [archaic] or German `-en` in *halten*).

In the word **`houden`**:
1.  **`houd`** is the **lexical root** (carrying the meaning of holding/keeping).
2.  **`-en`** is the **grammatical suffix** (marking the infinitive form).
3.  The word is a **strong verb**, meaning the root vowel undergoes internal change (ablaut) in past tenses, but the consonantal skeleton (`h-d`) remains the identifier of the morpheme.
