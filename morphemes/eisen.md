From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`eisen`** is a fascinating example of **morphological homophony**. It can be analyzed in two distinct ways depending on its grammatical function in a sentence: as an **infinitive verb** or as a **plural noun**.

In both cases, the segmentation is identical, but the grammatical function of the suffix differs.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
Regardless of whether it is a verb or a noun, the word breaks down into two morphemes:

> **`eis`** + **`-en`**

---

### 2. Analysis of the Root Morpheme: `eis-`

*   **Form:** `eis`
*   **Type:** Free Morpheme (Lexical Root)
*   **Meaning:** "Demand," "claim," or "requirement."
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun (Singular).
*   **Etymology:**
    *   Derived from Middle Dutch *ēse*.
    *   Traced back to Old Dutch *ēsa*.
    *   Cognate with Old High German *ēsa* (lawsuit, claim).
    *   It is semantically related to the concept of something being "due" or "owed" in a legal or formal context.
*   **Phonology:**
    *   The vowel combination `ei` represents the diphthong /ɛi/ (similar to the 'i' in English "eye" or "buy").
    *   The `s` is a voiceless alveolar fricative /s/.

---

### 3. Analysis of the Suffix Morpheme: `-en`

The suffix `-en` is a **bound morpheme** (it cannot stand alone). Its function changes based on the word class of the resulting word.

#### Case A: As an Infinitive Verb (*to demand*)
*   **Example:** *"Wij **eisen** recht."* (We demand justice.)
*   **Function:** **Infinitive Marker / Verbalizer.**
*   **Morphological Process:** **Denominalization.**
    *   In Dutch, many verbs are formed from nouns by adding `-en`.
    *   Process: Noun (*eis*) + Verbal Suffix (*-en*) $\rightarrow$ Verb (*eisen*).
    *   While `-en` marks the infinitive form (inflection), the derivation of the verb from the noun is often considered a derivational process in historical morphology.
*   **Conjugation:** It is a **weak verb** (regular).
    *   Present: *ik eis, jij eist, wij eisen*
    *   Past: *ik eiste, wij eisten*
    *   Past Participle: *ge-eist*

#### Case B: As a Plural Noun (*demands*)
*   **Example:** *"De **eisen** zijn hoog."* (The demands are high.)
*   **Function:** **Plural Marker.**
*   **Morphological Process:** **Inflectional Pluralization.**
    *   Process: Singular Noun (*eis*) + Plural Suffix (*-en*) $\rightarrow$ Plural Noun (*eisen*).
    *   This is the standard plural ending for many Dutch nouns (especially those ending in sibilants or specific vowel sounds).
*   **Phonology:** The `-en` is pronounced as a schwa followed by a nasal /ə(n)/.

| Feature | Verb Interpretation | Noun Interpretation |

| **Word** | `eisen` | `eisen` |

| **Category** | Verb (Infinitive) | Noun (Plural) |

### 5. Phonological Note
In rapid speech, the final `-n` in `-en` is often dropped (a process called **n-dropping** or *n-afval*), particularly in the verb form.
*   **Written:** *eisen*
*   **Spoken:** /ˈɛi̯sə/ (The /n/ is often elided unless followed by a vowel).

### Conclusion
Linguistically, **`eisen`** is a **bi-morphemic** word. It consists of the lexical root **`eis`** (demand) and the grammatical suffix **`-en`**. The ambiguity lies entirely in the function of that suffix: it acts as a **verbalizer** in the infinitive verb form and as a **pluralizer** in the noun form. This is a common feature in Dutch morphology, where the infinitive verb ending and the plural noun ending are identical.
