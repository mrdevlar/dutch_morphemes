From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`misgaan`** is a classic example of a **separable compound verb** (*scheidbaar werkwoord*).

Here is the morphological breakdown and analysis of its components.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into two distinct morphemes:

> **`mis-`** + **`gaan`**

*   **`mis-`**: A derivational prefix (bound morpheme).
*   **`gaan`**: The verbal root/infinitive (free morpheme).

---

### 2. Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Prefix: `mis-`
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix.
*   **Function:** It modifies the meaning of the base verb. In Germanic linguistics, this is a negative or privative prefix.
*   **Semantics:** It conveys the meaning of "wrongly," "incorrectly," "badly," or "failure."
*   **Productivity:** This is a highly productive morpheme in Dutch (and other Germanic languages). It can attach to many verbs to create new meanings indicating error.
    *   *Examples:* `mislukken` (to fail), `misbruiken` (to misuse), `misdrijven` (to commit a crime).
*   **Phonology:** In the compound verb, the primary stress falls on this prefix (**`MIS`**gaan). This distinguishes it from inseparable verbs where stress falls on the root.

#### B. The Root: `gaan`
*   **Type:** Verb (Infinitive).
*   **Semantics:** Literally "to go" or "to walk."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *gān*, from Proto-Germanic *\*gāną*. It is cognate with the English verb "to go" and the German "gehen."
*   **Function:** In this compound, `gaan` provides the core action, but its meaning undergoes a **semantic shift**. While `gaan` usually implies physical movement, in `misgaan`, it implies the progression or development of a situation.

---

### 3. Syntactic Behavior: Separability
The most distinct linguistic feature of `misgaan` is that it is a **separable verb**. While written as one word in the infinitive form, the morphemes separate in conjugated main clauses.

*   **Infinitive:** `Ik wil niet **misgaan**.` (I do not want to go wrong.)
*   **Present Tense (Main Clause):** `Het **gaat** vaak **mis**.` (It often goes wrong.)
    *   Here, the finite verb `gaat` moves to the second position, and the prefix `mis` is stranded at the end of the clause.
*   **Past Participle:** `Het is **misgegaan**.` (It went wrong.)
    *   In the participle, the prefix `ge-` is inserted between the two morphemes (`mis` + `ge` + `gaan`).

This separation confirms that `mis-` is not an inseparable prefix (like `be-` in `begaan`), but a separable particle that retains its independent phonological weight.

---

### 4. Semantic Composition
The meaning of `misgaan` is not simply the sum of its parts ("to go wrongly"), but an idiomatic expression.

*   **Literal Composition:** To go + wrongly.
*   **Actual Meaning:** To fail, to go awry, to turn out badly.
*   **Usage Context:** It is almost exclusively used for abstract situations (plans, events, relationships) rather than physical movement. You would not use it to describe walking down the wrong street (for that, you might use *verdwalen*); you use it when a plan fails.

### 5. Etymological Cognates
Looking at the morphemes through a comparative Germanic lens:

*   **English:** The prefix `mis-` is identical (e.g., *misunderstand*, *misfire*). However, English does not have a common verb *misgo*.
*   **German:** The equivalent is `missgehen` (archaic/rare) or more commonly `schiefgehen` (literally "to go crookedly").
*   **Frisian:** `misgean`.

**Conclusion:** Linguistically, `misgaan` is a **derivational compound** formed by a negative prefix and a motion verb. Its status as a **separable verb** dictates its syntax, while the combination creates a specific **idiomatic meaning** regarding failure or error.
