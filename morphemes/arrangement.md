From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`arrangement`** is a fascinating case study because it is a **loanword** (leenwoord) that has been fully integrated into the Dutch morphological system.

While it looks like a single unit to a casual speaker, a morphological analysis reveals its structure based on its origin (French) and how it functions in modern Dutch.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes:

### 1. The Morphemic Breakdown

| Segment | Morpheme Type | Origin | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`arrange-`** | **Root / Stem** | French (*arranger*) | Carries the core lexical meaning ("to organize/put in order"). |
| **`-ment`** | **Derivational Suffix** | French/Latin (*-mentum*) | Nominalizer (turns a verb into a noun indicating the result or action). |

**Total Morphemes:** 2 (in the context of Dutch loanword morphology).

---

### 2. Detailed Explanation

#### A. The Root: `arrange-`
*   **Status:** In modern Dutch, this is treated as the **lexical root**.
*   **Meaning:** It refers to the act of organizing, planning, or putting things in a specific order.
*   **Integration:** Although the root comes from French, it is productive in Dutch. We see this root in the verb **`arrangeren`** (to arrange).
*   **Note on `ar-`:** Etymologically, in French, the word is *ar-* (an intensifying prefix from Latin *ad-*) + *range* (from Germanic *rang*). However, **in Dutch morphology, `ar-` is not analyzed as a separate prefix.** It is fused into the root. A Dutch speaker does not perceive `ar-` as a prefix like *be-*, *ver-*, or *ont-*.

#### B. The Suffix: `-ment`
*   **Status:** This is a **foreign suffix** (vreemd achtervoegsel) that is highly productive in Dutch.
*   **Function:** It is a **nominalizer**. It takes a verbal concept and turns it into a concrete noun (a thing or a result).
    *   *Verb concept:* To arrange.
    *   *Noun result:* An arrangement (a plan, a package, a deal).
*   **Dutch Integration:** While native Dutch nouns often use suffixes like *-ing* (e.g., *ordering* → *ordening*), the suffix *-ment* is standard for loanwords (e.g., *document*, *moment*, *element*, *instrument*).
*   **Pluralization:** Because it is a Dutch noun, it follows Dutch pluralization rules. The suffix `-ment` does not block the addition of the Dutch plural suffix `-en`.
    *   *Singular:* `arrangement`
    *   *Plural:* `arrangement` + `en` = **`arrangementen`**

---

### 3. Synchronic vs. Diachronic Analysis

To fully understand the linguistics, we must distinguish between how the word is analyzed **now** (synchronic) versus how it **evolved** (diachronic).

*   **Diachronic (Historical/Etymological):**
    *   Old French: *araindier* / *aranger*
    *   Germanic root: *rang* (row/rank)
    *   Latin suffix: *-mentum*
    *   Structure: *ad-* (to) + *rang* (row) + *-mentum* (result).

*   **Synchronic (Modern Dutch):**
    *   The word is borrowed as a whole noun.
    *   However, because the suffix *-ment* is recognizable in Dutch, linguists analyze it as **Root + Suffix**.
    *   The internal structure of the root (`ar-` + `range`) is opaque to the Dutch speaker.

### 4. Related Derivatives in Dutch
The morphological integration of `arrangement` allows for further derivation within the Dutch system:

1.  **Verb:** `arrangeren` (Root `arrange` + Verbalizer `-eren`).
2.  **Noun (Plural):** `arrangementen` (Noun `arrangement` + Plural `-en`).
3.  **Adjective:** `arrangement-achtig` (Noun + Suffix `-achtig`, meaning "arrangement-like").

### Summary
In Dutch, **`arrangement`** is a **loan noun** consisting of the root **`arrange-`** and the nominalizing suffix **`-ment`**. While its etymology contains a prefix (`ar-`), in modern Dutch morphology, it is treated as an indivisible root combined with a recognizable foreign suffix that allows for standard Dutch inflection (like adding `-en` for the plural).
