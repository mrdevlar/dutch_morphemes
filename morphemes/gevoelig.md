From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`gevoelig`** (meaning *sensitive*, *delicate*, or *touchy*) is a complex word formed through derivational morphology. It consists of three distinct morphemes.

Here is the breakdown and explanation of each component.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented as follows:
> **ge-** + **voel** + **-ig**

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Root: `voel`
*   **Type:** Free morpheme (in isolation as a verb stem) / Bound root (in this context).
*   **Source:** Derived from the Dutch verb **`voelen`** (*to feel*).
*   **Etymology:** This traces back to Old Dutch *fōlen* and Proto-Germanic *\*fōlōną*.
*   **Cognates:** It is directly cognate with the English verb **`feel`** and the German verb **`fühlen`**.
*   **Semantic Function:** This is the core lexical meaning of the word, referring to the physical or emotional act of perception.

#### B. The Prefix: `ge-`
*   **Type:** Bound derivational morpheme.
*   **Function:** **Nominalization.** In Dutch, the prefix *ge-* is frequently attached to verb stems to create abstract nouns (often indicating the result, the collective, or the capacity of the action).
    *   *Example:* *werken* (to work) → *ge-werk* (work/labor); *lopen* (to walk) → *ge-loop* (walking).
*   **Application here:** When attached to *voel*, it creates the noun **`gevoel`** (*feeling* or *sense*).
*   **Note:** In the modern word *gevoelig*, the *ge-* is technically part of the noun stem (*gevoel*) upon which the adjective is built. However, morphologically, it remains a distinct prefix attached to the root *voel*.

#### C. The Suffix: `-ig`
*   **Type:** Bound derivational morpheme.
*   **Function:** **Adjectivalization.** This suffix turns a noun (or sometimes a verb) into an adjective. It indicates possession of a quality or characteristic.
*   **Meaning:** "Having the quality of," "full of," or "characterized by."
*   **Etymology:** Traces back to Proto-Germanic *\*-agaz*.
*   **Cognates:** It is the direct equivalent of the English suffix **`-y`** (e.g., *sunny*, *salty*) and the German suffix **`-ig`** (e.g., *sinnig*, *wichtig*).
*   **Application here:** It attaches to the noun *gevoel* to create the adjective *gevoelig* (*having the quality of feeling*).

---

### 3. Derivational Process (Step-by-Step)

To understand how the meaning is constructed, we can look at the hierarchy of formation:

1.  **Verb:** `voelen` (to feel)
    *   *Action:* To perceive via touch or emotion.
2.  **Noun:** `ge-` + `voel` = **`gevoel`** (feeling/sense)
    *   *Shift:* The action becomes a concept or capacity.
3.  **Adjective:** `gevoel` + `-ig` = **`gevoelig`** (sensitive)
    *   *Shift:* The concept becomes a quality attributed to a subject.

### 4. Semantic Nuance
While the literal translation is "having-feeling," the modern usage of **`gevoelig`** has expanded into three main contexts:

1.  **Emotional:** A person who is easily hurt or empathetic (*"Hij is erg gevoelig"*).
2.  **Physical/Technical:** Something that reacts easily to stimuli (*"Een gevoelige microfoon"* / A sensitive microphone).
3.  **Delicate:** A topic that requires careful handling (*"Een gevoelig onderwerp"* / A sensitive topic).
