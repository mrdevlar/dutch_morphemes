From a linguistic perspective, the analysis of the Dutch word **`materiaal`** depends on whether you are looking at it **synchronically** (how it functions in modern Dutch today) or **diachronically** (its historical etymology).

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes.

### 1. Synchronic Analysis (Modern Dutch)
In the context of modern Dutch morphology, **`materiaal`** is generally considered a **monomorphemic word** (a single morpheme).

*   **Status:** It is a **free morpheme** (it can stand alone as a word).
*   **Segmentation:** Native Dutch speakers do not actively parse this word into smaller meaningful parts. There is no independent Dutch word *`materia`* or suffix *`-al`* that carries meaning in isolation within this context.
*   **Classification:** It is a **loanword** (from Latin/French) that has been fully integrated into the Dutch lexicon.

### 2. Diachronic Analysis (Etymology)
If we look at the historical origins of the word, we can identify two etymological morphemes derived from Latin. This is often what is meant when asking for a "breakdown" of such words.

**Word:** `materiaal`
**Origin:** Latin *materia* + *-alis* (via French *matériel* or directly from Latin *materiale*).

| Morpheme | Form | Origin | Original Meaning | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Root** | `materia-` | Latin *materia* | "Substance," "timber," "matter," "source" | Carries the core lexical meaning. |
| **Suffix** | `-al` (spelled `-aal`) | Latin *-alis* / *-ale* | "Pertaining to" / Neuter noun ending | Originally adjectival (*materialis*), later substantivized (turned into a noun). |

*   **Note on the Suffix:** In Latin, *materia* is a noun. Adding *-alis* creates the adjective *materialis* ("pertaining to matter"). The neuter form *materiale* was used to denote "material things." Dutch adopted this neuter noun form as **`materiaal`**.

### 3. Morphological Behavior in Dutch
Even though it is historically divisible, its behavior in modern Dutch confirms it acts as a single unit (a stem).

*   **Pluralization:**
    *   Singular: *het materiaal*
    *   Plural: *de materialen*
    *   *Analysis:* The plural suffix *-en* is added to the entire stem *materiaal-*. If the word were actively segmented as *materia* + *al*, one might expect a different pluralization pattern, but the stem remains intact.
*   **Diminutive:**
    *   *het materiaaltje*
    *   *Analysis:* The diminutive suffix *-tje* is added to the end of the word.
*   **Compounding:**
    *   *bouwmaterialen* (construction materials)
    *   *grondstoffenmateriaal* (raw material)
    *   *Analysis:* In compounds, *materiaal* functions as the head noun.

### 4. Related Lexical Items
To understand the morphemes better, it helps to look at cognates where the root is more visible:

*   **`Materie`**: This is the direct Dutch cognate of the Latin root *materia*. It means "matter" or "subject matter."
    *   *Linguistic Note:* While *materia* and *materie* share the same root, in modern Dutch, *materiaal* and *materie* are distinct lexical items with slightly different nuances (*materiaal* often implies physical stuff or equipment, while *materie* is more abstract or scientific).
*   **`Nationaal`**: Shows the suffix *-aal* functioning as an adjective (National).
*   **`Potentieel`**: Shows the suffix *-eel/-aal* functioning as a noun (Potential).

### Summary
*   **Strict Linguistic View:** **1 Morpheme** (Root: `materiaal`). It is an unsegmentable loanword in modern Dutch.
*   **Etymological View:** **2 Morphemes** (Root: `materia-` + Suffix: `-al`).
*   **Meaning:** "Substance pertaining to matter" $\rightarrow$ "Material."

For most linguistic purposes regarding Dutch grammar, you should treat **`materiaal`** as a **single, indivisible root**.
