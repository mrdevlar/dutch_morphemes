From a morphological and linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`opkrijgen`** is a complex verb formed through compounding and derivation. It is classified as a **separable verb** (*scheidbaar werkwoord*).

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes, followed by an explanation of their linguistic functions.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation

The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **`op`** + **`krijg`** + **`-en`**

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

| Morpheme | Form | Category | Function & Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`op`** | Free Morpheme | **Particle / Separable Prefix** | This is a directional particle. It modifies the base verb to add a sense of direction ("up"), completion, or a change of state. In this specific compound, it often implies "mustering" (energy/courage) or physically lifting/starting something. |
| **`krijg`** | Bound Root | **Verb Stem** | This is the lexical root of the verb. It carries the core semantic meaning: "to get," "to receive," or "to obtain." (Note: The stem is *krijg*; the *n* is not part of the root). |
| **`-en`** | Bound Morpheme | **Infinitive Suffix** | This is a grammatical inflection. It marks the verb as being in the **infinitive** form. In Dutch, almost all infinitives end in *-en* (or *-n* after a vowel). |

### 3. Linguistic Features

#### A. Syntax: Separability (*Scheidbaarheid*)
In Dutch morphology, `opkrijgen` is a **separable verb**. This means that while the morphemes are written together in the infinitive, they split apart in conjugated sentences (specifically in main clauses).
*   **Infinitive:** Ik wil het **opkrijgen**. (I want to get it up.)
*   **Present Tense:** Ik **krijg** het **op**. (I get it up.)
*   **Past Tense:** Ik **kreeg** het **op**. (I got it up.)

The particle `op` moves to the end of the clause, while the conjugated root `krijg` stays in the second position (V2 word order).

#### B. Phonology: Stress Pattern
In Dutch separable verbs, the primary lexical stress falls on the **prefix/particle**, not the root.
*   **`OP`-krijgen** (Stress on *op*)
*   This distinguishes it from inseparable verbs (like *ontvangen*), where the stress falls on the root (*ont-VAN-gen*).
*   This stress pattern is a key phonological marker for Dutch speakers to identify the word as a separable compound.

#### C. Semantics: Compositionality
The meaning of `opkrijgen` is not always purely the sum of its parts (non-compositional), though it retains a strong link to the literal meaning.
*   **Literal:** To get something up (e.g., *Ik krijg de ladder op* - I get the ladder up).
*   **Idiomatic/Metaphorical:** To muster energy or courage (e.g., *Ik krijg geen moed op* - I can't muster any courage).
*   **Colloquial:** To receive something (often implying difficulty or a specific transaction) (e.g., *Ik heb het geld opgekregen* - I received the money).

### 4. Etymological Note
*   **`krijgen`**: Derived from Middle Dutch *krijgen*, related to the German *kriegen*. It originally had connotations of "warring" or "striving for," which evolved into "obtaining."
*   **`op`**: From Old Dutch *up*, cognate with English *up*.

### Summary
Linguistically, **`opkrijgen`** is a **separable compound verb** consisting of a **prepositional particle** (`op`), a **verbal root** (`krijg`), and an **infinitive suffix** (`-en`). Its behavior is defined by the stress on the particle and the syntactic rule that separates the particle from the root in finite clauses.
