From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch verb **`sluiten`** (meaning "to close" or "to shut") can be analyzed through its morphological structure, etymology, and grammatical function.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word consists of two distinct morphemes:

> **`sluit`** + **`-en`**

---

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Root/Stem: `sluit-`
*   **Type:** Free Morpheme (Lexical Root/Stem).
*   **Function:** This carries the core semantic meaning of the word. It denotes the action of closing, shutting, or locking.
*   **Phonology:** In standard Dutch, this is pronounced /slœyt/. The vowel combination *ui* represents a diphthong.
*   **Grammatical Role:** This is the **verb stem**. In Dutch conjugation, this stem is used for the present tense (e.g., *ik sluit* [I close], *hij sluit* [he closes]).
*   **Etymology:**
    *   Derived from Middle Dutch *sluten*.
    *   Traced back to Old Dutch *slūtan*.
    *   Originates from Proto-Germanic **\*slūtaną**.
*   **Cognates:**
    *   **English:** The direct cognate is **"shut"**. This relationship illustrates **Grimm's Law** (a set of sound shifts in Proto-Germanic), where the Proto-Indo-European *\*kʷ* or *\*s* shifted. Specifically, the Germanic *\*sl-* corresponds to the English *sh-* (similar to *slip/shlep* or *sleep/sleep*).
    *   **German:** *schließen* (to close).
    *   **Noun Derivative:** The noun *slot* (lock) shares this root.

#### B. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Type:** Bound Morpheme (Inflectional Suffix).
*   **Function:** This is the **infinitive marker**. In Dutch, adding *-en* to a verb stem creates the citation form (the dictionary form) of the verb.
*   **Grammar:**
    *   It distinguishes the infinitive from the conjugated stem.
    *   Example: *sluit* (stem/present tense) vs. *sluiten* (infinitive).
    *   Example: *sluit* (he closes) vs. *sluiten* (to close).
*   **Phonology:** Pronounced as a schwa /ə/ followed by a nasal /n/ (often reduced to just /n/ in rapid speech).
*   **Historical Note:** In Old Dutch and Middle Dutch, this ending was more distinct. In modern Dutch, it is the standard ending for the majority of verbs (the "weak" or regular verbs), though *sluiten* itself is historically a "strong" verb regarding its vowel changes in the past tense (*sloot*), the infinitive ending remains *-en*.

---

### 3. Grammatical Context: Stem vs. Infinitive
To understand why the morpheme break is significant, one must look at how the word changes in a sentence:

*   **Infinitive:** *Ik wil **sluit-en**.* (I want to close.)
*   **Present Tense (Stem):** *Ik **sluit** de deur.* (I close the door.)
*   **Past Tense (Strong):** *Ik **sloot** de deur.* (I closed the door.)
*   **Past Participle:** *De deur is **geslot-en**.* (The door is closed.)

Notice that the root vowel changes in the past tense (*ui* $\to$ *oo*), which is characteristic of **strong verbs** in Germanic languages. However, the morpheme structure of the infinitive remains **Root + Infinitive Suffix**.

### 4. Derivational Morphology (Related Words)
The root `sluit-` is highly productive in Dutch and forms the basis for many other words through the addition of different morphemes:

*   **`sluit`** + **`-ing`** = **`sluiting`** (Closing / Closure / Locking).
    *   *Suffix:* `-ing` (Nominalizer, turns verb into noun).
*   **`aan`** + **`sluit`** + **`en`** = **`aansluiten`** (To connect / to join).
    *   *Prefix:* `aan-` (Directional/Inceptive).
*   **`ge`** + **`sluit`** + **`en`** = **`gesloten`** (Closed / Closed off).
    *   *Prefix:* `ge-` (Past participle marker).
    *   *Suffix:* `-en` (Past participle ending).

### Summary
| Morpheme | Type | Meaning/Function | Origin |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`sluit-`** | Root / Stem | To close/shut | Proto-Germanic *\*slūtaną* |
| **`-en`** | Inflectional Suffix | Marks the infinitive form | Proto-Germanic *-aną* |

In summary, **`sluiten`** is a compound of a Germanic lexical root denoting the action of closing and a standard Germanic inflectional suffix marking the verb's non-finite (infinitive) state.
