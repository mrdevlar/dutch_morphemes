From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`afvallen`** is a compound verb consisting of three distinct morphemes. It is a classic example of a **separable verb** (*scheidbaar werkwoord*) in Dutch morphology.

Here is the breakdown and explanation of each component:

### 1. Morphological Breakdown

| Morpheme | Type | Function | Etymology / Cognates |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`af`** | Prefix / Particle | Directional/Separative | Old Dutch *af*; German *ab*; English *off* |
| **`val`** | Root (Stem) | Lexical Meaning | Old Dutch *vallen*; German *fallen*; English *fall* |
| **`-en`** | Suffix | Grammatical Marker | Proto-Germanic *-aną*; English *-en* (infinitive) |

---

### 2. Detailed Explanation of Morphemes

#### **A. `af` (The Separable Particle)**
*   **Linguistic Category:** In this context, `af` functions as a **verbal particle** (often analyzed as a separable prefix). It is technically an adverb that modifies the verb.
*   **Semantic Role:** It indicates **separation**, **movement away**, or **completion**.
*   **Syntax:** Because it is a separable verb, `af` detaches from the root in main clauses.
    *   *Infinitive:* "Ik wil **afvallen**." (I want to lose weight.)
    *   *Conjugated:* "Ik **val** 5 kilo **af**." (I lose 5 kilos off.)
*   **Etymology:** It stems from the Proto-Germanic *\*af*, sharing a direct lineage with the English preposition **off**.

#### **B. `val` (The Root)**
*   **Linguistic Category:** This is the **lexical root** (or stem) of the verb. It carries the core semantic weight of the action.
*   **Semantic Role:** It denotes the physical action of **falling** or dropping under gravity.
*   **Morphology:** In the infinitive `afvallen`, the root appears as `val`. In other conjugations, it may change due to vowel gradation (ablaut), though `vallen` is a weak verb in modern Dutch (past tense: *viel*, past participle: *gevallen*).
*   **Etymology:** It stems from the Proto-Germanic *\*fallaną*, cognate with the English verb **to fall**.

#### **C. `-en` (The Infinitive Suffix)**
*   **Linguistic Category:** This is a **bound morpheme** (specifically, a derivational/inflectional suffix).
*   **Semantic Role:** It carries no lexical meaning on its own. Its function is purely grammatical: it marks the verb as an **infinitive** (the base form used in dictionaries).
*   **Morphology:** In Dutch, almost all verbs in their base form end in `-en` (e.g., *lopen*, *werken*, *zien*).

---

### 3. Semantic Composition (How the meaning is built)

The meaning of `afvallen` is constructed through **compositionality** and **semantic shift**:

1.  **Literal Meaning:** "To fall off."
    *   *Example:* "De verf **valt af**." (The paint is falling off.)
    *   Here, the particle `af` modifies `vallen` to indicate that something is detaching from a surface.

2.  **Figurative/Idiomatic Meaning:** "To lose weight."
    *   This is a **metaphorical extension**. The body is conceptualized as a container or surface, and body fat is conceptualized as something that can "fall off" that surface.
    *   This is similar to the English phrasal verb "to drop off" (though less common for weight) or simply the concept of weight "dropping."

3.  **Other Contextual Meanings:**
    *   **To drop out:** "Hij **viel** uit de race **af**." (He dropped out of the race.)
    *   **To fail:** "Hij **viel** voor het examen **af**." (He failed the exam / fell off the pass list.)

### 4. Grammatical Note: The "Ge-" Rule
A key linguistic feature of `afvallen` is how it forms the past participle. Because `af` is a separable particle, the standard past participle prefix `ge-` is inserted **between** the particle and the root, rather than at the very front.

*   **Structure:** `af` + `ge` + `val` + `en`
*   **Result:** **`afgevallen`**
*   *Sentence:* "Ik ben 5 kilo **afgevallen**." (I have lost 5 kilos.)

### Summary
Linguistically, **`afvallen`** is a **separable compound verb** where the directional particle **`af`** (off) modifies the motion verb **`val`** (fall) to create a specific semantic meaning (detachment/loss), marked grammatically as an infinitive by the suffix **`-en`**.
