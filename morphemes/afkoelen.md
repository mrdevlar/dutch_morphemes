From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`afkoelen`** is a classic example of a **separable compound verb** (*scheidbaar werkwoord*). It is formed through the process of derivation, combining a prefix, a root, and a suffix.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation of the word.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **`af`** + **`koel`** + **`en`**

| Morpheme | Type | Category | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`af`** | Prefix | Separable Verbal Prefix | Modifies direction/result |
| **`koel`** | Root | Lexeme (Adjective base) | Semantic core (temperature) |
| **`en`** | Suffix | Infinitive Marker / Verbalizer | Turns adjective into verb |

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Prefix: `af`
*   **Linguistic Category:** Separable Prefix (*scheidbaar voorzetsel*).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from the Old Dutch preposition *af*, cognate with English *off* and German *ab*.
*   **Semantic Function:** In this context, `af` indicates a **change of state** or **removal**. It implies a downward movement or a reduction.
    *   When attached to verbs of temperature or motion, it often means "down" or "off."
    *   *Example:* *Afbreken* (to break off), *Aflopen* (to run out/finish).
*   **Grammatical Behavior:** This is the defining feature of the word. In Dutch syntax, this prefix separates from the verb stem in main clauses.
    *   *Infinitive:* Ik wil **afkoelen**.
    *   *Main Clause:* Ik **koel** het **af**. (The prefix moves to the end of the sentence).
*   **Phonology:** In separable verbs, the primary stress usually falls on the prefix. Therefore, the pronunciation is **`AF`-koelen**, not *af-KOEL-en*.

#### B. The Root: `koel`
*   **Linguistic Category:** Lexical Root (Stem).
*   **Part of Speech:** Originally an **adjective** (*koel* = cool).
*   **Etymology:** From Middle Dutch *kuel*, related to Old High German *kuoli*, and cognate with English *cool*.
*   **Semantic Function:** This is the semantic core of the word. It denotes the state of low temperature.
*   **Derivational Process:** This represents a **deadjectival verb** formation. The adjective *koel* is converted into a verb meaning "to make cool" or "to become cool."

#### C. The Suffix: `en`
*   **Linguistic Category:** Inflectional Suffix (Infinitive marker).
*   **Function:** In Dutch, the suffix `-en` is the standard marker for the **infinitive** form of a verb.
*   **Verbalizer:** While primarily an inflectional ending here, it also serves a derivational role by turning the adjective root `koel` into a verb `koelen` (to cool).
*   **Phonology:** In the infinitive form, the final `-en` is often reduced to a schwa sound /ə/ in spoken Dutch (e.g., *afkoelən*).

---

### 3. Semantic Composition (Meaning Construction)
The meaning of the compound is compositional, meaning it is derived from the sum of its parts, though with a specific idiomatic nuance:

1.  **`koelen`**: To cool (transitive: to make something cool; intransitive: to become cool).
2.  **`af`**: Down / Off.
3.  **`afkoelen`**: To cool **down**.

*   **Transitive:** *Ik laat de soep afkoelen.* (I let the soup cool down.)
*   **Intransitive:** *De temperatuur koelt af.* (The temperature cools down.)
*   **Metaphorical:** *Laat je emoties afkoelen.* (Let your emotions cool down / calm down.)

### 4. Comparative Linguistics
To understand the structure better, it helps to compare it with related Germanic languages:

*   **German:** *abkühlen*
    *   Structure: *ab* (prefix) + *kühl* (root) + *en* (suffix).
    *   Note: German also treats this as a separable verb (*trennbares Verb*).
*   **English:** *cool down*
    *   Structure: *cool* (verb) + *down* (particle).
    *   Note: English uses a phrasal verb structure rather than a single prefixed word, but the logic is identical.
*   **French:** *refroidir*
    *   Structure: *re-* (again/back) + *froid* (cold) + *-ir* (verb suffix).
    *   Note: French uses a different prefix (*re-*) to indicate the change of state, whereas Dutch/German use a directional prefix (*af/ab*).

### Summary
Linguistically, **`afkoelen`** is a **separable, deadjectival verb**. It is constructed by taking the adjective root **`koel`**, adding the infinitive suffix **`en`** to verbalize it, and attaching the directional prefix **`af`** to specify the reduction of temperature. Its syntax is governed by the rule of separability, distinguishing it from inseparable verbs like *bezoeken* (to visit).
