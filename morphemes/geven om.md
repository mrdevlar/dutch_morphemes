From a strict linguistic perspective, there is an important clarification to make before breaking down the morphemes: **"`geven om`" is not a single word.**

In Dutch morphology, a word is a single orthographic unit. "`geven om`" consists of two distinct words: the verb **`geven`** and the preposition **`om`**. Together, they form a **prepositional verb** (or phrasal verb equivalent), which functions as a single semantic unit (a collocation) meaning "to care about."

Here is the morphological and syntactic breakdown of the components.

### 1. Component Analysis

#### **A. `geven` (The Verb)**
*   **Lexical Category:** Verb (Werkwoord).
*   **Form:** Infinitive (Onbepaalde wijs).
*   **Morphological Structure:**
    *   **Root/Stem:** In modern synchronic Dutch, `geven` is analyzed as **monomorphemic** in its base form. While etymologically it stems from the Proto-Germanic *\*gebaną* (where `ge-` was a prefix), in contemporary Dutch, the `ge-` is no longer a separable morpheme in the infinitive. It is part of the lexical root.
    *   **Inflection:** It is a **strong verb** (sterk werkwoord). This means it changes its vowel to indicate tense rather than adding a suffix like `-te` or `-de`.
        *   *Infinitive:* geven
        *   *Present Stem:* geef (e.g., *ik geef*)
        *   *Past Singular:* gaf (e.g., *ik gaf*)
        *   *Past Participle:* **ge**-geven (Here, the prefix `ge-` appears as a morpheme marking the perfective aspect).
*   **Valency:** In isolation, `geven` is a ditransitive verb (giving something to someone). In this phrase, its valency changes to require a prepositional object.

#### **B. `om` (The Preposition)**
*   **Lexical Category:** Preposition (Voorzetsel).
*   **Morphological Structure:** **Monomorphemic**. It is an invariable word; it does not change form based on tense, number, or gender.
*   **Literal Meaning:** "Around" or "about" (spatial or temporal).
*   **Function in Phrase:** Here, it acts as a **prepositional marker**. It does not retain its literal spatial meaning ("around"). Instead, it forms a fixed bond with the verb to create a new meaning.

### 2. The Combination: Prepositional Verb
Linguistically, `geven` + `om` creates a **Prepositional Verb** (voegwoordelijk werkwoord).

*   **Lexicalization:** While they are two separate words, they are **lexicalized**. This means the meaning of the combination ("to care") cannot be derived simply by adding the meaning of "give" + "around." It is an idiom.
*   **Argument Structure:** The preposition `om` governs the object of the care.
    *   *Example:* "Ik geef **om** jou." (I care about you.)
    *   *Example:* "Hij geeft niets **om** geld." (He doesn't care about money.)

### 3. Syntactic Behavior (Morphosyntax)
Understanding how these morphemes interact in a sentence is crucial for Dutch linguistics.

**1. Word Order (V2 Rule):**
In a main clause, the conjugated verb (`geven`) moves to the second position, while `om` stays with the object.
*   *Ik **geef** om jou.* (I care about you.)

**2. Subordinate Clauses:**
In a subordinate clause, the verb moves to the end.
*   *...dat ik om jou **geef**.* (...that I care about you.)

**3. The Past Participle:**
This is a key morphological test. If `om` were a separable prefix (like in *opbellen* -> *opgebeld*), the `ge-` would go between them. However, because `om` is a preposition, the participle is formed only on the verb.
*   *Correct:* Ik heb er **om gegeven**. (I have cared about it.)
*   *Incorrect:* Ik heb er **omgegeven**. (This would imply the verb *omgeven* = "to surround".)

**4. Pronominal Adverbs:**
When the object is replaced by a pronoun, Dutch uses a pronominal adverb (`er` + `om` = `erom`).
*   *Ik geef **erom**.* (I care about it.)
*   *Ik geef **niet om**.* (I don't care.)

### Conclusion
From a morphological standpoint, you cannot break "`geven om`" into smaller morphemes *within* a single word because it is not a single word. It is a **syntagm** (a sequence of words) that functions as a single **lexeme** (dictionary entry).

*   **`geven`**: The verbal root (inflected for tense).
*   **`om`**: The prepositional head that dictates the object.
*   **Combined**: A fixed idiomatic unit meaning "to care."

*Note: Do not confuse this with the separable verb **`omgeven`** (to surround), which is a single word where `om-` is a prefix.*
