From a morphological perspective, the Dutch word **`tikken`** can be segmented into two distinct morphemes.

Here is the breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Segmentation
**`tik`** + **`en`**

---

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### **Morpheme 1: `tik`**
*   **Type:** Free Morpheme / Root (Stem)
*   **Category:** Lexical Morpheme
*   **Meaning:** This carries the core semantic meaning of the word. It refers to the action of tapping, knocking lightly, or the sound of a clock ticking.
*   **Etymology/Semantics:** This root is **onomatopoeic**. It mimics the sharp, short sound of a tap or a tick.
*   **Related Forms:**
    *   As a noun: *een tik* (a tap / a blow).
    *   As a verb stem: *ik tik* (I tap).
*   **Phonology:** The vowel is short (/ɪ/).

#### **Morpheme 2: `en`**
*   **Type:** Bound Morpheme / Inflectional Affix (Suffix)
*   **Category:** Grammatical Morpheme
*   **Function:** This is the **infinitive marker**. In Dutch, adding `-en` to a verb stem creates the dictionary form (the infinitive).
*   **Pronunciation:** In many dialects and casual speech, the final `n` is often dropped or nasalized (pronounced as /ə/ or /n̩/), but morphologically it remains a distinct suffix.

---

### 3. Orthographic Note (The Double "k")
A common point of confusion in Dutch morphology is the spelling of the word. You might ask: *If the morphemes are `tik` and `en`, why is there a double `k` in `tikken`?*

This is due to a **phonological spelling rule** in Dutch, not an additional morpheme.

*   **The Rule:** In Dutch orthography, if a verb stem has a **short vowel** (like the `i` in `tik`), and you add a suffix that begins with a vowel (like `-en`), you must **double the final consonant** of the stem.
*   **The Purpose:** This doubling preserves the short sound of the vowel.
    *   If written as *`tiken`*, the `i` would be read as a long vowel (/iː/) (like in *`fietsen`*).
    *   By writing *`tikken`*, the reader knows the `i` remains short (/ɪ/).
*   **Morphological Boundary:** The boundary lies between the first `k` and the second `k`.
    *   Underlying structure: `tik` + `en`
    *   Surface spelling: `tik` + `k` + `en`

### 5. Contextual Usage
To see the root `tik` in isolation, look at other conjugations where the suffix changes or disappears:
*   **Infinitive:** `tikken` (to tap)
*   **Present (1st person):** `ik tik` (I tap) — *Suffix removed, root stands alone.*
*   **Past Tense:** `tikte` (tapped) — *Suffix changes to `-te`.*
*   **Past Participle:** `getikt` (tapped) — *Prefix `ge-` + root + suffix `-t`.*

In all these forms, **`tik`** remains the constant semantic core.
