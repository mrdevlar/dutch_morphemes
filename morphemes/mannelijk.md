From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`mannelijk`** (meaning "male" or "masculine") can be broken down into three distinct morphemes. It is a derived adjective formed from a noun root.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented as follows:
> **man** + **-el-** + **-lijk**

*(Note: In surface spelling, the root `man` doubles its final consonant, resulting in `mannelijk`.)*

---

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### **A. Root: `man`**
*   **Type:** Free morpheme (Lexeme).
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun.
*   **Meaning:** "Man" (adult male human).
*   **Function:** This is the semantic core of the word. It provides the base meaning from which the adjective is derived.
*   **Etymology:** From Old Dutch *man*, related to the English *man* and German *Mann*.

#### **B. Derivational Suffix: `-el-`**
*   **Type:** Bound morpheme.
*   **Function:** This is a derivational suffix that creates a stem capable of accepting the adjectival suffix. It often indicates a relationship or belonging.
*   **Phonology:** It introduces a schwa vowel (/ə/) and an /l/ sound.
*   **Usage:** This suffix is common in Dutch when forming adjectives from nouns (e.g., *vriend* + *-el-* + *-ijk* = *vriendelijk* "friendly").

#### **C. Adjectival Suffix: `-lijk`**
*   **Type:** Bound morpheme.
*   **Part of Speech:** Adjective-forming suffix.
*   **Meaning:** Equivalent to the English suffix **-ly** (as in *manly*) or **-like**. It denotes "characterized by" or "having the nature of."
*   **Function:** It converts the noun stem into an adjective.
*   **Etymology:** From Old Dutch *-līk*, related to the English *-ly* and German *-lich*.

---

### 3. Orthographic Note: The Double "n"
You may notice the spelling is **`mannelijk`** (with two *n*s), rather than *`manelijk`*.

*   **Linguistic Rule:** In Dutch orthography, if a root word has a **short vowel** (like the /a/ in *man*) and is followed by a suffix starting with a vowel, the final consonant of the root is doubled.
*   **Purpose:** This doubling preserves the short pronunciation of the vowel. If it were spelled *`manelijk`*, a Dutch reader might pronounce the *a* as a long vowel (/aː/).
*   **Result:** *Man* + *elijk* $\rightarrow$ **Mannelijk**.

### 4. Etymological & Cross-Linguistic Comparison
*   **English Cognate:** *Manly*.
    *   Structure: *Man* + *-ly*.
    *   Difference: English does not use the intermediate *-el-* suffix, nor does it double the consonant in this specific case.
*   **German Cognate:** *Männlich*.
    *   Structure: *Mann* + *-lich*.
    *   Similarity: German also doubles the *n* (*Mann*) to preserve the short vowel, similar to Dutch.
*   **Dutch Internal Comparison:** *Vrouwelijk* ("female").
    *   Structure: *Vrouw* + *-el-* + *-ijk*.
    *   Note: *Vrouw* does not double its final consonant because the vowel is long/diphthongal, unlike the short vowel in *man*.
