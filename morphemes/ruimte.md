From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`ruimte`** (meaning "space," "room," or "volume") can be analyzed through **morphology**, the study of the internal structure of words.

Here is the breakdown and explanation of its morphemes.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word consists of two distinct morphemes:

> **`ruim`** + **`-te`**

### 2. Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Root: `ruim`
*   **Type:** Free Morpheme (Root/Stem).
*   **Part of Speech:** Adjective.
*   **Meaning:** "Spacious," "ample," "wide," or "roomy."
*   **Function:** This is the lexical core of the word. It carries the primary semantic content regarding the quality of being open or having volume.
*   **Linguistic Note:** In modern Dutch, *ruim* functions primarily as an adjective (e.g., *een ruime kamer* — "a spacious room"). However, it is historically related to the verb *ruimen* ("to clear" or "to make room").
*   **Cognates:** It is etymologically related to the English word **room** and the German word **Raum**. All three descend from the Proto-Germanic *\*rūmaz*.

#### B. The Suffix: `-te`
*   **Type:** Bound Morpheme (Derivational Suffix).
*   **Function:** **Nominalizer**.
*   **Meaning:** It does not carry a concrete meaning on its own but changes the grammatical category of the root.
*   **Process:** It converts the adjective *ruim* (a quality) into an abstract noun *ruimte* (the concept or instance of that quality).
*   **Pattern:** This is a productive pattern in Dutch for forming nouns from adjectives.
    *   *hoog* (high) + *-te* → *hoogte* (height)
    *   *breed* (wide) + *-te* → *breedte* (width)
    *   *diep* (deep) + *-te* → *diepte* (depth)
    *   *ruim* (spacious) + *-te* → *ruimte* (space)

### 3. Morphological Process: Derivation
The formation of *ruimte* is an example of **derivational morphology**.
*   **Input:** Adjective (*ruim*)
*   **Operation:** Suffixation (*-te*)
*   **Output:** Noun (*ruimte*)

Unlike inflectional morphology (which changes tense, number, or case without changing the word class), this derivation changes the **word class** (from adjective to noun) and creates a new lexical item with a related but distinct meaning.

### 4. Phonological and Orthographic Note
*   **Spelling:** The spelling remains consistent with the root (*ruim* + *te* = *ruimte*). There is no vowel change or consonant doubling.
*   **Pronunciation:**
    *   *ruim* is pronounced with a nasal vowel (roughly /rœy̯m/).
    *   When the suffix *-te* is added, the nasal quality of the *m* remains, but the syllable structure changes.
    *   In some dialects or rapid speech, the *m* may be slightly devoiced before the *t*, but in standard Dutch, the nasalization is preserved.

### 5. Etymological Context (Diachronic View)
While the synchronic (current) analysis is Adjective + Suffix, the **diachronic** (historical) view is slightly more complex:
*   The Proto-Germanic root *\*rūmaz* originally meant "space" or "room."
*   In Old Dutch, *rūm* was both an adjective ("spacious") and a noun ("space").
*   Over time, the noun form was reinforced by the suffix *-te* to distinguish the abstract concept of "space" from the adjective "spacious."
*   This distinguishes it from the English word *room*, which retained the original noun form without adding a suffix equivalent to *-te*.
