From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`geduldig`** (meaning "patient") can be analyzed through its morphology (structure of words) and etymology (historical origin).

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **`ge-`** + **`duld`** + **`-ig`**

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### **A. `ge-` (Prefix)**
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix.
*   **Historical Function:** In Dutch (and Germanic languages generally), *ge-* is most commonly the marker for the **past participle** of verbs (e.g., *maken* → *gemaakt*).
*   **Current Function:** In this specific word, the prefix is **lexicalized** (fossilized). It is no longer a productive marker for a verb in modern usage. Instead, it is part of the noun stem *geduld*.
*   **Etymology:** Historically, this comes from the past participle of an older verb meaning "to endure" or "to suffer." It indicates a state of having endured something.
*   **Pronunciation:** /ɣə/ (a voiced velar fricative followed by a schwa).

#### **B. `duld` (Root)**
*   **Type:** Lexical Root (Stem).
*   **Meaning:** "Endure," "tolerate," or "suffer."
*   **Etymology:** This root is cognate with the German verb *dulden* (to tolerate) and the English concept of "endurance." In Old High German, it was related to *dullan*.
*   **Modern Context:** In modern Dutch, *duld* does not exist as an independent word. It only appears within the noun *geduld* (patience) and the adjective *geduldig*.
*   **Phonology:** The vowel is a short /ʏ/ (as in *put*), not the long /yː/ (as in *duur*).

#### **C. `-ig` (Suffix)**
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix.
*   **Function:** Adjectivalizer. It turns a noun or verb stem into an adjective.
*   **Meaning:** "Having the quality of," "full of," or "-ous" (similar to English *-y* or *-ous*).
*   **Examples:**
    *   *Vast* (firm) → *Vastig* (firm-ish)
    *   *Zacht* (soft) → *Zachtig* (softish)
    *   *Geduld* (patience) → *Geduldig* (patient)
*   **Pronunciation:** /əx/ (a schwa followed by a velar fricative).

### 3. Synchronic vs. Diachronic Analysis

To fully understand the word linguistically, one must distinguish between how it is analyzed historically versus how it is analyzed by a modern speaker.

*   **Diachronic (Historical) Analysis:**
    *   **Structure:** *ge-* (participle prefix) + *duld* (root) + *-ig* (adjective suffix).
    *   **Logic:** "Having the quality of having endured."
    *   **Origin:** Derived from the nominalized past participle of the verb *dulden*.

*   **Synchronic (Modern) Analysis:**
    *   **Structure:** *geduld* (Noun) + *-ig* (Adjective suffix).
    *   **Logic:** Most modern Dutch speakers perceive *geduld* as a single, unbreakable unit (the noun for "patience"). They do not actively feel the *ge-* as a separate prefix.
    *   **Derivation:** The adjective is derived directly from the noun *geduld*.

### 4. Comparison with English
*   **Dutch:** *Geduldig* (Endure + -ig)
*   **English:** *Patient* (Latin *pati* "to suffer" + *-ent*)
*   **English:** *Enduring* (Endure + -ing)

While English uses the Latin root *pati* for "patient," Dutch uses the Germanic root *duld* (endure). Interestingly, the English word **"tolerate"** shares a similar semantic field but comes from a different etymological root (*tolerare*).
