From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch verb **`verlenen`** (meaning "to grant," "to bestow," or "to render") can be analyzed through its morphological structure, etymology, and semantic composition.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **ver-** + **leen** + **-en**

*   **ver-**: Derivational Prefix
*   **leen**: Lexical Root (Stem)
*   **-en**: Inflectional Suffix (Infinitive Marker)

---

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Prefix: `ver-`
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix (Inseparable).
*   **Function:** It modifies the meaning of the root verb. In Dutch, *ver-* is one of the most common prefixes. It often indicates a change of state, completion, or a transformation of the object.
*   **Semantic Role in *verlenen*:**
    *   In many verbs, *ver-* implies "away" or "loss" (e.g., *verliezen* = to lose).
    *   In *verlenen*, it functions to **intensify the act of giving** or to shift the meaning from a simple transaction to a formal bestowal. It transforms the concept of "lending" into "granting authority or rights."
    *   **Stress:** Because *ver-* is an inseparable prefix, the stress falls on the root syllable: *ver-LEEN-en*.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Proto-Germanic *\*far-*, cognate with the English prefix *for-* (as in *forbid* or *forgo*) and the German *ver-*.

#### B. The Root: `leen`
*   **Type:** Bound Lexical Root.
*   **Function:** This carries the core semantic meaning of the word.
*   **Meaning:** Historically, this root relates to "loan" or "lend."
*   **Modern Usage:** In contemporary Dutch, *leen* is not a standalone free morpheme (you cannot say "een leen" to mean a loan; you must say *een lening*). However, it survives as a bound root in this verb and in compounds like *leenbank* (pawnshop).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *lēn*, from Proto-Germanic *\*lēnaną*.
    *   **Cognates:** This is directly related to the English verb **to lend** and the noun **loan**.
    *   **German Cognate:** *verleihen* (to lend/grant).

#### C. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix.
*   **Function:** Marks the verb as the **infinitive** (the dictionary form).
*   **Grammar:** In Dutch, the infinitive of regular (weak) verbs typically ends in *-en*.
    *   *Stem:* *verleen-* (used for conjugation, e.g., *ik verleen*, *hij verleent*).
    *   *Infinitive:* *verleen* + *en* = *verlenen*.
*   **Note:** This suffix is phonologically necessary to complete the verb form but does not add lexical meaning beyond indicating the verb's mood/form.

---

### 3. Semantic Synthesis (How the parts create meaning)

The combination of these morphemes creates a specific semantic shift from the root meaning:

1.  **Base Concept (*leen*):** To lend something (usually physical or money) with the expectation of return.
2.  **Modification (*ver-*):** The prefix shifts the focus from a reciprocal loan to a **one-way bestowal**. It implies giving something that confers status, permission, or service, rather than just a temporary object.
3.  **Result (*verlenen*):** To grant, confer, or render.
    *   *Example:* *Een vergunning verlenen* (To grant a permit). You are not "lending" the permit; you are officially bestowing the right to use it.
    *   *Example:* *Hulp verlenen* (To render aid). You are providing service, not lending it.

### 4. Related Linguistic Context

*   **Verb Class:** *Verlenen* is a **weak verb** (regular).
    *   Past Tense: *verleende*
    *   Past Participle: *verleend*
*   **Noun Derivation:** The action can be nominalized as **`verlening`** (granting/bestowal), where the suffix *-ing* turns the verb into a noun.
*   **Comparison with *lenen*:**
    *   *Lenen* (to lend/borrow): Focuses on the temporary transfer of possession.
    *   *Verlenen* (to grant): Focuses on the authority or right being given.
