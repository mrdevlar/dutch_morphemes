From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`brandnetel`** is a **compound noun** (*samenstelling*). It consists of two free morphemes (roots) that combine to create a specific meaning.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into two distinct morphemes:

> **brand** + **netel**

There are no inflectional affixes (like plural markers or verb endings) and no derivational affixes (like prefixes or suffixes) within this specific form. It is a direct juxtaposition of two nouns.

---

### 2. Analysis of Morphemes

#### Morpheme A: `brand`
*   **Category:** Noun (used attributively as a modifier).
*   **Literal Meaning:** Fire, burning, blaze.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *brand*, from Proto-Germanic **\*brandaz*.
*   **Cognates:** English *brand*, German *Brand*, Swedish *brand*.
*   **Function in Compound:** It acts as the **modifier** (or determiner). It specifies the *type* of netel.
*   **Semantic Motivation:** This refers to the sensation caused by the plant. The stinging hairs (trichomes) on the leaves inject histamine and other chemicals that cause a burning, stinging sensation on the skin, similar to a burn.

#### Morpheme B: `netel`
*   **Category:** Noun.
*   **Literal Meaning:** Nettle (the plant *Urtica*).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *netela*, from Proto-Germanic **\*netilon*.
*   **Cognates:** English *nettle*, German *Nessel*, Danish *nælde*.
*   **Function in Compound:** It acts as the **head** (or determinatum). In Dutch compounds, the final element usually determines the grammatical category and the core semantic category of the word. Therefore, a *brandnetel* is a type of *netel*.
*   **Semantic Note:** The etymology of the root *\*netilon* is debated. Some linguists link it to the concept of "netting" or "binding" (referring to the fibrous stems used historically for rope), while others suggest it is onomatopoeic or related to the needle-like hairs.

---

### 3. Compound Structure (Syntax & Semantics)

*   **Type:** Endocentric Compound.
    *   The meaning of the whole word is a hyponym (a specific subtype) of the head word. A *brandnetel* is a specific kind of *netel*.
*   **Word Order:** Modifier + Head.
    *   Dutch follows the standard Germanic pattern where the descriptive element comes first (*brand*) and the core noun comes second (*netel*).
*   **Linking Element:** Zero.
    *   Unlike some Dutch compounds that require a linking morpheme (like *-s-* in *liefdesverklaring* or *-en-* in *boekenkast*), *brandnetel* joins the two roots directly without an interfix.

### 5. Cross-Linguistic Comparison
The logic behind this compound is shared across Germanic languages, though the specific words differ:

*   **Dutch:** *brand* (burn) + *netel* (nettle) = **Brandnetel**
*   **German:** *Brenn* (burn) + *nessel* (nettle) = **Brennnessel**
*   **English:** *Sting* (verb/noun) + *nettle* (nettle) = **Stinging nettle** (Note: English uses a participle/adjective "stinging" rather than a noun "burn," but the semantic logic is identical).

### Conclusion
Linguistically, **`brandnetel`** is a transparent compound where the semantic meaning is the sum of its parts: a "nettle" that causes a "burning" sensation. It is a classic example of Germanic compounding where the head noun is at the end and the modifier precedes it.
