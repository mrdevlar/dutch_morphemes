From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`groeien`** (meaning "to grow") can be analyzed through **morphology**, the study of the internal structure of words and the rules by which they are formed.

Here is the breakdown and explanation of its morphemes.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word consists of two distinct morphemes:

> **`groei`** + **`-en`**

*   **`groei`**: The lexical root (or stem).
*   **`-en`**: The inflectional suffix.

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Root: `groei`
*   **Type:** Free morpheme (in the sense that it carries the core lexical meaning, though it functions as a bound stem in conjugation).
*   **Function:** This is the **verbal stem**. It carries the semantic content of the word.
*   **Meaning:** It denotes the concept of increasing in size, developing, or maturing.
*   **Phonology/Orthography:**
    *   The grapheme **`ei`** represents a diphthong (a complex vowel sound), pronounced /ɛi/ (similar to the 'ay' in English "day" but starting with a more open 'e' sound).
    *   The **`g`** is typically pronounced as a voiced velar fricative /ɣ/ in standard Dutch (or a glottal fricative /h/ in some dialects).
*   **Cognates:** This root is cognate with the English word **`grow`** and the German word **`grauen`** (in the sense of dawning/growing light, though *wachsen* is the more common German equivalent for physical growth).

#### B. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Type:** Bound morpheme.
*   **Function:** **Infinitive Marker**.
*   **Grammar:** In Dutch morphology, the suffix `-en` is the standard marker used to form the **infinitive** (the dictionary form) of regular (weak) verbs.
*   **Inflectional Nature:** This is an **inflectional** morpheme, not a derivational one. It does not change the part of speech (it remains a verb) or the core meaning (it still means "grow"); it simply marks the grammatical form of the verb.
*   **Pronunciation:** In many contexts, the final `-n` is dropped in speech (a process called *n-dropping*), resulting in a schwa sound /ə/ at the end (e.g., /ˈɣrui̯ə/).

### 3. Morphological Classification

*   **Word Class:** Verb.
*   **Verb Type:** Weak verb (Regular).
    *   Because the stem `groei` does not undergo vowel changes (ablaut) in the past tense, it is classified as a weak verb.
    *   *Example:* *groeien* (infinitive) → *groeide* (past tense) → *gegroeid* (past participle).
*   **Derivational History:**
    *   Historically, the noun **`groei`** (growth) and the verb **`groeien`** share the same root. In modern Dutch, the noun is often treated as a nominalization of the verb stem.
    *   The suffix `-en` is the primary verbalizer in Dutch for creating infinitives from stems.

### Summary
Linguistically, **`groeien`** is a **monomorphemic stem** (`groei`) combined with an **inflectional suffix** (`-en`). The structure is transparent and regular, typical of Dutch weak verbs, where the suffix `-en` signals the non-finite, infinitive state of the action "to grow."
