From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`redden`** (meaning "to save" or "to rescue") can be analyzed through **synchronic morphology** (how the word is structured in modern Dutch) and **diachronic morphology** (its historical development).

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes.

### 1. Synchronic Morphological Breakdown
In modern Dutch, `redden` is a **weak verb** in the infinitive form. It consists of **two morphemes**:

| Morpheme | Form | Type | Function/Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Root/Stem** | `red-` | **Lexical Morpheme** | Carries the core semantic meaning: "to save," "to rescue," or "to deliver." |
| **2. Suffix** | `-en` | **Inflectional Morpheme** | Marks the verb as an **infinitive** (the base form used in dictionaries). |

**Total Morphemes:** 2

#### Important Orthographic Note (The Double 'd')
You might notice the word is spelled with a double `d` (`re**dd**en`), but the morphological root is technically `red-`.
*   **Phonology:** The vowel `e` in `redden` is short (/ɛ/).
*   **Orthographic Rule:** In Dutch spelling, a short vowel in a stressed syllable must be followed by a double consonant to prevent the vowel from being read as long (which would happen if it were spelled `reden`, pronounced like *ray-den*).
*   **Linguistic Reality:** The extra `d` is **not** a separate morpheme. It is a spelling convention to preserve the phonological quality of the root `red-`.
    *   *Evidence:* In the present tense conjugation, the double `d` disappears: *Ik **red*** (I save), *Jij **redt*** (You save). This confirms the underlying stem is `red-`.

### 2. Diachronic (Etymological) Perspective
Looking at the history of the word provides insight into the origin of the root morpheme `red-`.

*   **Middle Dutch:** *redden*
*   **Old Dutch:** *redian* / *redon*
*   **Proto-Germanic:** Reconstructed as related to the concept of "ordering" or "arranging."
*   **Cognates:**
    *   **German:** *retten* (to save)
    *   **English:** There is no direct modern cognate for "save," but it is distantly related to the archaic English word *rede* (meaning "counsel" or "advice").
*   **Semantic Shift:** Linguists hypothesize a semantic shift from "to arrange/set right" $\rightarrow$ "to put in order" $\rightarrow$ "to rescue/save." This suggests the root `red-` originally carried a meaning closer to "ordering" or "counseling" (related to Dutch *raad*, meaning advice/council) before specializing into "saving."

### 3. Morphological Family (Derivatives)
To prove that `red-` is a productive lexical morpheme, we can look at other words derived from it. In these cases, the root remains `red-`, and different suffixes are added:

1.  **`redding`** (Noun)
    *   Breakdown: `red` (root) + `-ing` (nominalizing suffix).
    *   Meaning: "Rescue" or "Salvation."
2.  **`redder`** (Noun/Agent)
    *   Breakdown: `red` (root) + `-er` (agentive suffix).
    *   Meaning: "Savior" or "Rescuer."
3.  **`gered`** (Past Participle)
    *   Breakdown: `ge-` (participle prefix) + `red` (root) + `-d` (past tense marker).
    *   Meaning: "Saved."

### Summary
*   **Word:** `redden`
*   **Morphological Structure:** `red` (Root) + `en` (Infinitive Suffix).
*   **Spelling Quirk:** The double `d` is orthographic (spelling), not morphological.
*   **Meaning:** The root `red-` conveys the concept of saving/rescuing, while `-en` marks the verb form.
