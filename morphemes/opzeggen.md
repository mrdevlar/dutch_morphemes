From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`opzeggen`** is a **separable compound verb** (*scheidbaar werkwoord*). To understand its structure, we must analyze it morphologically (how it is built from meaningful units) and syntactically (how those units behave in a sentence).

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes in **`opzeggen`**:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **`op`** + **`zeg`** + **`-en`**

---

### 2. Detailed Explanation of Morphemes

#### A. `op` (Separable Prefix / Particle)
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix (specifically a separable particle).
*   **Original Meaning:** As a preposition or adverb, *op* generally means "up," "on," or "open."
*   **Function in this context:** In this compound verb, *op* acts as a **verbal particle**. It modifies the meaning of the root verb.
*   **Semantic Shift:** The meaning has undergone **idiomaticization**. It no longer literally means "to say up." Instead, it implies a sense of **finality, termination, or completion**. It suggests "saying something off" or "declaring an end."
*   **Syntactic Behavior:** Because it is a *separable* prefix, it detaches from the root in main clauses.
    *   *Infinitive:* Ik wil mijn abonnement **opzeggen**. (I want to cancel my subscription.)
    *   *Main Clause:* Ik **zeg** mijn abonnement **op**. (I cancel my subscription.)

#### B. `zeg` (Lexical Root / Stem)
*   **Type:** Free Morpheme (Root).
*   **Source:** Derived from the verb **`zeggen`** (to say).
*   **Etymology:** From Old Dutch *seggen*, Proto-Germanic *\*sagjaną*.
*   **Function:** This carries the core semantic load of the word: **speech** or **utterance**.
*   **Orthographic Note:** In the infinitive form *opzeggen*, the root is spelled with a double *g* (`zeggen`). However, morphologically, the root is just **`zeg`**. The double *g* is an orthographic rule in Dutch to indicate that the preceding vowel (*e*) is short. If the vowel were long, it would be spelled differently (e.g., *zeg* vs. *zeeg*). Therefore, the second *g* is not a separate morpheme; it belongs to the spelling of the root `zeg`.

#### C. `-en` (Inflectional Suffix)
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix.
*   **Function:** This is the standard **infinitive marker** for Dutch verbs. It marks the verb as being in its base, unconjugated form.
*   **Morphological Boundary:** It attaches to the stem (`zeg`).
    *   *Stem:* `zeg`
    *   *Infinitive:* `zeg` + `en` = `zeggen` (orthographically adjusted).

---

### 3. Semantic Composition (How the meaning is built)

The meaning of `opzeggen` is not simply the sum of "up" + "say." It is a **phrasal verb** where the combination creates a specific idiomatic meaning.

1.  **Literal Composition:** "To say up."
2.  **Idiomatic Meaning:** "To cancel," "to terminate," or "to resign."
3.  **Usage Context:** It is almost exclusively used regarding contracts, subscriptions, memberships, or employment (e.g., *een abonnement opzeggen*, *je ontslag opzeggen*).
