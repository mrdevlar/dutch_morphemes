From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`pieken`** can be analyzed as a **denominal verb** (a verb derived from a noun). It consists of two distinct morphemes.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation

| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **pieken** | **piek** | **-en** |

*   **Total Morphemes:** 2
*   **Structure:** `[Root] + [Suffix]`

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### **A. The Root: `piek`**
*   **Type:** Free Morpheme (Lexical Root)
*   **Original Part of Speech:** Noun (*de piek*)
*   **Meaning:** "Peak," "spike," "summit," or "highest point."
*   **Function:** This morpheme carries the core semantic content (the lexical meaning) of the word. In the context of the verb *pieken*, it refers to the action of reaching that highest point.
*   **Etymology:** The root is a loanword. It likely entered Dutch from Middle Low German or directly from French (*pic*, meaning "point" or "peak"). It is distinct from the native Germanic word *pik* (which relates to pecking or a sharp point, as in *pik* or *pikant*).
*   **Morphological Status:** In modern Dutch, *piek* exists independently as a noun (e.g., *"De piek van de berg"* — The peak of the mountain).

#### **B. The Suffix: `-en`**
*   **Type:** Bound Morpheme
*   **Function:**
    1.  **Derivational:** In this specific context, it functions to convert the noun stem *piek* into a verb. This process is called **denominalization**.
    2.  **Inflectional:** It marks the **infinitive** form of the verb. In Dutch, the standard infinitive ending for verbs is *-en* (or *-n* after a vowel).
*   **Pronunciation:** /ə(n)/ (schwa sound, often elided in rapid speech).
*   **Grammar:** It indicates that the word is a weak verb (regular conjugation).

---

### 3. Morphological Process: Denominalization

The formation of *pieken* is an example of **conversion** or **zero-derivation** combined with standard verbal inflection.

1.  **Base:** The noun *piek* (peak).
2.  **Process:** The noun is verbalized. In Dutch, adding *-en* to a noun stem is a common way to create a verb (e.g., *de fiets* $\rightarrow$ *fietsen*, *de duik* $\rightarrow$ *duiken*).
3.  **Result:** The verb *pieken* means "to peak" or "to spike."

**Semantic Shift:**
The meaning shifts from a static object (the peak) to a dynamic process (the act of rising to a peak).
*   *Example:* *"De temperaturen **pieken**."* (The temperatures **are peaking**.)

---

### 4. Conjugation and Inflection

Because *pieken* is a **weak verb** (regular), the stem *piek-* remains stable, and standard inflectional suffixes are added to it.

*   **Infinitive:** piek**en**
*   **Present (1st/3rd person):** piek**t** (e.g., *hij piekt*)
*   **Past Tense:** piek**te** (e.g., *hij piekte*)
*   **Past Participle:** ge**piekt**

*Note the insertion of a 't' in the past tense and participle due to the 'k' at the end of the stem (following the 't' rule in Dutch phonology).*

---

### 5. Linguistic Note: Homophony and Confusion

From a descriptive linguistic standpoint, it is important to distinguish *pieken* from the similar-sounding verb **`pikken`**.

*   **`Pieken`**: To peak/spike (from noun *piek*).
*   **`Pikken`**: To peck, to pick, or colloquially to itch/sting (from noun *pik* or verb *pikken*).

While they share a similar phonological structure (CVC + en), they have different etymological roots and distinct meanings. *Pieken* is associated with graphs, statistics, and heights, whereas *pikken* is associated with birds, tools, or physical sensation.
