From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`uitmaken`** is a **separable compound verb** (*scheidbaar werkwoord*).

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation of its components.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **`uit`** + **`mak`** + **`-en`**

Alternatively, for a broader lexical analysis, it is often viewed as two parts:
> **`uit`** (prefix) + **`maken`** (base verb)

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### A. `uit` (Prefix / Preverb)
*   **Type:** Separable verbal prefix (derived from a preposition/adverb).
*   **Literal Meaning:** "out," "outside," or "forth."
*   **Function:** It modifies the semantic direction or aspect of the base verb. In this specific compound, it adds a sense of completion, separation, or finality.
*   **Phonology:** In Dutch separable verbs, the primary stress falls on the prefix. Therefore, the pronunciation is **`UIT`-maken** (not uit-`MAKEN`). This distinguishes it from inseparable verbs (like *`be`-zoeken*), where the stress falls on the stem.

#### B. `mak` (Root)
*   **Type:** Lexical Root (Free Morpheme).
*   **Meaning:** "make" or "do."
*   **Function:** This carries the core semantic weight of the action. It is the same root found in the standalone verb *maken*.

#### C. `-en` (Suffix)
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix.
*   **Function:** Marks the verb in the **infinitive** form.
*   **Grammar:** In Dutch, the infinitive ending is almost always *-en* (or *-n* after a vowel). Without this suffix, the word would be the imperative or stem form (*maak*).

### 3. Grammatical Classification: Separable Verb
Linguistically, `uitmaken` is classified as a **separable verb** (*scheidbaar werkwoord*). This is a specific morphological-syntactic feature of Germanic languages (like Dutch and German).

*   **Morphological Evidence:** In the past participle, the prefix `uit` separates from the stem to allow the insertion of the participle marker `ge-`.
    *   *Infinitive:* `uit` + `maken`
    *   *Participle:* `uit` + **`ge`** + `mak` + `t` $\rightarrow$ **`uitgemaakt`**
    *   *Note:* If it were an inseparable verb, the `ge-` would not be inserted (e.g., *`be`-zoeken* $\rightarrow$ *`be`-zocht*).

*   **Syntactic Evidence:** In a main clause, the prefix `uit` splits off and moves to the end of the sentence (V2 word order).
    *   *Example:* "Ik **maak** het **uit**." (I make it out / I decide it).
    *   *Subordinate clause:* "...om het **uit te maken**." (where the infinitive stays together).

### 4. Semantic Analysis (Polysemy)
From a semantic perspective, `uitmaken` is **lexicalized**. This means the meaning of the whole word is not simply the sum of its parts ("to make out"). It has developed specific idiomatic meanings:

1.  **To decide/determine:** (e.g., *Wie maakt de regels uit?* - Who decides the rules?)
2.  **To break up (a relationship):** (e.g., *Ze hebben het uitgemaakt.* - They broke up.)
3.  **To switch off:** (e.g., *Maak het licht uit.* - Turn off the light.)
4.  **To matter:** (e.g., *Wat maakt het uit?* - What does it matter?)
