From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`strategisch`** (meaning "strategic") can be analyzed through **morphology** (the study of word structure) and **etymology** (the history of the word).

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
In modern Dutch morphology, the word is divided into two distinct morphemes:

> **`strateg`** + **`-isch`**

*   **`strateg`**: The **root** (or stem).
*   **`-isch`**: The **derivational suffix**.

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Root: `strateg-`
*   **Type:** Bound Root (in this specific context).
*   **Function:** Carries the core lexical meaning of the word.
*   **Status in Dutch:** In modern Dutch, `strateg-` does not exist as a standalone word. It appears as the stem of the noun **`strategie`** (strategy) or the noun **`strateeg`** (strategist). When forming the adjective, the suffix is attached directly to this stem.
*   **Etymology (Diachronic Analysis):**
    *   The root originates from Ancient Greek **`stratēgós`** (στρατηγός), meaning "general" or "leader of an army."
    *   It is a compound of two Greek morphemes:
        1.  **`stratos`** (στρατός): "army" or "host."
        2.  **`agein`** (ἄγειν): "to lead" or "to drive."
    *   **Semantic Shift:** Over time, the meaning shifted from the *person* (the general) to the *art of the general* (strategy), and finally to the *adjective describing that art* (strategic).

#### B. The Suffix: `-isch`
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix.
*   **Function:** Changes the part of speech from a noun (or noun stem) to an **adjective**. It indicates "pertaining to," "characterized by," or "of the nature of."
*   **Pronunciation:** In Dutch, this is pronounced as a voiceless fricative /ɪs/ (rhyming with "is"), though the 'g' in the preceding syllable is a voiced velar fricative /ɣ/ (similar to a soft 'ch' in Scottish "loch" or a guttural 'g').
*   **Cognates:** This suffix is the direct equivalent of the English suffix **`-ic`** or **`-ical`** (e.g., *strategic*, *musical*, *poetic*) and the German suffix **`-isch`** (e.g., *strategisch*).
*   **Origin:** It derives from the Greek adjectival suffix **`-ikos`**, which passed into Latin as **`-icus`**, and eventually entered the Germanic languages (including Dutch) via Old French and Middle Low German.

---

### 3. Morphological Process
The formation of this word is an example of **Derivation**.

1.  **Base:** The concept of *strategy* (noun: *strategie*).
2.  **Operation:** The suffix `-isch` is added to the stem `strateg-`.
3.  **Result:** A new word class is created (Adjective).

**Note on Spelling:**
You might notice the spelling difference between the noun *strate**gie*** and the adjective *strate**gisch***.
*   In the noun *strategie*, the 'g' is followed by 'ie'.
*   In the adjective *strategisch*, the 'ie' is dropped to attach the suffix `-isch`. This is a common morphophonological adjustment in Dutch to maintain the syllable structure and pronunciation of the root.
