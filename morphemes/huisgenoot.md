### 2. Morpheme Breakdown

*   **Linguistic Note:** In this compound, `huis` does not carry its own plural marker. In Dutch compounds, the first element usually remains in the singular form (unlike English, where we might say "housemates" but not "housesmates").

#### Morpheme 2: `genoot`
*   **Category:** Noun (Free Morpheme)
*   **Meaning:** "Companion," "associate," "partner," or "fellow."
*   **Function:** Head. This is the core meaning of the word; a `huisgenoot` is primarily a type of `genoot`.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from the verb *genieten* (to enjoy/partake).
    *   Historically, this breaks down further diachronically (historically) into the prefix **`ge-`** + the root **`noot`**.
    *   The prefix `ge-` in older Germanic often indicated a collective or reciprocal action (similar to the English prefix *be-* or *a-* in some contexts, or the collective *ge-* in *gebrüder*).
    *   The root relates to sharing or partaking. Therefore, a *genoot* is literally "one who shares/partakes with another."
*   **Cognates:**
    *   German: *Genosse* (comrade/associate)
    *   English: *guest* (via a different but related Proto-Germanic root *\*gastiz*, though the semantic link of "sharing a meal" is similar).
*   **Usage Note:** In modern Dutch, `genoot` is somewhat formal or archaic when used alone. It is most commonly found in compounds (like `huisgenoot`) or in the word `genootschap` (society/association).

### 3. Semantic Composition
The meaning of the compound is derived from the combination of the two parts:

*   **Literal:** "House-companion."
*   **Actual Meaning:** A person with whom one shares a living space (roommate, housemate, cohabitant).
*   **Head-Marker Relationship:** This is an **endocentric compound**. The head is `genoot` (the person), and `huis` modifies it to specify *what kind* of companion.
    *   *Contrast:* If it were exocentric, the meaning would be something outside the parts (e.g., "pickpocket" is not a type of pocket). Here, a `huisgenoot` *is* a `genoot`.
