From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`lenen`** can be analyzed through its morphological structure, phonological properties, and semantic history.

Here is the breakdown and explanation of the morphemes.

### 1. Morphemic Segmentation
The word consists of two distinct morphemes:

> **`len`** + **`-en`**

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Root/Stem: `len-`
*   **Type:** **Free Root (Lexical Morpheme)**
    *   While it cannot stand alone as a complete grammatical word in modern Dutch (you cannot say *"Ik len"*), it carries the core lexical meaning of the word. It is the base to which inflectional and derivational affixes attach.
*   **Meaning:** The semantic core relates to the transfer of possession of an object temporarily. Crucially, this root is **polysemous** in Dutch; it covers the concepts of both "to borrow" (receiving) and "to lend" (giving).
*   **Phonology (Morphophonemics):**
    *   Underlyingly, the root contains a short vowel /ɛ/ (as seen in the noun *lening* /ˈlɛ.nɪŋ/).
    *   However, in the infinitive form *lenen*, the syllable structure changes. Because the syllable is **open** (the consonant /n/ moves to the second syllable: *le-nen*), Dutch phonology applies the rule of **Open Syllable Lengthening**.
    *   Therefore, the /ɛ/ lengthens to /eː/.
    *   **Pronunciation:** /ˈleː.nə(n)/.

#### B. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Type:** **Bound Inflectional Morpheme**
*   **Function:** **Infinitive Marker**
    *   In Dutch grammar, the suffix *-en* is the standard marker for the **infinitive** form of verbs (equivalent to "to" + verb in English, e.g., "to borrow").
    *   It also marks the plural form of the present tense (e.g., *wij lenen* - "we borrow").
*   **Grammar Class:** This suffix identifies *lenen* as a **weak verb** (regular conjugation), distinguishing it from strong verbs which often change their stem vowel in the past tense (e.g., *lopen* -> *liep*).

### 3. Semantic Nuance (The Borrow/Lend Ambiguity)
From a semantic perspective, *lenen* is interesting because it lacks the directional distinction found in English.
*   **English:** *Borrow* (take) vs. *Lend* (give).
*   **Dutch:** *Lenen* (both).
    *   *Ik **leen** een boek.* (I borrow a book.)
    *   *Ik **leen** een boek uit.* (I lend a book out.)
    *   *Note:* To specify "lend," Dutch often adds the separable particle **`uit`** (out), creating the compound verb **`uitleenen`**.

### 4. Etymological Context
*   **Proto-Germanic:** The root traces back to *\*laizjaną* or *\*lāną*.
*   **Cognates:**
    *   **English:** *Loan* (noun), *Lend* (verb).
    *   **German:** *Leihen* (to lend/borrow).
    *   **Old English:** *Lān* (loan).
*   **Historical Shift:** In older Germanic languages, there was often a clearer distinction between the noun (loan) and the verb (to lend). In modern Dutch, the verb *lenen* absorbed the general concept of the transaction.

### 5. Derivational Family
The root `len-` is productive and forms the basis for several other words, demonstrating its status as a core lexical unit:
