From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **"camping"** is a fascinating example of a **loanword** (specifically an Anglicism) that has been morphologically integrated into the Dutch language.

Here is the breakdown and explanation of its morphemes.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into two morphemes:

> **camp** + **ing**

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Root: `camp`
*   **Type:** Free Morpheme (Root)
*   **Origin:** English (borrowed).
*   **Function:** Carries the core lexical meaning.
*   **Linguistic Note:** While the Dutch word for "camp" (military or historical) is *kamp*, the English root *camp* was borrowed specifically for the context of leisure and tourism. In this specific word, it acts as the semantic base.

#### B. The Suffix: `-ing`
*   **Type:** Bound Morpheme (Derivational Suffix)
*   **Origin:** English (borrowed), but functionally identical to a native Dutch suffix.
*   **Function:** Nominalization (turns a verb/concept into a noun).
*   **Linguistic Note:** This is the most interesting part of the analysis.
    *   **In English:** *-ing* is a gerund suffix (e.g., *to camp* $\rightarrow$ *camping* = the activity).
    *   **In Dutch:** *-ing* is a highly productive native suffix used to create feminine nouns from verbs (e.g., *lezen* [to read] $\rightarrow$ *lezing* [lecture]; *werken* [to work] $\rightarrow$ *werking* [effect]).
    *   **Integration:** When the English word was borrowed, Dutch speakers analyzed the *-ing* not just as part of the English word, but as a Dutch nominalizer. This allowed the word to fit naturally into Dutch grammar (e.g., it can take a plural *-s*).

### 3. Semantic Shift (Crucial Context)
While the morphemes are the same as in English, the **meaning** has shifted. This is a classic example of a "False Friend" or semantic narrowing/broadening upon borrowing.

*   **English "camping":** Refers to the **activity** (the act of staying in a tent/caravan).
*   **Dutch "camping":** Refers to the **place** (the campsite, the facility).
    *   *Example:* "Ik ga op camping" (I am going to the campsite).
    *   *To express the activity in Dutch:* You would typically say *kamperen* (a verb derived from *kamp* + *-eren*).

### 4. Grammatical Integration
Because the word is treated as a standard Dutch noun, it follows Dutch morphological rules for inflection:

*   **Plural:** *campings* (adding the Dutch plural suffix *-s*).
*   **Diminutive:** *campingetje* (adding the Dutch diminutive suffix *-etje*).
*   **Compound Words:** It can act as a modifier in compounds, e.g., *campingplek* (camping spot).

| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |

### Conclusion
Linguistically, **"camping"** is a **borrowed compound-like structure**. While it originates from English, it has been **reanalyzed** by Dutch speakers. They perceive the *-ing* as a Dutch nominalizing suffix attached to the root *camp*, allowing the word to behave grammatically like a native Dutch noun (taking Dutch plurals and diminutives) while retaining a meaning distinct from its English etymological counterpart.
