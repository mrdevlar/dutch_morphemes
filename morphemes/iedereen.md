From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`iedereen`** (meaning "everyone" or "everybody") is a **compound indefinite pronoun**.

Here is the detailed morphological breakdown and explanation.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphological units:

> **`ieder`** + **`-e-`** + **`een`**

| Morpheme | Form | Type | Meaning | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Root** | `ieder` | Free Morpheme | "Every" / "Each" | Determiner / Quantifier |
| **2. Inflection** | `-e-` | Bound Morpheme | (N/A) | Attributive Ending |
| **3. Head** | `een` | Free Morpheme | "One" | Numeral / Pronoun |

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Root: `ieder`
*   **Meaning:** This morpheme carries the semantic weight of "every" or "each."
*   **Part of Speech:** It functions primarily as a determiner (e.g., *ieder kind* = every child) or a pronoun.
*   **Etymology:** It stems from Middle Dutch *ieder* or *yeder*, which traces back to Old Dutch *iader*. It is cognate with the German *jeder* and has distant roots in the Proto-Germanic concept of "ever" or "always" (related to the English word *ever*).
*   **Variation:** In modern Dutch, `ieder` changes form based on the following word.
    *   **`Ieder`**: Used before consonants (e.g., *ieder mens*).
    *   **`Iedere`**: Used before vowels or in the attributive position before common gender nouns (e.g., *iedere dag*).

#### B. The Inflection: `-e-`
*   **Function:** This is an **attributive inflectional suffix**.
*   **Explanation:** In Dutch grammar, when a determiner like *ieder* modifies a noun (or a noun-like element), it typically takes an *-e* ending. Since the second part of this compound (`een`) functions as a noun ("one"), the first part takes the attributive form `iedere`.
*   **Orthography:** In the compound word `iedereen`, this inflectional *-e-* is fused into the spelling. It distinguishes the word from the bare stem *ieder*.

#### C. The Head: `een`
*   **Meaning:** "One."
*   **Part of Speech:** Numeral / Indefinite Article / Pronoun.
*   **Function:** In this compound, `een` acts as the **head** of the noun phrase. It nominalizes the quantifier `ieder`.
*   **Etymology:** From Old Dutch *ēn*, cognate with German *ein* and English *one*.
*   **Semantic Shift:** While `een` literally means "one," in this context, it generalizes to mean "person" or "individual."

### 3. Semantic Composition
The meaning of the compound is compositional (the meaning of the whole is derived from the sum of its parts):

*   **`ieder`** (every) + **`een`** (one) = **"Every one"**
*   **Result:** "Everyone" / "Everybody."

This mirrors the English construction of **`every`** + **`one`** $\rightarrow$ **`everyone`**.

### 4. Grammatical Properties
From a syntactic perspective, `iedereen` exhibits specific behaviors typical of Dutch indefinite pronouns:

1.  **Singular Agreement:** Despite referring to a group of people, `iedereen` takes a **singular verb**.
    *   *Correct:* Iedereen **is** hier. (Everyone **is** here.)
    *   *Incorrect:* Iedereen **zijn** hier.
2.  **Closed Compound:** Unlike English, which often writes "every one" as two words when emphasizing individuality, Dutch writes `iedereen` as a single closed compound word.
3.  **Possessive Reference:** When referring back to `iedereen`, Dutch often uses the singular possessive *zijn* (his/its) in formal grammar, though colloquially *hun* (their) is frequently used due to semantic plurality.
    *   *Formal:* Iedereen heeft **zijn** eigen mening. (Everyone has **his/their** own opinion.)
    *   *Colloquial:* Iedereen heeft **hun** eigen mening.

### 5. Related Forms
To fully understand `iedereen`, it helps to look at its morphological family:

*   **`Ieder`**: The bare stem (e.g., *Ieder voor zich* = Each for themselves).
*   **`Iedere`**: The inflected determiner (e.g., *Iedere dag* = Every day).
*   **`Elk`**: A synonym for *ieder* (each).
*   **`Elkeen`**: A synonym for *iedereen* (literally *elk* + *een*). This is often used for emphasis or in slightly more formal contexts.

### Summary
Linguistically, **`iedereen`** is a **closed compound** consisting of the quantifier **`ieder`** ("every"), the attributive inflection **`-e-`**, and the numeral **`een`** ("one"). It functions as a singular indefinite pronoun meaning "every individual person."
