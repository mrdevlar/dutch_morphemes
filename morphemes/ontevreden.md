From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`ontevreden`** (meaning "dissatisfied" or "discontented") is a complex derived adjective. It is formed through a process of **derivation** involving multiple layers of affixation.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes, followed by a detailed explanation of each component.

### 1. Morphemic Segmentation
While the word is often analyzed in two main parts (prefix + base), a deeper morphological analysis reveals four distinct morphemes:

> **`ont-`** + **`te-`** + **`vred`** + **`-en`**

*(Note: There is an orthographic simplification regarding the letter 't', explained below.)*

---

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. `ont-` (Derivational Prefix)
*   **Type:** Bound morpheme (Prefix).
*   **Function:** Negation / Reversative.
*   **Meaning:** This prefix indicates the opposite state or the removal of a condition. It is cognate with the English prefix *un-* (as in *unhappy*) or *de-* (as in *devalue*), though in Dutch, *ont-* is specifically used to negate verbs or verbal adjectives (participles).
*   **Usage:** It attaches to the base word *tevreden* to create its antonym.
    *   *Example:* *ontwikkelen* (to develop) vs. *ontwikkelen* (to develop); *ontvangen* (to receive). Here, it negates the state of being satisfied.

#### B. `te-` (Prepositional Prefix / Adverbial Element)
*   **Type:** Bound morpheme (in this context).
*   **Function:** Intensifier / Directional.
*   **Meaning:** Historically, this is the preposition *te* (meaning "to" or "too"). In the word *tevreden*, it originally signified a state of being "to peace" or "satisfied enough."
*   **Status:** In modern Dutch, *te-* is no longer a productive prefix on its own but is fossilized within the lexicalized word *tevreden* (satisfied).
*   **Orthographic Note:** When *ont-* is added to *tevreden*, standard Dutch spelling rules usually dictate a double 't' (e.g., *onttrekken*). However, in *ontevreden*, the double 't' is simplified to a single 't' for euphony (ease of pronunciation), as the *t* in *te* is unstressed.
    *   Underlying structure: *ont-* + *te-* + ...
    *   Surface form: *ont-* + *e-* + ... (The second 't' is elided in writing).

#### C. `vred` (Root / Stem)
*   **Type:** Free morpheme (Root).
*   **Meaning:** "Peace" or "Satisfaction."
*   **Etymology:** This root comes from the noun **`vrede`** (peace).
*   **Relation:** It is related to the adjective *vredig* (peaceful). In this specific construction, it carries the semantic weight of "contentment" or "agreement."

#### D. `-en` (Inflectional/Derivational Suffix)
*   **Type:** Bound morpheme (Suffix).
*   **Function:** Adjectival / Participial ending.
*   **Meaning:** This suffix turns the stem into an adjective. Historically, *tevreden* was a past participle of the archaic verb *vredigen* (to make peace/satisfy). The *-en* ending marks it as a participle functioning as an adjective.
*   **Comparison:** Similar to the English *-en* in *broken* or *satisfied* (though English uses *-ed* for past participles, Dutch often uses *-en* for strong verbs).

---

### 3. Historical & Syntactic Context

To fully understand the morphology, one must look at the history of the word:

1.  **Phrase to Word:** Originally, this was a prepositional phrase in Middle Dutch: **`te vrede`** (literally "to peace").
2.  **Lexicalization:** Over time, the phrase fused into a single adjective: **`tevreden`** (satisfied).
3.  **Negation:** To negate this state, the verbal prefix **`ont-`** was added.
    *   *Why `ont-` and not `on-`?* In Dutch, **`on-`** is used to negate pure adjectives (e.g., *onmogelijk* - impossible). **`ont-`** is used to negate verbs or verbal adjectives (participles). Because *tevreden* retains its verbal history (as a participle), it takes the verbal negator *ont-*.

### 5. English Comparison
The closest English equivalent is **`unsatisfied`**.
*   **`un-`** corresponds to **`ont-`**.
*   **`sat-`** (from *satis*) corresponds to **`vred`** (from *vrede*).
*   **`-isfied`** corresponds to **`-en`** (participial adjective).

However, the Dutch structure is more transparent regarding its etymological layers (*te* + *vrede*), whereas the English word *satisfied* has undergone more phonological erosion.
