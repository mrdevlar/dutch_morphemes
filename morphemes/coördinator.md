From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`coördinator`** is a complex word derived from Latin. It is a classic example of a **borrowed word** (loanword) that has been integrated into Dutch morphology and orthography.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation

The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **`co-`** + **`ordin`** + **`-ator`**

#### A. The Prefix: `co-`
*   **Type:** Bound morpheme (Prefix).
*   **Origin:** Latin *cum* (meaning "with" or "together").
*   **Function:** It modifies the meaning of the root to indicate joint action or association.
*   **Meaning:** "Together," "with," or "jointly."
*   **Examples:** *cooperatie* (cooperation), *coëxistentie* (coexistence).

#### B. The Root: `ordin`
*   **Type:** Bound root (Latinate).
*   **Origin:** Latin *ordo, ordinis* (meaning "order," "rank," or "arrangement") and the verb *ordinare* (to arrange).
*   **Function:** This carries the core lexical meaning of the word.
*   **Meaning:** "Order" or "arrangement."
*   **Note:** In Dutch, this root does not exist as a standalone word (you cannot say "ordin"). It is a **bound root** that only appears in loanwords.

#### C. The Suffix: `-ator`
*   **Type:** Derivational suffix (Agentive).
*   **Origin:** Latin *-ator* (masculine agent noun suffix).
*   **Function:** It turns the verb/action implied by the root into a noun referring to the **person** who performs that action.
*   **Meaning:** "One who does [the action]."
*   **Examples:** *operator* (one who operates), *narrator* (one who narrates).

### 2. Orthographic Analysis: The Diaeresis (Trema)

The most distinct linguistic feature of the Dutch spelling **`coördinator`** (compared to the English *coordinator*) is the **diaeresis** (or *trema*) over the second 'o' (**ö**).

*   **Symbol:** `¨` (Diaeresis/Trema)
*   **Linguistic Function:** It is a **diacritical mark**, not a morpheme itself. Its purpose is **phonological disambiguation**.
*   **The Problem:** In Dutch orthography, the sequence `oo` typically represents a single long vowel sound (phoneme /oː/), as in the word *boot* (boat).
*   **The Solution:** If the word were spelled *coordinator*, a Dutch reader might instinctively pronounce the `oo` as a single syllable (/koːrdi.../).
*   **The Rule:** The diaeresis indicates that the two vowels belong to **separate syllables** and must be pronounced separately.
    *   **Without diaeresis:** /koːr.di.na.tɔr/ (Incorrect in Dutch)
    *   **With diaeresis:** /ko.ˈɔr.di.na.tɔr/ (Correct)

Therefore, the `ö` signals the boundary between the prefix morpheme **`co-`** and the root morpheme **`ordin`**.
