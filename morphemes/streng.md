### 2. Diachronic Analysis (Etymology)

While it is a single morpheme today, a historical linguistic perspective reveals that it was once part of a larger Proto-Germanic structure. This explains its relationship to English and German.

*   **Proto-Germanic:** *\*strangaz*
    *   **Root:** *\*strang-* (meaning stiff, rigid, strong)
    *   **Ending:** *-az* (masculine nominative singular ending)
*   **Old Dutch:** *streng*
*   **Middle Dutch:** *streng*
*   **Cognates:**

**Semantic Shift:**
The original meaning was physical ("stiff," "tight," or "strong"). Over time, in Dutch, the meaning shifted metaphorically from physical rigidity to moral or behavioral rigidity ("strict," "severe").

### 3. Homonymy (Two Meanings)

Linguistically, it is important to note that *`streng`* functions as two different lexical items (homonyms) that share the same form but have slightly different grammatical behaviors.

1.  **Adjective:** *streng* (strict, severe).
    *   *Example:* "De leraar is **streng**." (The teacher is strict.)
2.  **Noun:** *streng* (a strand, a bunch, a bundle).
    *   *Example:* "Een **streng** hooi." (A bundle of hay.)
    *   *Plural:* *strengen*.

**Etymological Connection:**
These two meanings are likely related. A "bundle" is something tied **tightly** (stiff/strong), which connects back to the Proto-Germanic root *\*strangaz*.
