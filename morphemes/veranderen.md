From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch verb **`veranderen`** (meaning "to change" or "to alter") can be segmented into three distinct morphemes.

Here is the morphological breakdown:

### 1. Segmentation
**`ver`** + **`ander`** + **`en`**

---

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### A. `ver-` (Prefix)
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix.
*   **Function:** This is a highly productive prefix in Dutch (and German). It typically indicates a **change of state**, a transition, or the completion of an action. It often transforms an adjective or noun into a verb.
*   **Semantics:** In this specific context, `ver-` adds the meaning of "making into" or "undergoing a process of."
*   **Grammar:** Unlike separable prefixes (like *op-* or *af-*), `ver-` is an **inseparable prefix**. This means the stress falls on the root (`ander`), not the prefix, and it does not detach in conjugated forms (e.g., *ik verander*, not *ik ver ander*).

#### B. `ander` (Root / Lexeme)
*   **Type:** Free Root Morpheme.
*   **Origin:** This root is cognate with the Dutch adjective **`ander`** (meaning "other" or "different") and the English word **`other`**. Both stem from the Proto-Germanic **`*aljanaz`**.
*   **Semantics:** The core meaning here is "otherness."
*   **Logic:** To "change" is semantically equivalent to "to become other" or "to make different."

#### C. `-en` (Suffix)
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix.
*   **Function:** This is the standard marker for the **infinitive** form of Dutch verbs.
*   **Grammar:** It indicates that the word is a verb in its base form. When the verb is conjugated for the first or second person singular (e.g., *ik verander*, *jij verandert*), this suffix is dropped or modified.
*   **Note:** While `-en` is also the plural marker for Dutch nouns (e.g., *hond* → *honden*), in this context, it is strictly verbal.

---

### 3. Etymological & Semantic Synthesis

When you combine these morphemes, the literal semantic construction is:
> **"To make [something] other"** $\rightarrow$ **"To change."**

*   **Cognates:** The word is very similar to the German **`verändern`**.
*   **English Connection:** While English does not use the `ver-` prefix, the root `ander` is related to the English word **`alter`** (via Latin *alter* meaning "other," which shares the Indo-European root with the Germanic *ander*).

### 4. Morphological Behavior (Conjugation)
To understand the morphemes fully, it helps to see how they behave in a sentence. The root `ander` remains stable, while the suffix changes based on tense and person.

*   **Infinitive:** `ver` + `ander` + `en` (*Ik wil **veranderen**.*)
*   **Present (1st Person):** `ver` + `ander` + `Ø` (*Ik **verander**.*) — *The -en is dropped.*
*   **Past Tense:** `ver` + `ander` + `de` (*Ik **veranderde**.*) — *The -en is replaced by the past tense marker -de.*
*   **Past Participle:** `ge` + `ver` + `ander` + `d` (*Ik heb **veranderd**.*) — *Note: The inseparable prefix `ver-` prevents the addition of the `ge-` prefix usually found in Dutch participles.*
