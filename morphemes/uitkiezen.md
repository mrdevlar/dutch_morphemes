From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`uitkiezen`** is a compound verb consisting of a separable prefix and a base verb. Below is a detailed morphological and semantic breakdown.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation

The word can be segmented into two primary morphemes, with the second morpheme containing a further internal structure:

> **`uit`** + **`kiez`** + **`-en`**

Or, more commonly analyzed in terms of word formation:

> **`uit`** (Prefix) + **`kiezen`** (Base Verb)

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Prefix: `uit`
*   **Type:** Separable Prefix (*scheidbaar voorvoegsel*).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *ut*, cognate with English *out* and German *aus*.
*   **Grammatical Function:** In this context, it is not functioning as a preposition (indicating location), but as a verbal particle that modifies the meaning of the verb.
*   **Semantic Contribution:**
    *   The base verb *kiezen* means "to choose" or "to decide."
    *   The addition of *uit* adds a **delimitative** or **resultative** aspect. It implies choosing *out* of a group, picking *out* specific items, or selecting from a larger set.
    *   *Nuance:* While *kiezen* can be abstract (e.g., "I choose to live here"), *uitkiezen* is almost always concrete (e.g., "I pick out a shirt").

#### B. The Root: `kiez-`
*   **Type:** Verbal Stem (*stam*).
*   **Etymology:** From Old Dutch *kiesen*, Proto-Germanic *\*keusaną*, and Proto-Indo-European *\*gʰeus-* (meaning "to taste, try, or choose").
*   **Phonology:** The *ie* represents a long vowel /i/.
*   **Morphological Class:** It is a **strong verb** (irregular).
    *   Infinitive: *kiezen*
    *   Past Tense: *koos*
    *   Past Participle: *gekozen*
*   **Note:** In the compound *uitkiezen*, the root remains *kiez-*, but the verb follows the conjugation patterns of the base verb *kiezen*.

#### C. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Type:** Infinitive Marker (*infinitiefuitgang*).
*   **Function:** This is the standard ending for Dutch verbs in their dictionary form (infinitive). It carries no lexical meaning but marks the grammatical category of the word as a verb.

---

### 3. Syntactic Behavior: Separability

A crucial linguistic feature of *uitkiezen* is that it is a **separable verb** (*scheidbaar werkwoord*). This affects how the morphemes behave in a sentence structure.

*   **Infinitive/Dictionary Form:** The morphemes are joined.
    *   *Ik wil een auto **uitkiezen**.* (I want to pick out a car.)
*   **Main Clause (Present Tense):** The prefix *uit* detaches and moves to the end of the clause.
    *   *Ik **kies** een auto **uit**.* (I pick out a car.)
*   **Past Participle:** The prefix *uit-* is separated by the participle marker *ge-*.
    *   *Ik heb een auto **uitgekozen**.* (I have picked out a car.)
    *   *Structure:* `uit` + `ge` + `koos` + `en`

### 4. Semantic Comparison

To understand the morphological impact, compare the base verb with the compound:

| Word | Morphemes | Meaning | Usage Context |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Kiezen** | `kiez` + `-en` | To choose / To decide | Abstract or general decisions (e.g., choosing a path, choosing a candidate). |
| **Uitkiezen** | `uit` + `kiez` + `-en` | To pick out / To select | Concrete selection from a visible array (e.g., picking out clothes, picking out a song). |

### 5. Summary of Linguistic Features

1.  **Derivational Morphology:** The prefix *uit-* is derivational; it creates a new lexical item with a slightly different meaning from the base verb *kiezen*.
2.  **Compound Verb:** It is a verb-verb compound (specifically, a particle verb).
3.  **Cognates:**
    *   **English:** *out* + *choose* (though English does not typically compound these into a single word like "outchoose").
    *   **German:** *aus* + *wählen* (similar meaning, different root).
    *   **Frisian:** *út* + *kieze*.

In conclusion, **`uitkiezen`** is a morphologically transparent Dutch compound verb where the directional prefix **`uit`** restricts the general action of **`kiezen`** to the specific act of selecting items from a group.
