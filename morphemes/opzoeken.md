From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`opzoeken`** is a complex verb formed through compounding and derivation. It is a classic example of a **separable verb** (*scheidbaar werkwoord*).

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation of its components.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **`op`** + **`zoek`** + **`-en`**

| Morpheme | Type | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`op`** | Free Morpheme (Particle) | Verbal prefix / Directional modifier |
| **`zoek`** | Bound Morpheme (Stem) | Lexical root (Verb) |
| **`-en`** | Bound Morpheme (Suffix) | Infinitive marker |

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. `op` (The Particle)
*   **Category:** Preposition / Adverb / Verbal Particle.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *up*, tracing back to Proto-Germanic **\*upp* and Proto-Indo-European **\*upo* (under, up, towards).
*   **Semantic Function:**
    *   **Literal:** Indicates direction "up" or position "on."
    *   **Figurative (in this context):** In the compound `opzoeken`, the spatial meaning is metaphorical. It implies **retrieval** or **access**. Just as you might lift something *up* to see it, you "lift" information out of a database or book.
    *   **Syntactic Behavior:** In Dutch, this is a **separable prefix**. In a main clause, it detaches from the verb stem and moves to the end of the sentence (e.g., *Ik **zoek** het woord **op***). In the infinitive form (`opzoeken`), it remains attached.

#### B. `zoek` (The Verb Stem)
*   **Category:** Lexical Root (Verb Stem).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *sōkian*, tracing back to Proto-Germanic **\*sōkōną* (to seek).
*   **Semantic Function:** This carries the core lexical meaning of the word: **to search, to seek, or to look for.**
*   **Morphological Note:** This is the base to which tense markers are applied. For example, in the past tense, the stem changes (ablaut) to *zocht* (strong verb conjugation).

#### C. `-en` (The Infinitive Suffix)
*   **Category:** Inflectional Suffix.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *-en*, tracing back to Proto-Germanic **\*-aną*.
*   **Semantic Function:** This is a grammatical marker with no lexical meaning of its own. It marks the verb as being in the **infinitive** form (the dictionary form).
*   **Phonology:** In many Dutch dialects, this is pronounced as a schwa /ə/ or a nasalized vowel, though in standard spelling, it remains `-en`.

---

### 3. Semantic Composition (The Whole)
When combined, the morphemes create a specific meaning that is more than the sum of its parts (idiomaticity):

*   **`zoeken`** = To search (general, open-ended).
*   **`opzoeken`** = To look up (specific, result-oriented).

**Linguistic Nuance:**
The particle `op` adds a sense of **completion** or **specific target**.
*   *Ik zoek een huis.* (I am searching for a house / I am house-hunting.)
*   *Ik zoek het adres **op**.* (I am looking up the address / I am retrieving the specific address.)

### 4. Related Morphological Forms
To fully understand the morphology of `opzoeken`, one must look at how the morphemes behave in other tenses, specifically the **Past Participle**.

*   **Infinitive:** `op` + `zoek` + `en` $\rightarrow$ **opzoeken**
*   **Past Participle:** `op` + **`ge`** + `zoek` + `t` $\rightarrow$ **opgezocht**

**The `ge-` Infix:**
In Dutch, when forming the past participle of a separable verb, the standard prefix `ge-` is inserted **between** the particle (`op`) and the stem (`zoek`). This is a crucial morphological rule in Dutch grammar.
*   *Incorrect:* *geopzoekt*
*   *Correct:* *op**ge**zocht*

### Summary
From a linguistic standpoint, **`opzoeken`** is a **separable compound verb**.
1.  **`op`**: A directional particle modifying the action to imply retrieval.
2.  **`zoek`**: The Germanic root for "seek."
3.  **`-en`**: The standard infinitive suffix.

Together, they form a verb meaning "to retrieve information by searching," distinguishing it from the general act of searching (`zoeken`).
