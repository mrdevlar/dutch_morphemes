From a morphological (linguistic) perspective, the Dutch verb **`kijken`** (meaning "to look" or "to watch") is a **monomorphemic root** combined with an **inflectional suffix**.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes:

### 1. The Segmentation
**`kijk`** + **`-en`**

---

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### A. Root / Stem: `kijk-`
*   **Type:** Lexical Root (Free Morpheme).
*   **Meaning:** This carries the core semantic content of the word: the action of directing one's gaze, seeing, or watching.
*   **Status:** While it appears bound in the infinitive form (`kijken`), this root is a **free morpheme** in other contexts. For example, it can stand alone as an imperative command: *"Kijk!"* (Look!).
*   **Phonology:** The `ij` represents a single vowel sound (a diphthong /ɛi/), and the `k` is a voiceless velar plosive.

#### B. Suffix: `-en`
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix (Bound Morpheme).
*   **Function:**
    1.  **Infinitive Marker:** In Dutch, the standard dictionary form of a verb (the infinitive) is formed by adding `-en` to the stem.
    2.  **Plural Present Tense:** This same suffix is used for the first, second, and third person plural in the present tense (e.g., *wij kijken* - we look).
*   **Category:** It does not change the part of speech (the word remains a verb); therefore, it is **inflectional**, not derivational.

---

### 3. Grammatical Context
In Dutch morphology, verbs are generally categorized by their stem.
*   **Stem:** *kijk*
*   **Infinitive:** *kijk* + *en* = *kijken*
*   **Present Tense (Singular):** *kijk* (e.g., *ik kijk* - I look)
*   **Present Tense (Plural):** *kijk* + *en* (e.g., *wij kijken* - we look)
*   **Past Tense:** *keek* (This is a strong verb conjugation, where the vowel changes, but the root remains recognizable).

### 4. Derivational Evidence
To prove that `kijk-` is the true root, we can look at other words derived from it. If we add different suffixes to `kijk-`, we create new words with related meanings:
*   **`kijk`** + **`-er`** = **`kijker`** (Viewer / Telescope / Binoculars)
*   **`kijk`** + **`-s`** = **`kijk`** (Noun form, colloquial)
*   **`kijk`** + **`-baar`** = **`kijkbaar`** (Viewable)

### 5. Etymological Note
From a historical linguistics perspective, `kijken` comes from Middle Dutch *kiken*, which is related to the sound of looking or peeping. It is cognate with the English word **`peek`** (via the sound symbolism of short, sharp sounds associated with looking) and the German colloquial *kicken* (to look/watch).
