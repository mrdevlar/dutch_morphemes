From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`oplossing`** (meaning "solution" or "dissolution") can be analyzed through **derivational morphology**. It is a noun formed from a verb.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **`op-`** + **`los`** + **`-ing`**

*(Note: While the spelling contains a double 's', morphologically the root is just `los`. The second 's' is orthographic, explained below.)*

---

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. Prefix: `op-`
*   **Type:** Separable Prefix (in the verb form).
*   **Meaning:** Literally "up" or "on." In the context of verbs, it often implies completion, opening, or a change of state.
*   **Function:** It modifies the meaning of the root.
*   **Linguistic Note:** In the verb *oplossen* (to solve), `op` is a **separable prefix** (e.g., *Ik los het probleem **op***). However, in the derived noun *oplossing*, it functions as an inseparable part of the lexical unit.

#### B. Root (Stem): `los`
*   **Type:** Lexical Root (Free Morpheme).
*   **Meaning:** "Loose," "free," or "detached."
*   **Etymology:** From Proto-Germanic *\*lausaz*.
*   **Semantic Shift:**
    *   **Physical:** To make something loose (e.g., a knot).
    *   **Abstract:** To "loosen" a problem, i.e., to solve it.
    *   **Chemical:** To make a solid loose within a liquid, i.e., to dissolve.
*   **Phonology:** The vowel `o` is short (/ɔ/).

#### C. Suffix: `-ing`
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix.
*   **Function:** **Nominalization**. It converts a verb (or verb stem) into a noun.
*   **Meaning:** It denotes the **action** of the verb or the **result** of that action.
*   **Grammar:** It creates a common noun (de-word).
*   **Etymology:** From Proto-Germanic *\*-ingaz*.

---

### 3. Orthographic Note: The Double 'S'
You may notice the word is spelled with two 's's (**`oplossing`**), but the morphological root is only **`los`**.

*   **The Rule:** In Dutch orthography, a short vowel (like the /ɔ/ in *los*) must be followed by a double consonant in the infinitive form of a verb to prevent the vowel from becoming long.
    *   Root: *los* (short vowel).
    *   Verb Infinitive: *oplos**s**en*.
*   **The Noun:** When deriving the noun *oplossing*, the spelling follows the verb stem *lossen*. Therefore, the second 's' is **not a separate morpheme**; it is an orthographic marker required to maintain the short vowel sound of the root `los`.
    *   *Incorrect Morphological Break:* `op` + `loss` + `ing`
    *   *Correct Morphological Break:* `op` + `los` + `ing` (with orthographic doubling).

---

### 4. Semantic Composition (How the meaning is built)
The meaning of the word is compositional, built from the parts:

1.  **`los`** (loose) + **`op`** (up/on) $\rightarrow$ **`oplossen`** (to loosen up / to solve).
2.  **`oplossen`** (verb) + **`-ing`** (noun suffix) $\rightarrow$ **`oplossing`** (the act of solving OR the result of solving).

**Summary of Meaning:**
*   **Literal:** The act of making something loose.
*   **Figurative:** A solution to a problem (the thing that "loosens" the difficulty).
*   **Scientific:** A liquid mixture where a solid has been dissolved.

### 5. Related Word Family
To understand the morphology better, it helps to look at the word family:
*   **`los`** (adjective): Loose.
*   **`oplossen`** (verb): To solve / to dissolve.
*   **`oplossing`** (noun): Solution.
*   **`oplosbaar`** (adjective): Soluble (using suffix *-baar* instead of *-ing*).
