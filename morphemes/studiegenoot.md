From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`studiegenoot`** is a **compound noun** (*samenstelling*). It is formed by combining two distinct lexical units (morphemes) to create a new meaning.

Here is the breakdown and explanation of the morphemes.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into two primary morphemes:

> **`studie`** + **`genoot`**

### 2. Analysis of Morpheme 1: `studie`
*   **Form:** `studie`
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun
*   **Meaning:** "Study," "education," or "academic pursuit."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from the Latin word *studium* (meaning zeal, diligence, or study), which entered Dutch via French (*étude*).
*   **Function in Compound:** In this compound, `studie` acts as the **modifier** (or determiner). It specifies the *type* of companion. It answers the question: "A companion of what?"
*   **Phonology:** In Dutch compounds, the primary stress usually falls on the first element. Therefore, the stress is on **`STU`**diegenoot.

### 3. Analysis of Morpheme 2: `genoot`
*   **Form:** `genoot`
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun
*   **Meaning:** "Companion," "associate," "fellow member," or "partner."
*   **Etymology:** This is a Germanic root. It stems from Middle Dutch *genoot* and Old Dutch *ginōt*.
    *   **Internal Structure:** Historically, `genoot` can be further analyzed derivationally as **`ge-`** + **`noot`**.
        *   **`ge-`**: A prefix often used to form collective nouns or nouns indicating a person associated with an action/state (similar to the English prefix *be-* or *a-* in some contexts, or the German *Ge-*).
        *   **`noot`**: The root is related to the verb *nieten* (to rivet/fasten) or the concept of being "bound together." Thus, etymologically, a *genoot* is someone "fastened" or "joined" to you.
*   **Function in Compound:** `genoot` acts as the **head** of the compound. The entire word refers to a type of *genoot*.

### 4. Syntactic and Semantic Structure
*   **Compound Type:** **Endocentric Compound**.
    *   This means the meaning of the whole word is a hyponym (a specific type) of the head word. A *studiegenoot* is a specific kind of *genoot*.
*   **Semantic Relation:** **Possession/Association**.
    *   The relationship is "A companion *of* a study."
*   **Linking Element:** There is **no linking element** (such as *-s-* or *-en-*) between the two morphemes.
    *   *Note:* In Dutch, linking elements are often added for phonological ease or grammatical agreement (e.g., *liefdes*verklaring). However, when the first noun ends in a vowel (like *studie*) and the second begins with a consonant, a direct concatenation is common.

### 5. Inflection (Pluralization)
When pluralizing this compound word, Dutch grammar dictates that the plural suffix attaches to the **head** of the compound (the last element).

*   **Singular:** `studiegenoot`
*   **Plural:** `studiegenoot` + **`en`** = **`studiegenoten`**
