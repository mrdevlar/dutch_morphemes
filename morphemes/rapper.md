From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`rapper`** is an interesting case because it is a **loanword** (specifically an anglicism) that has been fully integrated into Dutch morphology. While it originates from English, its structure aligns perfectly with native Dutch word-formation rules.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into two distinct morphemes:

> **`rap`** + **`-er`**

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Root: `rap`
*   **Type:** Free Morpheme (Lexical Root)
*   **Origin:** English (borrowed into Dutch).
*   **Etymology:** Originally onomatopoeic (imitating the sound of a knock or beat), evolving into the verb "to rap" (to speak rhythmically) and the noun "rap" (the music genre).
*   **Function in Dutch:** In the context of this word, `rap` functions as the semantic base. It denotes the action or the genre.
    *   *Note:* In Dutch, `rap` is also used as a loan verb (e.g., *"Hij rapt"* - He raps).
*   **Status:** While the root is foreign, it is treated as a standard lexical unit within the Dutch mental lexicon.

#### B. The Suffix: `-er`
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix (Agentive)
*   **Origin:** Germanic (Shared by English and Dutch).
*   **Function:** This is a highly **productive** suffix in Dutch. It attaches to verbs (or nouns) to create an **agent noun** (a person who performs the action).
    *   *Examples:* `werk` (work) + `-er` = `werker` (worker); `lees` (read) + `-er` = `lezer` (reader); `dans` (dance) + `-er` = `danser` (dancer).
*   **Integration:** Although the word `rapper` was borrowed from English, Dutch speakers analyze the `-er` ending as the native Dutch agentive suffix. This makes the word **transparent** to Dutch morphology. It is not perceived as an unanalyzable chunk, but as a constructed word.

### 3. Morphological Process
The formation of the word involves **Derivation**.
*   **Process:** `rap` (Verb/Noun) $\rightarrow$ `rapper` (Noun).
*   **Change:** The suffix changes the category from an action/genre to a person (Agent Nominalization).

### 4. Inflectional Morphology (Pluralization)
Once the word is established as a noun, it undergoes Dutch inflectional rules.
*   **Singular:** `rapper`
*   **Plural:** `rappers`
*   **Morpheme:** `-s`
*   **Analysis:** The plural marker `-s` is added *after* the derivational suffix `-er`.
    *   Structure: `rap` + `-er` + `-s`
    *   *Linguistic Note:* While some Dutch loanwords take the `-en` plural (e.g., *film* $\rightarrow$ *filmen*), words ending in `-er` typically take `-s` (e.g., *computer* $\rightarrow$ *computers*, *rapper* $\rightarrow$ *rappers*).

### 6. Linguistic Conclusion
The word **`rapper`** illustrates **morphological integration**. Even though the root (`rap`) is not native to Dutch, the suffix (`-er`) is native. Because the suffix is productive in Dutch, the word is not treated as a "frozen" loanword (like *koffie* or *auto*, where the internal structure is opaque to speakers). Instead, it is treated as a compound of a loan root and a native suffix, allowing it to follow standard Dutch pluralization rules (`-s`).

Therefore, a Dutch speaker intuitively parses the word as **[rap]-[er]**, just as they would parse **[dans]-[er]**.
