From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`verbieden`** (meaning "to forbid" or "to prohibit") can be analyzed as a **derived verb**. It consists of three distinct morphemes: a derivational prefix, a lexical root (stem), and an inflectional suffix.

Here is the morphological breakdown:

### 1. Segmentation
**`ver`** + **`bied`** + **`en`**

---

### 2. Morpheme-by-Morpheme Analysis

#### **A. `ver-` (Derivational Prefix)**
*   **Type:** Derivational Morpheme.
*   **Function:** This prefix modifies the meaning of the base verb. In Dutch, *ver-* is one of the most common inseparable prefixes.
*   **Semantic Role:** While *ver-* can indicate a change of state, completion, or error (e.g., *vergeten* - to forget), in this specific context, it creates a **privative** or **oppositional** meaning. It transforms the concept of "offering" into "not allowing."
*   **Phonological Feature:** This prefix is **inseparable**. In pronunciation, the stress falls on the root, not the prefix (ver-**BIE**-den). This distinguishes it from separable verbs (like *aanbieden*, where stress is on the prefix).

#### **B. `bied` (Lexical Root / Stem)**
*   **Type:** Bound Root (in this context).
*   **Source:** This is the stem of the base verb **`bieden`** (to offer).
*   **Semantic Shift:** This is a fascinating linguistic example of semantic drift.
    *   *Bieden* = to offer / to bid.
    *   *Verbieden* = to forbid.
    *   Historically, the meaning shifted from "to offer a command" to "to command" to "to prohibit." In modern Dutch morphology, `bied` carries the core lexical meaning, but its semantic value is heavily altered by the prefix `ver-`.
*   **Morphophonology:** The root contains the vowel /i/ (long 'ie'). This root is part of a **strong verb** conjugation pattern (ablaut), meaning the vowel changes in the past tense (*bood*) and past participle (*geboden*), though the infinitive form retains the /i/.

#### **C. `-en` (Inflectional Suffix)**
*   **Type:** Inflectional Morpheme.
*   **Function:** This is the standard marker for the **infinitive** form of Dutch verbs.
*   **Grammatical Role:** It indicates that the word is a verb in its dictionary form. It also serves as the ending for the plural present tense (e.g., *wij verbieden* - we forbid).
*   **Productivity:** This is a highly productive suffix in Dutch; almost all regular and irregular verbs take *-en* in the infinitive.

---

### 3. Summary of Morphological Structure

| Morpheme | Type | Meaning/Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **ver-** | Derivational Prefix | Changes meaning (Offer $\rightarrow$ Forbid); Inseparable. |
| **bied** | Root / Stem | Core lexical meaning (from *bieden* / to offer). |
| **-en** | Inflectional Suffix | Marks the infinitive verb form. |

### 4. Linguistic Notes

**1. Inseparability:**
It is crucial to note that *verbieden* is an **inseparable verb**.
*   **Correct:** *Ik **verbied** het.* (I forbid it.)
*   **Incorrect:** *Ik **ver** het **bied**.*
This contrasts with separable verbs like *opbieden* (to summon), where the prefix moves to the end of the clause (*Ik **bied** het **op***).

**2. Etymology:**
The word comes from Old Dutch *verbieden*, which traces back to Proto-Germanic *\*furbiodan*.
*   *\*fur-* (before/forth) + *\*biodan* (to offer).
*   The original sense was likely "to offer something before someone" (as a command), which evolved into "to order" and eventually "to prohibit."

**3. Strong Verb Class:**
While the morpheme breakdown remains the same, the root `bied` belongs to **Class 3 Strong Verbs**. This means the vowel changes during conjugation (Ablaut):
*   Infinitive: *verbied**en***
*   Past Singular: *verb**o**d*
*   Past Participle: *ge**bo**den*
