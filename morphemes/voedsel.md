From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`voedsel`** (meaning "food" or "nourishment") is a **deverbal noun**. It is formed by adding a derivational suffix to a verbal stem.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Morphological Breakdown

| Morpheme | Form | Type | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Root** | `voed-` | Verbal Stem | Carries the core lexical meaning ("to feed/nourish"). |
| **Suffix** | `-sel` | Derivational Suffix | Nominalizer; turns the verb into a neuter noun indicating the result or substance. |
| **Total** | **`voed` + `sel`** | **Noun** | **Food / Nourishment** |

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis

#### A. The Root: `voed-`
*   **Source:** This stem comes from the Dutch verb **`voeden`** (to feed, to nourish).
*   **Etymology:** The root traces back to the Proto-Germanic **`*fōd-`**.
*   **Cognates:** This is the direct etymological cousin of the English word **`food`**.
    *   *Dutch:* `voed` (stem) / `voedsel` (noun)
    *   *English:* `food` (noun) / `feed` (verb)
    *   *German:* `Futter` (fodder) / `Nahrung` (nutrition)
*   **Phonology:** The stem ends in the voiced alveolar plosive **/d/**. In the word *voedsel*, this sound remains voiced (unlike in some final-position Dutch words where devoicing occurs).

#### B. The Suffix: `-sel`
*   **Type:** This is a **deverbal nominalizing suffix**.
*   **Function:** It transforms an action (verb) into a concrete noun, typically indicating:
    1.  **The result of the action:** (e.g., *koken* "to cook" $\rightarrow$ *kooksel* "boiled mixture").
    2.  **The substance used for the action:** (e.g., *vullen* "to fill" $\rightarrow$ *vulsel* "stuffing").
    3.  **The instrument:** (e.g., *dekken* "to cover" $\rightarrow$ *deksel* "lid").
*   **Grammar:** Nouns ending in *-sel* are almost exclusively **neuter** (*het-words*). Therefore, we say **`het voedsel`**.
*   **Pluralization:** *Voedsel* is typically treated as a mass noun (uncountable). If a plural is required (e.g., referring to different types of food), Dutch speakers often use the compound **`voedingsmiddelen`** (foodstuffs) rather than *voedsele* or *voedsels*, though *voedsels* exists in technical contexts.

---

### 3. Semantic Shift
The morphology reflects a shift from **process** to **product**:
*   **Verb (`voeden`):** The *action* of providing sustenance.
*   **Noun (`voedsel`):** The *substance* that is provided.

### 4. Related Derivations
To understand the productivity of the root `voed-`, it is helpful to look at related words:
*   **`Voeding`**: Uses the suffix *-ing*. This refers to "nutrition" or "diet" (the concept or system of feeding) rather than the physical substance.
*   **`Voedingsmiddel`**: A compound noun (*voeding* + *s* + *middel*). Literally "nutrition-means," meaning "foodstuff" or "ingredient."
*   **`Voedzaam`**: Uses the suffix *-zaam* (adjective-forming). Means "nutritious" or "nourishing."

### Summary
The word **`voedsel`** is a transparent morphological construction in Dutch. It combines the stem of the verb **`voeden`** (to feed) with the nominalizing suffix **`-sel`** to create a neuter noun meaning "that which is used for feeding," which is cognate with the English word **`food`**.
