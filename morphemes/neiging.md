From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`neiging`** is a derived noun formed through a process of **derivational morphology**. It consists of two distinct morphemes: a verbal root and a nominalizing suffix.

Here is the breakdown and explanation of the morphemes:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented as follows:
> **`neig`** + **`-ing`**

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Root/Stem: `neig-`
*   **Type:** Free Morpheme (specifically, a verbal stem).
*   **Source Lexeme:** The infinitive verb **`neigen`**.
*   **Meaning:** To lean, to incline, to bend, or to tend toward something.
*   **Grammatical Function:** This is the base upon which the word is built. In Dutch morphology, to derive a noun from a verb, the infinitive ending **`-en`** is removed to reveal the stem.
    *   *Infinitive:* `neig` + `en`
    *   *Stem:* `neig`
*   **Etymology:** This root traces back to Proto-Germanic **`*nēganą`** (to bow, to lean). It is cognate with the English word **`nigh`** (in the sense of being near/leaning towards) and the German **`neigen`**.
*   **Phonology:** In the verb *neigen*, the `g` is pronounced as a voiced velar fricative (/ɣ/) or a velar stop (/g/), depending on the dialect.

#### B. The Suffix: `-ing`
*   **Type:** Bound Morpheme (Derivational Suffix).
*   **Function:** **Nominalizer**. It changes the part of speech from a verb (action/process) to a noun (concept/result).
*   **Meaning:** It indicates the action, process, or result of the verb. In this context, it transforms the physical act of "leaning" into the abstract concept of an "inclination" or "tendency."
*   **Comparison:** This suffix is cognate with the English suffix **`-ing`** (as in *building* or *feeling*) and the German suffix **`-ung`** (as in *Neigung*).
*   **Phonology:** In Dutch, the spelling `-ing` represents the phoneme sequence /ɪŋ/. The `g` is not pronounced as a separate consonant; instead, it merges with the `n` to form a **velar nasal** (/ŋ/).
    *   *Pronunciation:* /ˈnɛi.ɪŋ/

---

### 3. Morphological Process: Derivation
The formation of *neiging* is an example of **suffixation**.

1.  **Base:** The verb *neigen* (to incline).
2.  **Operation:** Removal of the infinitive marker *-en* and attachment of the nominalizing suffix *-ing*.
3.  **Result:** A feminine noun (de-woord) that refers to a propensity or tendency.

### 4. Semantic Shift
While the morphology is straightforward, there is a semantic shift from the concrete to the abstract:
*   **Literal (Verb):** To physically lean (e.g., *De toren neigt* — The tower leans).
*   **Abstract (Noun):** A mental or behavioral tendency (e.g., *Hij heeft een neiging tot optimisme* — He has a tendency toward optimism).
