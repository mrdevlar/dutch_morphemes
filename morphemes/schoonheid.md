From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`schoonheid`** (meaning "beauty") is a classic example of **derivational morphology**. It is formed by combining a lexical root with a derivational suffix to change the word's part of speech and meaning.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes:

### 1. Morphemic Segmentation
The word can be segmented into two distinct morphemes:

> **schoon** + **heid**

---

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Root: `schoon`
*   **Type:** Free Morpheme (it can stand alone as an independent word).
*   **Part of Speech:** Adjective.
*   **Meaning:** "Clean," "pure," or "beautiful."
    *   *Linguistic Note:* In Dutch, the adjective *schoon* covers a semantic range that English splits between "clean" and "beautiful." While it often means "clean" (e.g., *schoonmaken* = to clean), in the context of *schoonheid*, it carries the aesthetic meaning of "beautiful."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *scōn*, from Proto-Germanic *\*skōnaz*. It is cognate with the German *schön* and the archaic English sense of "shone" (as in "shining/beautiful").

#### B. The Suffix: `-heid`
*   **Type:** Bound Morpheme (it cannot stand alone; it must attach to a root).
*   **Category:** Derivational Suffix.
*   **Function:** Nominalizer. It converts an adjective into an abstract noun.
*   **Meaning:** "State of being," "quality of," or "condition of."
*   **English Equivalent:** It corresponds to the English suffixes **-hood** (e.g., *childhood*, *manhood*) or **-ness** (e.g., *kindness*, *darkness*).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *-hēdi*, from Proto-Germanic *\*-haiduz*. It is cognate with English *-hood* and German *-heit*.

---

### 3. Morphological Process
The formation of *schoonheid* is an instance of **Suffixation**.

*   **Input:** Adjective (*schoon*)
*   **Process:** Add derivational suffix (*-heid*)
*   **Output:** Abstract Noun (*schoonheid*)

This process changes the grammatical category of the word. You cannot say "He is schoonheid" (He is beauty) in the same way you say "Het is schoon" (It is clean/beautiful). The suffix abstracts the quality of the adjective into a concept.

**Interesting Divergence:**
Notice the vowel difference between Dutch and German.
*   German: *sch**ö**n* (with an umlaut/umlauted vowel).
*   Dutch: *sch**oo**n* (long 'oo' sound).
While German underwent a vowel mutation (umlaut) in the derivation process (*schön* → *Schönheit*), Dutch retained the original vowel quality of the root (*schoon* → *schoonheid*).

### 5. Phonology and Orthography
*   **Stress:** In Dutch, the primary stress typically falls on the root syllable.
    *   Pronunciation: **SCHOON**-heid (/sxon.hɛit/).
*   **Spelling:** There is no orthographic change (such as dropping a letter or adding a hyphen) when combining the root and the suffix. They are written as a single closed compound.

### Summary
**`Schoonheid`** is a **derived abstract noun** consisting of the adjectival root **`schoon`** (beautiful/clean) and the nominalizing suffix **`-heid`** (-hood/-ness). It represents the quality or state of being beautiful.
