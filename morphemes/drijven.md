### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

    *   *Infinitive:* drijf-
    *   *Past Singular:* dr**ee**f- (vowel shift: /ɛi/ → /e:/)
    *   *Past Participle:* ge-dr**ee**v-en (vowel shift + consonant change)
    *   *Note:* The final consonant changes from **`f`** to **`v`** in the past participle (*gedreven*) due to **final-obstruent devoicing** rules in Dutch phonology (the underlying phoneme is /v/, which devoices to [f] at the end of a syllable).

#### B. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Status:** This is a **derivational/inflectional suffix**. In the context of the infinitive, it is primarily inflectional.
*   **Function:** It marks the verb as the citation form (the dictionary form). It distinguishes the infinitive from the imperative (*drijf!*) or the present tense conjugations (*ik drijf*, *jij drijft*).
*   **Phonology:** In rapid speech, this suffix is often reduced to a schwa sound [ə] (e.g., *drijvən*).

### 3. Etymological and Semantic Perspective

To fully understand the morphemes, one must look at the **diachronic** (historical) evolution of the root.

*   **Proto-Germanic:** The root originates from **\*drībaną**.
*   **Cognates:**
    *   English: **Drive**
    *   German: **Treiben**
    *   Old English: *drīfan*
*   **Semantic Shift:**
    *   **Original Meaning:** The PIE root *\*dreibh-* meant "to push" or "to drive." This is preserved in English (*drive*) and German (*treiben* - to drive/urge).
    *   **Dutch Shift:** In Dutch, the meaning shifted from an active "driving" force to a passive "drifting" or "floating" state.
    *   **Modern Meaning:** *Drijven* primarily means "to float" (on water) or "to drift" (with the wind/current). However, the original meaning survives in compound words like **`aandrijven`** (to drive/urge someone to do something) or **`drijfkracht`** (driving force/motive).
