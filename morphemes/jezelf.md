From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`jezelf`** is a **compound** formed by two distinct free morphemes. It functions as a **reflexive pronoun** in the second person singular (informal).

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented as follows:
> **`je`** + **`zelf`**

### 2. Analysis of Morphemes

#### Morpheme 1: `je`
*   **Category:** Personal Pronoun (2nd Person Singular).
*   **Form:** This is the **clitic** (unstressed) form of the pronoun *jij*.
    *   *Jij* is used when the pronoun is stressed or emphasized (e.g., *"Jij moet dat doen"*).
    *   *Je* is used in unstressed positions (e.g., *"Ik zie je"*).
*   **Function:** It indicates the person (2nd) and number (singular) of the agent or experiencer.
*   **Register:** Informal. The formal equivalent would be *u* (which forms *uzelf*).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *gij* / *ghe*. It is cognate with the German *du* (though the historical development differs; *gij* was originally plural in Old Dutch before shifting to singular informal).

#### Morpheme 2: `zelf`
*   **Category:** Adverb / Intensifier / Reflexive Marker.
*   **Meaning:** "Self".
*   **Function:** In this context, it acts as a **reflexive intensifier**. It specifies that the action of the verb reflects back onto the subject itself, rather than onto another object. It adds the semantic feature of **[+REFLEXIVE]** and **[+IDENTITY]**.
*   **Independence:** Unlike English *-self* (which is a bound morpheme, e.g., *my-self*), the Dutch *zelf* is a **free morpheme**. It can stand alone as a word (e.g., *"Ik heb het zelf gedaan"* — "I did it myself").
*   **Etymology:** Cognate with English *self* and German *selbst*. It stems from Proto-Germanic *\*selbaz*.

### 3. Syntactic and Semantic Combination
When combined, these two morphemes create a **strong reflexive pronoun**.

*   **Weak vs. Strong Reflexivity:** Dutch distinguishes between "weak" and "strong" reflexives.
    *   **Weak:** Often just the object form of the pronoun (e.g., *Je wast **je*** — "You wash yourself").
    *   **Strong:** The pronoun + *zelf* (e.g., *Je wast **jezelf***).
*   **Usage of `jezelf`:** The form *jezelf* is typically used in three specific contexts:
    1.  **Emphasis:** To stress that the subject is acting upon themselves and no one else (*"Je moet voor jezelf zorgen"* — "You must take care of yourself").
    2.  **Prepositional Objects:** After prepositions, the strong form is required (*"Hij kijkt naar jezelf"* is incorrect; it must be *"Hij kijkt naar **jou**"* or *"Hij kijkt naar **jezelf**"* depending on the verb, but generally *zelf* is used for emphasis after prepositions).
    3.  **Contrast:** To distinguish the subject from others (*"Niet ik, maar jijzelf"* — "Not me, but yourself").

### 4. Orthographic Note
In modern Dutch orthography, this compound is written as **one word** (*jezelf*). Historically, it might have been written as two words (*je zelf*), but the lexicalization process has fused them into a single unit in the dictionary.

### 5. Cross-Linguistic Comparison
*   **English:** *yourself* (Pronoun *you* + Bound suffix *-self*).
*   **German:** *dich selbst* (Pronoun *dich* + Free word *selbst*).
*   **Dutch:** *jezelf* (Pronoun *je* + Free word *zelf* fused into one word).
