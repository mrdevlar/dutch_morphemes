From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`mogen`** is a modal verb in its infinitive form. Below is a detailed morphological, grammatical, and etymological breakdown.

### 1. Morphological Breakdown (Synchronic Analysis)
In modern Dutch morphology, the word can be segmented into two distinct morphemes:

| Morpheme | Form | Type | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Root** | `mog-` | Bound Lexical Morpheme | Carries the core semantic meaning ("permission/ability"). |
| **Suffix** | `-en` | Inflectional Suffix | Marks the verb as the **infinitive**. |

**Total Morphemes:** 2 (Polymorphemic)

#### Detailed Explanation:
*   **The Root (`mog-`):** This is the lexical core of the word. However, it is important to note that this root exhibits **allomorphy** (variation in form). In the present tense singular (e.g., *ik mag*, *hij mag*), the vowel changes from `o` to `a`. In the infinitive and plural forms, the vowel is `o`. Therefore, `mog-` is the specific allomorph of the root used before the infinitive suffix `-en`.
*   **The Suffix (`-en`):** In Dutch, the suffix `-en` is the standard marker for the infinitive form of verbs (e.g., *lopen*, *werken*, *zien* [exception]). It indicates that the verb is in its base, unconjugated form.

### 2. Grammatical Context (Ablaut and Irregularity)
While `mogen` looks like a regular verb, it is historically a **strong verb** (Class 3), which means it undergoes **ablaut** (vowel gradation) rather than taking a standard `-te` or `-de` suffix for the past tense.

*   **Infinitive:** `mogen` (Root: `mog-`)
*   **Present Singular:** `mag` (Root: `mag-`) — *Vowel change: o → a*
*   **Present Plural:** `mogen` (Root: `mog-`)
*   **Past Tense:** `mocht` (Root: `moch-`) — *Vowel change: o → o + consonant change*
*   **Past Participle:** `gemogen` (Prefix `ge-` + Root `mog-` + Suffix `-en`)

**Linguistic Note:** Because the root vowel changes depending on the tense and person, `mogen` is classified as an **irregular verb**. The morpheme `mog-` in the infinitive is not identical to the morpheme `mag-` in the singular present, though they are allomorphs of the same underlying lexical root.

### 3. Etymological Analysis (Diachronic Perspective)
To understand the morphemes fully, we must look at the word's history.

*   **Proto-Germanic:** `*mag-`
    *   Meaning: "To be able," "to have power."
*   **Proto-Indo-European (PIE):** `*mag-`
    *   Meaning: "To be able, to have power."
*   **Cognates:**
    *   **English:** *may* (modal verb), *might* (past tense).
    *   **German:** *mögen* (to like/want), *müssen* (must - related concept of necessity).
    *   **Old English:** *magan*.

**Semantic Shift:**
In English, "may" has largely lost the sense of "ability" (replaced by "can") and focuses on permission. In Dutch, `mogen` retains a strong sense of **permission** (deontic modality) but can also imply **possibility** (epistemic modality).
*   *Deontic:* "Je mag gaan." (You are allowed to go.)
*   *Epistemic:* "Dat mag waar zijn." (That may be true.)

### 4. Summary of Linguistic Features

1.  **Word Class:** Modal Auxiliary Verb.
2.  **Morphological Structure:** Root + Infinitive Suffix (`mog` + `en`).
3.  **Morphophonology:** The root undergoes vowel alternation (ablaut) between singular present (`mag`) and infinitive/plural (`mog`).
4.  **Bound vs. Free:** The root `mog-` is a **bound morpheme** in modern Dutch; it cannot stand alone as a word (you cannot say "Ik mog"). It must be inflected (e.g., `mag`, `mocht`, `mogen`). The suffix `-en` is also bound.
5.  **Productivity:** The suffix `-en` is highly productive for forming infinitives, but the root `mog-` is a closed-class lexical item (a modal verb) and is not productive (you cannot create new verbs using this root).

### Conclusion
The word **`mogen`** consists of the lexical root **`mog-`** (indicating permission/ability) and the infinitive suffix **`-en`**. While it appears simple, it represents a historical strong verb with vowel alternation (ablaut) that distinguishes it from regular weak verbs in the Dutch language.
