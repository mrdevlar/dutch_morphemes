### 3. Synchronic vs. Diachronic Analysis
To fully understand the word, one must distinguish between how it functions *now* (synchronic) and where it came from (diachronic).

#### A. Synchronic (Current Dutch)
In the current Dutch language system, `talent` is a **borrowed noun**. It behaves like a native Dutch noun regarding inflection, but it does not contain native Dutch affixes.
*   **Pluralization:** `talent` + `-en` (plural suffix) = `talenten`.

*   **Adjectival Derivation:** `talent` + `-vol` (suffix meaning "full of") = `talentvol`.
*   **Negation:** `talent` + `-loos` (suffix meaning "without") = `talentloos`.

#### B. Diachronic (Etymology)
If we look historically, the word *does* have internal structure, but this structure is no longer active in the minds of Dutch speakers.
*   **Origin:** Greek *talanton* (a unit of weight/money) $\rightarrow$ Latin *talentum*.
*   **Latin Structure:** *talent-* (stem) + *-um* (neuter noun ending).
*   **Biblical Influence:** The meaning shifted from "money" to "spiritual gift" via the Parable of the Talents in the Bible, which cemented the word in Dutch (and English) vocabulary.

### 4. Why it is not analyzed as `tal` + `ent`
A common linguistic trap is to assume that because English or Latin has the suffix *-ent* (as in *student*, *president*, *resident*), the Dutch word *talent* shares this structure.

*   **In Latin:** The suffix *-ent-* indicated a present participle or agent noun.
*   **In Modern Dutch:** The suffix *-ent* exists (e.g., *student*, *kandidaat*), but it is **not separable** from *talent*.
    *   You cannot remove *-ent* from *talent* and have a word *tal* that means anything related to aptitude.
    *   Therefore, the boundary between the root and the historical suffix has been **lexicalized**. The whole word is treated as one chunk.
