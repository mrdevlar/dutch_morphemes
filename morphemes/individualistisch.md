From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`individualistisch`** is a complex derived adjective. It is formed through a process of **derivational morphology**, where multiple suffixes are stacked onto a root to change its part of speech and meaning.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes, their functions, and their etymological origins.

### 1. Visual Segmentation
The word can be segmented into four distinct morphemes:

> **`individu`** + **`-al`** + **`-ist`** + **`-isch`**

---

### 2. Morpheme-by-Morpheme Analysis

#### A. Root: `individu`
*   **Form:** Free morpheme (can stand alone as a word).
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun.
*   **Meaning:** "Individual," "single entity," or "person."
*   **Etymology:** Borrowed from Latin *individuum* (meaning "indivisible," from *in-* "not" + *dividuus* "divisible").
*   **Linguistic Note:** In modern Dutch, this is the base lexical unit. Unlike English, where "individual" can be an adjective, in Dutch the standalone adjective is *individueel*. Here, `individu` acts as the semantic core.

#### B. Suffix: `-al`
*   **Form:** Bound morpheme (cannot stand alone).
*   **Part of Speech:** Derivational Suffix (Adjectival).
*   **Function:** Turns the noun `individu` into a relational stem.
*   **Meaning:** "Pertaining to" or "relating to."
*   **Etymology:** From Latin *-alis*.
*   **Linguistic Note:** This creates the stem **`individual-`**. In Dutch, this specific stem is not a standalone word (you cannot say *"Ik ben individual"*), but it serves as the necessary bridge to attach the agentive suffix `-ist`.

#### C. Suffix: `-ist`
*   **Form:** Bound morpheme.
*   **Part of Speech:** Derivational Suffix (Agentive Noun).
*   **Function:** Turns the stem `individual-` into a noun referring to a person.
*   **Meaning:** "One who practices," "believer in," or "advocate of."
*   **Etymology:** From Greek *-istes* (via Latin/French).
*   **Resulting Word:** **`individualist`** (Noun: An individualist).
*   **Linguistic Note:** This suffix shifts the meaning from a general concept (the individual) to a specific ideological stance (the person who prioritizes the individual).

#### D. Suffix: `-isch`
*   **Form:** Bound morpheme.
*   **Part of Speech:** Derivational Suffix (Adjectival).
*   **Function:** Turns the noun `individualist` back into an adjective.
*   **Meaning:** "Characterized by," "pertaining to," or "of the nature of."
*   **Etymology:** From Greek *-ikos* (via Latin *-icus* and Germanic *-isch*).
*   **Resulting Word:** **`individualistisch`** (Adjective: Individualistic).
*   **Linguistic Note:** This is the standard Dutch adjectival suffix for loanwords ending in `-ist` (e.g., *socialist* → *socialistisch*, *realist* → *realistisch*).

---

### 3. Derivational Hierarchy (The "Stack")
Linguistically, this word is built in layers. You cannot attach `-isch` directly to `individu` to get the correct meaning; the `-ist` layer is required to establish the ideological context.

1.  **Base:** `individu` (Noun: The person)
2.  **Layer 1:** `individu` + `-al` → `individual` (Adjectival Stem: Relating to the person)
3.  **Layer 2:** `individual` + `-ist` → `individualist` (Noun: The person who advocates individualism)
4.  **Layer 3:** `individualist` + `-isch` → `individualistisch` (Adjective: Characteristic of an individualist)

### 4. Linguistic Observations

*   **Agglutinative Tendency:** While Dutch is generally a fusional language, this word exhibits **agglutinative** properties, where distinct morphemes are stacked linearly without significant phonological changes to the root.
*   **Double Adjectivalization:** Notice the presence of two adjectival suffixes (`-al` and `-isch`). This is common in Romance/Germanic loanwords. The `-al` creates the abstract concept, and the `-isch` creates the descriptive adjective from the agent noun.
*   **Stress Pattern:** In Dutch, the stress typically falls on the syllable containing the `-ist` morpheme: *in-di-vid-u-a-**lis**-tisch*. This highlights the ideological core of the word.
*   **Cognate Comparison:** The word is nearly identical to the English **`individualistic`**.
    *   Dutch `-isch` = English `-ic` / `-istic`.
    *   Dutch `-ist` = English `-ist`.
    *   Dutch `-al` = English `-al`.
    *   Dutch `individu` = English `individual`.
    *   *Difference:* English uses "individual" as both noun and adjective, whereas Dutch distinguishes `individu` (noun) and `individueel` (adjective).

### Summary
The word **`individualistisch`** is a morphological chain that translates literally as:
*"Of the nature of a person who advocates pertaining-to-the-individual."*

It encapsulates a specific socio-political stance through the stacking of Latin and Greek morphological markers onto a Latin root.
