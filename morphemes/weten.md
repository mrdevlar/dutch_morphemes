From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch verb **`weten`** (to know) can be analyzed through **synchronic morphology** (how it is structured in modern Dutch) and **diachronic etymology** (its historical development).

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes:

### 1. Synchronic Morphological Breakdown (Modern Dutch)

In modern Dutch grammar, the word is analyzed as a verb stem combined with an infinitive suffix.

| Morpheme | Form | Type | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`weet-`** | *Stem* | **Lexical Root** | Carries the core semantic meaning ("to know"). |
| **`-en`** | *Suffix* | **Inflectional** | Marks the verb as an **infinitive** (non-finite form). |

**Total Morphemes:** 2 (Root + Suffix)

#### Important Orthographic Note
You might notice a discrepancy between the stem and the spelling:
*   **Stem:** *weet-* (e.g., *ik **weet*** — "I know").
*   **Infinitive:** *weten* (not *weeten*).

This is due to **Dutch orthographic rules regarding syllable structure**:
1.  In the present tense (*ik weet*), the syllable is closed by the *t*. To indicate a long vowel sound (/eː/), the vowel must be doubled (*ee*).
2.  In the infinitive (*weten*), the syllable is open (*we-te-n*). In an open syllable, a single vowel letter is sufficient to indicate a long sound. Therefore, the spelling simplifies to *weten*, even though the underlying morphological stem remains *weet-*.

### 2. Diachronic Analysis (Etymology)

To understand the root `weet-` fully, we must look at its historical evolution. The root is not a modern invention but a descendant of ancient Indo-European roots.

*   **Proto-Indo-European (PIE):** **`*weyd-`**
    *   **Meaning:** "To see," "to know."
    *   **Concept:** In ancient thought, "seeing" and "knowing" were closely linked (cognate with English *vision* and *video*).
*   **Proto-Germanic:** **`*witaną`**
    *   **Meaning:** "To know."
*   **Old Dutch:** **`witan`**
*   **Modern Dutch:** **`weten`**

#### Cognates
Because of this shared PIE root (`*weyd-`), *weten* shares a lineage with several English words:
*   **Wit:** (Mental capacity/knowledge).
*   **Wise:** (Having knowledge).
*   **Video:** (Latin *videre*, "to see").
*   **Vision:** (Latin *visio*, "sight").

*Note:* The Dutch noun **`wet`** (law) is also etymologically related to *weten*. Historically, a "law" was something that was "seen" or "established" as truth. However, in modern Dutch morphology, *weten* (verb) and *wet* (noun) are treated as separate lexical items.

---

### 3. Morphophonology (Verb Conjugation)

`Weten` is a **strong verb** (sterk werkwoord). This means it does not form its past tense by adding *-te* or *-de* (weak verbs), but by changing the vowel of the root (ablaut).

*   **Infinitive:** *weten* (Root vowel: **e**)
*   **Past Tense (Singular):** *wist* (Root vowel: **i**)
*   **Past Participle:** *geweten* (Root vowel: **e**)

This vowel alternation (**e – i – e**) is a morphophonological feature inherent to the root `weet-` in this specific verb class (Class 1 strong verbs).

### Summary
*   **Word:** `weten`
*   **Morphemes:** `weet-` (Root) + `-en` (Infinitive Suffix).
*   **Origin:** PIE `*weyd-` (to see/know).
*   **Classification:** Strong Verb (irregular conjugation).
