From a linguistic perspective, it is important to clarify first that **"zorgen voor"** is not a single compound word (like *zorgdrager*), but rather a **prepositional verb** (Dutch: *voorzetselwerkwoord*). It consists of two distinct lexical items that function together as a single semantic unit.

Here is the morphological and syntactic breakdown of the phrase.

### 1. Morphological Breakdown

Since this is a phrase, we must analyze the morphemes within each constituent word separately.

#### A. The Verb: *Zorgen*
*   **Lemma (Dictionary form):** *zorgen*
*   **Root Morpheme:** `zorg`
    *   **Meaning:** Care, worry, concern.
    *   **Etymology:** From Middle Dutch *sorghen*, related to the noun *zorg* (care/worry).
*   **Inflectional Suffix:** `-en`
    *   **Function:** Marks the **infinitive** form of the verb.
    *   **Note:** In conjugated forms (e.g., *ik zorg*, *hij zorgt*), this suffix is dropped or altered (e.g., *-t* for 3rd person singular).
*   **Part of Speech:** Verb (specifically, an intransitive verb that requires a preposition to take an object).

#### B. The Preposition: *Voor*
*   **Lemma:** *voor*
*   **Morpheme:** `voor` (Monomorphemic)
    *   **Meaning:** For, in front of, before.
    *   **Etymology:** From Proto-Germanic *\*fur*.
*   **Part of Speech:** Preposition (*voorzetsel*).
*   **Function:** In this specific construction, it does not indicate spatial location (in front of), but rather **benefactive** or **purpose** (for the sake of).

### 2. Syntactic and Semantic Interaction

While the morphemes belong to separate words, linguistically they form a **complex predicate**.

#### The "Prepositional Verb" Construction
In Dutch grammar, *zorgen voor* is classified as a **prepositional verb**. This means the verb *zorgen* has a fixed valency (argument structure) that requires the preposition *voor* to connect to its object.

*   **Literal Meaning:** "To worry for" or "to care for."
*   **Idiomatic Meaning:** "To take care of," "to provide for," or "to ensure."
    *   *Example:* "Ik zorg voor mijn moeder." (I take care of my mother.)
    *   *Example:* "Zorg voor een goede start." (Ensure a good start.)

#### Separability and Word Order
Unlike separable verbs (like *opbellen* -> *Ik bel op*), *zorgen voor* behaves slightly differently because *voor* is a preposition, not a prefix. However, the verb and preposition can be separated by the object in certain sentence structures (specifically in infinitive clauses).

*   **Main Clause (Verb Second):**
    *   *Ik **zorg** [voor] de kinderen.*
    *   (I take care of the children.)
    *   *Morphology:* `zorg` (1st person singular present, suffix `-en` dropped).
*   **Infinitive Clause (Verb at End):**
    *   *Ik moet [voor] de kinderen **zorgen**.*
    *   (I must take care of the children.)
    *   *Morphology:* `zorgen` (Infinitive, suffix `-en` restored).

### 4. Linguistic Note on "Morphemes" vs. "Lexemes"
Strictly speaking, **morphemes** are the smallest units of meaning within a *single word*. Because "zorgen voor" is two words, a linguist would analyze the **morphology** of the individual words and the **syntax** of the phrase.

However, in **Construction Grammar**, the entire phrase "zorgen voor" can be viewed as a single **constructional morpheme**. It has a specific meaning ("to provide care") that cannot be derived simply by adding the meaning of "worry" + "for." It is a lexicalized unit.
