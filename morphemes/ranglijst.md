From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`ranglijst`** is a **compound noun** (*samenstelling*). It consists of two free morphemes joined together to create a new lexical item.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Segmentation
The word can be segmented into two distinct morphemes:
> **rang** + **lijst**

There are no inflectional affixes (like plural markers or tense markers) within the word itself; it is a singular noun in its base form.

---

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### A. Morpheme 1: `rang`
*   **Type:** Free Root Morpheme (Noun).
*   **Meaning:** Rank, class, grade, or position within a hierarchy.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *rang*, from Old Dutch *rang*. It shares a common Proto-Germanic ancestor (*\*hrangaz*) with the English word **rank** and the German word **Rang**.
*   **Semantic Role:** In this compound, it functions as the **modifier** (or dependent). It specifies the *content* or *nature* of the list. It answers the question: "What kind of list?" (A list *of ranks*).

#### B. Morpheme 2: `lijst`
*   **Type:** Free Root Morpheme (Noun).
*   **Meaning:** List, roster, or strip.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *lijst*. It shares a common Proto-Germanic ancestor (*\*listō*) with the English word **list** and the German word **Liste**.
*   **Semantic Role:** In this compound, it functions as the **head** (or head noun). In Dutch compounding, the head usually appears at the end. The entire word is a type of *lijst*. If you pluralize the word, the plural suffix attaches to this morpheme (*ranglijst**en***).

### 3. Morphological Structure & Syntax

*   **Compound Type:** Endocentric Compound.
    *   This means the meaning of the whole word is a specific sub-category of the head word. A *ranglijst* is a specific type of *lijst*.
*   **Orthography:** Closed Compound.
    *   In Dutch, when two nouns are combined, they are typically written as a single word without a space or hyphen (unlike English, which might use "ranking list" or "rank list").
*   **Stress Pattern:** In Dutch compounds, the primary stress usually falls on the **first** element.
    *   Pronunciation: **RANG**-lijst (not rang-LIJST).

### 4. Semantic Composition
The meaning is compositional, meaning it is derived directly from the sum of its parts:
*   *Rang* (Rank/Position) + *Lijst* (List) = **A list showing the relative positions or rankings of items/people.**

### 5. Usage Context
This word is commonly used in contexts involving hierarchy or competition, such as:
*   **Sports:** *De wereldranglijst* (The world ranking list).
*   **Business:** *De top 100 ranglijst* (The top 100 list).
*   **Military:** *De ranglijst van officieren* (The list of officers by rank).
