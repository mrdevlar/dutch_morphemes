## Word Breakdown

The Dutch word **"opdrachten"** can be broken down into **three morphemes**:

| Morpheme | Type | Meaning/Function |
|----------|------|------------------|
| **op-** | Prefix | "up," "on," or "forth" |
| **dracht** | Root (noun stem) | Related to "carrying" (from verb *dragen*) |
| **-en** | Suffix | Plural marker |

---

## Detailed Explanation

### 1. **op-** (Prefix)
- **Type**: Separable prefix (in verbs) or derivational prefix
- **Function**: Adds directional or completive meaning
- **Common in Dutch**: Many Dutch verbs use "op-" (e.g., *opbellen* = to call up, *opeten* = to eat up)

### 2. **dracht** (Root)
- **Etymology**: Derived from the verb **"dragen"** (to carry)
- **Related forms**:
  - *dragen* = to carry (verb)
  - *dracht* = a carrying/bearing (noun stem)
  - *opdragen* = to assign/commission (verb: literally "to carry forth")
- **Semantic shift**: From literal "carrying" → metaphorical "assigning a task"

### 3. **-en** (Suffix)
- **Type**: Inflectional suffix
- **Function**: Marks **plural** (like English "-s" or "-es")
- **Pattern**: Common Dutch plural ending (especially for words ending in -t, -d, -s, -f)

---

## Morphological Structure

```
opdrachten = op- + dracht + -en
           │     │       │
           │     │       └─── plural marker
           │     └─────────── root (from "dragen" = to carry)
           └───────────────── prefix (up/forth)
```

## Key Pattern for Learning

This follows a **productive Dutch word-formation pattern**:

> **Prefix + Verb Root → Noun Stem + Plural Suffix**

Many Dutch nouns follow this structure where a verb + prefix creates a new noun meaning, then inflected for number.

**Mnemonic**: Think "opdrachten" = "**up**-**carried**-**things**" → tasks that have been "carried forth" to you.
