From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`splitsen`** (to split/divide) can be analyzed by breaking it down into its constituent morphemes (the smallest units of meaning).

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Morpheme Breakdown

| Morpheme | Form | Type | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`split-`** | Root | Lexical (Free) | Carries the core semantic meaning ("to divide"). |
| **`-s-`** | Extension | Derivational / Stem | A stem extension that appears in specific forms (infinitive/plural). |
| **`-en`** | Suffix | Inflectional | Marks the verb as an **infinitive**. |

**Total Structure:** `split` + `s` + `en`

---

### 2. Detailed Explanation

#### A. The Root: `split-`
*   **Category:** Lexical Root (Stem).
*   **Meaning:** This is the core semantic unit. It corresponds to the English word "split."
*   **Usage:** This root appears in the **singular present tense** without the `-s-` extension.
    *   *Example:* `Ik split` (I split).
*   **Note:** In modern Dutch, `split` is not typically used as a standalone noun (unlike English), though it exists in specific contexts (e.g., tennis, or a crack in wood). Historically, it relates to the noun *split* or the Middle Dutch verb *spliten*.

#### B. The Extension: `-s-`
*   **Category:** Stem Extension (Allomorphic variation).
*   **Function:** This is the most linguistically interesting part of the word. In Dutch morphology, this is an instance of **stem allomorphy**.
    *   The verb has two stem forms:
        1.  **`split-`**: Used in the 1st person singular present (`ik split`).
        2.  **`splits-`**: Used in the infinitive (`splitsen`), plural present (`wij splitsen`), and past tense (`splitste`).
*   **Why is it there?**
    *   **Historical:** It likely originated as a **denominal verbalizer**. In older stages of the language, nouns were often turned into verbs by adding an `-s-` (similar to how English uses `-ize`, though Dutch `-s-` is not productive anymore).
    *   **Synchronic (Current):** Today, it functions as a specific marker for the infinitive and plural forms of this specific verb class. It distinguishes the infinitive stem from the singular present stem.
    *   **Productivity:** This `-s-` extension is **not productive**. You cannot apply this rule to other verbs. For example, you cannot say `*bak` (noun) -> `*baksen` (verb); the correct form is `bakken`.

#### C. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Category:** Inflectional Suffix.
*   **Function:** This is the standard **infinitive marker** for Dutch verbs.
*   **Usage:** It indicates the base form of the verb (the "to" form in English).
*   **Examples:** `lopen` (to walk), `maken` (to make), `zien` (to see).
*   **Phonology:** In `splitsen`, the `-en` is pronounced as a schwa sound /ə/ (or /n/ depending on dialect), attached to the extended stem `splits-`.

---

### 3. Morphophonological Behavior (Conjugation)

To understand the morphemes fully, it helps to see how they interact during conjugation. The presence or absence of the `-s-` morpheme changes based on the grammatical context:

| Person/Number | Form | Morphological Analysis |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Infinitive** | **splitsen** | `split` + `s` + `en` |
| **1st Pers. Sing.** | **split** | `split` (The `-s-` and `-en` drop) |
| **2nd Pers. Sing.** | **splitst** | `split` + `s` + `t` (The `-s-` returns before the `t`) |
| **3rd Pers. Sing.** | **splitst** | `split` + `s` + `t` |
| **Plural** | **splitsen** | `split` + `s` + `en` |
| **Past Tense** | **splitste** | `split` + `s` + `te` (Weak verb conjugation) |
| **Past Participle** | **gesplitst** | `ge-` + `split` + `s` + `t` |

*Observation:* The `-s-` morpheme is present in all forms **except** the 1st person singular present tense. This makes `splitsen` a verb with a **variable stem**.

### 4. Etymological Context
*   **Origin:** Middle Dutch *spliten*.
*   **Development:** The modern form *splitsen* with the inserted `s` became standardized in the 17th and 18th centuries. The insertion of the `s` likely served to distinguish the verb from the noun or to regularize the pronunciation, aligning it with other verbs ending in `-s-` (like *dansen*, *wassen*), even though the root `split` does not naturally end in `s`.

### Summary
From a linguistic perspective, **`splitsen`** is a **weak verb** composed of the root **`split-`**, a non-productive **stem extension `-s-`** (specific to the infinitive and plural forms), and the standard **infinitive suffix `-en`**. The alternation between `split-` and `splits-` is a key feature of its morphological structure.
