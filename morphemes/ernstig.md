From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`ernstig`** is a **derived adjective**. It is formed through a process called **derivational morphology**, where a suffix is added to a base word to change its grammatical category or meaning.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into two distinct morphemes:

> **`ernst`** + **`-ig`**

---

### 2. Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Base Morpheme: `ernst`
*   **Type:** Free morpheme (it can stand alone as a word).
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun (*het woord*).
*   **Meaning:** "Seriousness," "gravity," or "earnestness."
*   **Etymology:**
    *   **Middle Dutch:** *ernst*
    *   **Old High German:** *arnust* / *arnost*
    *   **Proto-Germanic:** Reconstructed as *\*arnustaz* or related to *\*arnuz* (eagle).
    *   **Cognates:** This is a direct cognate of the English word **`earnest`**. While "earnest" has become somewhat archaic or formal in modern English (replaced often by "serious"), it retains the exact same Germanic root. It is also identical to the German word *ernst*.
*   **Semantic Role:** This morpheme provides the core lexical meaning (the concept of gravity or seriousness).

#### B. The Suffix Morpheme: `-ig`
*   **Type:** Bound morpheme (it cannot stand alone).
*   **Part of Speech:** Derivational suffix.
*   **Function:** Adjectivalizer (turns a noun into an adjective).
*   **Meaning:** "Having the quality of," "characterized by," or "full of."
*   **Etymology:**
    *   **Middle Dutch:** *-ich*
    *   **Old High German:** *-ag* / *-ig*
    *   **Proto-Germanic:** *\*-agaz*
    *   **Cognates:** This is cognate with the German suffix *-ig* (e.g., *könig* -> *königlich* is *-lich*, but *sonnig* is *-ig*) and the English suffix **-y** (e.g., *sun* -> *sunny*) or **-ish** (e.g., *child* -> *childish*).
*   **Phonology:** In standard Dutch, the final *-g* in this suffix is pronounced as a voiceless velar fricative [x] (similar to the "ch" in Scottish "loch" or German "Bach").

---

### 3. Semantic Composition
When combined, the morphemes create the following meaning:

*   **`ernst`** (Seriousness) + **`-ig`** (Having the quality of)
*   **Result:** "Having the quality of seriousness."

This explains why **`ernstig`** translates to **serious**, **grave**, or **earnest** in English.

### 4. Grammatical Context
*   **Derivation:** This is a case of **nominal-to-adjectival derivation**. The noun *ernst* is converted into the adjective *ernstig*.
*   **Inflection:** As an adjective, *ernstig* can be inflected for gender and number when used attributively (before a noun).
    *   *Een **ernstige** ziekte* (A serious illness) — *e* is added because the noun is common gender (*de ziekte*) and indefinite.
    *   *Het **ernstige** probleem* (The serious problem) — *e* is added because the noun is neuter (*het probleem*) and definite.
    *   *De ziekte is **ernstig*** (The illness is serious) — No ending when used predicatively.
