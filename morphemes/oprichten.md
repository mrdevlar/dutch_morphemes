From a morphological perspective, the Dutch verb **`oprichten`** (meaning *to establish, to set up, to found, or to erect*) can be segmented into three distinct morphemes.

Here is the breakdown:

### 1. Segmentation
**`op`** + **`richt`** + **`en`**

---

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### **A. `op` (Prefix)**
*   **Type:** Separable Verbal Prefix (*scheidbaar werkwoorddeel*).
*   **Origin:** Historically derived from the preposition/adverb *op* (meaning "up" or "on").
*   **Function:**
    *   **Semantic:** It modifies the meaning of the root verb. While *richten* implies "directing" or "aligning," the addition of *op* adds a directional or completive aspect, implying bringing something into an upright position or establishing it.
    *   **Syntactic:** In Dutch grammar, this makes the verb a **separable verb**. In a main clause, this prefix detaches from the stem and moves to the end of the sentence (e.g., *Ik richt een bedrijf **op*** — "I set up a company").
*   **Phonology:** In the infinitive form, the primary stress falls on this prefix (**`op`**-richten).

#### **B. `richt` (Root / Stem)**
*   **Type:** Lexical Root (*stam*).
*   **Base Verb:** *richten* (to aim, to direct, to align, to straighten).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *richten*, from Old Frankish *\*rehtjan*, related to the adjective *recht* (right/straight).
*   **Function:** This carries the core lexical meaning of the word. It denotes the action of directing or aligning something.
*   **Morphophonology:** The final consonant is a voiceless alveolar plosive /t/. In Dutch spelling rules (the "t-k-l" rule), this *t* is doubled in the past tense (*richtte*) because the vowel preceding it is short, but in the infinitive stem, it remains single.

#### **C. `en` (Suffix)**
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix (Infinitive Marker).
*   **Function:** This is the standard marker for the **infinitive** form of Dutch verbs. It indicates that the verb is in its dictionary form (non-conjugated).
*   **Productivity:** This is a highly productive morpheme in Dutch; almost all regular verbs form their infinitive by adding *-en* to the stem (e.g., *werk* + *en* = *werken*).

---

### 3. Semantic Composition (How the meaning is built)
The meaning of the compound is not merely the sum of its parts, but a result of **semantic composition**:

1.  **`richten`**: To direct/align (often implies aiming at a target or straightening).
2.  **`op`**: Up/On (implies a vertical direction or a state of completion).
3.  **`oprichten`**: To direct something *up* or *into place*.
    *   *Literal:* To erect (a building).
    *   *Figurative:* To establish (an organization, a company, a monument).

### 4. Summary Table

| Morpheme | Type | Meaning/Function | Example in Context |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **op** | Prefix | Directional/Aspectual (Up/On) | *Ik richt het **op**.* (I set it up.) |
| **richt** | Root | Lexical Core (Direct/Aim) | *Hij **richt** de camera.* (He aims the camera.) |
| **en** | Suffix | Infinitive Marker | *Ik wil **richten**.* (I want to aim.) |

### 5. Linguistic Note on Stress
In Dutch phonology, separable verbs have a specific stress pattern.
*   **Infinitive:** Stress is on the prefix (**`op`**-richten).
*   **Conjugated (Main Clause):** Stress shifts to the verb stem, and the prefix moves to the end (Hij **richt** een bedrijf **op**).
*   **Conjugated (Subordinate Clause):** Stress returns to the prefix (Dat hij een bedrijf **op**-richt).
