From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`eenvoudig`** (meaning "simple" or "easy") is a complex word formed through compounding and derivation.

Here is the morphological breakdown, etymology, and semantic analysis.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **een** + **voud** + **ig**

Structurally, this represents two layers of word formation:
1.  **Compounding:** *een* + *voud* $\rightarrow$ *eenvoud* (Noun)
2.  **Derivation:** *eenvoud* + *-ig* $\rightarrow$ *eenvoudig* (Adjective)

---

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

| Morpheme | Type | Meaning / Function | Etymology & Cognates |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`een`** | Free Morpheme (Numeral) | "One" | Old Dutch *ēn*. Cognate with English **one**, German *ein*, Latin *unus*. |
| **`voud`** | Bound Root | "Fold", "times", "multiplication" | Old Dutch *vout*. Related to the verb *vouwen* (to fold). Cognate with English **-fold** (e.g., *twofold*), German *-fach*. |
| **`-ig`** | Derivational Suffix | "Having the quality of", "characterized by" | Old Dutch *-ag*. Cognate with English **-y** (e.g., *cloudy*), German *-ig*. |

---

### 3. Etymological and Semantic Development

#### The Root: *Eenvoud* (One-fold)
The core noun is **`eenvoud`**.
*   **Literal Meaning:** "One-fold."
*   **Conceptual Shift:** In older Germanic thought, complexity was often visualized as multiple layers or folds. Something that is "one-fold" has only a single layer; it is not doubled, tripled, or complicated.
*   **Modern Meaning:** Simplicity, plainness, or singularity.
*   **Linguistic Context:** This root is still productive in Dutch grammar. For example:
    *   *Enkelvoud* (Singular) = "Single-fold"
    *   *Meervoud* (Plural) = "Many-fold"

#### The Suffix: *-ig*
The suffix **`-ig`** is a standard adjectival derivational suffix in Dutch. It transforms the noun *eenvoud* (simplicity) into the adjective *eenvoudig* (simple).
*   **Function:** It indicates possession of the quality described by the noun.
*   **Parallel:** Just as *zand* (sand) becomes *zandig* (sandy), *eenvoud* becomes *eenvoudig*.

#### The Full Semantic Chain
1.  **`een`** (One) + **`voud`** (Fold) = **`eenvoud`** (One-fold / Simplicity)
2.  **`eenvoud`** + **`-ig`** (Quality of) = **`eenvoudig`** (Characterized by simplicity)

**Semantic Evolution:**
The meaning shifted from a literal physical description (having one fold) to an abstract quality (lacking complexity). This is a common metaphorical path in language (similar to the English word *unfold* meaning to reveal or explain).

### 4. Phonological Notes
*   **`ee`**: Represents a long close-mid front unrounded vowel /eː/.
*   **`ou`**: Represents a diphthong /ʌu/ (similar to the 'ow' in English *cow*, but shorter).
*   **`ig`**: The pronunciation of the final suffix varies by dialect.
    *   **Standard Dutch (North):** Often pronounced as a voiceless velar fricative /əx/ (like the 'ch' in Scottish *loch*).
    *   **Southern Dutch/Belgian:** Often pronounced as a palatal fricative /ɪç/ (like the 'ch' in German *ich*).

### 5. Related Word Family
To understand *eenvoudig* fully, it helps to look at its morphological family:

*   **`Eenvoud`** (Noun): Simplicity / Singular.
*   **`Meervoudig`** (Adjective): Multiple-folded / Complex / Plural.
*   **`Tweevoudig`** (Adjective): Twofold.
*   **`Enkelvoudig`** (Adjective): Singular (often used in grammar contexts).

### Summary
**`Eenvoudig`** is a transparent compound-derivative. It literally means **"one-fold-y"**. It describes something that possesses the quality of being single-layered, and by extension, uncomplicated or easy.
