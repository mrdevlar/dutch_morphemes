*   **Prefix:** `uit`

### 4. Linguistic Features & Nuances

#### The Linking `s` (Tussenklank)
The **`s`** is a classic feature of Dutch morphology. While it looks like a plural or genitive ending, in modern compounds, it is analyzed as a **linking morpheme** (interfix).
*   **Function:** It eases pronunciation between the two nouns and historically marked possession (the program *of* the exchange).
*   **Rule:** It is often added when the first part of the compound ends in a schwa sound or specific consonants, though the rules are largely lexicalized (memorized) in modern Dutch.

#### Semantic Head
In Dutch compounds, the **last element** is the **head** (the main noun), and the preceding elements modify it.
*   **Head:** `programma` (It is a type of program).
*   **Modifier:** `uitwisseling` (It specifies *what kind* of program).
*   **Logic:** It is not an "exchange" that is a "program," but a "program" that is for an "exchange."

#### Stress Pattern (Phonology)
Dutch compound words typically place the **primary stress** on the first syllable of the **first constituent**.
*   **Stress:** **`UIT`**-wisselings-pro-**`GRAM`**-ma.
*   The modifier (`uit`) receives the strongest stress.
*   The head (`programma`) retains its inherent secondary stress on the first syllable (`pro`), but it is subordinate to the stress on `uit`.

#### Loanword Integration
The morpheme **`programma`** is a loanword (from Greek via Latin/French/English). Despite being foreign in origin, it is fully integrated into Dutch morphology:
*   It accepts the Dutch linking `s`.
*   It can be pluralized with the Dutch suffix `-s` (e.g., *uitwisselingsprogramma's*).
*   It can be further compounded (e.g., *studentenuitwisselingsprogramma*).

### Summary
**`uitwisselingsprogramma`** is a **right-headed compound noun**. It is derived from the separable verb *uitwisselen*, nominalized with *-ing*, and compounded with *programma* using the linking element *-s*. It literally translates to "exchange-program," referring to a structured plan for mutual exchange (usually of students or employees).
