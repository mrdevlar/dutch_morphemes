From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`onafhankelijkheid`** (meaning "independence") is a complex derived word. It is formed through a process of **derivational morphology**, where multiple affixes (prefixes and suffixes) are attached to a root to change its meaning and grammatical category.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes, analyzed from the outermost layer to the innermost root.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
Visually, the word can be segmented as follows:

> **`on-`** + **`af-`** + **`hang`** + **`-elijk`** + **`-heid`**

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

| Morpheme | Type | Function/Meaning | Linguistic Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`on-`** | **Prefix** | Negation ("not") | Cognate with English *un-* (e.g., *unhappy*). It attaches to adjectives to negate them. |
| **`af-`** | **Prefix** | Direction/Completion ("off", "away") | A separable prefix in Dutch verbs. Here it modifies the root to create the concept of "depending" (literally "hanging off"). |
| **`hang`** | **Root** | Lexical Core ("to hang") | The verb root comes from *hangen* (to hang). Cognate with English *hang*. |
| **`-elijk`** | **Suffix** | Adjectivalizer ("-ly") | Turns the verb stem into an adjective. Cognate with English *-ly* (e.g., *friend* → *friendly*). The *-e-* is an epenthetic vowel inserted for phonological ease. |
| **`-heid`** | **Suffix** | Nominalizer ("-hood", "-ness") | Turns the adjective into an abstract noun. Cognate with English *-hood* (e.g., *child* → *childhood*). |

### 3. Step-by-Step Derivation
To understand how the meaning is constructed, we can trace the word's formation chronologically:

1.  **Root:** `hang` (to hang).
2.  **Verb Formation:** `af-` + `hang` + `-en` (infinitive) → **`afhangen`** (to depend / to hang off).
3.  **Adjective Formation:** `af-` + `hang` + `-elijk` → **`afhankelijk`** (dependent).
    *   *Literal meaning:* "Hanging off."
4.  **Negation:** `on-` + `afhankelijk` → **`onafhankelijk`** (independent).
    *   *Literal meaning:* "Not hanging off."
5.  **Nominalization:** `onafhankelijk` + `-heid` → **`onafhankelijkheid`** (independence).
    *   *Literal meaning:* "The state/quality of being not hanging off."

### 4. Linguistic Insights

*   **Germanic Cognates:** This word is a perfect example of a **Germanic** construction, contrasting with the Latinate English word *independence*.
    *   Dutch `on-` ≈ English *un-* (Germanic) vs. English *in-* (Latinate).
    *   Dutch `hang` ≈ English *hang* (Germanic) vs. English *pend* (Latinate).
    *   Dutch `-heid` ≈ English *-hood* (Germanic) vs. English *-ence* (Latinate).
    *   *Comparison:* The German equivalent is *Unabhängigkeit*, which is structurally almost identical.
*   **Phonology (Epenthesis):** You may notice the `-e-` in `-elijk`. In Dutch morphology, the suffix is technically `-lijk`. However, when attached to a stem ending in a consonant cluster or specific sounds (like *ng* in *hang*), an epenthetic vowel `-e-` is inserted to break up the difficult consonant cluster (*hang-lijk* becomes *hang-elijk*).
*   **Productivity:** All the morphemes used here are **productive**.
    *   You can negate many adjectives with `on-` (*onmogelijk* - impossible).
    *   You can turn many adjectives into nouns with `-heid` (*vrijheid* - freedom).
    *   You can create adjectives with `-elijk` (*mooi* → *mooilijk* is rare, but *vriend* → *vriendelijk* is common).

### Summary
The word **`onafhankelijkheid`** is a morphological chain that literally translates to **"the state of not hanging off."** It demonstrates how Dutch builds abstract concepts using native Germanic roots and affixes rather than borrowing from Latin or French.
