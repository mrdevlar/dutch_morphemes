At the highest level, the word is a **compound noun** consisting of two parts:

> **`buurt`** + **`bewoner`**

*   **Type:** Endocentric Compound (The head is the second element, *bewoner*).
*   **Meaning:** A "neighborhood dweller" or "neighbor."
*   **Structure:** [Noun] + [Noun].

### 2. Micro-Segmentation (The Derivation)
The second component, **`bewoner`**, is itself a derived word. It is formed from a verb stem using a prefix and a suffix.

> **`be-`** + **`won-`** + **`-er`**

Here is the detailed analysis of each morpheme:

#### A. `buurt` (Free Morpheme / Root)
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun.
*   **Meaning:** Neighborhood, vicinity, area, or district.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *burt*, related to the word *buren* (neighbors). It shares a Germanic root with the English word *boor* (originally meaning a peasant or neighbor) and *burrow* (an enclosure).
*   **Function:** In this compound, it acts as a **modifier**. It specifies the location or scope of the dweller.

#### B. `be-` (Bound Morpheme / Prefix)
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix.
*   **Function:** **Verbalizer / Transitivizer.**
    *   In Dutch, the prefix *be-* often turns an intransitive verb or a noun into a transitive verb.
    *   Base verb: *wonen* (to live/dwell - intransitive).
    *   Derived verb: *bewonen* (to inhabit/occupy - transitive).
    *   *Example:* "Ik woon hier" (I live here) vs. "Ik bewoon dit huis" (I inhabit this house).
*   **Etymology:** Proto-Germanic *\*bi-*, cognate with English *by-* or *be-*.

#### C. `won-` (Bound Morpheme / Root Stem)
*   **Type:** Verbal Root.
*   **Meaning:** To live, dwell, or reside.
*   **Etymology:** From Proto-Germanic *\*wunōną*. Cognate with the English archaic/dialectal *won* (to dwell) and *wont* (accustomed).
*   **Orthographic Note:** In the infinitive verb *bewonen*, the vowel is long and written as double **`oo`** (*bewoon-en*) because the syllable is open. However, in the noun *bewoner*, the syllable is closed by the `n`, so the long vowel is written as a single **`o`** (*be-wo-ner*). This is a standard Dutch orthographic rule (open vs. closed syllable spelling).

#### D. `-er` (Bound Morpheme / Suffix)
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix.
*   **Function:** **Agentive Nominalizer.**
    *   It turns a verb into a noun indicating the person who performs the action.
    *   *Bewonen* (to inhabit) + *-er* = *Bewoner* (inhabitant).
*   **Etymology:** Proto-Germanic *\*-rōz*, cognate with English *-er* (as in *worker*, *teacher*).

1.  **`won-`** (dwell) + **`-er`** (one who) = **`woner`** (dwellers - *archaic/rare on its own*).

3.  **`buurt`** + **`bewoner`** = **`buurtbewoner`** (a resident of a specific neighborhood).
