From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`opzien`** is a classic example of a **separable compound verb** (*scheidbaar werkwoord*).

Here is the morphological, syntactic, and semantic breakdown of the word.

### 1. Morphemic Breakdown

The word consists of two distinct morphemes:

| Morpheme | Form | Category | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | `op` | Particle / Preposition | **Verbal Particle** (Prefix) |
| **2** | `zien` | Verb (Infinitive) | **Lexical Root** (Head) |

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Particle: `op`
*   **Category:** In this context, `op` functions as a **verbal particle** (often historically called a preverb). While it originates as a preposition (meaning "on" or "up"), when attached to a verb, it loses its independent syntactic status and modifies the verb's meaning.
*   **Semantics:** It contributes the directional or aspectual meaning of "upward" or "completion."
*   **Stress:** In Dutch separable verbs, the primary lexical stress falls on the particle. Therefore, the pronunciation is **`OP`-zien** (not *op-ZIEN*). This distinguishes it from inseparable verbs (like *bezoeken*), where the stress falls on the root.
*   **Etymology:** Cognate with English *up*, German *auf*, and Old English *up*.

#### B. The Root: `zien`
*   **Category:** Verb (Infinitive).
*   **Semantics:** The core lexical meaning is "to see" or "to look."
*   **Morphology:** `zien` is a **strong verb** (irregular).
    *   Infinitive: *zien*
    *   Past Singular: *zag*
    *   Past Participle: *gezien*
*   **Etymology:** Cognate with English *see*, German *sehen*, and Old English *seon*.

---

### 3. Syntactic Behavior: Separability

The most defining linguistic feature of `opzien` is its **separability**. In Dutch syntax, the particle `op` detaches from the root `zien` in main clauses.

*   **Infinitive (Dictionary Form):** `opzien` (Written as one word).
*   **Main Clause (Conjugated):** The particle moves to the end of the clause.
    *   *Example:* "Ik **zie** naar de sterren **op**." (I look up at the stars.)
    *   *Structure:* Subject + Conjugated Verb (`zie`) + ... + Particle (`op`).
*   **Subordinate Clause:** The verb returns to the end, but the particle remains attached to the infinitive.
    *   *Example:* "... omdat ik naar de sterren **opzie**." (... because I look up at the stars.)

*Note: In the idiomatic expression **`opzien baren`** (to cause a stir), `opzien` functions as a nominalized infinitive acting as the direct object of `baren`.*

---

### 4. Semantic Composition

The meaning of the compound is not always simply the sum of its parts (non-compositional), though it often retains a literal sense.

1.  **Literal Meaning:** "To look up."
    *   *Context:* Physically directing one's gaze upward.
    *   *Example:* "Hij **zag** naar de lucht **op**." (He looked up at the sky.)
2.  **Figurative Meaning:** "To admire" or "to look up to."
    *   *Context:* Metaphorical elevation of status.
    *   *Example:* "Ik **zie** naar hem **op**." (I look up to him / I admire him.)
3.  **Idiomatic Meaning:** "To cause a sensation/stir."
    *   *Context:* Used almost exclusively in the phrase **`opzien baren`**.
    *   *Logic:* Literally "to raise eyes" (attracting attention).
    *   *Example:* "Dat nieuws **baart** **opzien**." (That news causes a stir.)

---

### 5. Summary of Linguistic Features

*   **Word Class:** Verb (Infinitive).
*   **Morphological Type:** Endocentric compound (the head is `zien`, `op` modifies it).
*   **Verb Class:** Separable (*scheidbaar*).
*   **Stress Pattern:** Initial stress on the particle (`OP`-zien).
*   **Transitivity:** Can be intransitive (looking up) or transitive (in the idiom `opzien baren`).

In summary, **`opzien`** is a morphologically transparent compound where the particle **`op`** modifies the directional semantics of the root verb **`zien`**, governed by the syntactic rules of Dutch separable verbs.
