From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch verb **`bewegen`** (to move) can be analyzed through its morphological structure, etymology, and semantic composition.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **be-** + **weg** + **-en**

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Prefix: `be-`
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix.
*   **Function:** In Dutch, the prefix *be-* is **inseparable**. It is used to derive verbs from nouns, adjectives, or other verbs.
*   **Semantic Role:**
    *   **Transitivization:** It often turns an intransitive concept into a transitive one (making the action affect an object).
    *   **Causative/Thoroughness:** It can imply "to cause to be" or "to apply something thoroughly."
    *   **In this context:** When attached to the root *weg-*, it adds the sense of "setting into motion" or "affecting with motion." It shifts the meaning from a state of being to an active process of moving something or oneself.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Proto-Germanic *\*bi-*, cognate with the English prefix *be-* (as in *become*, *befriend*).

#### B. The Root: `weg`
*   **Type:** Bound Root (in this specific verb).
*   **Meaning:** This is the core semantic unit. It relates to **motion, weight, or carrying**.
*   **Crucial Distinction:** It is important not to confuse this root with the modern Dutch noun *weg* (meaning "road" or "way"). While they share a common ancient ancestor, they have diverged semantically.
    *   The root in *bewegen* is related to the concept of **shifting weight** or **waving**.
    *   **Cognates:** It is etymologically related to the English verb **to wag** (as in wagging a tail), **to wobble**, and **to weigh**. All these words stem from the Proto-Indo-European (PIE) root *\*wegh-*, meaning "to go, carry, or weigh."
*   **Modern Usage:** The root *weg-* does not function as a standalone verb in modern Dutch (unlike *lopen* or *gaan*), making it a bound morpheme in this context.

#### C. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix.
*   **Function:** This is the standard marker for the **infinitive** form of Dutch verbs.
*   **Grammar:** It indicates that the verb is in its base form, not conjugated for person or tense.
*   **Pronunciation:** In many dialects, the final *-n* is dropped (apocope), making it sound like *bewege*, but orthographically it remains *-en*.

---

### 3. Semantic Composition (How the meaning is built)
When you combine these morphemes, the meaning is constructed as follows:

1.  **`weg`**: The concept of motion/weight.
2.  **`be-`**: Applies that motion to an object or subject (causative/transitive).
3.  **`-en`**: Marks it as an action (verb).

**Result:** "To cause to move" or "to set in motion."

This explains why *bewegen* has two primary meanings in Dutch:
1.  **Physical Movement:** *Ik beweeg mijn arm.* (I move my arm.)
2.  **Influence/Persuasion:** *Hij kon mij niet bewegen.* (He could not move/persuade me.)
    *   *Linguistic Note:* The second meaning is a metaphorical extension of the first. Just as you physically move an object, you can "move" someone's mind or emotions.

### 4. Grammatical Classification
*   **Verb Class:** *Bewegen* is a **strong verb** (sterk werkwoord).
*   **Conjugation:** It undergoes a vowel change (ablaut) in the past tense, reflecting its Germanic heritage.
    *   Infinitive: *bewegen*
    *   Past Tense (Singular): *bewoog*
    *   Past Tense (Plural): *bewogen*
    *   Past Participle: *bewogen*
*   **Transitivity:** It can be used transitively (*Ik beweeg de tafel* - I move the table) or intransitively/reflexively (*Ik beweeg* / *Ik beweeg me* - I move myself).
