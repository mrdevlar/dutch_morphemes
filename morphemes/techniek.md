From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`techniek`** is a loanword that can be analyzed both **etymologically** (historical structure) and **synchronically** (current usage in modern Dutch).

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
In terms of etymology and derivational morphology, the word is **bimorphemic** (composed of two morphemes):

> **techn-** + **-iek**

### 2. Detailed Breakdown

#### A. The Root: `techn-`
*   **Type:** Bound Root (Lexical Morpheme)
*   **Origin:** Ancient Greek **τέχνη** (*tekhnē*).
*   **Original Meaning:** "Art," "craft," "skill," or "method."
*   **Function:** This carries the core semantic meaning of the word.
*   **Linguistic Note:** In modern Dutch, this is a **non-productive bound root**. This means Dutch speakers cannot freely attach this root to other native Dutch words to create new meanings (e.g., you cannot say *`huis-techniek`* to mean "house craft" in a standard way; you would use *`bouwkunde`* or similar). It only appears in learned loanwords (e.g., *technoloog*, *technisch*, *techniek*).

#### B. The Suffix: `-iek`
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix
*   **Origin:** Ancient Greek **-ική** (*-ikē*), passed through Latin *-ica* and French *-ique*.
*   **Function:**
    1.  **Adjectival Origin:** Historically, this suffix formed adjectives meaning "pertaining to" (e.g., *technique* in French).
    2.  **Nominalization (Substantivization):** In Dutch, this suffix has been reanalyzed to form an **abstract noun**. It transforms the concept of "skill" into "the field of study or practice of skill."
*   **Pronunciation:** In Dutch, the *ch* in the root is pronounced as a hard /k/ sound (unlike the German *ch* or the English *ch* in "church"), which aligns with the Greek etymology.

### 3. Etymological Path (Diachronic Analysis)
The word traveled through several languages before settling in Dutch:
1.  **Greek:** *tekhnē* (noun: art/craft) + *-ikē* (adjective suffix) $\rightarrow$ *tekhnikē* (pertaining to art).
2.  **Latin:** *technica* (adopted as a noun).
3.  **French:** *technique* (noun/adjective).
4.  **Dutch:** *techniek* (noun).

### 4. Synchronic Analysis (Modern Dutch)
While we can break it down etymologically, a linguist analyzing **modern Dutch morphology** might argue that for the average speaker, `techniek` functions as a **monomorphemic** word (a single unit).

*   **Why?** Because the root `techn-` is not recognized by speakers as an independent unit. They do not intuitively feel that `techniek` is made of `techn` + `iek` in the same way they feel `hond-` + `-en` (dogs) is made of `hond` + plural marker.
*   **Derivational Family:** However, the root becomes visible when looking at the word family:
    *   *Techniek* (Noun: Technology/Technique)
    *   *Technisch* (Adjective: Technical) $\rightarrow$ Suffix *-isch*
    *   *Technoloog* (Noun: Technologist) $\rightarrow$ Suffix *-oloog*

### Conclusion
Linguistically, **`techniek`** is a **learned loanword** consisting of a Greek bound root (**techn-**) and a nominalizing suffix (**-iek**). While historically bimorphemic, in the mental lexicon of a modern Dutch speaker, it is often stored as a single, unanalyzable unit (a simplex word).
