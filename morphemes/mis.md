However, because "mis" functions in three distinct ways in the language, we must analyze it as **three different lexical morphemes** that happen to share the same phonological and orthographic form (homonyms).

### 1. The Prefix: *mis-* (Bound Morpheme)
When attached to the beginning of a verb or noun, *mis-* functions as a **derivational prefix**.

*   **Morpheme Type:** Bound (cannot stand alone in this context).
*   **Function:** Derivational (changes the meaning of the root word).
*   **Meaning:** "Wrongly," "badly," "incorrectly," or "negatively."
*   **Examples:**
    *   *mislukken* (to fail) = *mis-* (wrongly) + *lukken* (to succeed).
    *   *misbruik* (abuse) = *mis-* (wrongly) + *bruik* (use).
    *   *misverstand* (misunderstanding) = *mis-* (wrongly) + *verstand* (understanding).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Proto-Germanic **\*mis-**, meaning "wrong" or "bad." It is cognate with the English prefix *mis-* (as in *mistake* or *mislead*).

### 2. The Noun: *de mis* (Free Morpheme)

*   **Morpheme Type:** Free (can stand alone as a word).

### 3. The Verb Stem: *mis-* (Free/Bound Root)

*   **Morpheme Type:** Free (as a root) / Bound (when conjugated).

*   **Morphological Breakdown of *missen*:**
