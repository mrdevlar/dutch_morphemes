From a linguistic perspective, it is important to clarify first that **"`afhangen van`" is not a single word**, but rather a **phrasal verb construction** consisting of a separable verb (`afhangen`) and a governing preposition (`van`).

Here is the morphological and syntactic breakdown of the components.

### 1. The Verb: `afhangen`
This is a **separable compound verb** (*scheidbaar werkwoord*). In Dutch morphology, this is analyzed as three distinct morphemes.

| Morpheme | Form | Type | Function/Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`af-`** | Prefix | **Derivational Prefix** | Indicates direction (down/off/away). In this context, it modifies the action of the root verb. |
| **`hang-`** | Root/Stem | **Lexical Root** | The core semantic meaning: "to hang" (from the strong verb *hangen*). |
| **`-en`** | Suffix | **Inflectional Suffix** | Marks the **infinitive** form of the verb. |

**Linguistic Note on Separability:**
Because `af-` is a separable prefix, the verb splits in main clauses.
*   *Infinitive/Subordinate:* "...dat het **afhangt** van..." (The prefix stays attached).
*   *Main Clause:* "Het **hangt** ervan **af**." (The prefix moves to the end of the clause).

**Semantic Shift:**
*   **Literal:** To hang off something (e.g., *De gordijnen hangen af van de stang* - The curtains hang down from the rod).
*   **Idiomatic:** To depend on (e.g., *Het hangt af van het weer* - It depends on the weather).
*   *Linguistic Mechanism:* This is a case of **grammaticalization** or **idiomatization**, where the physical action of "hanging off" metaphorically transforms into a state of "reliance."

### 2. The Preposition: `van`
This is a separate lexical item that forms a fixed collocation with the verb.

| Morpheme | Form | Type | Function/Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`van`** | Word | **Preposition** | Monomorphemic (cannot be broken down further). It marks the **oblique argument** (the object of dependency). |

**Linguistic Note on Valency:**
In linguistics, this is known as a **prepositional verb** (*voorzetselwerkwoord*). The verb `afhangen` has a specific **valency** requirement; it *must* be followed by the preposition `van` to complete its meaning in the context of "dependence." You cannot say "Het hangt het weer af" (incorrect); it must be "Het hangt **van** het weer af."

### 3. Full Phrase Analysis: `afhangen van`

When combined, these morphemes create a complex predicate.

*   **Morphological Structure:** `[Prefix: af-] + [Root: hang-] + [Suffix: -en] + [Preposition: van]`
*   **Syntactic Category:** Verb Phrase (VP)
*   **Semantic Role:**
    *   **Subject:** The entity that is dependent (e.g., *The result*).
    *   **Object of `van`:** The entity causing the dependency (e.g., *The weather*).

### Summary of Morphemes

1.  **`af`**: Bound morpheme (prefix). Adds directional/completive aspect.
2.  **`hang`**: Free morpheme (root). Carries the core lexical meaning.
3.  **`en`**: Bound morpheme (suffix). Grammatical marker for infinitive.
4.  **`van`**: Free morpheme (preposition). Governs the object of the dependency.

### Example in Context
> *"De beslissing **hangt af van** de directeur."*
> (The decision **depends on** the director.)

*   **`hangt`**: Root + 3rd person singular inflection (`-t`).
*   **`af`**: Separated prefix moved to the end of the clause.
*   **`van`**: Preposition introducing the agent of dependency (`de directeur`).

This structure is a classic example of how Dutch uses **separable prefixes** and **prepositional valency** to create specific idiomatic meanings that differ from the literal sum of their parts.
