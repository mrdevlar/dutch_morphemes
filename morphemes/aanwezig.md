From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`aanwezig`** (meaning "present" or "in attendance") is a complex adjective formed through derivational morphology. It consists of three distinct morphemes.

Here is the breakdown and explanation of each component.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented as follows:

> **aan** + **wees** + **ig**

*(Note: In the surface form, the 's' in the root becomes voiced to 'z', resulting in the spelling `aanwezig`.)*

---

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Prefix: `aan`
*   **Category:** Preposition / Inseparable Prefix
*   **Literal Meaning:** "on," "at," "to," or "towards."
*   **Function:** In this context, it functions as a locative prefix. It establishes a spatial or situational relationship.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *an*, cognate with English *on* and German *an*.
*   **Linguistic Note:** While *aan* is a free morpheme when used as a preposition (e.g., *aan de tafel* / "at the table"), in *aanwezig*, it is bound to the root. It transforms the concept of "being" into "being **at** [a specific place]."

#### B. The Root: `wees-`
*   **Category:** Verbal Stem
*   **Source:** The verb *zijn* (to be).
*   **Literal Meaning:** "to be" or "existence."
*   **Etymology:** This is the most historically interesting part. The modern Dutch verb for "to be" is *zijn*, which is suppletive (it uses different roots for different tenses).
    *   The root *wees-* comes from the Proto-Germanic *\*wesan-* (to be).
    *   It is cognate with the English past tense forms **was/were** and the German noun *Wesen* (being/nature).
    *   In modern Dutch, this root survives primarily in the noun *wezen* (being/creature) and in this specific adjective.
*   **Phonological Change:** The underlying root ends in an /s/ sound. However, in Dutch orthography and phonology, /s/ becomes voiced (/z/) when it appears between vowels or before a voiced suffix. Since the suffix *-ig* begins with a vowel sound, the spelling changes from *wees* to *wezig*.

#### C. The Suffix: `-ig`
*   **Category:** Derivational Suffix
*   **Function:** Adjectivizer. It turns a verb or noun stem into an adjective.
*   **Meaning:** "characterized by," "having the quality of," or "full of."
*   **Etymology:** Cognate with the English suffix *-y* (as in *weary* from *wear*) and the German suffix *-ig*.
*   **Example:** *vriend* (friend) + *-ig* → *vriendelijk* (friendly); *zond* (sin) + *-ig* → *zondig* (sinful).

---

### 3. Semantic Composition (How the meaning is built)

To understand the full meaning, we combine the semantics of the morphemes:

1.  **`wees-`** = Being / Existence
2.  **`-ig`** = Having the quality of [Being]
3.  **`aan`** = At / In the location of

**Literal Translation:** "Having the quality of being at [a place]."
**Actual Meaning:** "Present."

### 4. Related Linguistic Context

*   **Antonym:** The opposite is **`afwezig`** (absent).
    *   *af* (off/away) + *wees* (be) + *ig* (quality).
    *   "Having the quality of being away."
*   **Verb Form:** The verb form is **`aanwezig zijn`** (to be present).
*   **Noun Form:** The noun form is **`aanwezigheid`** (presence).
    *   *aanwezig* + *-heid* (suffix for abstract nouns, cognate with English *-hood*).
