From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`hoesten`** (meaning "to cough") can be analyzed morphologically as a combination of two distinct morphemes.

Here is the breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word is segmented as follows:
> **`hoest`** + **`-en`**

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### **Morpheme 1: `hoest-` (The Root/Stem)**
*   **Type:** Free Lexical Morpheme (Root).
*   **Meaning:** This morpheme carries the core semantic content of the word: the sound or action of coughing.
*   **Part of Speech:** This root is versatile. It functions as the stem for the verb *hoesten*, but it also exists independently as a noun (*de hoest* = "the cough").
*   **Etymology & Semantics:** This is an **onomatopoeic** root. It is a sound-symbolic word that imitates the actual sound of a cough. It is cognate with the German *Husten* and the English *hustle* (originally related to shaking or coughing, though the meaning has shifted in English).
*   **Phonology:** In the infinitive form, the vowel is long /uː/ (spelled *oe*). In some conjugated forms (like the past tense *hoestte*), the vowel remains long, but the consonant cluster changes slightly due to Dutch spelling rules (doubling the consonant to keep the vowel short in closed syllables, though here the vowel remains long in the stem).

#### **Morpheme 2: `-en` (The Inflectional Suffix)**
*   **Type:** Bound Inflectional Morpheme.
*   **Function:** This suffix marks the **infinitive** form of the verb. In Dutch grammar, the infinitive is the base form of the verb found in dictionaries.
*   **Grammar:** It indicates that the action is not yet conjugated for person or number.
    *   *Ik hoest* (I cough) — Suffix removed.
    *   *Wij hoesten* (We cough) — Suffix retained.
*   **Phonological Note:** In standard written Dutch, the suffix is always spelled *-en*. However, in spoken Dutch (and many dialects), the final *-n* is frequently elided (dropped) in pronunciation, resulting in /ˈhuːstə/. Despite this phonological variation, morphologically, it is analyzed as the suffix *-en*.

### 4. Contextual Morphology (Conjugation)
To understand the stability of these morphemes, it helps to look at how they behave in a conjugation paradigm. The root `hoest-` remains stable, while the suffix changes or disappears based on grammatical context:

*   **Infinitive:** `hoest` + `en` = **hoesten** (to cough)
*   **Present (1st/3rd person):** `hoest` + `∅` = **hoest** (I cough / he coughs)
*   **Present (Plural):** `hoest` + `en` = **hoesten** (we cough)
*   **Past Tense:** `hoest` + `te` + `n` = **hoestten** (coughed)
    *   *Note:* Here we see a derivational suffix `-te-` (past tense marker) inserted before the plural marker `-n`.

### 5. Linguistic Conclusion
The word **`hoesten`** is a **monomorphemic root** (`hoest`) combined with a **regular inflectional suffix** (`-en`). It represents a classic example of Dutch verb formation where a noun or sound-symbolic root is verbalized simply by adding the standard infinitive ending.
