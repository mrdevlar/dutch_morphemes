From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`dienstverlenen`** is a **compound word** (samengesteld woord) formed through a process of **noun-verb compounding**.

While standard Dutch orthography often writes the verbal form as two words (*dienst verlenen*), the single-word form *dienstverlenen* is frequently used as a nominalized verb (a noun derived from a verb) or in formal/bureaucratic contexts.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes:

### 1. Morphological Structure
The word consists of **three distinct morphemes**:

1.  **`dienst`** (Noun Stem)
2.  **`ver-`** (Derivational Prefix)
3.  **`lenen`** (Verb Stem)

**Formula:** `[dienst] + [ver-] + [lenen]`

---

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. `dienst` (Noun Stem)
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun (Substantive).
*   **Meaning:** Service, duty, work, or attendance.
*   **Function:** In this compound, it acts as the **object** or the **theme** of the action. It specifies *what* is being provided.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *dinst*. It is cognate with the German *Dienst* and the English word *thirst* (via the Proto-Germanic root *\*þeudō* meaning "people/nation," though the semantic shift to "service" happened early in the Germanic branch). It is directly related to the verb *dienen* (to serve).

#### B. `ver-` (Prefix)
*   **Part of Speech:** Derivational Prefix (Verbum prefix).
*   **Meaning:** This prefix often indicates a change of state, completion, or direction. In the context of *verlenen*, it shifts the meaning from "lending" to "granting" or "bestowing."
*   **Function:** It modifies the verb stem *lenen* to create a new lexical meaning. It makes the action transitive and formal.
*   **Linguistic Note:** In Dutch, *ver-* is a **bound morpheme**; it cannot stand alone as a word.

#### C. `lenen` (Verb Stem)
*   **Part of Speech:** Verb (Infinitive stem).
*   **Meaning:** To lend.
*   **Function:** This is the **head** of the compound. It provides the core action.
*   **Etymology:** From Old Dutch *lenen*, Proto-Germanic *\*launōną*. It is cognate with the English word *loan*.
*   **Semantic Shift:** In the compound *verlenen*, the original meaning of "lending" (temporarily giving possession) has undergone **semantic bleaching** or broadening. It no longer implies taking something back; instead, it means to "grant," "provide," or "render" something permanently or officially.

---

### 3. Semantic Composition (How the meaning is built)

The meaning of the compound is not simply the sum of its parts, but a specific idiomatic combination:

1.  **Literal:** "To lend a service."
2.  **Actual Meaning:** "To render a service," "to provide assistance," or "to serve."

The prefix *ver-* is crucial here. If you simply said *dienst lenen*, it would imply borrowing a service (which makes little sense). By adding *ver-*, the meaning shifts to **bestowing** the service upon someone else.

### 4. Syntactic and Orthographic Nuance

From a syntactic perspective, there is an important distinction in Dutch regarding this word:

*   **As a Verb (Separable):** In standard spoken and written Dutch, the verb is usually treated as **separable**.
    *   *Example:* "Ik **verleen** u graag **dienst**." (I gladly render you service.)
    *   *Past Tense:* "Ik **verleende** **dienst**."
    *   *Note:* In this case, it is written as two words: *dienst verlenen*.
*   **As a Noun (Nominalization):** When written as one word (*dienstverlenen*), it is often treated as a **substantive** (noun).
    *   *Example:* "Het **dienstverlenen** is belangrijk." (The rendering of service is important.)
    *   *Note:* This is why the prompt's one-word version is linguistically valid as a nominalized compound.
