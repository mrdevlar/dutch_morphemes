From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`doorgaan`** is a classic example of a **compound verb** (samenstellend werkwoord) formed through **derivation** and **composition**.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word consists of two distinct morphemes:

> **`door`** + **`gaan`**

*   **`door`**: A separable prefix (or verbal particle).
*   **`gaan`**: The lexical verb root (stam).

---

### 2. Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Prefix: `door`
*   **Category:** Preposition / Adverb / Separable Particle.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *dur*, related to the German *durch* and English *through*. It stems from the Proto-Germanic *\*duruh*.
*   **Function in this context:** In isolation, *door* is a preposition meaning "through" or "by." However, when attached to a verb, it functions as a **verbal particle**. It modifies the meaning of the root verb, adding a sense of continuity, penetration, or completion.
*   **Stress:** In Dutch separable verbs, the primary stress falls on the prefix. Therefore, the pronunciation is **`DOOR`-gaan** (not *door-GAAN*). This distinguishes it from inseparable verbs (like *bezoeken*), where the stress is on the root.

#### B. The Root: `gaan`
*   **Category:** Verb (Irregular/Strong Verb).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *gān*, related to German *gehen* and English *go*. It stems from the Proto-Germanic *\*gāną*.
*   **Meaning:** To go, to move, to proceed.
*   **Function:** This is the core semantic unit of the word. It provides the action.

---

### 3. Syntactic Behavior: Separability
A crucial linguistic feature of `doorgaan` is that it is a **separable verb** (*scheidbaar werkwoord*).

*   **Infinitive Form:** Written as one word (`doorgaan`).
    *   *Example:* "Ik wil **doorgaan**." (I want to continue.)
*   **Conjugated Form (Main Clause):** The prefix `door` separates from the root `gaan` and moves to the end of the clause.
    *   *Example:* "Ik **ga** vandaag **door**." (I am continuing today.)
*   **Past Participle:** In the perfect tense, the particle `door` remains at the front, and the standard past participle marker `ge-` is inserted between the prefix and the stem.
    *   *Example:* "Ik heb **doorgaan**." (Wait, correction: The participle of *gaan* is *gegaan*. Therefore, the participle of *doorgaan* is **`doorgaan`**.)
    *   *Note:* Because *gaan* is an irregular verb, the participle is *gegaan*. Combined with the prefix, it becomes *doorgaan*.

---

### 4. Semantic Composition (Meaning)
The meaning of `doorgaan` is not simply the sum of its parts; it exhibits **idiomaticity**.

1.  **Literal Meaning:** "To go through."
    *   *Context:* Physical movement through an obstacle or space.
    *   *Example:* "De trein gaat door de tunnel." (The train goes through the tunnel.)
2.  **Figurative/Idiomatic Meaning:** "To continue," "to proceed," or "to persist."
    *   *Context:* Continuing an action, conversation, or state.
    *   *Example:* "Ga maar door met praten." (Just continue talking.)
    *   *Example:* "Het gaat door." (It continues / It goes on.)

This shift from physical movement ("go through") to temporal continuity ("continue") is a common semantic shift in Germanic languages (similar to the English phrasal verb "go on").

### 5. Summary Table

| Morpheme | Type | Meaning | Role in Compound |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`door`** | Separable Prefix | Through / Across | Modifies direction/aspect (continuity) |
| **`gaan`** | Verb Root | To Go | Provides the core action |
| **`doorgaan`** | Compound Verb | To Continue / Go Through | Combined semantic unit |

### Conclusion
Linguistically, **`doorgaan`** is a **separable compound verb**. It demonstrates how Dutch morphology allows prepositions to function as verbal particles that alter the aspect of a verb (in this case, adding a sense of continuity). The separation of the morphemes in conjugated sentences is a defining syntactic feature of this word class in Dutch.
