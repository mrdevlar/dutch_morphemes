From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`zelfreflectie`** is a **compound noun** formed by combining a Germanic root with a Latinate loanword.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation of the morphemes.

### 1. Macro-Structure: The Compound
At the highest level of analysis, the word is a **closed compound** (two words joined without a space or hyphen).

*   **Word:** `zelf` + `reflectie`
*   **Type:** Endocentric Compound (The head is on the right; it is a type of *reflectie*).
*   **Orthography:** In Dutch, compounds are typically written as one word (unlike English, which often uses a space or hyphen for "self-reflection").

### 2. Micro-Structure: Morpheme Breakdown

We can analyze the word into four distinct morphemes:

| Morpheme | Type | Origin | Meaning/Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`zelf`** | Free Root | Germanic | "Self" |
| **`re-`** | Prefix | Latin | "Back" or "Again" |
| **`flect`** | Root (Bound) | Latin | "To bend" |
| **`-ie`** | Suffix | Latin/French | Nominalizer (turns verb to noun) |

#### A. `zelf` (Free Morpheme / Root)
*   **Part of Speech:** Pronoun functioning as a noun modifier.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *selb*, cognate with German *selbst* and English *self*.
*   **Function:** In this compound, it acts as a specifier. It indicates that the action of the head noun is reflexive (directed back at the subject).
*   **Pronunciation:** /zɛlf/

#### B. `reflectie` (Derived Noun)
This second part of the compound is itself a derived word. It comes from the verb *reflecteren* (to reflect).

**1. `re-` (Prefix)**
*   **Type:** Bound Morpheme.
*   **Etymology:** Latin *re-* (indicating direction back or repetition).
*   **Function:** Indicates that the "bending" action is directed backward.
*   **Note:** In Dutch, this prefix is productive in loanwords (e.g., *reageren*, *repetitie*).

**2. `flect` (Root/Stem)**
*   **Type:** Bound Root (in Dutch).
*   **Etymology:** Latin *flectere* (to bend).
*   **Function:** The core semantic meaning.
*   **Note:** Unlike in English, where the root survives as "flex," in Dutch this root does not exist as an independent word. It only appears within loanwords (e.g., *flexibel*, *reflecteren*).

**3. `-ie` (Suffix)**
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix.
*   **Etymology:** Latin *-ia* via French *-ie*.
*   **Function:** **Nominalizer.** It converts the verbal concept (*reflecteren*) into an abstract noun (*reflectie*).
*   **Pronunciation:** /i/ (long 'ee' sound).
*   **Note:** This is a very common suffix in Dutch for words of Latin/French origin (e.g., *politie*, *muziek* [from -ic], *academie*).

### 3. Semantic Composition
The meaning of the whole word is the sum of its parts, following a specific logical path:

1.  **`flect`** = To bend.
2.  **`re-` + `flect`** = To bend back (the mind/thought).
3.  **`re-` + `flect` + `-ie`** = The act/state of bending back (reflection).
4.  **`zelf` + `reflectie`** = The act of bending one's thoughts back upon oneself (Self-reflection).

### 4. Linguistic Observations

*   **Mixed Morphology:** This word illustrates a common feature of Dutch (and Germanic languages in general): the combination of native Germanic vocabulary (`zelf`) with international Latinate vocabulary (`reflectie`).
*   **Synchronic vs. Diachronic:**
    *   *Diachronically* (historically), the breakdown is `re-` + `flect` + `-ie`.
    *   *Synchronically* (in modern usage), a Dutch speaker might perceive `reflectie` as a single, unbreakable root noun, as they cannot independently use `flect` or `re-` in other contexts. However, the etymological structure remains transparent to educated speakers.
*   **Pluralization:** If pluralized, the suffix attaches to the end of the compound: `zelfreflectie` + `s` = `zelfreflecties`.

### Summary
**`zelfreflectie`** = **`zelf`** (Self) + **`re-`** (Back) + **`flect`** (Bend) + **`-ie`** (Noun suffix).
**Literal Translation:** "Self-bending-back-ness" $\rightarrow$ **Self-reflection.**
