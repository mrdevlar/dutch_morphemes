From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`sturen`** (meaning "to send," "to steer," or "to direct") is a monomorphemic verb base combined with an inflectional suffix.

Here is the morphological breakdown:

### 1. The Breakdown
**`stuur`** + **`-en`**

| Morpheme | Type | Function | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`stuur-`** | **Root / Stem** | Lexical | Carries the core semantic meaning (to send/steer). |
| **`-en`** | **Inflectional Suffix** | Grammatical | Marks the verb as the **infinitive** form. |

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis

#### A. The Root: `stuur-`
*   **Classification:** This is the **verbal stem**. In Dutch morphology, the stem is the part of the verb that remains constant during conjugation (mostly).
*   **Free vs. Bound:** While `stuur-` functions as a bound morpheme within the verb *sturen*, it is also a **free morpheme** in Dutch as a standalone noun (*het stuur*), meaning "the steering wheel" or "the helm." This is a case of **conversion** (or zero-derivation), where the same phonological form functions as both a noun and a verb stem.
*   **Etymology:** It derives from Middle Dutch *sturen*, Old Dutch *stōrōn*, and Proto-Germanic *\*stōrōną*. It is cognate with the English word *steer* and the German word *steuern*.
*   **Phonology:** The vowel `uu` represents a long close-mid back rounded vowel (/yː/).

#### B. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Classification:** This is an **inflectional morpheme**.
*   **Function:** In Dutch, `-en` is the standard marker for the **infinitive** of verbs (e.g., *lopen* - to walk, *maken* - to make). It does not change the part of speech (it remains a verb) but indicates the non-finite form used for dictionary entries and after modal verbs.
*   **Phonology:** In standard pronunciation, this suffix is often reduced to a schwa sound (/ə/). So, *sturen* is pronounced roughly as /ˈstyː.rə(n)/.
*   **Note on Plural:** Interestingly, `-en` is also the plural marker for many Dutch nouns (e.g., *hond* → *honden*). However, in *sturen*, it is strictly verbal inflection.

---

### 3. Morphological Context & Conjugation
To understand the stability of the root `stuur-`, it helps to look at how the word changes in different grammatical contexts. The root remains intact while the suffix changes:

*   **Infinitive:** `stuur` + `en` → **sturen**
*   **Present Tense (Ik):** `stuur` + `∅` (zero suffix) → **stuur**
*   **Present Tense (Jij):** `stuur` + `t` → **stuurt**
*   **Past Tense (Weak):** `stuur` + `de` → **stuurde**
*   **Past Participle:** `ge` + `stuur` + `d` → **gestuurd**

### 4. Derivational Potential
The root `stuur-` is highly productive and serves as the base for other derived words, showing its status as a core lexical unit:

*   **`besturen`**: *be-* (prefix) + *stuur* (root) + *-en* (infinitive). Meaning: "to govern" or "to manage."
*   **`stuurman`**: *stuur* (root) + *man* (noun). Meaning: "helmsman" or "pilot."
*   **`stuurbaar`**: *stuur* (root) + *-baar* (adjectival suffix). Meaning: "steerable."

### Summary
Linguistically, **`sturen`** is a **verb** composed of a **lexical root** (`stuur-`) carrying the semantic load of directing or sending, and an **inflectional suffix** (`-en`) that grammatically marks it as the infinitive.
