From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch verb **`snurken`** (meaning "to snore") can be analyzed through its morphological structure, etymology, and semantic function.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word consists of two distinct morphemes:

> **`snurk`** + **`-en`**

*   **`snurk-`**: The lexical root (stem).
*   **`-en`**: The grammatical suffix (infinitive marker).

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Root: `snurk-`
*   **Type:** Free/Bound Root (Lexical Morpheme).
*   **Function:** Carries the core semantic meaning of the word.
*   **Etymology & Origin:**
    *   **Onomatopoeia (Sound Symbolism):** This is the most significant linguistic feature of this root. The word is **imitative** in nature. The consonant cluster `/sn-/` combined with the back vowel and the velar stop `/k/` mimics the harsh, nasal, and rhythmic sound of snoring.
    *   **Proto-Germanic:** It stems from a Proto-Germanic root related to breathing sounds (reconstructed as *\*snurk-* or *\*snark-*).
    *   **Cognates:**
        *   **English:** *Snore* (Old English *snorran*). While they share the same semantic field and Germanic origin, the Dutch form underwent a vowel shift (u) compared to the English (o).
        *   **German:** *Schnarchen*. This is a closer structural cognate, sharing the nasal onset and the guttural ending.
        *   **Frisian:** *Snurkje*.
*   **Phonology:** In Dutch, the `u` is typically pronounced as a short, rounded front vowel /ʏ/ (similar to the 'u' in English "but" but rounded).

#### B. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix.
*   **Function:** Marks the verb as an **infinitive**.
*   **Grammar:**
    *   In Dutch, `-en` is the standard ending for the infinitive form of regular verbs (e.g., *lopen* "to walk", *werken* "to work").
    *   It also serves as the plural marker for the first and third person plural in the present tense (e.g., *wij snurken* "we snore").
    *   It distinguishes the verb from the noun form *snurk* (a snore) or the agent noun *snurker* (a snorer).

---

### 3. Derivational Family
Understanding the root `snurk-` allows us to see how it generates other words in the Dutch lexicon:

1.  **`snurk`** (Noun): The sound itself ("a snore").
2.  **`snurker`** (Noun): Agentive suffix `-er` + root. Means "a snorer" (one who performs the action).
3.  **`snurkachtig`** (Adjective): Suffix `-achtig` (like/resembling). Means "snore-like" or "noisy."
4.  **`snurkend`** (Participle): Suffix `-end`. Used as a present participle ("snoring").

### Linguistic Conclusion
The word **`snurken`** is a classic example of **phonosemantic motivation**. The morphology is simple (Root + Infinitive Suffix), but the root itself is a direct acoustic representation of the phenomenon it describes. It highlights how Germanic languages often utilize nasal-initial clusters (`sn-`, `sl-`, `sp-`) to describe sensory or breath-related actions (e.g., *sneeuwen* "to snow", *slapen* "to sleep", *spugen* "to spit").
