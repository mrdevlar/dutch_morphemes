From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`schilder`** (meaning "painter") can be analyzed through its morphological structure, etymology, and semantic development.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation

The word **`schilder`** consists of two morphemes:

| Morpheme | Type | Form | Function/Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`schild-`** | **Root** (Free/Bound) | *schild* | The base meaning: "shield" or "coat/layer". |
| **`-er`** | **Suffix** (Derivational) | *-er* | Agentive marker: "one who performs the action". |

**Formula:** `schild-` (Root) + `-er` (Suffix) = `schilder` (Noun)

---

### 2. Detailed Explanation of Morphemes

#### A. The Root: `schild-`
*   **Etymology:** This root comes from the Old Dutch word *schild*, which originally meant **"shield"** (a piece of armor).
*   **Semantic Shift:** Over time, the meaning expanded. Because shields were often painted with coats of arms or colors, the word *schild* began to be associated with the **coat of paint** itself (similar to the English word "coat" in "coat of paint").
*   **Modern Usage:** In modern Dutch, *schild* still exists as a free morpheme meaning "shield" (*het schild*) or a "coat/layer" of something (*een schild van verf*).
*   **Phonology:** In the standalone word *schild*, the final *d* is devoiced due to final-obstruent devoicing (pronounced /sɪlt/). However, in *schilder*, the *d* is followed by a vowel, so it remains voiced (/sɪld/).

#### B. The Suffix: `-er`
*   **Type:** This is an **agentive suffix** (agentief achtervoegsel).
*   **Function:** It derives a noun (specifically, a person) from a verb or a root. It indicates "the one who does [root action]."
*   **Examples:**
    *   *bakken* (to bake) → *bakker* (baker)
    *   *leren* (to teach/learn) → *leraar* (teacher) *(Note: irregular)*
    *   *schilder* (to paint) → *schilder* (painter)

---

### 3. Relationship to the Verb `schilderen`

To fully understand the morphology, one must look at the verb **`schilderen`** (to paint).

*   **Verb Structure:** `schild-` (Root) + `-er-` (Suffix) + `-en` (Infinitive ending).
*   **Noun Structure:** `schild-` (Root) + `-er` (Suffix).

In this specific case, the noun **`schilder`** is identical to the **verb stem** of *schilderen*.
*   **Verb:** *Ik schilder* (I paint).
*   **Noun:** *De schilder* (The painter).

This makes *schilder* a **deverbal noun** (a noun derived from a verb). However, because the verb stem already contains the *-er-* sound, the morphological boundary is slightly opaque to modern speakers. They perceive *schilder* as a single unit rather than *schild* + *er*.

### 4. Historical Context (Diachronic Perspective)

From a historical linguistics perspective, the word is a classic example of **semantic narrowing and generalization**:

1.  **Original Meaning:** "One who paints shields" (a specific craft).
2.  **Generalization:** The meaning broadened to "one who paints" (any surface).
3.  **Modern Meaning:** "Painter" (artist or house painter).

### 5. Linguistic Transparency

*   **Synchronic Transparency (Modern):** Low. Most modern Dutch speakers do not consciously link *schilder* (painter) to *schild* (shield). They link it to the verb *schilderen*.
*   **Diachronic Transparency (Historical):** High. The structure clearly shows *shield* + *agent*.

### Summary
The word **`schilder`** is a **derived noun** formed by adding the agentive suffix **`-er`** to the root **`schild-`**. While historically it meant "shield-painter," it now functions as the standard term for any painter, derived from the verb *schilderen*.
