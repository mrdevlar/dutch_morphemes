From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`duwen`** (meaning "to push") can be analyzed as a **monomorphemic root** combined with an **inflectional suffix**.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes:

### 1. The Morpheme Breakdown

| Morpheme | Form | Type | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`duw-`** | Root / Stem | Lexical (Content) | Carries the core semantic meaning ("push"). |
| **`-en`** | Suffix | Inflectional (Grammatical) | Marks the verb as an **infinitive** (and also the plural present tense). |

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis

#### A. The Root: `duw-`
*   **Category:** This is the **lexical root** (or stem). It contains the primary meaning of the word.
*   **Status:** In this specific context (`duwen`), it functions as a **bound morpheme** because it cannot stand alone as a verb in standard Dutch (you cannot say "Ik duw" without conjugation, though you can say "Ik duw" in the present tense singular, the form `duw` is technically the stem).
*   **Polysemy:** Interestingly, `duw` can also function as a **free morpheme** when used as a noun.
    *   *Example:* "Geef me een **duw**." (Give me a **push**.)
    *   In the noun form, `duw` is a free morpheme. In the verb form `duwen`, it is the verbal stem.
*   **Phonology:** The vowel `u` represents a long close front rounded vowel /yː/ in Standard Dutch. The `w` represents the labio-velar approximant /ʋ/.

#### B. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Category:** This is an **inflectional suffix**. It does not change the part of speech (it remains a verb) but changes the grammatical form.
*   **Function:**
    1.  **Infinitive Marker:** It indicates the base form of the verb (e.g., "Ik wil **duwen**" / I want to push).
    2.  **Plural Present Marker:** It is also used for the first, second, and third person plural in the present tense (e.g., "Wij **duwen**" / We push).
*   **Allomorphy:** In Dutch, this suffix can sometimes be reduced to just `-n` after a vowel (e.g., `gaan` vs. `gaen` in older spelling), but after a consonant like `w`, the full `-en` is retained.

---

### 3. Grammatical Paradigm (Conjugation)
To understand how the morphemes interact, we can look at how the root `duw-` combines with other morphemes in the verb's paradigm. This confirms that `duw-` is the stable stem.

*   **Infinitive:** `duw` + `en` = **duwen**
*   **Present (Singular):** `duw` + `t` = **duwt** (e.g., *hij duwt*)
*   **Past (Weak):** `duw` + `de` = **duwde**
*   **Past Participle:** `ge` + `duw` + `d` = **geduwd**

*Note: The verb `duwen` is a **weak verb** (regular), meaning it forms its past tense by adding a dental suffix (`-d-` or `-t-`) rather than changing the vowel (ablaut).*

### 4. Etymological Context (Diachronic Morphology)
Looking at the history of the word adds depth to the morphological analysis:

*   **Middle Dutch:** `dūwen`
*   **Old Dutch:** `dūwen` / `dūian`
*   **Proto-Germanic:** `*dūw-`
*   **Cognates:**
    *   **West Frisian:** `duwe`
    *   **Low German:** `dūen`
    *   **English:** There is no direct modern cognate in standard English. However, it is distantly related to the concept of "thrust" or "tow" within the West Germanic branch, though the semantic shift in English moved toward "tow" (pull) or "dew" (moisture), while Dutch retained the "push" meaning.

### Summary
Linguistically, **`duwen`** is a **verb** formed by taking the lexical root **`duw-`** (push) and attaching the infinitive inflectional suffix **`-en`**. It is a regular (weak) verb where the root remains stable across different tenses.
