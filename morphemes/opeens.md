From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`opeens`** is a fascinating example of **lexicalization** and **grammaticalization**. While it functions as a single unit in modern speech, it is historically composed of multiple morphemes.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes, moving from the surface structure to the deep etymological structure.

### 1. Surface Morphological Analysis (Synchronic)
In modern Dutch, `opeens` is treated as a **single lexicalized adverb**. Most speakers do not actively parse it into parts during daily use. However, linguistically, it can be segmented into two primary components based on its orthography and phonology:

*   **`op`** (Preposition/Particle)
*   **`eens`** (Adverb)

**Combined:** `op` + `eens` $\rightarrow$ `opeens`

### 2. Deep Etymological Analysis (Diachronic)
To truly understand the morphology, we must look at the historical formation of the word. The word `eens` itself is derived from the numeral `een`. Therefore, the deep structure consists of three morphemes:

1.  **`op`**
2.  **`een`**
3.  **`-s`**

#### Morpheme Breakdown:

| Morpheme | Original Form | Part of Speech | Literal Meaning | Function in `opeens` |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`op`** | *op* | Preposition | on / upon / up | Indicates a point in time or a state of change. |
| **`een`** | *een* | Numeral | one | Refers to a singular instance or moment. |
| **`-s`** | *-s* | Adverbial Suffix | (Genitive marker) | Turns the noun/numeral into an adverb of time. |

### 3. Etymological History and Semantic Shift

**The Phrase:**
Historically, the word existed as a phrase: **`op eens`**.
*   *Literal translation:* "On one [time/occasion]."
*   *Cognate:* This is directly comparable to the German phrase **`auf einmal`** (literally "on one time"), which also means "suddenly."

**The Adverbial Suffix `-s`:**
The suffix `-s` in `eens` is a fossilized **genitive of time**. In older Germanic languages (including Old Dutch and Old High German), the genitive case was often used to denote time.
*   *Examples:*
    *   `daags` (daily / of the day)
    *   `anders` (otherwise / of another kind)
    *   `eens` (once / of one time)

**Semantic Evolution:**
1.  **Stage 1 (Literal):** "On one occasion" (e.g., *Ik zag hem op eens* - I saw him on one occasion).
2.  **Stage 2 (Metaphorical):** The meaning shifted from a specific countable occasion to an **unpredictable moment**. If something happens "on one [moment]," it implies it happened without warning.
3.  **Stage 3 (Lexicalization):** The phrase fused into a single word, `opeens`. The meaning narrowed specifically to **"suddenly"** or **"all of a sudden."**

### 4. Linguistic Classification

*   **Word Class:** Adverb (Bijwoord).
*   **Morphological Type:** It is a **compound** (specifically a prepositional phrase that has become a compound) that has undergone **fusion**.
*   **Orthography:** In modern standard Dutch (based on the *Groene Boekje*), it is written as one word (`opeens`). However, in older texts or specific stylistic choices, you may still see it written as two words (`op eens`), though this is becoming archaic.

### 5. Summary of Meaning Construction

The logic of the meaning construction follows this path:

> **`op`** (at/on) + **`een`** (one) + **`-s`** (time marker)
> $\downarrow$
> "At one [time]"
> $\downarrow$
> "At a single, specific moment"
> $\downarrow$
> **"Suddenly"** (implying a shift from one state to another at that single moment).

### Conclusion
While `opeens` functions as a **monomorphemic adverb** in the mental lexicon of a modern Dutch speaker, its **morphological history** reveals it to be a **triple-morpheme compound** (`op` + `een` + `-s`). It is a classic example of how a prepositional phrase describing time (`op eens`) grammaticalizes into a fixed adverb of manner (`opeens`).
