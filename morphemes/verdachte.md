From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`verdachte`** can be analyzed through its morphology (structure), etymology (origin), and syntax (function).

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **`ver-`** + **`dacht`** + **`-e`**

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. Prefix: `ver-`
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix.
*   **Function:** In Dutch, the prefix *ver-* is highly productive and versatile. It often indicates a change of state, a completion of an action, or a turning of the action toward an object.
*   **In this context:** It modifies the root to create a specific meaning. When attached to mental verbs, *ver-* often implies a negative or erroneous judgment (e.g., *vergeten* "to forget," *verwachten* "to expect," *verraden* "to betray"). Here, it shifts the meaning from "thinking" to "thinking *about* someone in a specific way" (suspecting).

#### B. Root: `dacht`
*   **Type:** Bound Root / Lexical Stem.
*   **Etymology:** This is the most complex part of the word. Historically, this root is related to the verb **`denken`** (to think).
    *   *Diachronic (Historical) View:* The verb *verdachten* evolved from Middle Dutch *verdenken*. The root was originally *denk-* (from *denken*). However, over time, the stem shifted to the past tense form *dacht* (similar to how *denken* has the past tense *dacht*).
    *   *Synchronic (Modern) View:* In modern Dutch morphology, **`dacht`** functions as the lexical stem of the verb *verdachten*. It is no longer perceived by speakers as the past tense of *denken*, but as the core meaning carrier of "suspect."
*   **Meaning:** It carries the core semantic weight of "thought" or "judgment."

#### C. Suffix: `-e`
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix (or Nominalizer).
*   **Function:** The function of this suffix depends on how the word is used in a sentence:
    1.  **As an Adjective (Weak Inflection):** When used to describe a noun (e.g., *de verdachte man* / "the suspected man"), the *-e* is a grammatical ending required for adjectives following a definite article (*de*).
    2.  **As a Noun (Nominalization):** When used as a standalone noun (e.g., *de verdachte* / "the suspect"), the *-e* acts as a nominalizing suffix. It turns the past participle *verdacht* into a substantive noun referring to a person.
*   **Note:** The base form of the past participle is *verdacht*. The *-e* is added to create the specific form *verdachte*.

### 3. Syntactic and Semantic Context

To fully understand the morphology, one must look at the word's derivation chain:

1.  **Verb:** *Verdachten* (to suspect).
    *   Stem: *Verdacht-*
2.  **Past Participle:** *Verdacht* (suspected).
    *   Structure: *Ver-* + *dacht*
3.  **Derived Noun/Adjective:** *Verdachte* (the suspect / suspected).

**Semantic Shift:**
The word illustrates a semantic shift from a neutral cognitive process to a legal/judicial state.
*   *Denken* (to think) $\rightarrow$ *Verdachten* (to suspect) $\rightarrow$ *De Verdachte* (the suspect).

Linguistically, **`verdachte`** is a **derived nominalized participle**. It consists of a prefix (*ver-*) that alters the meaning of a fossilized root (*dacht*, from *denken*), followed by an inflectional suffix (*-e*) that allows it to function grammatically as either a noun or an inflected adjective.
