From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`misstappen`** is a complex verb formed through derivational and inflectional morphology. It is a classic example of a **separable verb** (*scheidbaar werkwoord*).

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation.

### 1. Visual Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **mis-** + **stap** + **-en**

### 2. Morpheme-by-Morpheme Analysis

#### A. `mis-` (Prefix)
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix (Separable).
*   **Origin:** Proto-Germanic *\*missaz* (cf. English *mis-*, German *miss-*).
*   **Meaning:** "Wrongly," "incorrectly," or "badly."
*   **Function:** It negates or alters the meaning of the root verb to indicate an error or a negative outcome.
*   **Linguistic Note:** In Dutch, this is a **separable prefix**. This means that in a main clause sentence, this prefix detaches from the verb stem and moves to the end of the clause (e.g., *Hij **stapt** **mis*** — "He steps wrongly").
*   **Stress:** In separable verbs, the primary lexical stress falls on the prefix. Therefore, the pronunciation is **MIS**-stap-pen (/mɪsˈstɑpə(n)/).

#### B. `stap` (Root / Lexeme)
*   **Type:** Free Morpheme (Root).
*   **Origin:** Proto-Germanic *\*stappōn* (cf. English *step*, German *Schritt*/*stap*).
*   **Meaning:** "Step" (noun) or the action of stepping (verb stem).
*   **Function:** This carries the core semantic content of the word. It is the stem of the base verb *stappen* (to step/walk).
*   **Relationship:** While *stap* exists as a noun (*de stap*), in this context, it functions as the verbal root.

#### C. `-en` (Suffix)
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix.
*   **Function:** This is the standard **infinitive marker** for Dutch verbs. It indicates that the word is a verb in its base form (to step).
*   **Grammar:** It marks the word as a weak verb (regular conjugation).

### 3. Morphological Process

The formation of *misstappen* involves two layers of morphology:

1.  **Derivation:** The prefix *mis-* is added to the root *stap*. This creates a new lexical item with a specific meaning ("to make a mistake" or "to stumble") distinct from the base verb *stappen* ("to walk/step").
2.  **Inflection:** The suffix *-en* is added to mark the grammatical category of the **infinitive**.

**Alternative Analysis (Denominal):**
Some linguists might analyze this as a **denominal verb**.
*   First, the noun *misstap* (a mistake) is formed (*mis-* + *stap*).
*   Second, the verbalizing suffix *-en* is added to the noun to create the verb *misstappen*.
*   *However*, the most common analysis treats it as a prefixed verb because *stappen* is already an active verb in the language.

### 4. Syntactic Behavior (Separability)
A crucial linguistic feature of *misstappen* is its behavior in syntax. Because *mis-* is a separable prefix, the word splits in finite verb forms.

*   **Infinitive:** *Ik wil **misstappen**.* (I want to make a mistake.)
*   **Present Tense:** *Ik **stap** **mis**.* (I make a mistake.)
*   **Past Participle:** *Ik heb **misgestapt**.* (I have made a mistake.)
    *   *Note:* In the past participle, the prefix *mis-* is reattached, and the ge- infix is inserted between the prefix and the stem (*mis-ge-stapt*).

### 5. Comparative Linguistics
*   **English:** The closest equivalent is the noun *misstep*. English rarely uses *misstep* as a verb (we usually say *to stumble* or *to err*).
*   **German:** The cognate is *Fehltritt* (noun) or *fehlstehen* (verb), though German uses *fehl-* more often than *miss-* for physical stepping errors.
*   **Dutch Specificity:** Dutch is unique in how productively it uses *mis-* as a separable prefix to create verbs indicating failure (e.g., *mislukken* - to fail, *mislukken* - to go wrong).
