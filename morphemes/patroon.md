*   **Morpheme Count:** 1
*   **Type:** Free Root (Noun)
*   **Segmentation:** `patroon` | (No internal segmentation)

**Explanation:**
To a modern Dutch speaker, the word cannot be broken down into smaller meaningful units (morphemes) within the Dutch language system.
*   There is no prefix `pat-`.
*   There is no suffix `-roon`.
*   The sequence of letters represents a single, indivisible lexical unit.

While it ends in `-oon`, this is not a productive Dutch suffix (unlike `-heid` or `-te`). It is simply part of the phonological shape of this specific loanword.

### 2. Diachronic Analysis (Etymology)
While opaque in modern Dutch, the word is historically **polymorphemic**. It is a loanword that entered Dutch via Middle French (*patron*) from Latin (*patronus*).

If we analyze the historical roots, the word breaks down as follows:

| Historical Morpheme | Language of Origin | Meaning | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`pater`** | Latin | Father | Root (Noun) |
| **`-ōnus`** | Latin | Agent/Protector | Derivational Suffix |

**The Evolution:**
1.  **Latin:** *Pater* (father) + *-ōnus* (suffix indicating a relationship or agent) $\rightarrow$ **`patronus`** (protector, defender, master).
2.  **French:** The word evolved into **`patron`** (boss, employer, pattern).
3.  **Dutch:** Adopted as **`patroon`** (with the characteristic Dutch long vowel spelling `oo` and the final `n`).

**Linguistic Note:** Because this word entered Dutch as a loanword, the internal Latin morphological structure (`pater` + `-ōnus`) has become **opaque**. Modern speakers do not perceive the connection between *patroon* and *vader* (father), even though they are cognates.

### 3. Semantic Morphology (Polysemy)
Linguistically, `patroon` is a fascinating example of **polysemy** (one word form, multiple related meanings). The meanings have shifted metaphorically over time:

1.  **Protector/Boss:** From the Latin *patronus* (a legal protector or master).
    *   *Dutch:* "De patroon van het bedrijf" (The boss of the company).
2.  **Model/Template:** A "boss" or "master" implies an example to follow. This shifted to mean a physical template or mold.
    *   *Dutch:* "Een patroon voor een jurk" (A pattern for a dress).
3.  **Ammunition:** In the 19th century, the meaning shifted from the "mold" used to cast bullets to the cartridge itself (which holds the bullet and powder).
    *   *Dutch:* "Een patroon voor een geweer" (A cartridge for a rifle).
