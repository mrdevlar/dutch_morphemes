From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`telkens`** is a fascinating example of a **fossilized adverb**. It is a morphologically complex word that has undergone historical changes, making its modern structure slightly opaque.

Here is the breakdown and explanation of the morphemes in **`telkens`**.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
While modern speakers perceive it as a single unit, linguists generally analyze it in two ways: synchronically (how it looks now) and diachronically (its historical origin).

**Synchronic Breakdown (Modern Dutch):**
> **`tel`** + **`kens`**

**Diachronic Breakdown (Historical/Etymological):**
> **`tel`** + **`en`** + **`k`** + **`s`**

---

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Root: `tel-`
*   **Source:** This is the stem of the verb **`tellen`** (to count).
*   **Type:** Bound root (in this specific context). While `tel` appears in compounds (e.g., *teltabel*), it is not a standalone free morpheme in modern Dutch.
*   **Meaning:** "Count" or "Number."
*   **Function:** It provides the semantic core of the word.

#### B. The Suffix: `-kens` (or `-en` + `s`)
This is the most complex part of the word. Historically, it is a combination of the infinitive marker and an adverbial genitive suffix.

1.  **`-en` (Infinitive Suffix):**
    *   In Middle Dutch, the infinitive of the verb was *tellen*. The `-en` is the standard infinitive ending.
    *   *Function:* It nominalizes the verb stem, turning "to count" into "the counting" (the infinitive noun).

2.  **`-s` (Adverbial Genitive Suffix):**
    *   In older stages of Dutch (and German), the genitive case was often used to form adverbs.
    *   *Examples:* *soms* (sometimes, from *som* + *s*), *anders* (otherwise, from *ander* + *s*).
    *   *Function:* It turns the noun/infinitive into an adverb indicating manner or frequency.
    *   *Literal meaning:* "Of the counting" or "By counting."

3.  **The Epenthetic `k`:**
    *   **The Puzzle:** If the word is `tellen` + `s`, why is there a `k`? (Why not *tells*?)
    *   **Explanation:** The `k` is an **epenthetic consonant** (an inserted sound). In Middle Dutch, a `k` was frequently inserted between a nasal (`n`) and a sibilant (`s`) to facilitate pronunciation or by analogy with the diminutive suffix `-ken`.
    *   **Analogy:** It is likely influenced by the diminutive suffix `-ken` (e.g., *kind* → *kindje*, but historically *kind* → *kindken*), which was often used to form adverbs (e.g., *stukkens* in dialects).
    *   **Result:** The form solidified as *telkens* rather than *tells*.

---

### 3. Semantic Evolution (Meaning Shift)
The meaning of `telkens` has shifted from a literal action to a temporal frequency.

1.  **Literal:** "By counting" or "In counts."
2.  **Metonymy:** If you count something, you do it one by one. Therefore, "by counting" implies "one by one" or "in succession."
3.  **Modern Meaning:** "Repeatedly," "each time," or "again and again."
    *   *Example:* *Hij komt telkens terug.* (He comes back **each time** / **repeatedly**.)

### 4. Linguistic Classification
*   **Word Class:** Adverb (Bijwoord).
*   **Morphological Type:** **Fossilized Derivation**.
    *   You cannot productively apply this suffix to other verbs. You cannot say *`loopkens`* (from *lopen*) or *`werkens`* (from *werken*). The suffix `-kens` is no longer productive in Modern Dutch; it exists only in this specific lexicalized word.
*   **Case:** Genitive (historically). The `-s` marks the genitive case, which was used to create adverbial phrases in Old and Middle Dutch.

### Conclusion
From a linguistic standpoint, **`telkens`** is a **genitive of the infinitive** that has been phonologically modified. It represents a "fossil" of an older grammatical system where the genitive case was used to create adverbs. The `k` is a historical artifact inserted to smooth the transition between the nasal `n` and the sibilant `s`, resulting in the unique form we use today.
