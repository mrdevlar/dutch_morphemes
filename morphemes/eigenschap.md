From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`eigenschap`** is a **derived noun** formed through the process of **nominalization**. It consists of two distinct morphemes: a lexical root and a derivational suffix.

Here is the breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented as follows:

> **`eigen`** + **`-schap`**

*   **`eigen`**: Root (Free morpheme)
*   **`-schap`**: Suffix (Bound morpheme)

---

### 2. Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Root: `eigen`
*   **Part of Speech:** Adjective (in isolation).
*   **Meaning:** "Own," "proper," "characteristic," or "peculiar."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *eigian*, which stems from Proto-Germanic **\*aikwaz**.
*   **Cognates:**
    *   **English:** *Own* (verb/noun), *Owe* (historically related to possession).
    *   **German:** *Eigen* (as in *eigenartig* - peculiar/characteristic).
*   **Function:** In this compound, *eigen* provides the semantic core. It denotes something that belongs to a specific subject or defines the nature of that subject.

#### B. The Suffix: `-schap`
*   **Part of Speech:** Derivational Suffix (Nominalizer).
*   **Meaning:** Indicates a state, condition, quality, or collective body.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *-scap*, stemming from Proto-Germanic **\*-skapiz** (meaning "shape," "form," or "condition").
*   **Cognates:**
    *   **English:** *-ship* (e.g., *friendship*, *hardship*, *ownership*).
    *   **German:** *-schaft* (e.g., *Freundschaft*, *Eigenschaft*).
*   **Function:** This suffix converts the adjective *eigen* into an abstract noun. It transforms the concept of "being own" into "the quality of being own."

---

### 3. Semantic Synthesis (How the meaning is constructed)

When combined, the morphemes create a specific semantic shift:

1.  **Literal Composition:** "Own-ship" or "Quality of being own."
2.  **Modern Meaning:** In contemporary Dutch, *eigenschap* does not usually refer to legal ownership (for which the word *eigendom* is used). Instead, it refers to an **inherent quality**, **property**, or **characteristic**.
    *   *Example:* "Deze auto heeft goede eigenschappen." (This car has good properties/characteristics.)
    *   *Example:* "Zijn belangrijkste eigenschap is geduld." (His most important trait is patience.)

**Linguistic Note on Semantic Shift:**
While *eigen* implies possession ("my own"), the suffix *-schap* abstracts this. It moves the meaning from "what I possess" to "what defines the entity." This is similar to how the English word *property* (from Latin *proprius*, meaning "one's own") evolved to mean "attributes of an object" rather than just "ownership."

### Summary
*   **Word:** `eigenschap`
*   **Structure:** Adjective + Nominalizing Suffix
*   **Literal:** "The state of being own/characteristic."
*   **Actual:** "A distinguishing quality, trait, or property."
