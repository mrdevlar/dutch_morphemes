From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch verb **`behandelen`** (to treat, to handle, to deal with) can be analyzed through **morphology** (the study of word structure) and **etymology** (word history).

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes, followed by an explanation of their function and meaning.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation

The word can be segmented into three primary morphemes:

> **`be-`** + **`handel`** + **`-en`**

However, to understand the full depth of the word, we must also look at the internal structure of the root **`handel`**, which historically consists of two smaller morphemes:

> **`be-`** + **`hand`** + **`-el`** + **`-en`**

---

### 2. Detailed Explanation of Morphemes

#### A. The Prefix: `be-`
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix.
*   **Function:** In Dutch, the prefix *be-* is highly productive. Its primary grammatical function is **transitivization**. It often takes an intransitive verb or a noun and turns it into a transitive verb (a verb that requires a direct object).
*   **Semantic Nuance:** It implies an action directed **at** or **upon** an object. It can also imply "covering" or "affecting."
    *   *Example:* `spelen` (to play) $\rightarrow$ `bespelen` (to play *on* an instrument).
    *   *Example:* `handelen` (to act/trade) $\rightarrow$ `behandelen` (to act *upon* something/someone).
*   **In `behandelen`:** It shifts the meaning from the general act of "acting" to the specific act of "treating" or "processing" a specific target (e.g., a patient, a topic, or a material).

#### B. The Root: `handel` (or `hand` + `-el`)
This is the core semantic unit. It has two layers of analysis:

**Layer 1: Synchronic (Current Usage)**
*   **Morpheme:** `handel`
*   **Source:** Derived from the verb *handelen* (to trade, to act).
*   **Meaning:** Action, trade, or conduct.
*   **Note:** In modern Dutch grammar, `handel` is often treated as the stem of the verb *handelen*.

**Layer 2: Diachronic (Historical/Etymological)**
*   **Morpheme:** `hand` (Noun)
    *   **Meaning:** The body part (hand).
    *   **Cognates:** English *hand*, German *Hand*.
*   **Morpheme:** `-el` (Suffix)
    *   **Type:** Verbalizing suffix (Frequentative/Instrumental).
    *   **Function:** It turns the noun *hand* into a verb meaning "to use the hand" or "to act."
    *   **Cognates:** Similar to the English suffix *-le* in *handle* or *sparkle*.
*   **Combined Meaning:** *Handelen* originally meant "to use one's hands" or "to manipulate," which evolved into "to trade" or "to act."

#### C. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix.
*   **Function:** This is the standard marker for the **infinitive** form of Dutch verbs.
*   **Grammar:** It indicates that the word is the base form of the verb (e.g., "to treat").
*   **Conjugation:** When conjugated, this suffix is often dropped or changed (e.g., *ik behandel* [I treat], *hij behandelt* [he treats]).

---

### 3. Semantic Evolution (How the meaning changed)

The combination of these morphemes creates a specific semantic shift:

1.  **`Hand`** (Body part)
2.  **`Hand` + `-el`** $\rightarrow$ **`Handelen`** (To use the hand $\rightarrow$ To act, to trade).
3.  **`Be-` + `Handelen`** $\rightarrow$ **`Behandelen`** (To act *upon* something).

**Modern Meanings:**
Because of the prefix *be-*, the word implies a specific relationship between the subject and the object:
*   **Medical:** *Een patiënt behandelen* (To treat a patient) $\rightarrow$ Applying medical action to the body.
*   **Academic:** *Een onderwerp behandelen* (To discuss a topic) $\rightarrow$ Applying intellectual action to a subject.
*   **Industrial:** *Hout behandelen* (To treat wood) $\rightarrow$ Applying chemical/physical action to material.

### 4. Comparison with English

| Dutch | English | Linguistic Note |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`hand`** | **`hand`** | Cognates (Germanic root). |
| **`handelen`** | **`handle`** | Cognates. *Handle* in English is closer to the root *handelen*. |
| **`behandelen`** | **`treat`** | Not a cognate. *Behandelen* is semantically equivalent to *treat*, but structurally equivalent to *be-handle* (which is archaic/rare in English). |
| **`be-`** | **`be-`** | Cognate prefix (e.g., *befriend*, *bedevil*), though less productive in modern English than in Dutch. |

### Summary
*   **`be-`**: Makes the action transitive (directed at an object).
*   **`handel`**: The core concept of action or trade (historically "hand" + "suffix").
*   **`-en`**: Marks the word as an infinitive verb.

**Literal Translation:** "To be-handled" or "To act upon."
**Actual Translation:** "To treat" or "To handle."
