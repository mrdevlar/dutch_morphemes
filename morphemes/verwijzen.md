From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch verb **`verwijzen`** (to refer, to point to, to send) can be analyzed through its morphological structure, etymology, and phonological behavior.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word consists of three distinct morphemes:

> **ver** + **wijz** + **en**

| Morpheme | Type | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **ver-** | Derivational Prefix | Changes the meaning of the root; creates a new verb. |
| **wijz-** | Root (Stem) | The core lexical meaning (to point/show). |
| **-en** | Inflectional Suffix | Marks the infinitive form of the verb. |

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Prefix: `ver-`
*   **Type:** Inseparable Derivational Prefix.
*   **Function:** In Dutch, *ver-* is one of the most common prefixes used to derive new verbs from existing roots. It often implies a change of state, a completion of an action, or a movement "away" or "thoroughly."
*   **Phonology (Stress):** Crucially, *ver-* is **inseparable**. This means the primary stress falls on the root syllable, not the prefix.
    *   *Pronunciation:* /vər**w**ɛi̯.zə(n)/ (Stress on *wijz*).
    *   *Consequence:* In the past participle, the standard *ge-* prefix is omitted (e.g., *verwezen*, not *geverwezen*).
*   **Semantic Contribution:** When added to *wijzen* (to point), *ver-* adds a sense of directionality toward a third party or a destination. It transforms the physical act of pointing into the communicative act of "referring" or "directing."

#### B. The Root: `wijz-`
*   **Type:** Lexical Root (Verb Stem).
*   **Base Form:** *wijzen* (to point, to indicate).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Proto-Germanic **\*wīsjaną*.
    *   **Cognates:** English *wise* (originally meant "to show the way"), German *weisen* (to point/show), and Old English *wīsan*.
*   **Phonology:** The root contains the diphthong *ij* (/ɛi̯/).
*   **Morphophonology (Ablaut):** This is a **strong verb** (Class 1). The root vowel changes during conjugation (ablaut).
    *   *Infinitive:* ver**wijz**en (/ɛi̯/)
    *   *Past Tense:* ver**wees** (/e:/)
    *   *Past Participle:* ver**wezen** (/e:/)
    *   *Note:* The *z* remains voiced throughout.

#### C. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix.
*   **Function:** Marks the **infinitive** mood.
*   **Variation:** In spoken Dutch or certain dialects, this may be reduced to just *-n*, but in standard morphology, it is analyzed as *-en*. It distinguishes the verb from the noun form *wijze* (way/method) or the adjective *wijs* (wise).

---

### 3. Semantic Composition (How meaning is built)

The meaning of *verwijzen* is compositional but idiomatic:

1.  **`wijzen`**: To point a finger or direct attention physically.
2.  **`ver-`**: Adds the nuance of "away" or "to another."
3.  **`verwijzen`**: To point someone *away* to another source, person, or location.

**Contextual Meanings:**
*   **Citation:** "Ik verwijs naar de bron." (I refer to the source.) -> *Pointing attention to text.*
*   **Medical/Administrative:** "De huisarts verwijst naar de specialist." (The GP refers [sends] to the specialist.) -> *Directing a person to a different location.*
*   **Accusation:** "Hij verwees mij naar mijn fouten." (He pointed out my mistakes.) -> *Directing attention to a fact.*
