From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch verb **`overtuigen`** (to convince/persuade) can be analyzed by breaking it down into its constituent morphemes.

Here is the morphological breakdown, followed by a detailed explanation of each component.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation

The word consists of three morphemes:

> **`over`** + **`tuig`** + **`-en`**

*   **`over`**: Prefix (Derivational)
*   **`tuig`**: Root (Lexical Stem)
*   **`-en`**: Suffix (Inflectional)

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Prefix: `over-`
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix.
*   **Literal Meaning:** "Over," "across," "above," or "too much."
*   **Function in this word:** In this specific lexicalized compound, `over-` does not indicate spatial movement (like *oversteken* - to cross). Instead, it functions as an **intensifier** or indicates **completeness/transference**. It implies moving someone's belief "across" to a new position or bringing a matter to a "complete" state of proof.
*   **Grammatical Note:** In Dutch, the prefix `over-` can be either separable or inseparable depending on the verb. In *overtuigen*, it is **inseparable**.
    *   *Evidence:* The past participle is *overtuigd* (not *ge-overtuigd*), and the stress falls on the root (*tuig*), not the prefix.

#### B. The Root: `tuig`
*   **Type:** Bound Root (in this context).
*   **Etymology:** This is the most etymologically interesting part of the word. It is related to the Old Dutch word *theghen* and the modern noun **`getuige`** (witness).
*   **Original Meaning:** "Proof," "evidence," or "testimony."
*   **Modern Confusion:** In modern colloquial Dutch, the word *tuig* exists as a standalone noun meaning "gear," "stuff," or derogatorily "scum" (e.g., *snuiter* or *tuig*). However, in *overtuigen*, this modern meaning is irrelevant. The root retains its older, legalistic sense of "proof."
*   **Semantic Logic:** To *overtuigen* literally means to provide someone with enough **proof** (`tuig`) to cross (`over`) their doubt.

#### C. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix.
*   **Function:** Marks the **infinitive** form of the verb.
*   **Phonology:** In standard pronunciation, this is often reduced to a schwa sound /ə/ or is barely audible in rapid speech (e.g., /ˈtœy.ɣə/).
*   **Grammar:** It distinguishes the verb from the noun form (though the noun form *overtuiging* uses a different suffix, *-ing*).

---

### 3. Semantic Composition (How the parts create meaning)

When you combine these morphemes, the literal semantic composition is:

> **"To bring over (to a state of) proof."**

1.  **`tuig`** establishes the domain: Evidence, testimony, or certainty.
2.  **`over`** establishes the action: Transferring that certainty to another person.
3.  **`-en`** establishes the category: It is an action (verb).

Therefore, **`overtuigen`** means to cause someone to accept a fact or opinion by providing them with sufficient evidence or argumentation.

### 4. Related Words (Morphological Family)

To understand the root `tuig` better, it helps to look at cognates within the Dutch language:

*   **`Getuige`** (Noun): Witness. (Literally: "One who gives proof/testimony").
*   **`Getuigen`** (Verb): To testify.
*   **`Overtuiging`** (Noun): Conviction / Belief. (The state of being convinced).
*   **`Tegen`** (Preposition): Against. (Etymologically related to the same Germanic root regarding standing opposite or opposing, which evolved into "witnessing" or "proving" against something).

### 5. Grammatical Behavior

From a syntactic perspective, `overtuigen` behaves as a **weak, regular verb** with an **inseparable prefix**:

*   **Infinitive:** *overtuigen*
*   **Present:** *Ik overtuig* (I convince)
*   **Past Simple:** *Ik overtuigde* (I convinced)
*   **Past Participle:** *overtuigd* (Convinced) — *Note the absence of the 'ge-' prefix.*
*   **Separability:** It is **not** separable. You cannot say *Ik heb hem over overtuigd*.
