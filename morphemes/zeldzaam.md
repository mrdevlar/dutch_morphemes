From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`zeldzaam`** (meaning "rare" or "scarce") is a **derivational compound**. It consists of two distinct morphemes: a lexical root and a derivational suffix.

Here is the morphological breakdown and analysis:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented as follows:

> **`zeld`** + **`-zaam``**

*   **`zeld`**: The lexical root (bound morpheme).
*   **`-zaam`**: The derivational suffix (bound morpheme).

---

### 2. Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Root: `zeld-`
*   **Source:** This morpheme is the stem of the Dutch adverb **`zelden`** (meaning "seldom" or "rarely").
*   **Morphophonology:** In Dutch morphology, when deriving an adjective from an adverb ending in *-en*, the final *-en* is typically dropped to create the stem.
    *   *Zelden* (Adverb) $\rightarrow$ *Zeld-* (Stem).
*   **Etymology:**
    *   **Old Dutch:** *seldan* / *seldo*.
    *   **Proto-Germanic:** *\*saldaz* or *\*seldaz*.
    *   **Cognates:** It is directly related to the English word **`seldom`** and the German word **`selten`**.
*   **Semantic Function:** It provides the core semantic content regarding **frequency**. It implies something that does not happen often.

#### B. The Suffix: `-zaam`
*   **Type:** Derivational suffix (specifically, an adjectival suffix).
*   **Function:** It transforms a verb or adverb stem into an adjective. It generally conveys the meaning of "characterized by," "full of," or "inclined to."
*   **Etymology:**
    *   **Old Dutch:** *-sam*.
    *   **Proto-Germanic:** *\*-samaz*.
    *   **Cognates:** It corresponds to the English suffix **`-some`** (as in *handsome*, *wondrous*, *fearsome*) and the German suffix **`-sam`** (as in *langsam* - slow, *wirksam* - effective).
*   **Semantic Function:** It adds a qualitative layer to the root. It turns the concept of "seldom" into a quality of "being characterized by being seldom."

---

### 3. Semantic Composition (The Whole)

When combined, the morphemes create the following semantic logic:

1.  **`zeld`** (seldom/rarely)
2.  **`-zaam`** (characterized by / having the quality of)
3.  **`zeldzaam`** = "Characterized by being seldom."

In modern usage, this translates simply to **"rare."**

### 5. Summary
*   **Word Class:** Adjective.
*   **Structure:** Root + Suffix.
*   **Process:** Derivation (creating a new word from an existing stem).
*   **Literal Meaning:** "Seldom-like" or "Having the quality of seldomness."
*   **Actual Meaning:** Rare.
