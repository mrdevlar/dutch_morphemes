From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`opmerking`** (meaning "remark," "comment," or "observation") is a **deverbal noun**. It is formed through derivational morphology from a separable verb.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation of its components.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **`op`** + **`merk`** + **`ing`**

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Prefix: `op-`
*   **Category:** Separable Prefix / Preposition.
*   **Literal Meaning:** "Up," "on," or "upon."
*   **Function:** In the context of the verb *opmerken*, this prefix adds a directional or aspectual nuance. It implies directing attention **onto** something or bringing something **up** into awareness.
*   **Etymology:** Cognate with the English preposition **up** and the German *auf*.
*   **Linguistic Note:** In Dutch grammar, *op* is a separable prefix. In a main clause, it splits from the verb (e.g., *Ik **merk** het **op*** — "I notice it"). However, in the nominalized form *opmerking*, it remains attached.

#### B. The Root: `merk-`
*   **Category:** Verbal Stem (Root).
*   **Source Verb:** *merken* (to notice, to perceive, to mark).
*   **Meaning:** The core semantic content relates to perception or marking. Historically, it implies making a sign or noticing a sign.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Proto-Germanic *\*markōną*. It is a cognate with the English verb **mark** and the German *merken*.
*   **Semantic Shift:** While English *mark* often refers to making a physical sign, Dutch *merken* evolved to focus more on the cognitive act of **noticing** or **perceiving**.

#### C. The Suffix: `-ing`
*   **Category:** Derivational Suffix (Nominalizer).
*   **Function:** This suffix converts the verb (*opmerken*) into a noun (*opmerking*). It indicates the **action**, **process**, or **result** of the verb.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Proto-Germanic *\*-ingō*. It is a direct cognate with the English suffix **-ing** (as in *building* or *feeling*) and the German suffix **-ung** (as in *die Bildung*).
*   **Grammar:** This suffix typically creates feminine nouns in Dutch (though grammatical gender is less distinct in modern Dutch, it takes the definite article *de*).

### 3. Derivation Process
The word formation follows this logical path:

1.  **Base Verb:** *merken* (to notice/mark).
2.  **Compound Verb:** *opmerken* (to notice something specific / to remark).
    *   *Literal sense:* To "mark up" or "put a mark on" something mentally.
3.  **Nominalization:** *opmerken* + *-ing* $\rightarrow$ **`opmerking`**.
    *   *Result:* The act of noticing, or the specific thing noticed (the comment).

### 5. Semantic Nuance
Linguistically, there is a slight shift between the literal and idiomatic meaning:
*   **Literal:** "A marking up" or "an observation."
*   **Idiomatic:** In modern usage, an *opmerking* is rarely a physical mark. It is almost exclusively a **verbal or written comment**, often one that points out a detail, an error, or an opinion.

### Summary
**`opmerking`** is a **deverbal noun** constructed from the separable verb **`opmerken`**.
*   **`op-`**: Directs attention upward/onto.
*   **`merk-`**: The root meaning "to notice" or "mark."
*   **`-ing`**: Turns the action into a concrete noun.

It is a classic example of Germanic compounding and derivation, sharing deep roots with English words like *mark*, *up*, and the suffix *-ing*.
