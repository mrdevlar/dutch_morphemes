From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`werknemer`** (meaning "employee") is a complex word formed through compounding and derivation.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation.

### 1. Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **`werk`** + **`nem`** + **`-er`**

Alternatively, it can be viewed as a compound of two words, where the second word is itself derived:
> **`werk`** + **`nemer`**

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### A. `werk`
*   **Type:** Free Morpheme (Root)
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun (also functions as a verb stem)
*   **Meaning:** "Work," "labor," or "task."
*   **Function:** In this compound, it acts as the **modifier** (the first part of the compound). It specifies *what* kind of "taker" is being discussed.
*   **Etymology:** From Middle Dutch *werck*, from Old Dutch *werk*, from Proto-Germanic **\*werką*.

#### B. `nem`
*   **Type:** Bound Morpheme (Verbal Root/Stem)
*   **Part of Speech:** Verb Root
*   **Meaning:** "To take," "to receive," or "to accept."
*   **Function:** This is the lexical core of the action. It cannot stand alone as a word in modern Dutch (you cannot say "ik nem"). It appears in the infinitive verb **`nemen`** (to take).
*   **Note:** In the verb *nemen*, the stem vowel lengthens (*neem-*), but in this nominalized form, the short stem vowel *e* is retained (*nem-*).

#### C. `-er`
*   **Type:** Bound Morpheme (Derivational Suffix)
*   **Part of Speech:** Agentive Suffix
*   **Meaning:** "One who [verbs]" or "The doer of the action."
*   **Function:** This suffix turns the verbal root `nem` into an agent noun (`nemer` = "taker").
*   **Productivity:** This is a highly productive suffix in Dutch (e.g., *lezer* from *lezen* [reader], *schrijver* from *schrijven* [writer]).

### 3. Morphological Structure (Hierarchy)
Linguistically, the structure is hierarchical. It is not just a flat list of three parts; it is a compound containing a derived word.

1.  **Level 1 (Derivation):** `nem` + `-er` $\rightarrow$ **`nemer`**
    *   *Meaning:* "Taker" (One who takes).
2.  **Level 2 (Compounding):** `werk` + `nemer` $\rightarrow$ **`werknemer`**
    *   *Meaning:* "Work-taker."

### 4. Semantic Composition
The meaning of the compound is **endocentric**, meaning the head of the compound is the second element (`nemer`).
*   **Head:** `nemer` (The person).
*   **Modifier:** `werk` (Describes the type of person).

**Literal Translation:** "Work-taker."
**Actual Meaning:** "Employee."
**Logic:** An employee is someone who "takes" work (accepts employment/tasks) from an employer.

### 5. Linguistic Context & Contrast
To fully understand the morphology, it helps to compare it with its antonym, **`werkgever`** (employer).

*   **`werkgever`** = `werk` (work) + `ge` (give) + `-er` (one who).
*   **`werknemer`** = `werk` (work) + `nem` (take) + `-er` (one who).

Both follow the exact same morphological template:
> **[Object] + [Verb Root] + [Agent Suffix]**
