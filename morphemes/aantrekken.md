From a morphological perspective, the Dutch word **`aantrekken`** is a complex verb consisting of three distinct morphemes. It is classified as a **separable verb** (*scheidbaar werkwoord*).

Here is the breakdown and linguistic explanation of each component:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented as follows:
> **`aan`** + **`trek`** + **`-en`**

---

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### A. `aan` (Separable Prefix / Particle)
*   **Type:** Prefix (specifically a separable verbal particle).
*   **Category:** Preposition/Adverb derived.
*   **Function:**
    *   **Syntactic:** In Dutch main clauses, this prefix separates from the verb stem and moves to the end of the sentence (e.g., *Ik **trek** mijn jas **aan***). In subordinate clauses or infinitives, it remains attached (e.g., *...om mijn jas **aantrekken***).
    *   **Semantic:** It modifies the direction or aspect of the action. The preposition *aan* generally implies "on," "at," or "towards." When combined with a verb of motion or force, it often implies attachment, initiation, or directionality toward the speaker or an object.
*   **Etymology:** Cognate with the English preposition "on" (via Proto-Germanic *\*ana*).

#### B. `trek` (Root / Stem)
*   **Type:** Lexical Root (Stam).
*   **Category:** Verb stem.
*   **Meaning:** "To pull," "to draw," or "to tug."
*   **Phonology:** The vowel is short (/ɛ/).
*   **Orthographic Note:** In the infinitive form, Dutch orthography requires the doubling of the consonant `k` before the suffix `-en` to preserve the short vowel sound. Therefore, while the underlying morpheme is `trek`, the surface form appears as `trekk`.
    *   *Underlying:* `trek` + `en`
    *   *Surface:* `trekken`
*   **Etymology:** From Middle Dutch *trecken*, related to German *ziehen* (though *trekken* is the direct cognate).

#### C. `-en` (Inflectional Suffix)
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix.
*   **Function:**
    *   **Infinitive Marker:** It marks the verb as being in the infinitive form (*onbepaalde wijs*).
    *   **Plural Marker:** In the present tense, this suffix is also used for the first, second, and third person plural (e.g., *wij trekken*).
    *   **Past Participle Base:** It serves as the base for the past participle (which adds the prefix *ge-* and changes the vowel: *getrokken*).
*   **Category:** Grammatical morpheme (does not add lexical meaning, only grammatical function).

---

### 3. Semantic Composition (Polysemy)
Linguistically, `aantrekken` is an interesting case of **semantic composition**, where the combination of the prefix and root creates multiple distinct meanings depending on context:

1.  **Physical Attraction:** To pull something towards something else.
    *   *Example:* "De magneet trekt het ijzer **aan**." (The magnet attracts the iron.)
2.  **Dressing:** To pull clothing onto the body.
    *   *Example:* "Ik **trek** mijn schoenen **aan**." (I put on my shoes.)
3.  **Appeal:** To draw interest or attention (metaphorical extension).
    *   *Example:* "Dat boek trekt veel lezers **aan**." (That book attracts many readers.)

### 4. Related Derivations
To understand the morphological productivity of these morphemes, consider these related forms:

*   **`aantrekkelijk`** (Adjective): `aan` + `trek` + `-elijk` (suffix meaning "-able" or "-like"). Meaning: Attractive.
*   **`getrokken`** (Past Participle): `ge-` (past participle prefix) + `trek` + `-en` (with vowel change). Meaning: Pulled/Drawn.
*   **`trek`** (Noun): The root used as a standalone noun meaning "a pull," "a trait," or "a draft" (of air).
