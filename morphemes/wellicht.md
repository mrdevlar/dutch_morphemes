From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`wellicht`** is a compound adverb. To understand it fully, we must look at its morphological structure, the etymological history of its components, and the semantic shift that occurred over time.

Here is the breakdown:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word is a **compound** consisting of two free morphemes:

> **`wel`** + **`licht`** → **`wellicht`**

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### A. Morpheme 1: `wel`
*   **Category:** Adverb / Adjective.
*   **Current Meaning:** Well, good, properly.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *wel*, Old Dutch *wella*.
*   **Cognates:** English *well*, German *wohl*, Old English *wel*.
*   **Function in Compound:** In this context, `wel` acts as an **intensifier**. It modifies the second element to mean "very" or "quite."

#### B. Morpheme 2: `licht`
*   **Category:** Noun / Adjective / Adverb.
*   **Current Modern Meaning:**
    1.  (Noun) Light (illumination).
    2.  (Adjective) Light (in weight).
    3.  (Adjective) Light (in color).
*   **Etymological Meaning in this Compound:** **Easily / Readily.**
    *   This is the most critical linguistic nuance. In Middle Dutch, the adjective *licht* (light in weight) was frequently used adverbially to mean "easily" (because something light is easy to move).
    *   *Example:* *Dat gaat licht* (That goes easily).
*   **Cognates:** English *light* (weight), German *leicht* (easy/light).
*   **Function in Compound:** It provides the core semantic meaning of "possibility" or "probability."

### 3. Semantic Evolution (The "Why")
The meaning of `wellicht` ("perhaps") is not immediately obvious from the modern meanings of "well" and "light" (illumination). The semantic shift occurred through the following logic:

1.  **Literal Construction:** `wel` (well/very) + `licht` (easily).
2.  **Original Meaning:** "Very easily" or "With good reason."
    *   *Context:* If an event happens "very easily," it is probable.
3.  **Semantic Bleaching:** Over time, the certainty of "very easily" weakened.
    *   "It happens very easily" $\rightarrow$ "It is likely" $\rightarrow$ "It is possible" $\rightarrow$ "Perhaps."
4.  **Modern Reanalysis:** Modern Dutch speakers often associate `licht` with *light* (illumination) rather than *light* (weight). While this is etymologically secondary, it has influenced the spelling and the feeling of the word (as if "shedding light" on a possibility).

### 4. Grammatical Classification
*   **Word Class:** Adverb (Bijwoord).
*   **Syntactic Function:** It modifies a verb, adjective, or entire clause to indicate probability.
    *   *Example:* "Hij komt **wellicht** morgen." (He will **perhaps** come tomorrow.)
*   **Negation:** It is often used in negative contexts to soften a statement, similar to "probably not" or "unlikely," though it retains its core meaning of uncertainty.

### 5. Comparative Linguistics (Cognates)
The etymology of `wellicht` is best understood by comparing it to its Germanic siblings, which confirms the "easily" derivation:

*   **German:** *vielleicht*
    *   *viel* (much/very) + *leicht* (easy/light).
    *   Literal: "Very easily."
    *   Meaning: "Perhaps."
*   **English:** *likely*
    *   *like* (similar) + *-ly*.
    *   While not a direct cognate, it follows a similar semantic path of moving from "likeness" to "probability."

### Conclusion
Linguistically, **`wellicht`** is a fossilized compound where the second morpheme (`licht`) retains an archaic adverbial meaning ("easily") that has largely disappeared from modern usage outside of this specific word. It is a classic example of **semantic shift**, where a phrase describing physical ease ("well easily") evolved into a marker of epistemic modality ("perhaps").
