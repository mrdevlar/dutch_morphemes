From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`groentebak`** is a **compound noun** (samenstelling). It is formed by combining two distinct lexical units.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 1. High-Level Segmentation
At the surface level, the word consists of two free morphemes combined into a single lexical item:

> **`groente`** + **`bak`**

*   **Word Type:** Endocentric Compound (the meaning of the whole is a specific type of the head).
*   **Grammatical Category:** Noun.
*   **Linking Element:** None (Zero interfix). In Dutch, compounds sometimes require a linking *-s-* or *-e-* (e.g., *bloemenbak*), but here the roots are joined directly.

---

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. Morpheme 1: `groente`
*   **Type:** Free Morpheme (can stand alone as a word).
*   **Meaning:** "Vegetable" or "vegetables."
*   **Internal Structure (Derivational):**
    While `groente` functions as a single root in modern compounding, it is historically and morphologically derived from two smaller units:
    1.  **`groen`** (Adjective): Meaning "green."
    2.  **`-te`** (Derivational Suffix): A nominalizing suffix (similar to English *-ness* or *-th*).
    *   *Etymology:* Originally, *groente* meant "greenery" or "green stuff." Over time, the meaning underwent **semantic narrowing** (lexicalization) to refer specifically to edible plant matter (vegetables).
    *   *Synchronic View:* For modern speakers, `groente` is usually treated as an unanalyzable root when forming compounds, though the etymological link to the color green remains visible.

#### B. Morpheme 2: `bak`
*   **Type:** Free Morpheme (can stand alone as a word).
*   **Meaning:** "Box," "bin," "tub," or "container."
*   **Internal Structure:** Monomorphemic (cannot be broken down further into meaningful units in modern Dutch).

---

### 3. Compound Structure & Semantics

In the compound **`groentebak`**, the relationship between the morphemes is defined by the **Head-Modifier** structure:

*   **The Head (`bak`):** The rightmost element is the head of the compound. It determines the grammatical category (it is a *bak*) and the core semantic category.
*   **The Modifier (`groente`):** The leftmost element modifies the head. It specifies the *purpose* or *content* of the container.
*   **Semantic Relation:** "A `bak` (container) intended for `groente` (vegetables)."

### Linguistic Note on Orthography
In Dutch orthography, compound nouns are written as one word (closed compounds). There is no space or hyphen between `groente` and `bak`. This distinguishes it from a phrase like *groene bak* (a green box), where the adjective agrees in gender/number and is written separately. In `groentebak`, `groente` acts as a noun adjunct, not an adjective.
