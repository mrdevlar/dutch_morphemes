From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`uitkijken`** is a classic example of a **separable compound verb** (*scheidbaar werkwoord*).

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation of its components.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **`uit`** + **`kijk`** + **`-en`**

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. `uit` (Prefix / Particle)
*   **Category:** Separable Prefix (derived from a preposition/adverb).
*   **Original Meaning:** "Out" or "outside."
*   **Function:** In this compound, it functions as a **directional particle**. It modifies the action of the verb to indicate directionality (looking *outward* rather than inward or at something specific).
*   **Linguistic Note:** In the dictionary form (infinitive), it is attached to the verb. However, syntactically, it is a **separable** element. In a main clause, it detaches and moves to the end of the sentence (e.g., *Ik **kijk** naar buiten **uit***).

#### B. `kijk` (Verbal Stem / Root)
*   **Category:** Verb Root.
*   **Meaning:** "Look" or "watch."
*   **Function:** This is the core lexical meaning of the word. It carries the primary semantic load of the action.
*   **Phonology:** In Dutch separable verbs, the primary stress usually falls on the prefix (`**UIT**-kijken`), whereas in inseparable verbs, the stress falls on the stem (e.g., `be-**KIJ**-ken`). This stress pattern is a morphological cue for the listener.

#### C. `-en` (Inflectional Suffix)
*   **Category:** Infinitive Marker.
*   **Function:** This is a bound morpheme that marks the verb as being in the **infinitive** form.
*   **Grammar:** It is the standard ending for Dutch verbs in the dictionary form (e.g., *lopen, zien, zijn*). It carries no tense or person information on its own but signals the base form of the verb.

### 3. Syntactic Behavior (Separability)
From a syntactic perspective, `uitkijken` behaves differently than a standard single-word verb. This is known as **verb splitting**.

*   **Infinitive/Perfect Tense:** The parts stay together.
    *   *Ik wil **uitkijken**.* (I want to look out.)
    *   *Ik heb **uitgekeken**.* (I have looked out.) -> *Note: The past participle `ge-` is inserted between the prefix and the stem.*
*   **Main Clause (V2 Position):** The prefix `uit` separates and moves to the end of the clause.
    *   *Ik **kijk** uit.* (I look out.)
    *   *Je moet **kijken** uit.* (You must look out.)

### 4. Semantic Compositionality
The meaning of `uitkijken` is largely **compositional** (the meaning is derived from the sum of its parts), but it also has idiomatic extensions:

1.  **Literal:** To look out (e.g., from a window or a vehicle).
    *   *Hij kijkt uit het raam.* (He looks out the window.)
2.  **Idiomatic:** To be careful / to watch out.
    *   *Kijk uit voor de auto!* (Watch out for the car!)
    *   In this idiomatic sense, the directional meaning of `uit` is metaphorical (looking out for danger).
