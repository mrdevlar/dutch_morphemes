From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`moeten`** is a modal verb meaning "to have to," "must," or "to need to."

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation of its components.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into two distinct morphemes:

> **`moet`** + **`-en`**

---

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Root/Stem: `moet-`
*   **Type:** Lexical Root / Verbal Stem
*   **Function:** Carries the core semantic meaning (obligation, necessity, or probability).
*   **Linguistic Classification:** This is a **Preterite-Present Verb** (*Preteritum-praesens*).
    *   **Explanation:** In Proto-Germanic, this verb originally had a past tense form that was used to express present meaning. This is why it behaves irregularly in modern Dutch.
    *   **Evidence of Irregularity:**
        *   **3rd Person Singular:** Unlike regular verbs which add a *-t* (e.g., *werken* → *hij werk**t***), this verb does not add a *-t* in the present tense (*hij moet*). This is a fossilized remnant of its past-tense origin.
        *   **Past Tense:** The past tense is formed by changing the vowel and adding *-t* (or *-te*), resulting in *moest* (singular) and *moesten* (plural), rather than the regular *-de* or *-te* suffix added to the stem (e.g., *werk**te***).
*   **Etymology:**
    *   **Proto-Germanic:** *\*mōtaną* (to be allowed to, to have opportunity).
    *   **Old Dutch:** *mōtan*.
    *   **Cognates:**
        *   **German:** *müssen* (to must/have to).
        *   **English:** *must* (Note: English *must* is actually the fossilized past tense form of the Old English *mōtan*, whereas Dutch retained the infinitive form).
        *   **English:** *may* (shares the same Proto-Germanic root *\*maganą*, related to ability/permission).

#### B. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Type:** Grammatical Suffix (Inflectional)
*   **Function:** Marks the **Infinitive** form of the verb.
*   **Productivity:** This is the standard, productive suffix for the infinitive of the vast majority of Dutch verbs (e.g., *lopen*, *slapen*, *werken*).
*   **Phonology:** In rapid speech, the final *-n* is often elided (dropped), leaving the pronunciation closer to /ˈmuːtə/.

---

### 3. Syntactic and Semantic Context
While morphology deals with the structure of the word, the linguistic function of `moeten` relies on its status as a **Modal Auxiliary**.

*   **Valency:** It is a defective verb in terms of syntax; it cannot stand alone as the main predicate of a sentence without a second verb in the infinitive form.
    *   *Example:* "Ik **moet** werken." (I must work.)
    *   *Incorrect:* "Ik moet." (I must [what?]) — *Unless used elliptically in conversation.*
*   **Semantic Shift:** Historically, the root implied "to be allowed to" or "to have the opportunity" (similar to English *may*). Over time, the meaning shifted from **permission** to **obligation** (necessity).

### Conclusion
Linguistically, `moeten` is a **monomorphemic root** (`moet-`) combined with a **standard infinitive suffix** (`-en`). Its primary linguistic interest lies in its classification as a **Preterite-Present verb**, which explains its irregular conjugation patterns (lack of *-t* in 3rd person singular, vowel change in past tense) compared to regular Dutch verbs.
