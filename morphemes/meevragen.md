From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`meevragen`** is a **separable compound verb** (*scheidbaar werkwoord*). It consists of two distinct morphemes that combine to create a specific semantic meaning and syntactic behavior.

Here is the breakdown and explanation of the morphemes:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented as follows:
> **mee** + **vragen**

Both components are **free morphemes** (they can stand alone as independent words), but in this context, they function together as a single lexical unit.

---

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Particle: `mee`
*   **Form:** Adverb / Preverb / Particle.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *me*, related to the preposition *met* (with). It shares a Germanic root with the English concept of "with" or "along."
*   **Function:** In this compound, `mee` acts as a **separable prefix** (or preverb). It modifies the directionality or the scope of the action.
*   **Literal Meaning:** "Along," "with," or "together."
*   **Grammatical Status:** It is a **free morpheme**. It can function independently as an adverb (e.g., *Kom je mee?* / "Are you coming along?").

#### B. The Verb Root: `vragen`
*   **Form:** Verb (Infinitive).
*   **Etymology:** From Middle Dutch *vraghen*, Old Dutch *fregan*. It is cognate with the German *fragen* and the English *fray* (in the sense of questioning/striving, though English *ask* is from a different root).
*   **Function:** This is the **lexical head** of the compound. It carries the core semantic weight of the action.
*   **Meaning:** "To ask," "to inquire," or "to request."
*   **Grammatical Status:** It is a **free morpheme** (can stand alone as a verb).

---

### 3. Morphosyntactic Properties (Syntax & Morphology)

The most distinct linguistic feature of `meevragen` is its **separability**. In Dutch grammar, this classifies it as a *scheidbaar werkwoord*.

*   **Infinitive Form:** The morphemes are joined (`meevragen`).
    *   *Example:* "Ik wil het **meevragen**." (I want to ask for it along.)
*   **Main Clause (V2 Position):** The finite verb moves to the second position, and the particle `mee` is "kicked" to the end of the clause.
    *   *Example:* "Ik **vraag** het **mee**." (I ask for it along.)
    *   *Structure:* Subject + Finite Verb (`vraag`) + Object + Particle (`mee`).
*   **Past Participle:** When forming the perfect tense, the prefix `ge-` is inserted **between** the particle and the verb stem.
    *   *Formation:* `mee` + `ge` + `vraagd` = **`meegevraagd`**.
    *   *Example:* "Ik heb het **meegevraagd**." (I have asked for it along.)

---

### 4. Semantic Composition (Meaning)

The meaning of the compound is not merely the sum of its parts (compositionality), but rather a specific pragmatic usage.

*   **Literal Composition:** "To ask with."
*   **Actual Meaning:** To request something **in addition** to something else, or to include a request within a broader transaction.
*   **Contextual Usage:** It is frequently used in commercial or social contexts where a primary request is made, and a secondary item is added to that request.
    *   *Scenario:* Ordering a meal.
    *   *Sentence:* "Ik neem de friet, en kan ik de saus **meevragen**?"
    *   *Translation:* "I'll take the fries, and can I **ask for** the sauce **along with it**?"

### Conclusion
Linguistically, `meevragen` is a **phrasal verb** equivalent in English (like "ask for" or "add on"), but structurally it is a **separable compound**. Its morphology dictates that the particle `mee` detaches from the root `vragen` in finite main clauses, and the past participle marker `ge-` must be sandwiched between them. Semantically, it shifts the focus of the verb `vragen` from a general inquiry to a specific request for inclusion.
