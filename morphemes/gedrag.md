From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`gedrag`** (meaning "behavior" or "conduct") is a deverbal noun derived from a verb. It is a classic example of Germanic nominalization using the prefix *ge-*.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Morpheme Breakdown

The word can be segmented into two primary morphemes:

1.  **`ge-`** (Prefix)
2.  **`drag`** (Root/Stem)

*(Note: Historically, there was a nominal ending *-e* in Middle Dutch, as in `gedraghe`, but in Modern Dutch, this has been lost, leaving the two morphemes above.)*

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis

#### A. The Prefix: `ge-`
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix.
*   **Function:** Nominalization.
*   **Explanation:** In Dutch, the prefix *ge-* is frequently used to turn verbs into nouns. It often indicates a **result**, a **collective action**, or a **completed state**.
    *   *Example:* *zien* (to see) $\rightarrow$ *gezicht* (sight/face).
    *   *Example:* *bidden* (to pray) $\rightarrow$ *gebed* (prayer).
    *   In the case of *gedrag*, *ge-* transforms the action of "carrying" into the abstract concept of "the carrying" (i.e., how one carries oneself).

#### B. The Root: `drag`
*   **Type:** Bound Root (derived from the verb *dragen*).
*   **Base Verb:** *dragen* (to carry, to wear).
*   **Etymology:** From Proto-Germanic **`*draganą`** (to draw, pull, carry). This is cognate with the English word **`drag`**.
*   **Morphophonology (Sound Changes):**
    1.  **Vowel Shortening:** The infinitive verb is *dr**aa**gen* (with a long vowel /aː/). However, in the nominalized form *gedr**a**g*, the vowel is shortened to /ɑ/. This is a common morphophonemic alternation in Dutch when forming nouns from strong verb participles (similar to *zien* /ziːn/ $\rightarrow$ *gezicht* /zɪxt/).
    2.  **Final Devoicing:** The underlying root ends in a voiced velar fricative /ɣ/ (spelled *g*). However, because it appears at the end of the word, Dutch phonological rules dictate **final devoicing**. Therefore, the *g* is pronounced as a voiceless velar fricative /x/ (like the *ch* in Scottish *loch*).
        *   *Underlying:* /ɣəˈdraɣ/
        *   *Surface:* /ɣəˈdrɑx/

---

### 3. Semantic Composition (Meaning Construction)

To understand the meaning of *gedrag*, we must look at how the morphemes combine semantically:

1.  **Literal Meaning:** *Ge-* (result/state) + *drag* (carry) = "The carrying" or "The state of being carried."
2.  **Reflexive Shift:** In Dutch (and German, *Gebaren*), the verb *dragen* is often used reflexively in the context of behavior (*zich gedragen* = to behave, literally "to carry oneself").
3.  **Metonymy:** The noun *gedrag* shifted from the physical act of "carrying" to the metaphorical act of "carrying oneself" in a social context.
    *   **Result:** "Behavior," "Conduct," or "Demeanor."

### 4. Grammatical Properties
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun (Substantive).
*   **Gender:** Common (*de* word).
*   **Plural:** *gedrag* (usually uncountable/abstract), though *gedrags* can be used in specific contexts (e.g., *verschillende gedrags* - different behaviors).
*   **Derivatives:** *Gedragslijn* (line of conduct), *Gedragscode* (code of conduct), *Gedragsstoornis* (behavioral disorder).

### Conclusion
Linguistically, **`gedrag`** is a **nominalized past participle**. It consists of the nominalizing prefix **`ge-`** attached to the stem of the strong verb **`dragen`** (with vowel shortening and final devoicing). The word encapsulates the concept of "the way one carries oneself," which has lexicalized into the standard term for "behavior."
