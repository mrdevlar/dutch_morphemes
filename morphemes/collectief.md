From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`collectief`** is a loanword (specifically a *leenwoord*) derived from Latin via French. It is a complex word composed of multiple morphemes that can be analyzed on two levels: the surface level (Dutch morphology) and the etymological level (Latin roots).

Here is the breakdown and explanation of the morphemes.

### 1. Surface Morphological Analysis (Dutch Level)
At the level of modern Dutch morphology, the word is divided into two primary morphemes: a **bound root** and a **derivational suffix**.

| Morpheme | Type | Function/Origin | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`collect-`** | Bound Root | Latin stem (*collectus*) | "Gathered" or "Brought together" |
| **`-ief`** | Derivational Suffix | Latin *-ivus* (via French *-if*) | "Pertaining to" / "Characterized by" |

**Explanation:**
*   **`collect-`**: This is the lexical core of the word. In Dutch, this root is **bound**, meaning it cannot stand alone as an independent word (you cannot say *"Ik ga collect"*). It carries the core semantic weight of "gathering."
*   **`-ief`**: This is a productive suffix in Dutch used to form adjectives from verbs or nouns (often of Latin/French origin). It converts the concept of "gathering" into an adjective meaning "related to gathering."
    *   *Note:* In native Germanic Dutch, the equivalent suffix would be *-ig* (e.g., *krijg* $\rightarrow$ *krijg-ig*), but *-ief* is standard for international vocabulary.

### 2. Etymological Analysis (Latin Level)
To fully understand the internal structure, we must look at the Latin etymology from which the Dutch word was borrowed (*collectivus* $\rightarrow$ French *collectif* $\rightarrow$ Dutch *collectief*).

| Morpheme | Type | Origin | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`col-`** | Prefix | Latin *com-* | "Together" (assimilated before *l*) |
| **`-lect-`** | Root | Latin *legere* | "To gather," "to choose," or "to read" |
| **`-iv-`** | Suffix | Latin *-ivus* | "Tending to" or "Pertaining to" |
| **`-us`** | Ending | Latin | Masculine adjective ending (dropped in Dutch) |

**Semantic Composition:**
1.  **`col-` + `-lect-`**: *Colligere* (to gather together).
2.  **`-lect-` + `-iv-`**: *Collectivus* (tending to gather).
3.  **Adoption**: The Dutch language adopted the stem *collect-* and the suffix *-ief*, dropping the Latin gender ending *-us*.

Understanding the morphemes helps explain how the word behaves grammatically in a sentence.

    *   *Example:* `collectief` + `-e` = **`collectieve`**.

### Summary
The word **`collectief`** is a morphological compound of a **Latin root** (`collect-`) and a **Latinate suffix** (`-ief`).
*   **`collect-`** provides the meaning of "gathering."
*   **`-ief`** provides the grammatical category of "adjective" (pertaining to).

Together, they create a word describing something that is "characterized by being gathered together" or "shared by a group."
