Here is the breakdown of the morphemes, moving from the smallest units to the full structure.

### 1. The Full Segmentation

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The First Constituent: `afdeling` (Department)
The first part of the compound is itself a derived noun. It consists of three morphemes:

1.  **`af-` (Prefix)**
    *   **Type:** Separable Prefix.
    *   **Function:** Modifies the meaning of the verb stem.
    *   **Meaning:** Indicates separation, completion, or direction "away/off."
    *   **Context:** It turns the general action of dividing into a specific action of "dividing off" or "allocating."

2.  **`del` (Root/Stem)**
    *   **Type:** Bound Root.
    *   **Source:** Derived from the verb **`delen`** (to divide, to share).
    *   **Function:** Carries the core lexical meaning of "part" or "division."
    *   **Note:** In modern Dutch, `del` does not exist as a free-standing word; it is a bound morpheme that requires affixes to function.

3.  **`-ing` (Suffix)**
    *   **Type:** Derivational Suffix (Nominalizer).
    *   **Function:** Converts the verb (*afdel*en) into a noun (*afdeling*).
    *   **Meaning:** Indicates the result of the action or the entity resulting from the action.
    *   **Result:** *Afdeling* literally means "the thing that has been divided off" $\rightarrow$ **Department/Division**.

#### B. The Linking Element: `-s-`
4.  **`-s-` (Interfix / Fugue)**
    *   **Type:** Interfix (Linking Element).
    *   **Function:** Phonological and orthographic connector between two nouns in a compound.
    *   **Linguistic Note:** Historically, this was a genitive marker (possessive 's'), but in modern Dutch morphology, it is analyzed as a linking element without specific possessive meaning. It is inserted to facilitate pronunciation and mark the compound structure.
    *   **Alternative:** Sometimes an `-en-` is used (e.g., *kinderen*), but here `-s-` is the standard interfix for this specific lexical combination.

#### C. The Second Constituent: `hoofd` (Head)
5.  **`hoofd` (Root)**
    *   **Type:** Free Root / Noun.
    *   **Meaning:** Head.
    *   **Function:** This is the **Head of the Compound**. In Dutch (and Germanic languages generally), the right-most element determines the grammatical category and gender of the whole word.
    *   **Semantic Role:** It indicates the *role* or *position* (the person who is the "head").

### 3. Syntactic and Semantic Composition

To understand the word linguistically, we look at how these parts combine:

1.  **Derivational Layer:**
    *   *delen* (verb) $\rightarrow$ *af* + *del* + *ing* $\rightarrow$ **`afdeling`** (noun).
    *   *Meaning:* A division or department.

2.  **Compounding Layer:**
    *   *afdeling* (noun) + *hoofd* (noun) $\rightarrow$ **`afdelingshoofd`**.
    *   *Structure:* [Noun] + [Interfix] + [Noun].
    *   *Head Rule:* The word is a type of *hoofd* (head). It is not a type of *afdeling*.
    *   *Meaning:* The head *of* the department.
