From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`thuiskomen`** is a classic example of a **separable compound verb** (*scheidbaar werkwoord*).

Here is the breakdown of its morphemes, followed by an explanation of its morphology, syntax, and etymology.

### 1. Morphemic Segmentation

The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

| Morpheme | Form | Type | Meaning/Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | `thuis` | Root / Prefix | Adverb (Locative): "at home" / "home" |
| **2** | `kom` | Root | Verb Stem: "come" |
| **3** | `-en` | Suffix | Inflectional: Marks the infinitive form |

**Full Segmentation:** `thuis` + `kom` + `-en`

---

### 2. Detailed Morphological Analysis

#### A. `thuis` (The Locative Element)
*   **Part of Speech:** Originally an adverb of place.
*   **Function:** In this compound, it functions as a **separable prefix**. It specifies the *direction* or *goal* of the motion.
*   **Semantic Role:** It provides the locative context. Unlike the English "home" (which can be a noun or adverb), *thuis* is strictly an adverb in modern Dutch.
*   **Stress:** In Dutch separable compounds, the primary lexical stress falls on the prefix. Therefore, the word is pronounced **`THUIS`-komen** (not *thu-KO-men*). This stress pattern distinguishes it from inseparable verbs (like *bekomen*, where stress is on the root).

#### B. `kom` (The Verbal Root)
*   **Part of Speech:** Verb stem.
*   **Function:** This carries the core action of the word (motion towards the speaker or a reference point).
*   **Inflection:** This is the bare stem. In other tenses, this stem changes (e.g., *ik kom* [present], *ik kwam* [past]).

#### C. `-en` (The Infinitive Suffix)
*   **Part of Speech:** Inflectional suffix.
*   **Function:** This is the standard marker for the **infinitive** in Dutch verbs.
*   **Note:** If the word were conjugated in the present tense for the first person singular (*I come home*), the suffix would drop: *Ik **thuis-kom***. If it were past tense, it would be *Ik **thuis-kwam***. The form `thuiskomen` specifically denotes the dictionary/infinitive form.

---

### 3. Syntactic Behavior: The Separable Verb

The most distinct linguistic feature of `thuiskomen` is its behavior in sentence structure. It is a **separable verb** (*scheidbaar werkwoord*).

*   **In the Infinitive:** The morphemes stay together.
    *   *Ik wil **thuiskomen**.* (I want to come home.)
*   **In a Main Clause:** The prefix `thuis` separates from the verb `komen` and moves to the end of the clause.
    *   *Ik **kom** vanavond **thuis**.* (I come home tonight.)
    *   *Structure:* Subject + Conjugated Verb + ... + Separable Prefix.
*   **In a Subordinate Clause:** The prefix and verb rejoin at the end.
    *   *...dat ik vanavond **thuiskom**.* (...that I come home tonight.)

This behavior is similar to English phrasal verbs (e.g., "come home" vs. "I come home"), but in Dutch, it is treated morphologically as a single lexical unit that splits syntactically.

---

### 4. Etymology

*   **`thuis`**: Derives from Middle Dutch *thuis*, Old Dutch *thius*. It is related to the Proto-Germanic *\*þeusaz* (people/household). Historically, it implies "of the house" or "within the household." It is cognate with the English word "thou" (in the sense of belonging to the group) and the German *Haus* (house), though the semantic shift in Dutch moved specifically to the location "at home."
*   **`komen`**: Derives from Proto-Germanic *\*kumaną*, which comes from the Proto-Indo-European root *\*gʷem-* (to go, to step). It is cognate with English "come" and German "kommen."

### Summary
Linguistically, **`thuiskomen`** is a **derivational compound** consisting of a locative adverb (`thuis`) and a motion verb (`komen`), marked by an infinitive suffix (`-en`). Its defining characteristic is its status as a **separable verb**, where the semantic components split syntactically in main clauses, governed by specific stress rules that place emphasis on the directional prefix.
