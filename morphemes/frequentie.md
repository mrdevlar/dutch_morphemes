From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`frequentie`** is a classic example of a **loanword** (specifically a *cultuurleenwoord* or cultural loanword) that has been fully integrated into the Dutch morphological system.

Here is the breakdown and explanation of its morphemes.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into two distinct morphemes:

> **`frequent`** + **`-ie`**

*   **`frequent`**: The bound root (stem).
*   **`-ie`**: The derivational suffix.

---

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Root: `frequent-`
*   **Type:** Bound Root (in Dutch).
*   **Origin:** Latin.
*   **Source Word:** Latin *frequens* (genitive *frequentis*).
*   **Original Meaning:** "Crowded," "numerous," "repeated," or "occurring often."
*   **Status in Dutch:** In modern Dutch, `frequent` does not exist as a standalone adjective (you cannot say *"Hij is frequent"*). It is a **bound morpheme** that only appears when combined with a suffix (like *-ie*, *-eel*, or *-eren*).
    *   *Example:* *frequentie* (frequency), *frequent* (rarely used as adjective, usually *vaak*), *frequenteren* (to frequent/visit often).
*   **Orthography:** The letter **`q`** is a strong orthographic marker of its Latin origin. Native Dutch words (Germanic roots) do not contain the letter `q` (except in very recent loanwords).

#### B. The Suffix: `-ie`
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix.
*   **Origin:** Latin *-ia* (transmitted via French *-ie*).
*   **Function:** It forms abstract nouns indicating a **state, quality, or condition**.
*   **Phonology:** In Dutch, this is pronounced as a close front unrounded vowel /i/ (spelled `ie`).
*   **Productivity:** This suffix is highly productive in Dutch for scientific, technical, and academic terminology (e.g., *energie*, *universiteit*, *democratie*, *frequentie*).
*   **Grammatical Gender:** Historically, nouns ending in *-ie* were often feminine, though in modern Dutch, grammatical gender is largely neutralized (mostly *de*-words). *De frequentie* is the standard usage.

---

### 3. Etymological Path
The word traveled through several languages before settling in Dutch:

1.  **Latin:** *frequentia* (noun) derived from *frequens* (adjective).
2.  **French:** *fréquence* (The French spelling influenced the Dutch spelling, particularly the `q` and the `e` sound).
3.  **English:** *frequency* (Dutch often aligns with English scientific terminology).
4.  **Dutch:** *frequentie* (Adopted directly from the Latin/French form).

### 4. Linguistic Classification

*   **Morphological Process:** **Derivation**. The suffix *-ie* changes the lexical category (conceptually) from an adjectival quality (being frequent) to a noun (the concept of frequency).
*   **Word Class:** Common Noun (*Zelfstandig naamwoord*).
*   **Loanword Status:** It is a **non-native** word. It was not formed using native Germanic morphemes.
    *   *Native Equivalent:* If Dutch had formed this word using native Germanic roots, it might look something like **`vaakheid`** (*vaak* = often + *-heid* = -ness/hood). However, *vaakheid* is not standard usage; *frequentie* is preferred in technical and formal contexts.

### 6. Pluralization Note
When pluralized, the suffix *-ie* remains intact, and the standard Dutch plural marker *-s* is added:
*   Singular: *de frequentie*
*   Plural: *de frequentie**s***

This confirms that *-ie* is part of the stem in the pluralization process, not a separate inflectional ending itself.
