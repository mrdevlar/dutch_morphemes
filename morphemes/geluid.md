From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`geluid`** (meaning "sound" or "noise") is a classic example of **derivational morphology**. It is formed by combining a prefix with a root to create a new part of speech (a noun) from a verbal concept.

Here is the breakdown and explanation of its morphemes.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into two distinct morphemes:

> **`ge-`** + **`luid`**

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Prefix: `ge-`
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix (Bound Morpheme).
*   **Function:** Nominalization.
*   **Explanation:** In Dutch, the prefix *ge-* is one of the most common markers used to turn verbs into nouns. Specifically, it often creates **deverbal nouns** that denote the **result**, **collective**, or **process** of the action described by the verb.
*   **Linguistic Pattern:** This follows a productive pattern in Germanic languages.
    *   *Example:* *gebruiken* (to use) $\rightarrow$ *gebruik* (use).
    *   *Example:* *zien* (to see) $\rightarrow$ *gezicht* (face/sight).
    *   *Example:* *wonen* (to live) $\rightarrow$ *gewoon* (custom/habit - though irregular).
*   **In `geluid`:** The *ge-* indicates that this word represents the phenomenon or the result of the action associated with the root.

#### B. The Root: `luid`
*   **Type:** Lexical Root (Free Morpheme).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *lūden* and Proto-Germanic *\*hlūdaz*.
*   **Original Meaning:** "Loud," "resounding," or "to ring/call."
*   **Related Forms:**
    *   **Adjective:** *luid* (loud).
    *   **Verb:** *luiden* (to ring, to sound, to call).
*   **Semantic Shift:** This is the most interesting linguistic aspect of the word.
    *   Historically, the root *luid* referred specifically to something **loud** or **ringing** (like a bell).
    *   However, when nominalized with *ge-*, the meaning **generalized**. *Geluid* no longer implies "loudness"; it now refers to **sound in general** (whether quiet or loud).
    *   This is a process known as **semantic broadening**.

---

### 3. Syntactic and Grammatical Context

*   **Part of Speech:** Noun (Substantive).
*   **Gender:** Neuter (*het geluid*).
*   **Plural:** *geluiden* (sounds/noises).
*   **Verb Relationship:** The word is directly related to the verb **`luiden`**.
    *   *De klok luidt.* (The bell rings.)
    *   *Het geluid van de klok.* (The sound of the bell.)
    *   *Note:* In modern Dutch, *luiden* is somewhat archaic or specific to bells/calls. The general verb for "to make sound" is often *maken* (to make) or *klinken* (to ring/sound), but the noun *geluid* remains the standard term for "sound."

### 4. Summary Table

| Morpheme | Type | Meaning/Function | Origin |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`ge-`** | Prefix | Nominalizer (indicates result/phenomenon) | Proto-Germanic *ga-* |
| **`luid`** | Root | To ring/sound (originally "loud") | Proto-Germanic *\*hlūdaz* |
| **`geluid`** | **Word** | **Sound / Noise** | Middle Dutch *gelude* |

### Conclusion
Linguistically, **`geluid`** is a **deverbal noun**. It takes the root concept of "ringing/calling" (*luid*) and uses the nominalizing prefix (*ge-*) to abstract that action into a concrete noun representing the physical phenomenon of sound. While the root retains its connection to "loudness" (as seen in the adjective *luid*), the noun has undergone semantic broadening to encompass all auditory vibrations.
