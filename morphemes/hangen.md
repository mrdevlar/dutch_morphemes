From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`hangen`** (meaning "to hang") can be analyzed as a **monolexemic verb** composed of two distinct morphemes.

Here is the breakdown and explanation of its morphology, etymology, and grammatical function.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation

The word is segmented as follows:

> **hang** + **en**

| Morpheme | Type | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`hang-`** | **Root / Stem** | Carries the core lexical meaning (the concept of hanging). |
| **`-en`** | **Inflectional Suffix** | Marks the verb as an **infinitive** (or plural present tense). |

---

### 2. Detailed Explanation of Morphemes

#### A. The Root: `hang-`
*   **Status:** This is a **free root morpheme** in the sense that it carries the primary semantic weight. While it cannot stand alone as a complete sentence in the infinitive form, it appears as the stem in various conjugated forms (e.g., *ik hang* "I hang", *hang!* "hang! [imperative]").
*   **Phonology:** In IPA, this is typically pronounced as /hɑŋ/ (with a velar nasal /ŋ/ at the end).
*   **Semantic Field:** It denotes the state of being suspended or the action of suspending something.
*   **Verb Class:** In Dutch morphology, `hang-` belongs to a class of **strong verbs** (sterke werkwoorden). This means the vowel in the root changes during conjugation to indicate tense (specifically, the vowel shifts from *a* to *i* in the past tense: *hangen* → *hing*).

#### B. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Status:** This is a **bound morpheme**. It cannot exist independently and must attach to a verb stem.
*   **Function:**
    1.  **Infinitive Marker:** In the context of the word `hangen`, this suffix marks the verb as the dictionary form (the infinitive).
    2.  **Plural Present Tense:** This same suffix is used for the first and third person plural in the present tense (e.g., *wij hangen* "we hang", *zij hangen* "they hang").
*   **Historical Note:** This suffix originates from the Proto-Germanic infinitive ending *-aną*.

### 3. Grammatical Context (Morphosyntax)

While the word consists of only two morphemes, its usage involves complex morphosyntactic rules in Dutch:

*   **Transitivity:**
    *   **Intransitive:** `hangen` is most commonly used intransitively (e.g., *De jas hangt aan de haak* – "The coat hangs on the hook").
    *   **Transitive:** To express the action of hanging something (transitive), Dutch often uses the separable compound **`ophangen`** (to hang up). However, `hangen` can be used transitively in specific contexts, though it is less common in modern Standard Dutch.
*   **Conjugation (Strong Verb):**
    Because `hangen` is a strong verb, the root morpheme `hang-` undergoes **ablaut** (vowel gradation) in the past tense, which is a morphological process internal to the root:
    *   Infinitive: *hangen* (Root: **a**)
    *   Past Singular: *hing* (Root: **i**)
    *   Past Participle: *gehangen* (Root: **a**)

To understand the morphemes historically, we look at the word's lineage:

*   **Proto-Germanic:** `*hangjaną` (causative "to cause to hang") or `*hanganą` (intransitive "to hang").
*   **Proto-Indo-European (PIE):** Likely derived from the root `*keng-` or `*kenk-`, meaning "to bend" or "to curve."
*   **Cognates:**
    *   **English:** *hang* (The English word is cognate and shares the same Germanic root, though English "hang" is irregular in its past tense: *hang-hung-hung*).
    *   **German:** *hängen* (Shares the same structure: root *häng-* + infinitive *-en*).
    *   **Old English:** *hangian*.

### Summary
Linguistically, **`hangen`** is a **verb** formed by a **Germanic lexical root** (`hang-`) combined with a **productive infinitive suffix** (`-en`). It is a **strong verb**, meaning its root vowel changes morphologically to indicate past tense, distinguishing it from weak verbs that simply add a dental suffix (like *-te* or *-de*) for the past tense.
