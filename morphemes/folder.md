From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`folder`** presents a fascinating case of **loanword morphology** (specifically, an Anglicism).

To analyze it correctly, we must distinguish between its **etymological structure** (how it was built in its source language) and its **synchronic structure** (how it functions within the current Dutch language system).

### 1. Classification: Loanword (Leenwoord)
In Dutch linguistics, *folder* is classified as a **loanword** (or *leennwoord*) from English. It is not derived from native Dutch roots (like *vouwen*, the Dutch verb for "to fold").

Because it is a loanword, its internal morphological structure is often opaque to native Dutch speakers, who tend to treat it as a single, unanalyzable unit. However, etymologically, it retains the structure of the English word.

### 2. Etymological Morpheme Breakdown
If we analyze the word based on its English origin, it consists of two morphemes:

| Morpheme | Type | Origin | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`fold-`** | Root (Lexical) | English verb *to fold* | To bend or double over. |
| **`-er`** | Suffix (Derivational) | English agentive suffix | One who/that which performs the action. |

*   **`fold-`**: This is the verbal root. In English, it denotes the action of bending paper.
*   **`-er`**: This is an **agentive/instrumental suffix**. In English, it turns a verb into a noun indicating the tool or person that performs the action (e.g., *teach* $\rightarrow$ *teacher*, *bake* $\rightarrow$ *baker*). Here, it creates a noun meaning "something that is folded."

### 3. Synchronic Morphology (Dutch Integration)
Once borrowed into Dutch, the word undergoes **morphological integration**. It is no longer treated as a verb + suffix combination by Dutch speakers, but rather as a **monomorphemic noun root**.

Here is how it behaves in Dutch grammar:

*   **Lexical Category:** Noun (*zelfstandig naamwoord*).
*   **Gender:** Common gender (*de*-word).
    *   *Example:* **De** folder.
*   **Pluralization:** It takes the standard Dutch plural suffix **`-s`**.
    *   *Example:* **De** folder $\rightarrow$ **De** folder**s**.
    *   *Note:* It does *not* take the English plural *-s* as a morphological marker of the word itself, but rather the Dutch plural marker is added to the loanword root.
*   **Verb Derivation:** Unlike English, Dutch does not typically derive a verb from this noun (you do not say *ik folder*). If one needs to express the action, they use the native verb *vouwen* (to fold) or the loan verb *folden* (in informal/tech contexts).

### 4. Semantic Shift (Semantics)
From a semantic perspective, the meaning of *folder* has shifted slightly between English and Dutch:

*   **English:** A "folder" is primarily a container (a file folder) used to hold loose papers.
*   **Dutch:** A "folder" is primarily a **brochure** or **pamphlet** (a printed sheet of paper folded for distribution).
    *   *Linguistic Note:* This is a case of **semantic narrowing** or **specialization**. In Dutch, the word specifically refers to the promotional material, whereas the "file folder" is usually called a *map* (or *ordner* for a ring binder).

### Summary
*   **Word:** `folder`
*   **Status:** Anglicism (Loanword).
*   **Etymological Morphemes:** `fold` (Root) + `-er` (Suffix).
*   **Dutch Morphological Status:** Treated as a single root noun.
*   **Dutch Plural:** `folders` (Root + Dutch plural suffix `-s`).
*   **Meaning:** Promotional brochure/pamphlet.

In conclusion, while the word is **bimorphemic** in its etymological history (English), it functions as a **monomorphemic** lexical item in modern Dutch morphology, subject to Dutch grammatical rules (gender, pluralization).
