From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`grootouder`** (meaning "grandparent") is a complex word formed through two distinct morphological processes: **compounding** and **derivation**.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes, moving from the largest structural units down to the smallest roots.

### 1. Macro-Structure: The Compound
At the highest level, `grootouder` is a **compound noun** (*samengesteld zelfstandig naamwoord*). It consists of two free morphemes joined together.

*   **Structure:** `[groot]` + `[ouder]`
*   **Type:** Endocentric Compound (The meaning is a specific type of the head noun).
*   **Head:** `ouder` (The core meaning is a type of parent).
*   **Modifier:** `groot` (Specifies which generation of parent).

---

### 2. Micro-Structure: Internal Morphology
While `groot` is a simple root, the second component, `ouder`, is itself a derived word. Therefore, a full morphological analysis requires breaking `ouder` down further.

#### Morpheme A: `groot`
*   **Type:** Free Morpheme (Root).
*   **Part of Speech:** Adjective.
*   **Literal Meaning:** Big, large, great.
*   **Function:** In this compound, it functions as a **degree modifier**. In kinship terminology, "big" or "great" indicates a generation removed from the immediate one.
*   **Etymology:** From Old Dutch *grōt*, Proto-Germanic *\*grōtanaz*. Cognate with English *great* and German *groß*.

#### Morpheme B: `oud`
*   **Type:** Free Morpheme (Root).
*   **Part of Speech:** Adjective.
*   **Literal Meaning:** Old.
*   **Function:** This is the semantic base for the second part of the compound.
*   **Etymology:** From Old Dutch *oud*, Proto-Germanic *\*auđaz*. Cognate with English *old* and German *alt*.

#### Morpheme C: `-er`
*   **Type:** Bound Morpheme (Suffix).
*   **Function:** **Comparative Suffix** (Derivational).
*   **Meaning:** "More" or "-er".
*   **Process:** `oud` (old) + `-er` = `ouder` (older).
*   **Lexicalization:** In modern Dutch, `ouder` has been lexicalized as a noun meaning "parent." Historically, it literally meant "the older one" (relative to the child).
*   **Etymology:** The Germanic comparative suffix *\*-iz-*.

---

### 3. Semantic Composition
To understand the meaning linguistically, we look at how the morphemes combine semantically:

1.  **`oud` + `-er`** $\rightarrow$ **`ouder`**: "The older one" $\rightarrow$ **Parent**.
2.  **`groot` + `ouder`** $\rightarrow$ **`grootouder`**: "The big/great parent" $\rightarrow$ **Grandparent**.

**Note on "Great" vs. "Big":**
In English, we use "Grandparent" (from Old French *grant*, meaning great/large). In Dutch, they use "Groot" (meaning big/large). Both languages use the concept of "size/greatness" metaphorically to denote **generational distance**.

### 5. Pluralization (Additional Context)
If we were to pluralize this word to *grootouders* (grandparents), a fourth morpheme would be added:
*   **`-s`**: A bound morpheme (inflectional suffix) marking **plural**.
*   **Structure:** `[groot]` + `[oud]` + `[-er]` + `[-s]`

### Conclusion
Linguistically, `grootouder` is a **compound** of the adjective `groot` and the noun `ouder`. However, the noun `ouder` is internally **derived** from the adjective `oud` plus the comparative suffix `-er`. Thus, the word literally translates to "big-older-one," which semantically evolved to mean "grandparent."
