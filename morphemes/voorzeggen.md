From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`voorzeggen`** is a **compound verb** formed through **prefixation**. It consists of two distinct morphemes that combine to create a specific semantic meaning.

Here is the breakdown and explanation of the morphemes:

### 1. The Morphological Breakdown

| Morpheme | Type | Form | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`voor-`** | Prefix | Separable | "Before" (in time or space) |
| **`zeggen`** | Root (Lexeme) | Verb | "To say" / "To tell" |

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Prefix: `voor-`
*   **Grammatical Category:** Separable Prefix (*scheidbaar voorvoegsel*).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *fora*, cognate with the English preposition **fore-** and the German **vor**.
*   **Semantic Function:** It indicates temporal precedence. It modifies the action of the verb to indicate that the action happens *prior* to the event in question.
*   **Stress Pattern:** In this specific compound, the stress falls on the prefix (**VOOR**-zeggen). This is a key indicator in Dutch phonology that the verb is **separable**.
*   **Syntactic Behavior:** Because it is a separable prefix, in main clauses, the prefix detaches and moves to the end of the sentence.
    *   *Infinitive:* Ik wil het **voorzeggen**. (I want to predict it.)
    *   *Present Tense:* Ik **zeg** het **voor**. (I predict it.)

#### B. The Root: `zeggen`
*   **Grammatical Category:** Verbal Stem (Lexeme).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *seggian*, cognate with English **say** and German **sagen**.
*   **Verb Class:** It is a **strong verb** (irregular).
    *   Infinitive: *zeggen*
    *   Past Singular: *zei*
    *   Past Participle: *gezegd*
*   **Semantic Function:** This provides the core action of the word: the act of speaking or communicating information.

---

### 3. Morphological Process & Syntax

The combination of these morphemes creates a **separable compound verb**. This is a crucial distinction in Dutch morphology because the prefix *voor-* can sometimes be inseparable (e.g., *voorkomen* meaning "to prevent," where stress is on the stem: voor-KO-men).

**Evidence of Separability:**
The strongest linguistic evidence that *voor-* is a separable prefix in this word is found in the formation of the **past participle**.
*   **Rule:** In separable verbs, the participle marker *ge-* is inserted **between** the prefix and the stem.
*   **Formation:** *voor-* + *ge-* + *zeggen* (stem) $\rightarrow$ **voorgezegd**.
*   **Contrast:** If it were an inseparable verb, the *ge-* would be omitted (e.g., *voorkomen* $\rightarrow$ *voorgekomen* is actually incorrect; *voorkomen* [to occur] is inseparable $\rightarrow$ *voorgekomen* is actually *voorgekomen*?     *   *Correction on Inseparable Example:* *Verzorgen* (to care for) $\rightarrow$ *verzorgd* (no *ge-*).
    *   *Correct Application to `voorzeggen`:* Since we say **voorgezegd**, it confirms the prefix is separable.

### 4. Semantic Shift (Idiomatization)

While the morphemes literally translate to "say before," the compound word has undergone a slight semantic shift (idiomatization):
1.  **Literal:** To say something before someone else does (e.g., reading a script aloud for someone to write down).
2.  **Figurative (Primary):** To predict or foretell the future (to say something before it happens).

### 5. Cross-Linguistic Cognates

From a comparative linguistics perspective, this structure mirrors English and German compounds:

*   **Dutch:** *voor* (before) + *zeggen* (say) = **voorzeggen** (predict)
*   **German:** *vor* (before) + *sagen* (say) = **voraussagen** (predict)
*   **English:** *fore* (before) + *tell* (say) = **foretell** (predict)
    *   *Note:* English uses *tell* rather than *say* in this specific compound, but the morphological logic is identical.

### Summary
The word **`voorzeggen`** is a **separable compound verb**. It is constructed by attaching the temporal prefix **`voor-`** ("before") to the verbal root **`zeggen`** ("to say"). The insertion of the participle marker *ge-* in the form *voorgezegd* linguistically confirms the separable nature of the prefix.
