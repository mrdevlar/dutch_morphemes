From a morphological perspective, the Dutch word **`yogadocent`** is a **compound noun** (samenstelling). It consists of two distinct free morphemes joined together to create a new lexical item.

Here is the breakdown and linguistic explanation:

### 1. Segmentation
The word can be segmented as follows:
> **`yoga`** + **`docent`**

There is no linking element (known in Dutch as a *voegklank* or *fugenelement*, such as an *-s-* or *-e-*). This is a direct juxtaposition of two nouns.

---

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### Morpheme A: `yoga`
*   **Type:** Free morpheme (can stand alone as an independent word).
*   **Grammatical Category:** Noun.
*   **Etymology:** Loanword from Sanskrit (*yoga*).
*   **Function:** In this compound, it functions as the **modifier** (or dependent). It specifies the domain or subject matter of the head noun.
*   **Semantic Role:** It narrows the meaning of the second morpheme. It answers the question: "What *kind* of teacher?"

#### Morpheme B: `docent`
*   **Type:** Free morpheme (can stand alone as an independent word).
*   **Grammatical Category:** Noun.
*   **Etymology:** Loanword from Latin (*docens*, the present participle of *docere*, meaning "to teach").
*   **Function:** In this compound, it functions as the **head** (or nucleus).
*   **Semantic Role:** It carries the core grammatical number and gender properties of the compound. The entire word refers to a person who teaches, specifically defined by the first element.

---

### 3. Morphological Process: Endocentric Compounding
The formation of `yogadocent` is an example of **endocentric compounding**.
*   **Endocentric:** The meaning of the compound is a hyponym (a specific subtype) of the head. A `yogadocent` is a type of `docent`.
*   **Structure:** [Noun + Noun] $\rightarrow$ Noun.
*   **Stress Pattern:** In Dutch compounds, the primary lexical stress typically falls on the **first element**. Therefore, the pronunciation is **`YOGA`-docent** (not yoga-`DOCENT`). This distinguishes it from a phrase where stress might fall differently.

### 5. Linguistic Note on Productivity
This word demonstrates the **productivity** of Dutch compounding. The suffix `-docent` is not a suffix in the traditional sense (like *-er* in English *teacher*), but a bound or free root that acts as a head in many compounds (e.g., *sportdocent*, *muziekdocent*, *taaldocent*). The structure allows speakers to easily coin new terms for instructors of specific subjects by simply placing the subject noun before `docent`.
