From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`meegaan`** is a classic example of a **separable compound verb** (*scheidbaar samengesteld werkwoord*).

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation of its components.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word consists of two distinct morphemes:

> **mee** + **gaan**

### 2. Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Prefix/Particle: `mee`
*   **Category:** Adverb / Separable Particle (*scheidbaar deel*).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *me*, Old Dutch *mē*. It is cognate with the German *mit* and the English *with* (via the Proto-Germanic root *\*miþ-*).
*   **Semantic Meaning:** "Along," "with," or "together."
*   **Function:** In this context, it acts as a directional or associative modifier. It indicates that the action of the main verb is performed in accompaniment with someone or something else.
*   **Stress:** In Dutch separable verbs, the primary stress falls on the prefix. Therefore, the word is pronounced **`MEE`-gaan** (not mee-GAAN).

#### B. The Verb Root: `gaan`
*   **Category:** Lexical Verb (Infinitive).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *gān*, Old Dutch *gān*. It is cognate with the English *go* and German *gehen*.
*   **Semantic Meaning:** "To go."
*   **Function:** This is the **head** of the compound. It provides the core action and determines the grammatical conjugation (e.g., *ik ga*, *jij gaat*, *wij gaan*).

### 3. Syntactic Behavior (Separability)
The most defining linguistic feature of `meegaan` is its **separability**. While it is written as a single word in the dictionary (infinitive form), it behaves syntactically as two separate units in a sentence.

*   **Infinitive (One word):**
    *   *Ik wil **meegaan**.* (I want to come along.)
*   **Finite Verb (Split):**
    *   In a main clause, the verb moves to the second position, and the particle `mee` moves to the end of the clause.
    *   *Ik **ga** met je **mee**.* (I go with you.)
    *   *Zij **gaat** **mee**.* (She is coming along.)
*   **Past Participle (Infix):**
    *   When forming the past participle, the prefix `ge-` is inserted **between** the two morphemes. This is a hallmark of separable verbs in Germanic languages.
    *   *mee* + *ge* + *gaan* = **`meegegaan`**.
    *   *Ik ben **meegegaan**.* (I have come along.)

### 4. Semantic Composition
The meaning of the compound is compositional but idiomatic:
*   **Literal:** "With-go."
*   **Actual:** "To accompany," "to come along," or "to go with."

It implies movement in the same direction as a reference point (usually another person).

| **gaan** | Verb Root | to go | Unstressed |

### 6. Linguistic Classification
*   **Language Family:** Indo-European > Germanic > West Germanic > Low Franconian.
*   **Word Formation:** Derivation via Compounding (specifically Verb-Particle Compounding).
*   **Type:** Separable Compound Verb (*Scheidbaar Werkwoord*).

In summary, `meegaan` is not a single atomic unit but a syntactic combination of an adverbial particle (`mee`) and a motion verb (`gaan`) that functions as a single semantic unit in the lexicon but splits apart during sentence construction.
