From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`beeldend`** is a complex word formed through derivation. It consists of two distinct morphemes.

Here is the breakdown and explanation of the morphemes:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented as follows:
> **beeld** + **end**

---

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### **Morpheme 1: `beeld`**
*   **Type:** Free Morpheme (Root)
*   **Original Part of Speech:** Noun (*het beeld*)
*   **Meaning:** Image, picture, statue, representation, or mental picture.
*   **Etymology:**
    *   Derived from Old Dutch *beld*.
    *   Cognate with Old High German *bild* and English *bale* (archaic) or *picture* (via the concept of "shining/appearing").
    *   Traces back to the Proto-Germanic *\*bildaz* and the Proto-Indo-European root *\*bhel-* (meaning "to shine," "to look," or "to appear").
*   **Function in this word:** It serves as the semantic base. It provides the core meaning related to "images" or "visual representation."

#### **Morpheme 2: `-end`**
*   **Type:** Bound Morpheme (Suffix)
*   **Grammatical Category:** Derivational Suffix (Present Participle)
*   **Origin:** Proto-Germanic *\*-andz*.
*   **Cognates:** English *-ing* (as in *painting*), German *-end* (as in *spielend*).
*   **Function:**
    1.  **Verbalization:** It attaches to a verb stem to create a present participle.
    2.  **Adjectivalization:** In Dutch, present participles frequently function as adjectives.
*   **Derivational Chain:**
    1.  Start with the noun: *beeld* (image).
    2.  Derive a verb (Denominal): *beelden* (to image, to sculpt, to depict).
    3.  Derive the participle/adjective: *beeldend* (image-making, visual).
*   **Meaning Contribution:** It adds a sense of **activity**, **process**, or **characteristic**. It transforms the static concept of an "image" into the active quality of "creating images" or "having the quality of an image."

---

### 3. Syntactic and Semantic Function

*   **Part of Speech:** Adjective (derived from a present participle).
*   **Usage:** It is most commonly encountered in the fixed phrase **`beeldende kunst`** (visual arts).
*   **Semantic Shift:**
    *   *Beeld* = The object (the picture).
    *   *Beeldend* = The quality of the object or the action (visual, plastic, pictorial).
    *   When used in *beeldende kunst*, it distinguishes arts that create visual forms (painting, sculpture) from arts that create auditory forms (music) or literary forms (literature).

### 4. Summary Table

| Morpheme | Type | Meaning | Role |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **beeld** | Root (Noun) | Image / Picture | Semantic core |
| **-end** | Suffix (Participle) | -ing / -al | Converts to adjective indicating process/quality |

### 5. Linguistic Note on Spelling
In Dutch orthography, the suffix *-end* is added directly to the stem of the verb *beelden*. Since the stem ends in a *d* (*beeld-*), and the suffix begins with a vowel (*-e-*), no doubling of the consonant occurs (unlike in verbs with short vowels). The spelling remains **beeldend**, not *beelddend*.
