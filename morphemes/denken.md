From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`denken`** (meaning "to think") is a verb in its infinitive form. It is composed of two distinct morphemes.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Segmentation
The word can be segmented as follows:
> **`denk`** + **`-en`**

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### **Morpheme 1: `denk-`**
*   **Type:** **Root / Stem** (Lexical Morpheme)
*   **Status:** Bound (in this specific form)
*   **Function:** Carries the core semantic meaning of the word.
*   **Explanation:** This is the base of the verb. It contains the lexical information regarding the action of "thinking."
    *   In the present tense conjugation, this stem appears without the suffix (e.g., *ik **denk*** — "I think").
    *   In the imperative mood, it also appears as the stem (e.g., ***Denk**!* — "Think!").
    *   **Etymology:** This root is cognate with the English word *think* and the German word *denken*. It derives from the Proto-Germanic **\*thankijaną**.
    *   **Note on Ablaut:** Historically, this is a "strong" verb (specifically Class 5 in Germanic). While the infinitive uses the vowel /ɛ/ (as in *bed*), the past tense undergoes vowel gradation (ablaut) to /ɑ/ (e.g., *ik **dacht*** — "I thought"). However, within the specific structure of the infinitive *denken*, the root remains stable as `denk-`.

#### **Morpheme 2: `-en`**
*   **Type:** **Inflectional Suffix** (Grammatical Morpheme)
*   **Status:** Bound
*   **Function:** Marks the **infinitive** form of the verb.
*   **Explanation:** In Dutch morphology, the suffix *-en* is the standard marker for the non-finite infinitive form of regular and irregular verbs. It distinguishes the dictionary form (*denken*) from the conjugated forms (*denk*, *denkt*, *denken* [plural]).
    *   Unlike English, which often uses the bare stem for the infinitive (e.g., "to think"), Dutch requires this suffix for the infinitive citation form.
    *   In some dialects or after certain vowels, this may reduce to just *-n*, but in Standard Dutch, it is consistently *-en* for this verb.

### 3. Morphological Classification
*   **Word Class:** Verb
*   **Derivational Status:** This is a **simplex** word (not a compound or a derived word with prefixes/suffixes that change the part of speech). It is formed by inflectional morphology only.
*   **Morpheme Count:** 2 (Monomorphemic roots + Inflectional suffix).
