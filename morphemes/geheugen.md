From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`geheugen`** (meaning "memory") is a deverbal noun. It is formed through a specific morphological process involving a prefix, a root, and a suffix.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **ge-** + **heug-** + **-en**

---

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### **A. Prefix: `ge-`**
*   **Type:** Derivational Prefix.
*   **Function:** Nominalization.
*   **Explanation:** In Dutch, the prefix *ge-* is highly productive. While it is most commonly known for forming past participles (e.g., *lopen* → *gelopen*), it also forms nouns from verbs (deverbal nouns).
*   **Semantic Role:** In this context, *ge-* abstracts the action of the verb into a concept or a faculty. It signals that the word is a noun representing the result or the capacity associated with the root.
*   **Comparison:** Similar to *gebed* (prayer, from *bidden* - to pray) or *gezicht* (face/sight, from *zien* - to see).

#### **B. Root: `heug-`**
*   **Type:** Lexical Root (Stem).
*   **Meaning:** "Mind," "thought," or "to remember."
*   **Etymology:** This root derives from Old Dutch *hug-* or *hugon*. It is cognate with the Old High German *hug* (mind, thought) and the Old English *hycgan* (to think).
*   **Verb Connection:** The root comes from the verb **`heugen`**. In modern Dutch, this verb is largely archaic or dialectal, surviving primarily in the impersonal construction *het heugt mij* ("it remembers to me" / "I remember").
*   **Phonology:** The vowel *eu* in *heug* represents a diphthong that evolved from the older Germanic *u* (via umlaut processes in Middle Dutch).

#### **C. Suffix: `-en`**
*   **Type:** Derivational Suffix (Nominalizer).
*   **Function:** Marks the word as a noun.
*   **Explanation:** This suffix often coincides with the infinitive ending of Dutch verbs. In the construction *ge-...-en*, it completes the nominalization pattern. It turns the verbal stem into a neuter noun (*het* word).
*   **Note:** While many Dutch infinitives end in *-en* (e.g., *lopen*), the combination with the prefix *ge-* creates a specific type of abstract noun that refers to the *faculty* or *organ* of the action, rather than just the action itself.

---

### 3. Synthesis and Semantic Shift

When combined, the morphemes create a specific semantic meaning:

1.  **`heug-`** provides the core concept of **mental activity/thought**.
2.  **`ge-`** and **`-en`** wrap this concept into an **abstract noun**.
3.  **Result:** The word shifts from the action of "thinking/remembering" to the **faculty** or **capacity** of memory.

### 4. Linguistic Context & Cognates

*   **Related Dutch Words:**
    *   *Herinneren* (to remember): Uses a different root (*-inneren*), showing that Dutch has multiple lexical paths for "memory."
    *   *Gedachtenis* (remembrance): Uses the root *dacht* (thought), related to *denken* (to think).
*   **Germanic Cognates:**
    *   There is no direct modern English cognate for *heugen*. However, the Proto-Germanic root *\*hug-* is related to the Old English *hycgan* (to think).
    *   Do not confuse this with the English word *hug* (to embrace), which comes from a different Low German root (*huggen*).
