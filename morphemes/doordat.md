From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`doordat`** is a **lexicalized compound conjunction**. It is formed through a process of grammaticalization where a prepositional phrase fused into a single functional word.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word consists of two distinct morphemes:

> **`door`** + **`dat`**

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### **Morpheme 1: `door`**
*   **Original Category:** Preposition (*voorzetsel*).
*   **Literal Meaning:** "Through," "by means of," or "via."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *door*, Old Dutch *dur*. It is cognate with the English word **through** (both stem from the Proto-Germanic root *\*duruh*).
*   **Function in `doordat`:** In this compound, the prepositional meaning of "instrumentality" or "channel" is retained but abstracted. It indicates the **mechanism** or the **means** by which a result is achieved. It answers the question "How did this happen?" rather than just "Why?"

#### **Morpheme 2: `dat`**
*   **Original Category:** Demonstrative pronoun (*aanwijzend voornaamwoord*) / Complementizer (*voegwoord*).
*   **Literal Meaning:** "That."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *dat*, Old Dutch *dat*. It is cognate with the English word **that**.
*   **Function in `doordat`:** Originally, this was a demonstrative pronoun referring to a specific fact or situation. In the compound, it has grammaticalized into a **subordinator** (complementizer). It introduces the subordinate clause that contains the causal information.

### 3. Syntactic and Semantic Evolution (Grammaticalization)

The formation of `doordat` is a classic example of **grammaticalization**:

1.  **Stage 1 (Phrase):** Historically, speakers would use the preposition `door` followed by the pronoun `dat` in a sentence structure like:
    *   *"...door dat..."* (through that...)
    *   Example: *Hij viel door dat hij struikelde.* (He fell through that he stumbled.)
2.  **Stage 2 (Fusion):** Over time, the phrase became fixed. The space between the words disappeared, and the stress pattern shifted to treat it as a single unit.
3.  **Stage 3 (Lexicalization):** It became a dedicated conjunction meaning "because" or "due to the fact that."

**Literal Semantic Composition:**
*   **`door`** (through/means of) + **`dat`** (that fact)
*   **Literal Translation:** "Through that fact"
*   **Functional Meaning:** "Because"

### 4. Linguistic Nuance: `doordat` vs. `omdat`
In Dutch linguistics, it is important to distinguish `doordat` from its synonym `omdat` ("because"). Both are compounds of a preposition + `dat`, but the preposition dictates the semantic nuance:

*   **`omdat`** (`om` + `dat`): `Om` implies "around" or "for the sake of." This conjunction focuses on the **reason** or **motive** (subjective intent).
    *   *Example:* *Ik bleef thuis omdat ik ziek was.* (I stayed home **because** I was sick [reason].)
*   **`doordat`** (`door` + `dat`): `Door` implies "through" or "by means of." This conjunction focuses on the **cause** or **mechanism** (objective fact). It is often used when the cause is an external event or a physical process.
    *   *Example:* *De plant is dood doordat hij vergeten werd.* (The plant died **due to the fact that** it was forgotten [mechanism/cause].)
