From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`contraproductief`** is a complex word formed through **derivational morphology**. It consists of three distinct morphemes combined to create a specific semantic meaning.

Here is the breakdown and explanation of the morphemes.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented as follows:

> **`contra-`** + **`product`** + **`-ief`**

### 2. Morpheme-by-Morpheme Analysis

| Morpheme | Type | Origin | Meaning/Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`contra-`** | **Prefix** (Bound) | Latin (*contra*) | "Against," "opposite," or "counter." It negates or reverses the meaning of the base word. |
| **`product`** | **Root** (Free) | Latin (*productum*) | "Something produced," "result," or "output." In modern Dutch, this functions as a standalone noun. |
| **`-ief`** | **Suffix** (Bound) | Latin (*-ivus* via French *-if*) | Adjectival suffix. It turns a noun or verb into an adjective meaning "tending to," "characterized by," or "relating to." |

### 3. Detailed Linguistic Explanation

#### A. The Prefix: `contra-`
*   **Function:** This is a **derivational prefix**. It attaches to the front of a base word to alter its meaning.
*   **Semantics:** It indicates opposition. When attached to an adjective or noun, it suggests that the action or quality is working in the opposite direction of what is intended.
*   **Dutch Usage:** In Dutch, `contra-` is a productive prefix used in loanwords (e.g., *contrasteren*, *contrabas*). It is written directly attached to the following word without a hyphen (according to standard Dutch spelling rules).

#### B. The Root: `product`
*   **Function:** This is the **lexical root** (or base). It carries the core semantic weight of the word.
*   **Status:** In synchronic Dutch morphology (how the language works today), `product` is a **free morpheme**. It can stand alone as a noun (*een product* = a product).
*   **Historical Note:** Etymologically, `product` comes from the Latin *producere* (*pro-* "forward" + *ducere* "to lead"). However, in modern Dutch analysis, we treat `product` as a single unit rather than breaking it down further into `pro-` + `duct`.

#### C. The Suffix: `-ief`
*   **Function:** This is a **derivational suffix**. It changes the part of speech from a noun (`product`) to an adjective (`productief`).
*   **Semantics:** It implies a quality or tendency. `Productief` means "characterized by production" or "yielding results."
*   **Phonology:** The addition of `-ief` often triggers stress patterns in Dutch. In `productief`, the stress typically falls on the syllable preceding the suffix (*pro-DUC-tief*).

### 4. Semantic Composition (How the meaning is built)
The meaning of the whole word is constructed hierarchically:

1.  **Base:** `product` (Production/Output)
2.  **Derivation 1:** `product` + `-ief` = **`productief`**
    *   *Meaning:* "Productive" (yielding results).
3.  **Derivation 2:** `contra-` + `productief` = **`contraproductief`**
    *   *Meaning:* "Counterproductive" (yielding results opposite to the desired goal).

### 5. Orthographic Note
In Dutch orthography (spelling), this is written as a **single closed compound** (`contraproductief`).
*   Unlike English, which often uses a hyphen for clarity in complex compounds (e.g., *counter-productive*), Dutch generally prefers to fuse the prefix `contra-` directly to the root without a hyphen, provided there is no vowel clash that makes pronunciation difficult.
*   Since `a` (from *contra*) and `p` (from *product*) do not create a vowel clash, no hyphen is used.

### Summary
Linguistically, **`contraproductief`** is a **derived adjective**. It is formed by taking the noun root **`product`**, adjectivalizing it with the suffix **`-ief`**, and then negating/reversing that quality with the prefix **`contra-`**.
