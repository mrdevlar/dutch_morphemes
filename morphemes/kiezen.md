From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch verb **`kiezen`** (to choose / to elect) can be analyzed by breaking it down into its constituent morphemes, examining its morphophonology (how sounds change), and looking at its grammatical classification.

Here is the breakdown:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word consists of two morphemes:

| Morpheme | Form | Type | Function/Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Root** | `kiez-` | Free / Lexical | Carries the core meaning ("to choose"). |
| **Suffix** | `-en` | Bound / Inflectional | Marks the verb as an **infinitive**. |

**Structure:** `kiez-` + `-en`

---

### 2. Detailed Explanation of Morphemes

#### A. The Root: `kiez-`
*   **Lexical Category:** This is the lexical morpheme (the dictionary meaning).
*   **Etymology:** It derives from the Proto-Germanic **\*kēsaną**. It is cognate with the English verb **"choose"** and the German verb **"kiesen"** (archaic) or **"wählen"** (modern, though etymologically distinct in modern usage, the Germanic root is shared).
*   **Phonological Note:** The underlying phoneme is the voiced alveolar fricative **/z/**. However, in Dutch orthography and phonology, this sound is subject to alternation (see below).

#### B. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Grammatical Category:** This is an inflectional suffix.
*   **Function:** In Dutch, adding `-en` to a verb stem creates the **infinitive** form (the base form of the verb, e.g., "to choose").
*   **Usage:** It is also used for the plural present tense (e.g., *wij kiezen* - we choose) and the plural past tense (e.g., *zij kozen* - they chose).

---

### 3. Linguistic Nuance: Morphophonology (The `s` vs. `z` Issue)
A strictly linguistic analysis must address why the spelling changes between the infinitive and the conjugated forms. This is a classic example of **Final Devoicing** (*Eindklankverharding*) in Dutch.

*   **Underlying Form:** The root is phonologically **/kiez-/**.
*   **Surface Form 1 (Infinitive):** `kiez` + `en` = **`kiezen`**.
    *   Because the root is followed by a vowel (`-en`), the /z/ remains voiced.
*   **Surface Form 2 (Present Singular):** `kiez` + `Ø` (no ending) = **`kies`**.
    *   When the root stands alone at the end of a syllable or word (as in *ik kies* / I choose), Dutch phonology dictates that voiced obstruents become voiceless. Therefore, /z/ becomes /s/.
*   **Surface Form 3 (Present Plural):** `kiez` + `en` = **`kiezen`**.
    *   The vowel follows, so it remains voiced.

**Linguistic Takeaway:** While the orthographic stem is often taught as `kies` (because you add endings to that), the **morphological root** is `kiez-`. The `s` in `kies` is an **allomorph** of the `z` in `kiez`, conditioned by the phonological environment.

---

### 4. Grammatical Classification: Strong Verb
From a morphological typology perspective, `kiezen` is a **Strong Verb** (Sterk werkwoord).

*   **Definition:** Strong verbs form their past tense and past participle through **ablaut** (vowel gradation) rather than adding a dental suffix (like `-t` or `-d` in weak verbs).
*   **Conjugation Pattern:**
    *   **Infinitive:** `kiezen` (Vowel: `ie`)
    *   **Past Singular:** `koos` (Vowel: `oo`)
    *   **Past Plural:** `kozen` (Vowel: `oo`)
    *   **Past Participle:** `gekozen` (Vowel: `oo` + prefix `ge-` + suffix `-en`)

This vowel change (`ie` $\to$ `oo`) is a remnant of the Proto-Indo-European ablaut system and is a key morphological feature of this word.

### Summary
*   **Word:** `kiezen`
*   **Morphemes:** `kiez-` (Root) + `-en` (Infinitive Suffix)
*   **Key Linguistic Feature:** The root undergoes **final devoicing** (`z` $\to$ `s`) when not followed by a vowel, and the verb utilizes **ablaut** (`ie` $\to$ `oo`) for past tense formation.
