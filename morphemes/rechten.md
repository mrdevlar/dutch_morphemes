From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`rechten`** is a classic example of **morphological ambiguity** (or homophony). Without context, it can be analyzed in two distinct ways: as a **plural noun** or as a **verb infinitive**.

In both cases, the morphological segmentation is identical, but the grammatical function of the suffix differs.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word consists of two morphemes:

1.  **`recht`** (Root / Lexeme)
2.  **`-en`** (Suffix)

---

### 2. Analysis by Grammatical Category

Because the segmentation is the same, we must look at the syntactic context to determine the meaning.

#### A. As a Noun (Plural)
*   **Context:** *De mensenrechten* (The human rights).
*   **Morpheme 1: `recht`**
    *   **Type:** Free morpheme (Root).
    *   **Category:** Noun (Neuter: *het recht*).
    *   **Meaning:** "Right," "Law," or "Justice." In the singular (*het recht*), it often refers to the abstract concept of law or a specific entitlement.
*   **Morpheme 2: `-en`**
    *   **Type:** Bound morpheme (Inflectional suffix).
    *   **Function:** **Plural Marker.**
    *   **Explanation:** In Dutch, the most common way to pluralize a noun is by adding *-en* (e.g., *hond* → *honden*). Here, it changes the singular *recht* into the plural *rechten* (rights).
*   **Full Meaning:** "Rights" (specific entitlements or laws).

#### B. As a Verb (Infinitive)
*   **Context:** *Ik wil de schroef rechten.* (I want to straighten the screw.)
*   **Morpheme 1: `recht`**
    *   **Type:** Free morpheme (Stem).
    *   **Category:** Verb Stem.
    *   **Meaning:** Derived from the adjective *recht* ("straight" or "correct").
*   **Morpheme 2: `-en`**
    *   **Type:** Bound morpheme (Inflectional suffix).
    *   **Function:** **Infinitive Marker.**
    *   **Explanation:** In Dutch, the citation form (dictionary form) of almost all verbs ends in *-en* (e.g., *lopen*, *werken*, *rechten*). It marks the verb as non-finite.
*   **Full Meaning:** "To straighten," "to correct," or "to right" (make straight).

---

### 3. Etymological & Semantic Connection
Linguistically, it is interesting to note the semantic link between the two meanings, which stems from Proto-Germanic.

*   **Root:** The root **`recht`** comes from the Proto-Germanic **\*rehtaz**, meaning "straight" or "correct."
*   **Semantic Shift:**
    1.  **Physical:** Straight (Adjective: *recht*).
    2.  **Moral/Legal:** What is "straight" is considered "correct" or "just." Hence, *recht* evolved to mean "law" or "right" (Noun).
    3.  **Action:** To make something "straight" or "correct" becomes the verb *rechten*.

This is similar to the English word **"right,"** which functions as an adjective (straight/correct), a noun (entitlement), and a verb (to correct/straighten).

### Conclusion
From a morphological standpoint, **`rechten`** is a **monomorphemic root** (`recht`) combined with a **bound inflectional suffix** (`-en`). The linguistic challenge lies in **disambiguation**: the suffix `-en` serves a **nominal function** (pluralization) in one context and a **verbal function** (infinitive marking) in the other.
