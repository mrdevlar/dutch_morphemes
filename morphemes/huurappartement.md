From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`huurappartement`** is a **compound noun** (*samengesteld zelfstandig naamwoord*). It is formed by combining two distinct lexical units (morphemes) to create a new, specific meaning.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Segmentation
The word can be segmented into two primary morphemes:

> **huur** + **appartement**

There is no linking element (such as an *-s-* or *-e-*) between the two parts, which is common in Dutch compounds where the first element ends in a vowel or specific consonant clusters, though often an *-s-* is added (e.g., *fiets* + *bel* → *fietsbel*). In this specific case, the juxtaposition is direct.

---

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### A. `huur`
*   **Type:** Free Morpheme (can stand alone as a word).
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun (*de huur*) or Verb Stem (*huren* - to rent).
*   **Function in Compound:** Nominal Modifier. It modifies the head noun to specify the *type* or *status* of the object.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *hūr* and Middle Dutch *huur*. It stems from the Proto-Germanic root **\*hīrō*.
    *   **Cognate:** It is etymologically related to the English word **"hire"**.
*   **Meaning:** Rent, lease, or the act of renting.
*   **Semantic Role:** In this compound, it indicates that the apartment is not owned by the occupant but is leased from a landlord.

#### B. `appartement`
*   **Type:** Free Morpheme (treated as a single lexical unit in modern Dutch).
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun (*het appartement*).
*   **Function in Compound:** Head of the Compound. The entire word refers to a type of *appartement*.
*   **Etymology:** A loanword from French *appartement*.
    *   **Internal Etymology:** Historically, the French word comes from *partir* (to divide/separate) + the suffix *-ment*. It originally referred to a "separate part" of a building.
    *   **Dutch Status:** While etymologically complex, in modern Dutch morphology, *appartement* is **lexicalized**. Dutch speakers do not actively decompose it into *apart* + *ment* when processing the word; it is treated as one atomic morpheme.
*   **Meaning:** A self-contained housing unit that occupies part of a building.

---

### 3. Syntactic and Semantic Structure

#### Compound Type: Endocentric
This is an **endocentric compound**. This means the meaning of the whole word is a hyponym (a specific subtype) of the head.
*   **Head:** *appartement* (The core category).
*   **Modifier:** *huur* (The specific characteristic).
*   **Logic:** A *huurappartement* is a type of *appartement*.

#### Semantic Contrast
The morpheme *huur* serves to distinguish this noun from its antonym in the housing market:
*   **`huurappartement`** (Rental apartment)
*   **`koopappartement`** (Owned apartment / Condo)

#### Orthography
In Dutch, compound nouns are typically written as one word (closed compounds). Therefore, *huur* and *appartement* are fused without a space or hyphen.
