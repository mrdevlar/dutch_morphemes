From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`familielid`** (meaning "family member") is a **compound noun**. It is formed through the process of **compounding**, where two independent words are joined to create a new lexical unit with a specific meaning.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation:

### 1. Macro-Structure (The Compound)
The word consists of two primary morphemes joined together:
*   **`familie`** (Modifier)
*   **`lid`** (Head)

**Type of Compound:** Endocentric.
*   In an endocentric compound, the meaning of the whole word is a hyponym (a specific type) of the head.
*   A `familielid` is a type of `lid` (member).
*   The second element (`lid`) determines the grammatical category (noun) and the core meaning, while the first element (`familie`) specifies *what kind* of member it is.

---

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### Morpheme A: `familie`
*   **Status:** Free Morpheme (can stand alone as a word).
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun.
*   **Meaning:** "Family."
*   **Etymology:** This is a loanword from Latin (*familia*), entering Dutch via French (*famille*).
*   **Internal Morphology (Derivation):**
    *   While treated as a single unit in this compound, `familie` itself is historically derived.
    *   **Root:** `famil-` (Latin stem).
    *   **Suffix:** `-ie` (A Dutch nominalizing suffix, often used to turn foreign stems into Dutch nouns, e.g., *academie*, *industrie*).
    *   *Note:* In the context of `familielid`, we generally treat `familie` as a single lexical morpheme, but recognizing the suffix `-ie` helps explain the spelling.

#### Morpheme B: `lid`
*   **Status:** Free Morpheme (can stand alone as a word).
*   **Part of Speech:** Noun.
*   **Meaning:** "Member" (of a group) or "Limb" (of a body). In this context, it means "member."
*   **Etymology:** Germanic origin (Proto-Germanic *\*līdą*).
*   **Cognates:** It is distantly related to the English word **"limb"** (via semantic shift from "part of a body" to "part of a group"). It is *not* related to the English word "lid" (eyelid/cover), which comes from a different Germanic root.
*   **Grammatical Behavior:** `Lid` is an irregular noun regarding its pluralization.
    *   Singular: `lid`
    *   Plural: `leden` (This is a suppletive or strong plural form).
    *   Therefore, the plural of `familielid` is **`familielieden`**.

---

### 3. Morphophonology & Orthography

*   **Linking Element:** There is **no linking element** (interfix) between the two morphemes.
    *   In Dutch, compounds sometimes use an *-s-* (e.g., *liefdes*verklaring) or an *-e-* (e.g., *kinder*boek).
    *   Here, `familie` ends in a vowel (`e`) and `lid` begins with a consonant (`l`), so they are simply concatenated: `familie` + `lid`.
*   **Stress Pattern:** Dutch compounds typically carry primary stress on the **first element**.
    *   Pronunciation: **`FAM`**-i-li-lid.
    *   This distinguishes it from a phrase where stress might fall differently, though in this specific case, the distinction is subtle.

### Linguistic Takeaway
The word `familielid` is a classic example of **Dutch nominal compounding**. It demonstrates how Dutch combines a loanword (`familie`) with a native Germanic word (`lid`) to create a precise term. The lack of a linking consonant and the retention of the final `-e` in `familie` are standard orthographic rules for this specific combination.
