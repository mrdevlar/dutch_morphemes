From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`veiligheid`** (meaning "safety" or "security") is a classic example of **derivational morphology**. It is formed by combining a lexical root with a derivational suffix to change the part of speech and meaning.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes:

### 1. Segmentation
The word can be segmented into two distinct morphemes:
> **`veilig`** + **`-heid`**

---

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### **Morpheme 1: `veilig`**
*   **Form:** *veilig*
*   **Part of Speech:** Adjective (Stam/Root)
*   **Meaning:** "Safe," "secure," or "free from danger."
*   **Etymology:**
    *   Derived from Middle Dutch *veilich*.
    *   It is related to the concept of being "free" or "unharmed." Some etymologists link it to the Old High German *feil* (free, available) or a root implying protection.
    *   **Phonology:** In standard Dutch, the *g* is pronounced as a voiceless velar fricative (similar to the *ch* in Scottish "loch" or German "Bach"), represented phonetically as /x/.
*   **Function:** This is the **base** or **stem** of the word. It carries the core semantic content (the concept of safety).

#### **Morpheme 2: `-heid`**
*   **Form:** *-heid*
*   **Part of Speech:** Derivational Suffix (Nominalizer)
*   **Meaning:** Indicates a state, condition, quality, or abstract noun.
*   **Function:** This suffix transforms the adjective *veilig* (safe) into an abstract noun *veiligheid* (the state of being safe).
*   **Etymology:**
    *   Derived from Middle Dutch *heit*, Old Dutch *heit*.
    *   Traces back to Proto-Germanic *\*-haiti* (meaning "command, order, condition").
    *   **Cognates:** It is directly cognate with the English suffix **-hood** (e.g., *childhood*, *brotherhood*) and the German suffix **-heit** (e.g., *Freiheit* = freedom).
*   **Phonology:**
    *   **Final Devoicing:** In Dutch phonology, voiced consonants at the end of a word are devoiced. Therefore, although spelled with a **d**, the suffix is pronounced as a **t** (/t/).
    *   Pronunciation of the whole word: /ˈʋɛi̯.ləx.ɦɛi̯t/

---

### 3. Semantic Construction
The meaning is constructed through **composition**:

1.  **`veilig`** (Safe) + **`-heid`** (State of being)
2.  **Literal:** "The state of being safe."
3.  **Lexicalized Meaning:** "Safety" or "Security."

### 4. Linguistic Context & Usage
*   **Productivity:** The suffix *-heid* is highly productive in Dutch. It can be attached to almost any adjective to create a noun (e.g., *snel* (fast) → *snelheid* (speed); *moe* (tired) → *moeheid* (fatigue)).
*   **Compounding:** Because *veiligheid* is a noun, it can serve as the head of a compound noun.
    *   *Verkeers* + *veiligheid* = **Verkeersveiligheid** (Traffic safety).
    *   *Brand* + *veiligheid* = **Brandveiligheid** (Fire safety).
*   **Pluralization:** While abstract nouns are often uncountable, *veiligheid* can take a plural form *veiligheden* in specific contexts (e.g., referring to different types or instances of safety measures), though it is most commonly used as a singular mass noun.
