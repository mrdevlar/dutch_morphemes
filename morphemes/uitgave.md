From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`uitgave`** is a complex noun formed through derivational morphology. It is derived from a separable verb.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes, their functions, and the phonological changes involved.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
At the surface level, the word can be segmented as:
> **`uit`** + **`gave`**

However, to understand the underlying structure, we must look at the verb from which it is derived: **`uitgeven`** (to publish / to spend / to give out).

Therefore, the deeper morphological structure is:
> **`uit`** (prefix) + **`geven`** (root verb) + **`-e`** (nominalizing suffix)

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### A. `uit` (Prefix / Particle)
*   **Type:** Separable prefix (part of a separable verb).
*   **Etymology:** From Old Dutch *ut*, Proto-Germanic *\*ut*.
*   **Meaning:** "Out," "outward," or "forth."
*   **Function:** It adds a directional or completive aspect to the root verb. In this context, it implies an action directed outward (giving something out to the public or giving money out).
*   **Grammar:** In the infinitive verb (*uitgeven*), it is attached. In the past tense or main clause, it separates (e.g., *Hij geeft het boek **uit***). In the noun form (*uitgave*), it remains attached.

#### B. `gave` (Nominalized Root)
This is the most complex part of the word because it involves a stem change.

*   **Root:** **`geven`** (verb: "to give").
    *   *Etymology:* Old Dutch *gefan*, Proto-Germanic *\*gebaną*.
*   **Suffix:** **`-e`** (Nominalizer).
    *   This suffix turns the verb into a noun (a deverbal noun).
*   **Morphophonology (The Vowel Change):**
    *   Notice that the root is *geven* (with an **e**), but the noun form is *gave* (with an **a**).
    *   This is a historical **ablaut** or stem alternation common in Dutch nominalizations. When the verb *geven* is nominalized, the stem vowel shifts from *e* to *a*.
    *   *Example:* *geven* (to give) $\rightarrow$ *gave* (the gift/act of giving).
    *   *Note:* The standalone noun *gave* exists in Dutch meaning "talent" or "gift" (e.g., *een gave spreker* - a gifted speaker). In *uitgave*, it retains the literal sense of "the act of giving out."

### 3. Semantic Composition
The meaning of the compound is compositional but has undergone semantic specialization:

1.  **Literal Meaning:** "The act of giving out" (*uit* + *gave*).
2.  **Specialized Meaning 1 (Publishing):** "Edition" or "Issue."
    *   *Logic:* A book or magazine is something "given out" to the public.
    *   *Example:* *De eerste uitgave van dit tijdschrift.* (The first issue of this magazine.)
3.  **Specialized Meaning 2 (Finance):** "Expenditure" or "Expense."
    *   *Logic:* Money that is "given out" from a budget.
    *   *Example:* *De uitgaven van de maand.* (The expenses of the month.)

### 4. Related Forms & Paradigm
To fully understand the morphology, it helps to see the word in its family:

*   **Verb:** *uitgeven* (to publish/spend).
*   **Noun (Singular):** *de uitgave* (the edition/expense).
*   **Noun (Plural):** *de uitgaven* (the editions/expenses).
    *   *Morpheme:* *-en* (plural suffix).
*   **Agent Noun:** *de uitgever* (the publisher).
    *   *Morpheme:* *-er* (agentive suffix).
*   **Related Noun:** *de gave* (the talent/gift).
    *   *Note:* While etymologically linked, in modern Dutch, *gave* usually refers to a talent, whereas *uitgave* refers to publication or cost.

### Conclusion
Linguistically, **`uitgave`** is a **deverbal noun** derived from the separable verb **`uitgeven`**. It consists of the directional prefix **`uit`** and the nominalized stem of **`geven`**, which undergoes a vowel shift (*e* to *a*) during the nominalization process.
