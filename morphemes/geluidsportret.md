### 2. Morpheme Analysis

*   **Category:** Noun.

#### B. `-s-` (Bound Morpheme / Interfix)
*   **Category:** Linking element (often called a *voegklank* or interfix).
*   **Meaning:** It carries **no semantic meaning** on its own.
*   **Function:** It serves a phonological and morphological purpose. In Dutch compounding, when two nouns are joined, a linking element is often inserted to facilitate pronunciation or to mark the relationship between the words.
    *   Historically, this was often a genitive *'s* (possessive), but in modern synchronic Dutch morphology, it is treated as a neutral linker.
    *   It distinguishes the compound from a plural form. For example, *geluiden* (sounds) + *portret* would look different. Here, *geluid* remains singular.
*   **Linguistic Note:** This is a classic example of a **Fugen-s** (linking s).

#### C. `portret` (Free Morpheme / Head)
*   **Category:** Noun.
*   **Meaning:** "Portrait."
*   **Etymology:** A loanword from French (*portrait*), derived from the verb *portraire* (to draw forth/depict).
*   **Function in Compound:** It acts as the **head** (the second constituent). In Dutch endocentric compounds, the head determines the grammatical category and the core meaning of the whole word.
    *   Because the head is *portret*, the whole word is a noun.
    *   Because the head is *portret*, the whole word is a *type* of portrait.

### 3. Syntactic and Semantic Structure

**Type of Compound:** Endocentric Noun-Noun Compound (*Zelfstandig naamwoord + Zelfstandig naamwoord*).

*   **Head:** *portret*
*   **Modifier:** *geluid*
*   **Relationship:** "A portrait **made of** sound."

In linguistics, this is a **semantic modification**. The modifier (*geluid*) restricts the meaning of the head (*portret*). A standard portrait is visual; a *geluidsportret* is auditory.
