From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`zingen`** (meaning "to sing") is a verb in its **infinitive** form.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation of its components.

### 1. Morphemic Segmentation
The word consists of **two morphemes**:

> **`zing`** + **`-en`**

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Root/Stem: `zing-`
*   **Type:** Lexical Root (Free/Bound Root)
*   **Function:** This is the core semantic unit of the word. It carries the primary meaning: "to produce musical sounds with the voice."
*   **Phonology:** In standard Dutch, this is pronounced /zɪŋ/.
    *   **`z`**: Voiced alveolar fricative.
    *   **`i`**: Short vowel /ɪ/ (as in English "bit").
    *   **`ng`**: Voiced velar nasal /ŋ/.
*   **Morphological Status:** In isolation, `zing-` cannot function as a complete verb in modern Dutch; it requires a suffix to indicate tense, mood, or grammatical form. Therefore, in this context, it acts as a bound root or a verb stem.
*   **Verb Class:** This root belongs to the class of **strong verbs** (sterke werkwoorden). This is linguistically significant because the vowel in the root undergoes **ablaut** (vowel gradation) in different tenses.
    *   *Infinitive:* **zing**en
    *   *Past Singular:* **zong** (vowel change /ɪ/ → /ɔ/)
    *   *Past Participle:* ge**zon**gen

#### B. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix (Infinitive Marker)
*   **Function:** This morpheme marks the verb as an **infinitive**. In Dutch grammar, the infinitive is the citation form (the form you find in a dictionary).
*   **Grammar:**
    *   It distinguishes the verb from the noun form (though Dutch often uses the infinitive as a noun, e.g., *het zingen* "the singing").
    *   It distinguishes the verb from other conjugated forms. For example, in the first person singular present tense ("I sing"), the suffix is dropped: *ik zing*.
*   **Phonology:** Pronounced as a schwa /ə/ followed by a nasal /n/ (often realized as /ən/).

### 4. Linguistic Context & Etymology

*   **Germanic Heritage:** The word is cognate with English *sing*, German *singen*, and Old English *singan*.
*   **Proto-Germanic:** It derives from **\*singwaną**.
*   **Morphological Process:** The formation of the infinitive in Dutch is a regular **agglutinative** process. You take the verb stem and attach the standard infinitive ending *-en*.
    *   *Example:* *werk* (work) + *en* = *werken*.
    *   *Example:* *loop* (walk) + *en* = *lopen*.
    *   *Example:* *zing* (sing) + *en* = *zingen*.

### 5. Important Note on Homophony
Linguistically, it is important to note that the form `zingen` is **homophonous** (sounds the same) with the **plural present tense** form.
*   **Infinitive:** *Ik wil **zingen**.* (I want to sing.)
*   **Plural Present:** *Wij **zingen**.* (We sing.)

While the surface form is identical, the morphological analysis differs slightly based on syntax:
1.  In the **infinitive**, `-en` is the lexical infinitive marker.
2.  In the **plural present**, the stem is `zing-` and `-en` is the **person/number agreement suffix** (indicating 1st/2nd/3rd person plural).

However, when analyzing the word `zingen` as a standalone dictionary entry, it is treated as **Root + Infinitive Suffix**.
