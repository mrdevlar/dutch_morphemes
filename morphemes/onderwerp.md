From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`onderwerp`** is a compound noun formed through a process known as a **calque** (loan translation). It is composed of two distinct morphemes.

Here is the breakdown and explanation of the morphemes:

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented as follows:
> **onder** + **werp**

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### **Morpheme 1: `onder-`**
*   **Type:** Prefix / Preposition.
*   **Literal Meaning:** "Under," "below," or "sub-".
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Proto-Germanic **\*under**.
*   **Cognates:** English *under*, German *unter*, Old Norse *undir*.
*   **Function:** In this context, it indicates subordination or position beneath something else.

#### **Morpheme 2: `-werp`**
*   **Type:** Root (Verbal Stem / Nominalized Past Participle).
*   **Source Verb:** *Werpen* (to throw).
*   **Literal Meaning:** "Thrown" or "Cast."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Proto-Germanic **\*werpaną**.
*   **Cognates:** English *warp* (originally "to throw"), German *werfen*.
*   **Form:** This is the stem of the past participle. In the verb *werpen*, the past participle is *geworpen*. In this compound, the suffix *-en* is dropped, leaving the stem *werp* to function as a noun.

### 3. Etymological Context (The Calque)
The most important linguistic feature of *onderwerp* is that it is a direct translation of the Latin term **`subiectum`**.

*   **Latin:** *sub-* (under) + *iacere* (to throw) + *-tum* (past participle suffix).
*   **Literal Latin Meaning:** "That which is thrown under."
*   **Dutch Translation:** *onder-* (under) + *werp* (thrown).

In medieval scholastic logic and grammar, the "subject" was conceptually viewed as the foundation upon which the predicate (what is said about the subject) is "thrown" or placed. The Dutch language adopted this conceptual metaphor by translating the Latin components into their Germanic equivalents.

### 4. Semantic Evolution
While the literal meaning is "under-thrown," the semantic meaning has shifted through metonymy and specialization:

1.  **Logic/Grammar:** Originally referred to the "subject" of a sentence or proposition (the entity the statement is about).
2.  **General Usage:** Extended to mean "topic," "theme," or "matter" (e.g., *het onderwerp van het gesprek* — the topic of the conversation).
3.  **Divergence:** There is a related verb, *onderwerpen* (to subject/subjugate). While *onderwerp* (noun) usually means "topic," the verb *onderwerpen* implies forcing something to be "under" something else (submission).

### 5. Summary Table

| Morpheme | Category | Meaning | Origin |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **onder-** | Prefix | Under / Sub- | Proto-Germanic *\*under* |
| **werp** | Root | Thrown / Cast | Proto-Germanic *\*werpaną* |

### 6. Related Forms
*   **Verb:** *onderwerpen* (to subject, to submit).
*   **Noun (Process):** *onderwerping* (subjection).
*   **Adjective:** *onderworpen* (subjugated, subjected).

In summary, **`onderwerp`** is a transparent compound where the meaning of the whole is derived from the sum of its parts ("under" + "thrown"), preserving a Latin grammatical metaphor within a Germanic morphological structure.
