From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`oppervlakkig`** (meaning *superficial* or *shallow*) is a complex word formed through derivation and compounding.

Here is the morphological breakdown and explanation of its components.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes (the smallest units of meaning):

> **`op`** + **`vlak``** + **`-ig`**

*(Note: The double `k` in the middle is an orthographic feature, not a separate morpheme. See section 3 below.)*

---

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. `op-` (Prefix / First part of Compound)
*   **Category:** Preposition / Prefix.
*   **Meaning:** "On," "upon," or "up."
*   **Function:** In this specific compound, it functions as the first element of a noun compound. It indicates position or location relative to the second element.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *up*, cognate with English *up* and German *auf*.

#### B. `vlak` (Root / Noun Stem)
*   **Category:** Noun (in this context) / Adjective (in isolation).
*   **Meaning:**
    *   As a standalone adjective: "Flat."
    *   As a noun (in this compound): "Plane," "level," or "surface."
*   **Function:** This is the lexical root of the word. It carries the core semantic weight regarding geometry or physical extent.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *vlac*, related to the English word *flack* (in the sense of a flat surface) and German *Fläche* (surface).
*   **Compound Formation:** `op` + `vlak` creates the noun **`oppervlak`** (surface). Literally, "the on-flat" or "the top plane."

#### C. `-ig` (Derivational Suffix)
*   **Category:** Adjectival Suffix.
*   **Meaning:** "Having the quality of," "characterized by," or "-y" / "-ish" (in English).
*   **Function:** This suffix converts the noun `oppervlak` into an adjective. It indicates that something possesses the characteristics of a surface.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Old Dutch *-ag*, cognate with English *-y* (as in *cloudy*) and German *-ig* (as in *oberflächlich*).

---

### 3. Orthographic Note: The Double `k`
You may notice the spelling is `oppervlak`**`k`**`ig` rather than `oppervlakig`.

*   **Phonological Rule:** In Dutch orthography, when a stem ends in a short vowel followed by a single consonant (like *vlak* /vlɑk/), and a vowel-initial suffix is added (like *-ig*), the final consonant is doubled.
*   **Purpose:** This doubling preserves the **short vowel sound** of the root. If spelled `oppervlakig`, a reader might incorrectly pronounce the `a` as a long vowel (/a:/) or shift the stress.
*   **Linguistic Status:** The second `k` is **not a separate morpheme**. It is a graphological marker indicating the phonology of the root `vlak`.

---

### 4. Semantic Development (Meaning Shift)
The meaning of the word has shifted from a literal physical description to a figurative abstract concept.

1.  **Literal Meaning:** "Surface-like." Something that exists only on the top layer (e.g., a superficial scratch).
2.  **Figurative Meaning:** "Superficial." This is the most common usage today. It describes knowledge, analysis, or personality that lacks depth, thoroughness, or substance.
    *   *Example:* "Een oppervlakkige kennis" (A superficial knowledge).

### 5. Summary of Structure
The morphological tree structure looks like this:

1.  **Level 1 (Compounding):** `op` + `vlak` → **`oppervlak`** (Noun: Surface)
2.  **Level 2 (Derivation):** `oppervlak` + `-ig` → **`oppervlakkig`** (Adjective: Superficial)

**Full Translation:** "Having the quality of a surface" → "Superficial."
