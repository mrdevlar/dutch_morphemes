From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`hoofdkussen`** is a **compound noun**. It consists of two distinct morphemes joined together to create a new lexical item.

Here is the morphological breakdown and analysis:

### 1. Segmentation
The word can be segmented into two free morphemes:
> **hoofd** + **kussen**

There is **no linking morpheme** (also known as a *fugue* or *interfix*) between the two parts. In Dutch, compounds often use an *-s-* (e.g., *liefdesbrief*) or *-en-* (e.g., *handenwerk*), but in this specific case, the roots are juxtaposed directly.

### 2. Morpheme Analysis

#### **Morpheme 1: `hoofd`**
*   **Type:** Free morpheme / Root.
*   **Grammatical Category:** Noun.
*   **Meaning:** "Head."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Proto-Germanic *\*haubidą*. Cognates include German *Haupt* and English *head* (though English *head* underwent a sound shift, while Dutch retained the *d*).
*   **Function in Compound:** It acts as the **modifier** (or dependent). It specifies the type or purpose of the head noun.

#### **Morpheme 2: `kussen`**
*   **Type:** Free morpheme / Root.
*   **Grammatical Category:** Noun.
*   **Meaning:** "Cushion" or "Pillow."
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *kussen*. Cognates include German *Kissen*.
    *   *Note on Homonymy:* In Dutch, *kussen* is also the infinitive verb meaning "to kiss." However, in this compound, it is strictly the noun.
*   **Function in Compound:** It acts as the **head** (or head noun).

### 3. Structural Analysis

*   **Compound Type:** **Endocentric Compound**.
    *   An endocentric compound is one where the meaning of the whole is a hyponym (a specific type) of the head. A *hoofdkussen* is a specific type of *kussen*.
    *   The grammatical category of the compound is determined by the head (*kussen* is a noun, so *hoofdkussen* is a noun).
*   **Word Order:** **Noun-Noun (N+N)**.
    *   Dutch compounds typically follow a modifier-head structure where the modifier comes first.
*   **Stress Pattern:**
    *   In Dutch compounds, the primary stress usually falls on the **first element**.
    *   Pronunciation: **`HOOFD`**-kussen (IPA: /ˈɦoːftˌkʏ.sə(n)/).
    *   This distinguishes it from a phrase where stress might be distributed differently.

### 4. Semantic Compositionality
The meaning of the compound is largely **compositional**, meaning the meaning of the whole can be derived from the sum of its parts:
*   *Hoofd* (Head) + *Kussen* (Cushion) = A cushion for the head.

However, over time, lexicalization can occur. While the meaning is transparent, *hoofdkussen* is the specific lexicalized term for "pillow" in Dutch, whereas *kussen* alone can refer to any cushion (sofa cushion, seat cushion, etc.).
