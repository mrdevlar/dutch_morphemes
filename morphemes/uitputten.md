From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`uitputten`** is a complex verb formed through derivational morphology. It is a **separable verb** (*scheidbaar werkwoord*) composed of a prefix, a root, and an infinitive suffix.

Here is the breakdown and explanation of its morphemes.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into three distinct morphemes:

> **`uit`** + **`put`** + **`-en`**

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. The Prefix: `uit`
*   **Type:** Separable Prefix (Particle).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Proto-Germanic **\*ut**, cognate with English *out* and German *aus*.
*   **Function:** It indicates direction or completion. In this context, it adds the meaning of "outward" or "completely."
*   **Grammatical Behavior:** As a separable prefix, `uit` detaches from the verb stem in main clauses (e.g., *Ik **put** mezelf **uit*** — "I exhaust myself"). In subordinate clauses or infinitives, it remains attached (e.g., *...dat ik mezelf **uitput***).

#### B. The Root: `put`
*   **Type:** Lexical Root (Noun-derived Verb).
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Middle Dutch *putte*, Old Dutch *putta*, and Proto-Germanic **\*puttaz**.
*   **Original Meaning:** "Well," "pit," or "hole." (Cognate with English *pit* and German *Pfütze* [puddle]).
*   **Verbalization:** In Dutch, nouns can easily be converted into verbs. The noun *de put* (the well) became the verb *putten* (to draw from a well / to dig).
*   **Orthography Note:** You will notice the root is spelled with a double **`tt`** in the infinitive (*putt**en***). This is not a separate morpheme; it is an orthographic rule in Dutch to preserve the short vowel sound of the root *put* (the "korte klank" rule). The underlying root is phonemically /pʏt/.

#### C. The Suffix: `-en`
*   **Type:** Inflectional Suffix.
*   **Function:** This is the standard marker for the **infinitive** form of Dutch verbs.
*   **Etymology:** Derived from Proto-Germanic **\*-an**, cognate with English *-en* (as in *to walk* vs. *walked*, though English has largely lost this infinitive marker).

### 3. Semantic Composition (Meaning Construction)
The meaning of the word is constructed through a metaphorical extension of its literal components:

1.  **Literal Meaning:** *uit* (out) + *put* (well) + *en* (verb).
    *   Literally: "To draw [water] **out** of a **well**."
2.  **Figurative Meaning:**
    *   If you draw water out of a well repeatedly, the well eventually runs dry.
    *   Therefore, *uitputten* evolved to mean "to drain," "to use up completely," or "to exhaust" (resources, energy, patience).

### 4. Grammatical Evidence (The Past Participle)
The morphological structure is most clearly revealed in the past participle form: **`uitgeput`**.

*   **`uit`**: The prefix.
*   **`ge-`**: The participial prefix (inserted between the separable prefix and the stem).
*   **`put`**: The root.
*   **`-t`**: The past participle suffix (for weak verbs).

The insertion of **`ge-`** confirms that `uit` is a separable prefix. If `uit` were an inseparable prefix (like *be-* in *bezorgen*), the participle would be *uitgeput* (incorrect) or simply *uitput* (incorrect). The standard form *uitgeput* proves the structure is **[Prefix] + [ge-] + [Stem] + [Suffix]**.
