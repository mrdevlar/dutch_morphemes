From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`eigenaar`** (meaning "owner" or "proprietor") is a **derived noun**. It is formed through derivational morphology, where a suffix is added to a base word to change its part of speech or meaning.

Here is the breakdown of the morphemes, their functions, and their etymological history.

### 1. Morphological Segmentation
The word can be segmented into two distinct morphemes:

> **`eigen`** + **`-aar`**

*   **`eigen`**: The root/base morpheme.
*   **`-aar`**: The derivational suffix.

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis of Morphemes

#### A. The Root: `eigen`
*   **Part of Speech:** Adjective (primarily).
*   **Meaning:** "Own," "one's own," "characteristic," or "peculiar."
*   **Function:** In this compound, it provides the semantic core. It denotes possession or belonging.
*   **Etymology:**
    *   Derived from Middle Dutch *eigen*.
    *   Traces back to Old Dutch *eigen*.
    *   Ultimately from Proto-Germanic **\*aigaz** (meaning "property" or "own").
    *   **Cognates:** English **own**, German **eigen**, Old Norse **eigen**.
*   **Phonology:** Pronounced /ˈɛiɣə(n)/. The *ei* is a diphthong, and the *g* is a voiced velar fricative (similar to the 'g' in Dutch *gaan* or a soft 'ch' in Scottish *loch*).

#### B. The Suffix: `-aar`
*   **Part of Speech:** Derivational Suffix (Agentive).
*   **Meaning:** Indicates a person who performs an action or holds a specific status/role.
*   **Function:** It nominalizes the adjective *eigen*. It transforms the concept of "being own" into "the person who owns."
*   **Etymology:**
    *   Derived from Middle Dutch *-are* or *-er*.
    *   Traces back to Proto-Germanic **\*-araz**.
    *   **Cognates:** English **-er** (as in *owner*, *worker*), German **-er** (as in *Besitzer* - though German uses a different root, the suffix function is identical).
*   **Linguistic Note:** In modern Dutch, the productive agentive suffix is usually **-er** (e.g., *verkoper* from *verkopen*). However, **-aar** is a lexicalized variant found in specific words like *eigenaar*, *onderdaan* (subject), and *onderwijzer* (teacher - though this uses -er, it shows the variation). In *eigenaar*, the suffix is fossilized as *-aar*.

---

### 3. Synthesis and Semantic Shift
When combined, the morphemes create a specific semantic meaning:

1.  **Literal Construction:** "One who is own" $\rightarrow$ "One who has what is own."
2.  **Semantic Shift:** Over time, the meaning narrowed from a general state of possession to the specific legal or social role of an **owner**.
3.  **Stress Pattern:** The primary stress falls on the first syllable of the root: **`EI`**-gen-aar (/ˈɛi.ɣəˌnaːr/). This is typical for Dutch compounds and derivatives where the root carries the primary semantic weight.

### 4. Cross-Linguistic Comparison (English)
The morphological structure of *eigenaar* is nearly identical to its English cognate, **owner**.

| Feature | Dutch (`eigenaar`) | English (`owner`) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Root** | `eigen` (own) | `own` |
| **Suffix** | `-aar` (agentive) | `-er` (agentive) |
| **Structure** | Adjective + Agent Suffix | Verb/Adj + Agent Suffix |

*Note: While English uses the suffix `-er` (from Proto-Germanic *\*-araz*), Dutch uses the variant `-aar` in this specific lexical item. Both suffixes share the same Proto-Germanic ancestor.*

### 5. Summary
*   **Word:** `eigenaar`
*   **Type:** Derived Noun
*   **Morphemes:** `eigen` (Root: "own") + `-aar` (Suffix: "person who")
*   **Translation:** Owner
*   **Linguistic Classification:** Agentive derivation from an adjectival stem.
