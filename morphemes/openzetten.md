From a linguistic perspective, the Dutch word **`openzetten`** is a compound verb consisting of three distinct morphemes. It is a classic example of a **separable verb** (*scheidbaar werkwoord*) in Dutch morphology.

Here is the breakdown and explanation of the morphemes:

### 1. Morphemic Segmentation
The word can be segmented as follows:
> **open** + **zet** + **-en**

### 2. Detailed Morpheme Analysis

#### A. `open` (Free Morpheme / Particle)
*   **Category:** Originally an **adjective** (*bijvoeglijk naamwoord*), functioning here as a **verbal particle** (*werkwoordelijk deelwoord*).
*   **Meaning:** "Open" (not closed).
*   **Function:** In this compound, it acts as a **separable prefix**. It modifies the meaning of the main verb to indicate the *result* or *state* achieved by the action.
*   **Linguistic Note:** Unlike a standard prefix (like *be-* or *ver-* which are bound and inseparable), `open` retains its lexical independence. It can stand alone as an adjective (*De deur is open* / "The door is open"). In the verb complex, it carries the semantic weight of the resulting state.

#### B. `zet` (Bound Morpheme / Verb Stem)
*   **Category:** **Lexical Root** (Stem of the verb *zetten*).
*   **Meaning:** "To put," "to set," or "to place."
*   **Function:** This is the core action of the verb. It provides the dynamic component of the meaning.
*   **Morphology:** This is the stem used for the present tense (e.g., *ik zet* / "I set") and the imperative. It is a **weak verb** stem (regular conjugation in the past tense: *zette*).

#### C. `-en` (Bound Morpheme / Inflectional Suffix)
*   **Category:** **Inflectional Suffix**.
*   **Function:** This suffix marks the verb as the **infinitive** form.
*   **Linguistic Note:** In Dutch, the infinitive marker is almost always *-en* (or *-n* after a vowel). It does not change the lexical meaning of the verb but indicates its grammatical form (non-finite).

---

### 3. Syntactic Behavior: The Separable Verb
From a syntactic perspective, `openzetten` is a **separable compound verb**. This is a defining feature of Dutch morphology.

*   **In the Infinitive:** The morphemes stay together.
    *   *Ik ga de kraan **openzetten**.* (I am going to turn on the tap.)
*   **In a Main Clause:** The particle `open` separates from the stem `zet` and moves to the end of the clause.
    *   *Ik **zet** de kraan **open**.* (I turn the tap open.)
    *   *Structure:* Subject + **Stem** + Object + **Particle**.

### 4. Semantic Composition (Resultative Construction)
Linguistically, `openzetten` is a **resultative verb**.
*   **`zetten`** implies an action of placing or positioning.
*   **`open`** implies the resulting state.
*   **Combined:** The action of "setting" is performed specifically to achieve the state of "openness."

This is similar to English phrasal verbs like "set open" or "open up," though Dutch is more systematic in treating these as single lexical units in the dictionary.

### 6. Related Forms
To fully understand the morphology, it helps to see how these morphemes behave in other tenses:
*   **Past Participle:** *ge-open-zet-t* $\rightarrow$ **`opengezet`** (The prefix *ge-* is inserted between the particle and the stem).
*   **Present Tense:** *Ik **zet** ... **open*** (Stem conjugated, particle separated).
*   **Noun Derivation:** *het **openzetten*** (The infinitive nominalized with the article *het*).
